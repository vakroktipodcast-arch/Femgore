**Verdict:** REVISE

**Findings** (most serious first)

1. **The suite-side thumb-turn breaks the Service's reason for the seal.** "On the suite side there was a thumb-turn, small and plain". The advance sweep goes through the room before the principal arrives. Any agent who found a working thumb-turn on the inside of the room's second door would throw it and seal the corridor face as well. Ledger, Places, Alder Suite (l. 424), gives the reason for the seal as "the hotel can't lock it from the corridor side". That reason doesn't hold if the door can be locked from inside. A protective-detail reader will stop here and ask why the door was open to her at all, and the only answer the page offers is luck. **Fix:** add one clause, sourced to the security annex she lifted on Sat 24 Oct (ledger, Timeline, l. 249), saying the hotel required the pantry door kept off the lock from the suite side, for example as the servers' way in or the fire marshal's second exit, so the Service sealed it instead. Iris noticing this when she turns the thumb-turn ("the annex had said it would be unlocked; I locked it") turns luck into research. Log it under `## New facts` in `beats/ch22.md` (CLAUDE.md rule 3).

2. **The lift plan was never "clean", and Iris would know that.** "could be laid back down afterwards and read… as a sticker nobody had touched". A VOID label leaves its legend in the adhesive however carefully it is lifted, and warming a blade in a gloved fist adds almost no heat. Iris seals evidence bags with this same make (ledger, Objects, l. 351), and Ch 21 has her say flatly "I knew what it would do. It would say so." As written, the Ch 22 plan contradicts Ch 21 and makes a careful woman look naive. **Fix:** make the plan modest. A lift would leave the VOID ghosted faintly in the adhesive, readable to anyone who looked closely but not to a glance down a corridor, whereas a cut shows to the glance. That keeps the Turn ("a shorter clock") and makes it a judgement she has to make, not a surprise. Drop "warmed in my closed fist" or make it "slid flat".

3. **The count doesn't fit the actions on the page.** "Thirteen. The scrap of label in my pocket." The ledger (Fixed numbers, l. 296) has: door locked about 9, Holt looks up about 12, chapter ends about 15. Between the lock and the look-up the prose has her look through the hinge, peel the scrap off a nitrile fingertip, pocket it and walk four paces of damp carpet, which is at least 3–4 s of physical action. After "Thirteen" he stands, does the cuff turn with eyes down for a fixed two seconds (ledger, l. 134), says a nine-word line and then waits. That brings the chapter end to about 19–20, not 15. A reader who is tracking the clock, as the chapter asks them to, will notice. **Fix:** move the scrap-peeling into the four paces ("I peeled it off as I walked"), and change "Thirteen" to "Fifteen". Flag the true end figure (about 19) to the continuity agent so Ch 23's 40 s and 70 s still hold. Ch 23 has plenty of slack.

4. **Habit contradiction.** "I looked first. I never look first." S1 has already had her look into the room through the hinge before she smells it ("I smelled the room once I had stopped moving"). **Fix:** in S2, narrow the claim to the face: "I had looked at the room. I had never let myself look at him first." Or swap S1's order so the smell comes before the hinge view.

5. **The Duchenne claim is overstated.** "That second muscle is the one you can't do on purpose." The lateral orbicularis oculi is hard to fire voluntarily for most people, but not for everyone, and trained performers in particular can. A pathologist hedges. **Fix:** "the one most people can't do on purpose." It still lands, and "by every sign I know how to read" already carries the doubt.

6. **Pupillometry at table distance.** "The pupils didn't change. In the lamp they were small". From across a skirted banquet table under one lamp, a half-second startle dilation is at the edge of what anyone can see. **Fix:** add "as far as I could see them from there", or "his face was turned into the lamp". One clause.

7. **Minor: "I have sealed several hundred of them".** At about 1,400 autopsies, with several bags on each homicide, the number is in the thousands. **Fix:** "more of them than I could count", or "thousands".
