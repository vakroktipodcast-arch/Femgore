- **Verdict:** PASS. Appetite is at the right Act III stage: hungry going in, nothing in the mouth at the Turn. The body carries a chapter with no violence, and she never apologises for the hunger or moralises about it.

- **Findings:** (minor and optional. Nothing here blocks the chapter.)
  1. "I was thinking. That was all the touch meant, and I let it mean it." This explains the wound motif out loud when the touch could carry it alone (style/05, wound: "She touches it when thinking"; style/04 pt 13, everything through her, nothing annotated). Cut the second sentence and keep "I was thinking."
  2. "I report it because it is what I saw. I don't have anything to add to it." With "I noted it. I let it go." and "I am only showing you where it was.", that makes three disclaimers to the reader in two scenes. They start to sound like she is excusing herself, which works against the no-apology rule (strategy/02, voice; style/12, The Test). Keep "I am only showing you where it was." It does the most work, because it is the withheld joke. Cut "I don't have anything to add to it."

- **What works:**
  1. "I looked first. I never look first." This flips the bible's smell-first, sight-last order (style/04 pt 5) exactly where the appetite fails. Keep it.
  2. The appetite is shown in the body and then denied: "Not in the stomach... behind the face". Then "Not even the coin at the back of the tongue". And "the faint sweetness of a body that had been burning itself since Sunday morning". This is Act III, starved (strategy/10), done physically and without self-pity.
  3. The humour still carries a scene with no violence. Lines: "thought well of its honesty", "pleased to see me", "the kind of person who reads labels", and the network joke she sees and withholds. Do not explain any of them further.
