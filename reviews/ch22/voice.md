- **Verdict:** REVISE. The voice is right and the narrator is the same woman. The fixes below are line-level: places where she explains herself, and a few stock thriller beats.

- **Findings:**

1. "I am telling you that. I can hear how small my voice has got." (scene 3). This breaks 01 point 1: direct address is for implicating, never for explaining. It also goes against the spirit of 11 last point: show the absence, don't name it. Here she comments on her own diminishment, which is the job the prose is already doing. Cut the second sentence. Keep "The syllabus began with his recognition." and let the paragraph stop there, or end it on "I had no line for this."

2. "In Brandt's suite I could have gone on for a page." (scene 1, smell paragraph). This breaks 02 point 3 and 11 last point. It tells the reader that the senses have thinned. The short smell list already shows it, which is how ch25 handles "The room smelled of the carpet cleaner... and under that, of him." Cut the sentence.

3. "A long moment." (opening of scene 3). This breaks the any-other-novel test. It is a stock beat-filler and it opens a scene. Cut it and open on "I stood across the table from him and waited."

4. "The room filled the silence for me." / "He was good at waiting." / "My clock had become a shorter clock." All three are generic thriller cadence. Cut the first and go straight to the inventory: "The drums through the wall." For the second, make it specific to Holt, for example "He waited the way he waited at a lectern, with interest." For the third, cut it, because "Someone would see it." already lands the Turn.

5. "Then I put my shoulder to the door... and the scar..." (scene 1, para 3). This breaks 02 point 1: short declaratives for action. It is the most time-critical action in the chapter, but it runs as two long "and... and... and" chains with the count already at five. Break it into short sentences at "Then I put my shoulder to the door." and at "The guard went back on the blade by feel." Keep the plaster simile as its own sentence.

6. "I ran the ways out the way I'd run a differential" (scene 1). The paragraph drifts into procedural thriller logistics ("widen the cordon", "a man with an earpiece") and spells out the escape geometry. That is explanation, against 01 point 1 and 02 point 1. Keep the differential frame and the final line ("ruled out by the walls"). Compress the middle to two clipped sentences: one door goes through the detail, the other is clean only until the minute ends.

7. "Behind me the service door was locked... he didn't know that either." and "I could see where *network* had come from... kind about it." (scene 3). These restate points already made: the locked doors in scene 1, and the lanyard reading in scene 2. Both break 02 point 1 (explanation, not exactness). Cut the first paragraph. Cut the second paragraph down to "He had read the badge, and been kind about it."

8. "thought well of its honesty" (scene 1, para 1). This is a light leak of the evaluative voice in a chapter where 02 point 4 says it disappears completely. It is set earlier in the evening, so it can be defended, but it is the only grade in the chapter. Change it to "had noticed its honesty" so the absence stays clean.

- **What works:**
  - The missing mark: "reached for one the way you reach for a pen... nothing came to hand." This is exactly how 02 point 4 should be honoured, by showing the grading voice failing in the scene. The withheld joke ("I didn't make the joke. I am only showing you where it was.") does the same job for the thinning humour.
  - The smile reading. Zygomaticus and orbicularis oculi set against "it was for me, and it meant nothing" is the Latinate/plain collision at its best. The dry mouth ("Not even the coin at the back of the tongue") shows the lost appetite through the body, as 11 requires.
  - "I looked first. I never look first." and "He saw me. He did not recognise me." These are the right short declaratives at the right weight, and they invert ch17's "Smell first". Do not pad them.
