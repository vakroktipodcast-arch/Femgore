# Dent review — ch22 (The Point of No Return)

- **Verdict:** REVISE. All three Turns and the Snapper land as events or admissions, but the chapter runs about 450 words over target and Scene 2 loses the act's pressure.

## Turn locations

- **Scene 1:** lands at "The sticker was torn. Someone would see it." (final paragraph, after the glove-scrap event). Present, and landed as an event.
- **Scene 2:** lands at "Then he smiled." and "I had been hungry for this for two years, and I found there was nothing in my mouth." Present, as an event and an admission. It is followed by a short coda (finding 5).
- **Scene 3:** lands at "I had no line for this. The syllabus began with his recognition." Present, as an admission. It runs straight into the Snapper, "Between us on the table, the Marine, facing him."

## Findings

1. **Over length, almost all of it in Scene 1.** By my estimate (no word-count tool was available) Scene 1 is about 1,500 words against the sheet's ~1,100, Scene 2 about 1,100, Scene 3 about 1,000, so the chapter is about 3,600 against ~3,150. Breaks style/10 point 1 ("nothing else may" run long except ch17). Fix: cut Scene 1 by about 400 words. Cut the evidence-bag backstory in "It was the same make I put on evidence bags" to one sentence. Trim the "I ran the ways out the way I'd run a differential" paragraph to its three load-bearing facts: two doors, the stair post steps back at forty, nothing else. Cut the "I was hungry. Not in the stomach" paragraph to two sentences. Cut "we were, for the moment, the only two people in the building who could say that".

2. **Scene 2 drops the act's pressure.** Strategy/03 menace bullet and style/10 point 4 require the named pressure (Reyes closing, the clock, the knowledge from 18) in every scene. Scene 1 has it ("a woman had a list with my name on it"). Scene 3 has it ("Thirteen. The scrap of label in my pocket"). Scene 2 has no clock, no detail and no Reyes. The only link is the page in the pocket, which is merely mentioned. Fix: add one clause in the "Through the wall the ballroom had gone up a notch" paragraph, for example the earpiece radio on the far side of the main door, or the count, so the shortening clock is audible while she waits for the second expression.

3. **Scene 3 does not shorten as it goes.** The beat sheet and the Turn ("how small my voice has got") need the sentences to contract across the scene. The middle of the scene is long: the viewing-room recognition paragraph (about 140 words), the stand-up and cufflink paragraph, and the "kindness" paragraph. Only the last few lines are short. Fix: cut the viewing-room paragraph by half, break the cufflink sequence into shorter sentences, and drop "Everybody who had ever worked for him said so." Then the Turn arrives as the end of a visible narrowing, not as three short sentences after long ones.

4. **Scene 1's Turn is partly spent before it lands.** The reader already hears the tear at "a small dry sound, a zip. It sounded exactly like a plaster coming off a heel", and "I heard it and kept going" names the damage. The scrap on the glove then confirms the tear but does not discover it. Before the final line the chapter also runs through the Reyes sentence, the hunger paragraph and the smell paragraph, which slow the approach to the Turn. Fix: once finding 1 is applied, keep the order glove-scrap, pocket, three-sentence Turn with nothing between the Reyes line and the glove. Consider moving the Reyes line to before the hunger paragraph so the scrap follows directly.

5. **Scene 2 ends on a coda, not on its Turn.** After "nothing in my mouth" come "No water came. No heat came up the throat. Not even the coin…" and the simile "as dry as the felt on the back of the frame." The beat sheet asks for one clause for the penny taste. The felt simile also pre-spends the Snapper's "back of the frame" image. Fix: end on "there was nothing in my mouth", with at most one clause about the coin, and cut the felt simile so the back of the frame belongs to the Snapper alone.

6. **The Snapper's object is not set up in this chapter.** "The Marine" appears for the first time in the last line. The chapter says only "Mark" and "his brother". A reader skimming Scene 2 will not connect "Marine" to the frame. Fix: add "in dress blues" or "the Marine" to the table description in Scene 2 (the paragraph "The table had been dressed by somebody…"), for example "I couldn't see the Marine. He could." The last two sentences stay as they are.

7. **Minor: Scene 3's near-close paragraph competes with the Snapper.** "Behind me the service door was locked… he didn't know that either" is a mini-cliffhanger beat (a trap-and-lock stinger) sitting two paragraphs before the close. The Snapper survives it, but it is the one place the chapter edges toward the thriller beat the Snapper rule forbids. Fix: cut it or fold it into the "Thirteen." line. It restates Scene 1's egress arithmetic.

## Other checks (pass)

- **Scene 1 trouble on the first page:** yes. The first sentence has her hand on the sticker at the count of five.
- **Hero rule:** passes. She improvises the cut when the lift plan fails, and she locks the thumb-turn herself. The screen, the click covered by the brass, and Holt not turning are ledger-fixed set-dressing, not rescue. The one near-convenient item is "He hadn't heard anything worth turning for", which is adequately motivated by the brass.
- **Appetite:** present (denied) in Scenes 1 and 2.
- **Snapper:** an image, not a cliffhanger.
- **First and last sentences:** both carry weight.

## What works

- The opening line ("My hand was flat on the sticker, and the count was at five") and the closing pair ("Between us on the table, the Marine, facing him. All I had of him was the back of the frame.").
- "He saw me. He did not recognise me." and "It didn't come." as isolated lines, with nothing around them.
- The declined joke ("I didn't make the joke. I am only showing you where it was.") and the missing grade ("nothing came to hand"), each used once and left uncommented.
