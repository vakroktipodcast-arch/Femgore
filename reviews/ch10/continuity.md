# Continuity review: Chapter 10

- **Verdict:** REVISE. The dates, durations, names, voicemail details and hand-offs to Ch 9, Ch 11, Ch 14 and Ch 18 all check out. Five line-level fixes are needed, one of which touches a ledger rule (hands).

## Findings

1. **Medium. S3, the ruling of the line.** "My hands were steadier than they had been all week." This breaks `ledger/continuity.md`, People, Iris, point 1: hands have not shaken since her second year of residency, with one fine tremor (Ch 2, left fingertips). "Steadier than" implies that earlier in the week they were less steady. Fix: "My hands were as steady as they had been all week, and I noticed what that cost" or "I watched them be steady", so the effort reads without a baseline wobble. Do not use "than".

2. **Low. S2, the closing reasoning paragraph.** "with the lamb going in the oven and the lemon on my fingers". The lamb is already in the oven earlier in the scene ("While it went I chopped the salsa verde"), and Theo's laptop comes "Later". Her reasoning therefore happens with the lamb already braising. This is an internal chronology slip. Fix: "with the lamb in the oven and the lemon on my fingers".

3. **Low. S3, the inventory.** "Nobody ever asks me for anything, and I know everything anyway." The same chapter has Tessa asking her for something ("If you can") and Mara asking her to talk about the word that leaked, both earlier that day. As written it is a flat contradiction inside the chapter. It does not breach the Ch 24 "first question" scope, which concerns the campaign and what she wants. Fix, keeping the beat's joke: "Nobody ever asks me what I know, and I know everything anyway." If the author wants the contradiction as self-deception, leave it, but then it needs the reader to see it.

4. **Low. S2, the voicemail.** Tessa "hadn't written the name down" and asks only for "the pathologist in the news, the woman". Nothing says how the message reached Iris's own desk mailbox, which Ch 18 relies on (county voicemail Archive folder, Iris's own). Fix: half a clause, for example "the switchboard had put her through to the only pathologist the news had named", or "she had been routed by whoever answered".

5. **Low. S2, pressing 7.** The handset is against her ear in one hand and "the container's lid in my other hand", so both hands are occupied, yet "my finger" presses 7. Ch 18's "both times I was busy" is served either way. Fix: name the hand, for example "I pressed 7 with the knuckle of the hand that held the lid", or move the lid to the crook of the handset arm.

6. **Low, cross-unit (for the Ch 11 reviser as much as this one). S3, the warehouse.** "I knew where it stood and nothing about what was in it." Ch 11 says she watched from outside on Thu 24 Sep, read the generator permit and found the occupier "Lantern Data Services" before Thu 1 Oct. The ledger puts the register chain on Thursday morning but not the occupier's name. If she already had "Data Services" by Wednesday night, "nothing" overstates. Fix: "and almost nothing about what was in it", or leave Ch 10 as written and keep the occupier lookup on the morning of 1 Oct in Ch 11. Ch 10 also omits the 24 Sep watch. That is consistent, and the beat sheet's own flag allows it.
