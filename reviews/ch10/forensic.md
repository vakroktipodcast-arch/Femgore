- **Findings:**

1. **"so it was mine to approve" / "quoting Dr. Iris Vale, the senior pathologist"** (S2). Iris's name becoming public on 29 Sep is fixed (ledger, Who knows what: "Iris's name is public from 29 Sep"; Objects: "Iris's ME statement: one line"). The way it happens is the problem. In an ME office the Chief or the PIO signs off press lines, not the senior reviewer. Even when they do sign, a staff pathologist's name does not reach a wire story because someone countersigned a no-comment line. Anyone who has worked in such an office will stop at this. *Fix:* add one clause showing the line went out *over her name*, as the attributed speaker. For example, the Chief wanted Mara kept out of it and his own name further out, so it went out over the reviewer's. Then "I signed it" means she is quoted, and "I was in the news for a sentence that said nothing" follows.

2. **"the message light on the desk phone was going"** (S2). Tessa did not know the name ("she hadn't written the name down"), yet her call lands on Iris's own extension. In a homicide that is in the news, a switchboard sends a call like that to the PIO or a log, not to a pathologist. As written, it is luck. A professional would also expect Iris to know that a call from someone the police are questioning in a case she reviewed has to be logged and passed to the investigating agency. Deleting it is a breach of procedure, not just a private choice. *Fix:* one clause on routing (the front desk, hearing "the pathologist in the news", put her through to the name on the wire story). Then one short line where Iris names the rule she is skipping, e.g. there is a form for calls like that, and it goes to the investigator. This makes the 7 more exact and more culpable. It doesn't soften the Turn.

3. **"My hands were steadier than they had been all week."** (S3). This implies her hands were *less* steady earlier in the week. That contradicts ledger, People/Iris: hands have not shaken since residency, with no tremor anywhere except the left fingertips in Ch 2. *Fix:* "My hand was as steady as it always is", or cut the sentence. A crack shown against a perfectly steady line is the stronger image anyway.

4. **"full still from lunch"** (S3). This is about twelve hours after a lunch of cold lamb. The stomach empties in four to six hours, and a pathologist who reads gastric contents for a living (Ch 8) knows that. The paragraph also leans on "I want to be exact… I am reporting it." *Fix:* "still not hungry", not "full". The unexplained loss of appetite is the finding, so leave it unexplained.

5. **"a small photograph of a woman's face"** then **"The pores across the nose"** (S1). Pores, a millimetre of alar asymmetry and white regrowth in a brow scar can't be seen in a gossip-site thumbnail. *Fix:* have the close reading happen on the full-size image from the cached archive copy or the image search ("at full size, on the clean machine"). Then the resolution supports what she sees.

6. **"I scrubbed back to the lunch hour and watched the War Room find the item"** (S1). The item went up at 6:00 a.m. A campaign comms shop reads its morning clips, and an anonymous site that ran blind items about campaign staff would be on that list well before lunch. Six hours' lag reads as a slow room. *Fix:* "the morning clips" or "the eight o'clock meeting". Nothing else changes.

7. **"the nib dragging very slightly where the card had softened"** (S3). The nib runs on the syllabus page, and the manila edge only guides it, so a softened guide can't make the nib drag. *Fix:* "where the paper had softened at the fold", or have the nib catch on the edge itself. This is a minor point.
