**Findings**

1. **Length (likely over target).** The beat sheet splits ~1,050 / ~1,100 / ~1,000 (3,150 total). My by-eye count is S1 ~1,250, S2 ~1,150 and S3 ~1,200, so roughly 3,500–3,700 in all. Style 10 point 1 says "nothing else may" run long (only Ch 17 may), and strategy 03 gives 1,000–1,100 per scene. I could not run the lint script, so check the real count. If it is over, trim S1 first. The tannin-pellicle simile ("like the pellicle on a wound") duplicates the later cold-table image. The Mara paragraph (the sinks, the gloves "inside out") and the counsel call ("in due course" twice in four minutes) can each lose a sentence. In S3, cut one of the two "in my professional experience"-type gags (the furniture line and the "moved up in the world" line).

2. **S3 Turn is partly buried.** Style 10 point 3 says every scene ends on its Turn, and the Turn here is the "check for flaws" paragraph. It comes after the witness paragraph ("The widow's peak and the parting... The hands."), and then the Snapper follows straight on with no scene break. The witness paragraph is the stronger beat. It plants the Reyes pressure, but it is a list in the same register as the grading paragraph before it. Fix: cut "I read it back to myself, and it would have fitted on half a page, with room for the signature" or move it before the grade, so the region-by-region check comes immediately after "The jaw. The widow's peak..." and the Turn reads as the escalation. Keep a line of white space before the Snapper line.

3. **S2 Turn is told, not struck.** "I thought it in a whole sentence... he had told me the truth while I was inside him" gives the sentence in indirect form, and "I said the sentence aloud" then summarises the speech. Per the outline, the Turn is the sentence said to the mirror to test whether it is a joke. As written, the reader never hears it spoken, so "It didn't." answers a line that was never delivered. Fix: put the sentence in quotation marks at the mirror ("He told me the truth while I was inside him.") and drop the indirect version above it. This keeps the admission as an event (style 10 point 3). "Brandt's gift, confirmed." can stay as the flat lead-in.

4. **Footage is a slightly convenient door.** Strategy 03 hero rule: no luck, no unlocked doors she didn't unlock. "a local station's raw livestream, which had started early and left its camera on a holding-area door" is happenstance. It is earned in part, because Brandt told her to watch the footage and she says "I had the patience." Add one clause showing she found it by searching (for example, that she went through the station's archive for exactly this angle), so the discovery is her competence and not luck.

5. **Act pressure is felt, but Reyes in S1 is thin.** Style 10 point 4 requires the named pressure in every scene. S2 (the distribution list) and S3 (the witness) carry it well. S1 has only "a young woman said 'Reyes'... in the voice people use for weather." That is acceptable, since the beat sheet says the same, but the line "The request was still stalled... stalls end" is where the closing-on-paper pressure should bite. Consider making the stall end sooner in her own reckoning (for example, one concrete figure for how long stalls last) so the threat has a clock.

6. **Joke ratio does not fall as far as Act III intends.** Style 10 point 7 says the ratio falls on purpose. The beat sheet asks for one or two per scene. S1 has the receipts, the tape ("with less tape than that, and mine held") and the standing line. S3 has the furniture, "moved up in the world", the chef, the coat, "ought to be sent home". That is about 3–5 per scene. Keep the tape joke and the furniture line, and cut the rest. This also helps finding 1.

7. **First and last sentences.** The first ("The syllabus had cost me everything outside it...") carries weight. The last, "and closed it.", is correct per the outline but flat on its own. It works because of "bare feet" and the chicken, so keep those details in the last sentence. No change needed.
