**Verdict:** REVISE

**Findings:**

1. "For the first time in two years of planning, I felt nothing." (scene 2). Breaks 11 (no naming the absence; "felt nothing" does the job of *numb*) and 02 (it explains). The paragraph before it already shows the absence through the body: pulse 58, dry mouth, no metal. This line then states it outright. Fix: cut "I felt nothing. Not dread. Not hunger." and let "Paperwork." follow the missing taste, e.g. "It didn't come. It tasted of paperwork."

2. "I had never once marked the woman doing the marking." (scene 3). Breaks 02 point 5 (she is reliable about everything except why). The fourth page, the one with her name on it, sits face-down in the drawer as of scene 1, so she has marked herself and this line is false. It also spells out the thesis of the scene. Fix: "I had marked her once, on a page now face-down in a drawer. I had not looked at her since." Or cut the line and go straight to "Competent."

3. "I found them. I didn't look away." (scene 3, last full paragraph). This could close any novel. The flaws stay unnamed in a chapter that names everything, and "didn't look away" tells the reader the book's test instead of letting them sit it. Breaks 02 (drift toward explanation) and 06 (bodies anatomically exact). Fix: give one finding plainly, e.g. a bruise she doesn't remember or the cracked knuckles again, and end the paragraph on the finding.

4. "The syllabus had cost me everything outside it" (opening line). This is a stated thesis in generic thriller register ("cost me everything"). The anchors open inside a scene or a document, never with a summary. Breaks 02 (explanation). Fix: cut the first sentence and keep "I had, at least, kept the receipts." as a dry lead-in to the Items, or open on "It was Sunday".

5. "I hadn't decided anything. The thumb had." Also "My hand put it there now" and "My body had filed this one". Three uses in scene 1 of the body acting without a decision. That is Ch25's move ("I had not decided anything", "My hands didn't"), nearly word for word, and using it here spends the anchor's payoff early. Fix: keep one, the thumb over the play arrow, and make the other two plain actions she reports without comment.

6. "the long rising noise of a crowd being told to get ready" (scene 2) and "the voice people use for weather in another state" (scene 1). Both lift Ch25 anchor phrasing ("telling a crowd to get ready", "a long rising noise", "weather in another city"), which blunts it when it lands there. Fix: "the field house filling, a sound like surf with a PA over it". For the second: "in the voice people use for a cousin's divorce".

7. "Every seam had been taped twice over" … "She had sealed every seam twice." The fact is repeated two sentences apart, which slackens the setup for the airway joke. Fix: cut "She had sealed every seam twice." so it runs straight into "I have occluded an airway with less tape than that, and mine held."

8. "a fine brown film like the pellicle" (scene 1). Breaks 06 point 1 (no unmodified brown). Fix: "a film the colour of weak liver", or "tannin-dark".

9. "I wanted it the way you want to finish a book you have stopped enjoying" (scene 1). A stock simile in a generic "you". Breaks the any-other-novel test. Fix: use one from her trade, e.g. "the way you want to finish a case you stopped caring about at the first incision: because the report is due, and the body is already open."

**What works:**

- The "Item." ledger, "Those are the findings", grading the security "sound" with "about as much pleasure as signing for a parcel". This is the syllabus voice still present at the start of Act III but already joyless. That is correct for Ch19.
- "adequately nourished", "started on the furniture", "a change of tenant", the ketosis and pear-drops passage, the arrector pili sentence. The Latinate/plain collision is at full strength and the jokes are thinning at the right rate.
- "It is still there. I have never played it." and the closing fridge. Both show the absence through objects and the body, which is what 11 asks for. Do not add commentary to either.
