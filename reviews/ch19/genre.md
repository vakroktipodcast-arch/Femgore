- **Verdict:** PASS

The appetite is starved at the right stage, and the body carries every page: cold, the uneaten food, ketosis, the wound, the hands. Humour holds up a chapter with no violence. She never apologises and never moralises, and the self-autopsy makes her harder to look away from. The two notes below are optional polish and do not block.

- **Findings:**
  1. "which has come every time the old thing surfaces" (Scene 2). "The old thing" is a euphemism for an appetite she chose in Ch18 to keep with her eyes open (strategy/10, Ch18 and Act II "she stops pretending"; strategy/04). She is reporting the absence of that appetite, and calling it a coy name makes it look as if she is avoiding it. Fix: name it plainly, e.g. "every time the hunger came up", so the starvation is a finding and not a dodge.
  2. "faintly, of the suite at work, which lives in my skin" (Scene 3). This is smell first, but the morgue smell is named by its place, not its odour, while the ketosis next to it gets full treatment (style/04, render, smell first; style/05, Smell). "Suite" also echoes "the week after the suite" (Brandt's) in Scene 2. Fix: give it one odour noun, e.g. "of formalin and the steel wash at work", and keep "suite" for Brandt's.

- **What works:**
  1. The ketosis passage ("Pear drops… started on the furniture"). The starved appetite is shown in her body, diagnosed as a finding, and made funny. It is the chapter's genre centre. Keep it whole.
  2. "I have occluded an airway with less tape than that, and mine held." This is the one flash of the old relish, dry and unapologetic, in the middle of the grief over the gift tag. Do not soften it or cut it.
  3. The dictated self-report and its amendment from "well-nourished" to "adequately nourished". The self-examination is clinical and holds back nothing, and it passes The Test: the reader can't dismiss her as a case study, because she has already written herself up as one.
