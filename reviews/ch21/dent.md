# Dent review — ch21 The Infiltration

- **Verdict:** REVISE. Structure, Turns and Snapper are sound. The chapter runs about a quarter over length, Reyes's pressure is thin outside Scene 2, and two Turns are slightly blunted by what surrounds them.

## Findings (most serious first)

1. **Over word target.** Total is roughly 3,700-3,900 words by my paragraph count (I had no word-count tool, so this is an estimate) against ~3,150. Scene 1 is about 1,350-1,400 against ~1,100, and Scene 2 is about 1,300 against ~1,000. Scene 3 is close to its ~1,050. Rule: style/10 point 1 ("nothing else may" run long except Ch 17); strategy/03, three scenes of 1,000-1,100. Fix: cut about 600 words, mostly from Scenes 1 and 2.
   - Scene 1: shorten the four-person crowd paragraph ("I read the line while it moved...") by a third, and collapse the ketone paragraph and the wand/belt-hole paragraph.
   - Scene 2: compress the kit-spreading paragraph ("The hard case, the marker and the pin...") to a sentence plus the scalpel line, and drop the closet's "par sheet" and "starch and bleach" detail. Cut the radios opener to two sentences.

2. **Reyes ("closing on paper") is felt in Scene 2 only.** Rule: style/10 point 4, the named pressure in every scene. Scene 1 has the knowledge from 18 (the young women) and the Compliance audit, but no Reyes. Scene 3 has neither Reyes nor the knowledge from 18. Sloane's "known enough... not to know any more" brushes it but doesn't name it. Fix: add one clause in Scene 3 where she scans the corridor, for example reading the aide's tablet or any woman in a lanyard as "someone who reads paper", or glancing for an FBI liaison on the detail. Add one line in Scene 1, for example checking the checker's list for a second sheet or a "liaison" tab.

3. **Scene 1 Turn is pre-empted.** Scene 1's Turn lands at "I passed because the checker was a campaign volunteer..." The event ("You're all set") is followed by a ~70-word explanatory paragraph ("Diligence, full marks. Authority, none...") that spells out the hole before the Turn states it, so the Turn reads as summary, not as the hit. Rule: style/10 point 3, and the "event or admission, not summarised" test. Fix: cut the audit explanation to "Diligence, full marks. Authority, none." and move the Compliance and "paper in her mouth" line after the Turn, or drop it. The Turn should be the first thing she concludes after "You're all set."

4. **Scene 3 Turn is not the scene's last beat, and is not answered.** Scene 3's Turn lands at "In the gap before it shut: a framed photograph..." and is followed by about four paragraphs (latch, count, nitrile, "five") before the Snapper. Iris's only reaction is "Somebody had set it out for him." Dent wants the Turn to land and the scene to close on the Snapper, which this does. But the photograph is the emotional reversal and the reader gets nothing of her body or admission in response. Fix: add one beat of reaction, in the body or in a flat statement, between "the door met its frame" and "The latch." Do not name the absence (style 11).

5. **Appetite is dropped in Scene 3.** Rule: style/10 point 6, the appetite present at least once. It is present in 1 (ketones, stomach fist) and 2 ("stomach had gone quiet"). Scene 3 has only "felt nothing about that either way", which is adjacent but not appetite. Acceptable for the chapter. Optional: one gastric or ketone note in the corridor to keep the starved thread running into 22.

6. **Minor luck/convenience watch, all pass on inspection.**
   - The stair post leaves the landing and turns his back "which is what he was for", and Holt never looks up. Both are set up by her counting in Scene 2, so this is competence, not luck.
   - The officer's hand skims the paper and the pale nitrile without questions ("Everybody's got one"). This is held by the pathologist's calm, and the flinch is suppressed on the page. Keep it.
   - Make sure "exactly as I had counted it" (Scene 3) stays; it is what keeps the post's movement earned.

7. **Tense slip in Scene 2.** "When the detail arrives, the stair post steps forward... the landing has nobody on it" is in present tense inside past narration. It is a generalisation, but it reads as a lapse. Fix: put it in past perfect ("When the detail arrived, the post stepped forward...").

8. **First sentence is functional, not weighted.** "The loading dock smelled of diesel." is the beat sheet's sensory lead and is fine, but it is not carrying much. Rule: style/10 point 8. Optional fix: open on the line itself or the badge, so the first sentence carries Iris's trouble (a false name) earlier. The last sentence ("I wished, briefly, that it weren't steady now.") is strong.

## Turns and Snapper, by scene

- **Scene 1:** Turn lands at "I passed because the checker was a campaign volunteer..." (see finding 3). Present and on target.
- **Scene 2:** Turn lands at "The rhythm was tighter than the footage." The latch is an event ("The latch went. Voices. He was out."), then the two-line statement closes the scene. Lands cleanly.
- **Scene 3:** Turn lands at "In the gap before it shut: a framed photograph of a Marine..." (mid-scene, see finding 4). The Snapper, "I counted to five and put my hand flat on the sticker...", is an admission with an image. It is not a cliffhanger and is correctly placed last.

## What works

- The Snapper: "It had been steady in the suite too, and then it had been happy." It is an admission, not a cliffhanger. Do not touch the ending.
- The Scene 1 recovery through pathologist's calm (the flinch in the external oblique, then "let the muscle go soft"). It is competence, and the near-failure is real. Keep it.
- Scene 2's count of the Service and the 7:21 test, which turn "sixty to forty" into a plan-breaking event that Iris earns by her own observation.
