**Findings** (most serious first)

1. **"and I knew what it was. A habit. From the inside a habit can pass for an appetite"** (Scene 1, para after the line-reading). This states the chapter's thesis instead of letting the body show it. It drifts toward explanation, which breaks 02 (she is unreliable only about *why*, so she should not be diagnosing her own appetite here) and 11, last point (show the absence of appetite through the body and never name it). The line-reading paragraph before it has already done the work. Fix: keep "It was the only pleasure I had left" if you want it, cut the rest, and end on a bodily report in the style of ch01's "I noticed the ratio". For example: "My mouth did nothing while I did it."

2. **"and that I felt nothing about that either way."** (Scene 3, after "I did the sum again"). This names the absence, which breaks 11 (show it through the body, never name it). It also uses up ch25's beat early: "I didn't feel anything about that either" belongs to the numb pole. Fix: replace it with a finding, as "Sixty-four" does earlier. For example: "…which queue to stand in at a supermarket. My pulse didn't move for it."

3. **"They were competent. They were not omniscient. Nobody is."** (Scene 2). This is the outline's wording lifted into the prose, and "Nobody is" is a trailer line that could sit in any thriller. Grading is still allowed in ch21 (02, syllabus-voice point), so let her grade them instead. Fix: "Competent. Not omniscient. I'd have passed them, narrowly."

4. **"I passed because the checker was a campaign volunteer, nineteen, with a laminated list."** (end of Scene 1). This copies the outline Turn word for word and repeats what the previous three paragraphs already showed ("She was nineteen", "laminated list", the Compliance line). It drifts toward explanation (02). The Turn has already been delivered on the page. Fix: cut the sentence, so the scene runs from "…two days before I put the paper in her mouth." straight to "The hole was exactly where I'd said it would be."

5. **"My plan was built for sixty."** (end of Scene 2). Another copy of the outline, with a tense slip: the plan was made before this moment, so in past narration it should be pluperfect (01). Fix: "My plan had been built for sixty."

6. **"like a cold hand that had forgotten it was there"** (Scene 3, opening). This simile could appear in any novel. Elsewhere her comparisons come from her trade or her kitchen, which breaks 06 (precise, sourced texture). Fix: give it her source. For example: "lay across the back of my neck like the draught off a drawer at four degrees."

7. **"A woman who had known enough, for a long time, not to know any more."** (Scene 3, Sloane). The judgement is fine, but the line is a generic epigram. Low priority. Fix: make it a finding rather than an aphorism. Tie it to the carotid she has just counted, e.g. "Her pulse said she knew. Her back said she had decided not to."
