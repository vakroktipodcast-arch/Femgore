- **Findings:**
  1. Snapper meal is described but not tasted. "out of the container with my fingers, and then I licked" (l. 91). The oxtail gets the precision of tissue on l. 89 (set fat, gelatin, coccygeal vertebrae), but nothing happens in her mouth. Breaks `style/05` Eating ("Food named, cooked, tasted"). It also breaks `strategy/02` Appetite, because this is the chapter's appetite payoff and the reader has to feel it. Fix: add one clause of mouth and hands between "all of it, cold" and the container. For example, the gelatin going liquid on the tongue, or sucking each vertebra clean, smallest last. Give it no explanation and keep it unnamed. The "corners first" ending stays.
  2. Too many notes to the reader about her own appetite. "I'm giving you that as a fact about the morning" (l. 15) and "I am recording that I did" (l. 37), together with "I want you to have both of those in the same hand" (l. 11). Three of these in one chapter turn the unnamed appetite into a case she is building against herself. Breaks `strategy/10` Act I ("does not call it enjoyment"), because pointing at it is a way of naming it. It also trips `style/12` (case study, easier to dismiss). Fix: keep l. 11, which is the best of them. Cut the second sentence of l. 15 so the hunger is just there. Cut "I am recording that I did" on l. 37 so the laugh stays hers and isn't evidence.
  3. Scene 3 never lets the room touch Diane's body, so the prep reads as pure procedure. "put my weight back on it, all of it" (l. 69). The bracket test is the one point where the place meets a body, and Iris has no response to it. Breaks `style/04` (no violent intent without her in the same line), in its Act I form under `strategy/10` (appetite as competence). Fix: tie the test back to Scene 1's appraisal in one clause, e.g. that her weight on the bracket was more than seventy kilograms would ever put on it. The prep then carries the appraisal without naming the use.
