## Turn check

- **Scene 1:** lands. "I capped the lenses, front and back..." is the abort, followed by "The target had a daughter in frame now, not in a file." It is an event, done with the hands, and it is procedural, as the beat sheet requires.
- **Scene 2:** lands. "Yes," Diane said. / "No pause. Not before that word." Then "I had to put the laptop down." The paragraph ends on the unfinished peach ("did not finish it").

## Findings

1. **Over length.** By my count the chapter runs about 3,850 to 3,900 words against the 3,150 target. Scene 1 is about 1,400 and Scene 3 about 1,350, against a ~1,050 target each. This breaks 10-chapter-architecture point 1 ("nothing else may" run long) and 03-structural-frameworks bullet 1 (1,000–1,100 per scene). Fix:
   - Scene 1: cut about 300 words. Halve the smell paragraph ("The reservoir smelled the way still water smells...") and trim the body paragraph ("I had her body in the glass...") by about a quarter. Keep the liver line and "both of those in the same hand."
   - Scene 3: cut about 250 words. Compress the preparations paragraph ("I worked it the way I set out a tray") and the rods paragraph.
   - Scene 2 needs only a light trim.

2. **Scene 3 explains the liking before the Turn.** "My patients are very quiet. It had never occurred to me to want that from a room." sits inside the rods paragraph, ahead of the Turn. It does the job the beat sheet (S3 point 9) reserves for the reader: "Hold on the Turn and don't explain it." It also steps on the Turn's own sentence. Fix: cut it, or move it earlier to the "dignified way to applaud" joke slot. Let "I found I liked it" arrive with nothing leading up to it. "longer than I needed to" can also go, because it duplicates the admission.

3. **The Snapper's run-up spends its weight.** "like a sentence that knows how to end" in the oxtail paragraph announces the closing move. The last line then has to do work the simile has already done, which weakens the final sentence under 10-chapter-architecture point 8 ("first and last sentences are load-bearing"). Fix: end the oxtail paragraph on "the transverse processes running down to nothing," and drop the simile. The last line stays as written.

4. **Scene 1 delays the trouble.** Iris is on the stakeout from the first sentence, so the point-6 requirement is met. The turn-driving event (Olivia on the bench) does not arrive until about 700 words in, behind two long descriptive paragraphs. This is a pacing risk, not a rule break, and the cuts in finding 1 fix it. Do not move the Olivia arrival.
