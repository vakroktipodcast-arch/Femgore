# Voice review: ch04

- **Verdict:** REVISE

**Findings**

1. **Line 25, "The target had a daughter in frame now, not in a file."** "Target" and "in frame" are surveillance-thriller jargon. Iris says "Diane", and her nouns come from her own trades, pathology and grading. This is the line in the chapter most likely to turn up in another novel, and it sits on the scene's Turn. Breaks 02 (voice register: exact, anatomically literate) and the brief's "any other novel" test. Fix: put the Turn in her own idiom, e.g. "Diane had a daughter on the bench now, not in the dossier," or end on the heritable gait plus one clipped line about the daughter.

2. **Line 49, "it was also exactly what had been done to me."** The narrator states her motive. Iris is unreliable about one thing only, why she does this (02, point 5), and a line like this drifts toward explanation. The joke before it ("very funny") is right. Fix: end on "and I thought that was very funny," or replace the clause with a body finding, as the chapter does at line 59, and let the reader make the link.

3. **Line 15, "I'm giving you that as a fact about the morning."** This is nearly a copy of the ch01 anchor's "I tell you the facts about lunch." Line 11 also has "I want to give it to you the way I had it," which repeats ch01's "I want you to have that the way I had it," and the same paragraph closes with "I want you to have both of those in the same hand." That makes three explain-to-the-reader formulas in two paragraphs. Under 01 (point 1), direct address is to be used sparingly and only to implicate, never to explain. Fix: keep "both of those in the same hand", which does implicate the reader. Cut "I want to give it to you the way I had it" and cut "I'm giving you that as a fact about the morning" (end on "drank the coffee").

4. **"I noted it" appears four times** (lines 15, 23, 31, 47), alongside "logged it" (21) and "marked it as one" (73). The filing verb is becoming a tic, and each use flattens the moment. Breaks 02 (point 1: exact, alive). Fix: keep line 47's "I noted it and went on" because it carries weight there. Cut "I noted it" after "Heritable gait" (the fragment stands alone) and rephrase line 31 without "noted", e.g. "I registered his absence the way you register a missing instrument on a tray."

5. **Line 39, "she still checks it every time she walks past."** Line 35, "He is always right about grammar," and line 37, "He has always been very good at doing other people," do the same thing. At the time of narration both Diane and Holt are dead, so the habitual present makes them sound alive. The ch01 anchor keeps Holt in the past ("He was better… I gave him"). Breaks 01 (point 1: past tense). Fix: "she had taught herself… she checked it every time she walked past"; "He was always right about grammar"; "He was always very good at doing other people." The last one hits harder in the past tense.

6. **Line 41, Holt's "She get back all right?"** Holt has the best syntax in the book until Chapter 24 (07, point 4), and two lines earlier he has corrected "less" to "fewer." The elided auxiliary reads as the drafter's casualness, not his. Fix: "Did she get back all right?" Let the warmth come from the fact that he asks, not from dropping a word.

7. **Line 87, "I did not mind it… I found I liked it."** Three sentences make the same point in three steps. The anchor's equivalent is one beat ("I liked hearing it."). Fix: cut "I did not mind it." Keep "I stood in it longer than I needed to. I found I liked it."

**What works**

- Lines 59 and 89–91: the peach left unfinished and then the whole oxtail eaten, container licked. Appetite is shown through the body and never named (11, last point). The "coin held there too long" taste and the callback to the cold in the volar forearms are exactly right. Do not add commentary to either.
- Line 7 "heard anyway" paired with line 23 "did not supply": a reliability device that implicates the reader. Keep both.
- The joke rate meets the Act I floor throughout (herons, fitness-app distinction, "Fewer," the donor's dog, applauding a building, "the only way I do tape"). The Latinate/plain collisions also work: oestrogen seating fat "like a guest", rhodopsin, coccygeal vertebrae "like a sentence that knows how to end."
