## Findings

1. **Length.** By my estimate the chapter runs about 3,850-3,950 words against a target of about 3,150, roughly 700 over. Each scene is over its 1,000-1,100 band: Scene 1 about 1,250, Scene 2 about 1,250, Scene 3 about 1,400. This breaks style/10 point 1 ("~3,150 words. Chapter 17 may run long; nothing else may") and strategy/03 ("roughly 1,000-1,100"). I counted by eye; confirm with the linter. Fix by cutting about 200 words per scene.
   - Scene 1: compress the lamb paragraph ("I want to be exact. The fat at the edge...") by half, and merge the staff-grading paragraphs. The outline needs only the finance man, the liver, and the admission "It was not the ranking I had come to do."
   - Scene 2: trim the opening donor and video-link paragraphs, and keep the pause, Evan, the first-ring call and the pale band.
   - Scene 3: trim the suite inventory and the War Room smell and sight paragraph.

2. **Convenient doors (Hero rule).** Three doors open for Iris without her doing anything: "a flap of water-case cardboard folded into its strike", the suite "still stood ajar on its case of water", and the connecting door that "went down and the door came towards me". This breaks style/10 point 5 ("no unlocked doors she didn't unlock") and strategy/03 (hero rule). Iris names the campaign's carelessness, which is the act's pressure, but she earns the entry only through her first-pass scouting. Nothing in Scene 3 is a competent act on a lock. Fix by giving her one active move that she makes and the reader sees. For example, she checks the strike with a fingertip and re-seats the cardboard so it holds against the next staffer, or she tests the connecting door's latch play and not just the bolt. Cut "That wasn't chance" to a plain statement of what she saw on the first pass, so the reader credits her recon and not the building.

3. **Desmond is a soft test.** The improvisation passes only because he is kind: "He didn't see it. He was looking round for somewhere to put the backing paper." The cover story is never pressed, and the live-feed tablet is turned face-down and then ignored. That is thin on "gets out by her own competence" and on menace in Scene 3. Fix by letting him ask one question that the lie must answer, such as which outlet she files for or whether the room is booked. Iris's answer, clipped and specific, should show her skill. The tremor can then still come with the plaster.

4. **Act pressure thin in Scene 2.** The campaign's certainty that nothing is coming is carried in Scenes 1 and 3 ("nobody checks a lanyard", "believed nothing was coming"). In Scene 2 it shows only as invisibility. This breaks style/10 point 4 (pressure felt in every scene). Fix with one beat: a staffer or the reporter's recorder passes Iris and ignores the lanyard, or Diane's security is visibly nothing but phones and people. It could be a single clause in the "never alone" paragraph, saying that the arm's-length ring around her is staff, not security.

5. **First sentence is not yet load-bearing.** "The service entrance smelled of chafing-dish fuel..." opens with a smell list and no stake. The chapter needs the line that puts her inside the trouble. The strongest candidate is already in the paragraph: "nobody looked up". Fix by leading with the entry (the badge, the lintel, nobody looking up) and letting the smell follow. This is the style/10 point 8 "first sentence" test. The final sentence ("I didn't know when that had started.") passes.

6. **Scene 2 grief is thin.** strategy/03 says "Scene 2 shovels grief". The original injury appears in one clause and the NDA memory in one sentence: "she had watched my hand the whole time". The Turn is stronger if that memory is given one more concrete beat before "She registered nothing." For example, what the hand signed or what Diane said. This is a small addition and must be offset by the cuts in finding 1.

## What works

- The Snapper lands as an admission: the lift grip, the brushed-steel walls with no mirror, and a last sentence that is the last thing she admits.
- The appetite thread runs without being named: the lamb, the coin taste, the tremor and the smile. The reader feels the danger before Iris does.
- Scene 3's Turn is an admission and not a summary. "You're holding it too, now." implicates the reader and sets up the Snapper.
