- **Verdict:** REVISE

- **Findings:**

1. **Units handled bare-handed.** "I laid my things on the desk... I put the gloves on." She unpacks and lays out the two units before she gloves, so her prints and DNA could be on devices that will sit in the War Room for two months. Cast.md (Iris: evidence handling, "knowledge of what investigators look for and therefore what to remove") rules this out, and a pathologist reader would catch it at once. She can't know the units will never be found (ledger, Who knows what). **Fix:** glove before anything comes out of the bag, or have the units arrive in a zip bag that was wiped and packed gloved at home. One clause either way; the "left to right in the order of use" image can stay.

2. **No power and no route out for weeks of live feed.** "Two units, each the size of a coat button... bought for cash." A self-contained coat-button camera with a microphone runs for hours on its battery, not the seven-plus weeks the ledger needs (Objects, Surveillance: "live and recorded", watched from home Ch 4 S2, Ch 8, Ch 9, Ch 14, Ch 20). Pairing it to a tablet in the next room also doesn't explain how she watches it from across the city. A surveillance professional would laugh. **Fix:** keep "button" for the lens and give each lens a lead to a matchbox-sized body that takes power from something that is always on: the wall screen's USB service port behind the bezel, and the socket strip the urn is plugged into under the credenza. Add half a clause saying the units send out over the hotel's own network (or a SIM) and that the tablet is only for setting them up. This is consistent with the ledger, since "button camera" is the trade term for exactly that build.

3. **Hotel cameras are never mentioned.** She walks a service entrance, back corridors, a service lift (twice) and an executive-floor corridor, and doesn't once think about CCTV. The same hotel is the Election Eve venue (ledger, Places, the Calloway), so this is the reader's first lesson in how she handles that building. Not noticing the cameras reads as luck, not skill. **Fix:** one dry line, for example that she keeps her face below the dome over the lift door, and that hotels keep about thirty days of footage while nobody will ask for a donor reception inside that time. It covers the night and pays off on Election Eve.

4. **She enters as press through the service door.** "I came in under the steel lintel... nobody looked up." Press go through the front to a press check-in. A hotel's staff entrance normally has a security podium or a timekeeper's desk, and you'd expect someone there at 7 p.m. on a banquet night. Then she walks *from* the service side *to* the press table, which is the wrong order for her cover. **Fix:** either have her come in through the lobby like any reporter and save the service corridors for after the press pen (beat 4 already does this), or give the service entrance a podium that is empty or a guard with his back turned. Don't make it plain luck that nobody is there.

5. **Connecting door.** "The handle went down and the door came towards me." In a hotel, connecting rooms have two doors back to back, each with its own deadbolt (the ledger's "deadbolts both sides"). Opening hers would leave her facing theirs. **Fix:** "and behind it their door, unbolted, which gave to a push." That keeps the joke about bolting a door against yourself and makes the hardware right.

6. **Urn state.** "coffee stewed on its element since lunch" / "the element ticking as it cooled". An urn can't still be stewing on its element and also be cooling, and nobody is in the room to have switched it off. **Fix:** make it an urn that switches itself off after a few hours, or cut "on its element".

7. **Minor, optional. A single cycle is not a tremor.** "One cycle, quicker than I could have made it on purpose." Clinically, tremor means a rhythm, and one oscillation is a twitch. Iris would know that. The ledger fixes the word "tremor", so keep it, but she could say so herself in half a line ("One cycle is not, strictly, a tremor. I wrote it down as one."). That suits the clinical-description Turn and stops a clinician reader pushing back.

- **What works:**
  - Clinical readings of the staff (icterus with spider naevi, scoliotic tilt, nails bitten past the quick) and the estimate of Diane at 165 cm and 70 kg, taken off the donor beside her and the way the suit sits. These read like someone who does it every day.
  - The tremor described as a case note (fingers involved, amplitude, onset, resolution, thumb uninvolved), and "quicker than I could have made it on purpose", which fits Ch 7's 8–12 Hz.
  - The campaign's carelessness is planted as the campaign's own doing (cardboard in the strike, a case of water propping the door, nobody bolting against themselves), so the access doesn't read as Iris's luck.
