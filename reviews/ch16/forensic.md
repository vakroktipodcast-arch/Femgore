- **Verdict:** REVISE (light). One mechanical problem in the binding, plus four small professional false notes.

- **Findings:**

1. **Moderate. The binding as described won't hold.** "a zip-tie round each wrist, through a grommet and back"; "grommets ran along the front edge". A tie that goes down through a desk grommet and comes back up the same hole is anchored to nothing. Someone who has done this would anchor it to structure. There's a second gap: with grommets only on the front edge, the far wrist has nothing to tie to. Brandt lies supine with his arms "along the edges", so one wrist is at each long edge. The ankles, "the same, at the far end", need an anchor too. The beat sheet's own New facts ("zip-tied through cable grommets") doesn't settle the mechanics. Fix: give the tie its anchor in one clause, e.g. "through the grommet and round the steel rail of the cable tray beneath". Then either put grommets along both edges or tie the far wrist and the ankles to the desk frame or legs. Keep the watch-winding ratchet.

2. **Low. A floor plan wouldn't show grommets, so the confirmation reads as luck.** "exactly where the floor plan had put them". An architectural floor plan doesn't show cable grommets in a desktop. A fit-out drawing or the furniture supplier's spec would, and could plausibly sit in the same facilities attachments (ledger, Objects, Lantern tap: the first full card held the "facilities and property attachments"). Fix: "where the fit-out drawing had put them". The building-agrees-with-its-paperwork joke survives.

3. **Low. A senior pathologist doesn't tie toe tags.** "I have tied a great many tags to a great many toes." Tagging is done by morgue techs and investigators at intake, and many offices now use wrist or bag tags. A pathologist with 1,400 cases would read tags, not tie them. Fix: "I have read a great many tags on a great many toes. None of them had ever helped." Alternatively, have the line about the tags she has *cut* off, which is truer to her job.

4. **Low. Foil doesn't make a phone go dark.** "it went dark in my hand". A sleeved phone stays powered and goes off-network; the screen doesn't go dark. Iris's point is that it can't transmit, and she says so at once ("I needed it not to talk"). Fix: "it went deaf in my hand" or "it fell off the network in my hand".

5. **Low, optional. The roll implies a mounted blade.** "Small handle and blade, retractor". This says a blade is mounted on the handle inside the roll. A careful pathologist transports handles bare with the blades sleeved and loads a blade at the table (ledger, Ch 15 kit: handles and "spare blades in a sleeve"; Ch 17 opens the nitrile). Fix: "Small handle, retractor, …", leaving the loading for Ch 17.
