# Ch16 — Genre contract review

- **Verdict:** REVISE (light; three line-level fixes, no structural problem)

## Findings

1. **"I was hungry, and I told myself it was because I hadn't eaten."** (Scene 3, penultimate paragraph). This is the Act I alibi: she treats appetite as physiology. Act II is the act where "she stops pretending" (`strategy/10`, Act II). Scene 1 has already been honest about the hunger ("on the way to a dinner you booked long ago"), so ending her last appetite beat on a disavowal is a step back, and it comes on the threshold of 17. **Fix:** let her give the alibi and drop it in the same line, e.g. "I was hungry. I had not eaten since the stock, and that was not why."

2. **"I like the forearms bare. That is as far as I will go about it here."** (Scene 3). This is the one coy moment in a chapter that is otherwise frank. A few lines later she files the "other finding, frank and plain" in her report hand. Holding something back on purpose reads as tact, and tact softens her. That breaks "she reports it the way she reports a finding" (`style/04`, point 3) and makes her easier to dismiss (`style/12`). **Fix:** cut the second sentence, or swap it for one exact clinical observation of the forearm (the flexor tendons standing when he tests the tie, the veins at the cubital fossa) and no comment on it.

3. **The foreplay never looks at him as a site of work.** In Scene 3 she inventories the desk, the tape and the instruments, but the only anatomy she reads on his body is his hands. "Every cut names what it cuts. The precision is the eroticism" (`style/04`, point 2). The planning "is foreplay" (`strategy/10`, Act II). The instrument roll is vague too: "Small handle and blade… large handle" from a pathologist. **Fix:** (a) name the kit as she would: a No. 3 handle with a 10 blade, a No. 4 with a 22. (b) Add one line, near "The height of my work", where she reads him the way she reads a body before she starts: the suprasternal notch, the carotid moving under the collar, where the first line will go. Don't cut anything; leave the act itself to 17.

## What works

- The register is right for the arc. "Not cold; I want that on the record", the high, unhurried pulse and the arousal noted "in the same hand I note everything" put the Act II heat on the page, and she never apologises for it.
- The humour carries a chapter with no violence: the rib shears as "the cheese course", the wine list "he couldn't" afford, "I had assumed it was a special effect", "It isn't a thing the dead go in for." Keep all of them.
- The body is present: the rye tasted with the precision she gives tissue, smell first on arrival, the gloved hand pressing the wound without her deciding to, gloves off "a finger at a time" down to bare hands. The Snapper lands as written.
