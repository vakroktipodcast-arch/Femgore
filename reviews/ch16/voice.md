# Voice review: Chapter 16

- **Verdict:** REVISE

## Findings

1. **High.** "I didn't know that the woman who had keyed in the code…" / "…neither of them would be told." This is stock "little did I know" foreshadowing. It is a sentence any thriller could contain, and it has the narrator explaining the arc. 01 bullet 1 says direct address and narration exist to implicate, never to explain. 02 bullet 1 asks for exact, not portentous. The Turn requires the *reader* to understand; Iris must not announce it. **Fix:** cut both sentences. Put the knowledge on the reader through implication instead, for example: "If you are keeping a list, this is where to start it: a quarter past ten, the code, the stairs. You were there too." Or end the paragraph on "That was all I knew."

2. **High.** "It was going to be the best night of my life." / "I told myself it was because I hadn't eaten." These lines spend Chapter 17's closing payoff a chapter early: "It was the best night of my life… I told myself the hunger was physiology, and I believed me" (anchors/ch17.md, final scene). When the anchor repeats them, the repeat goes flat. **Fix:** keep the hunger but report it bodily without the self-deception gloss, for example: "And I was hungry, high under the ribs, and the instrument roll was on the table like a menu." Swap "best night of my life" for a forward-leaning image that doesn't use the phrase, for example: "I had never in my life wanted a night to be long."

3. **Medium.** "Lower down there was the other finding, frank and plain". This calls the appetite frank without being frank. "I like the forearms bare. That is as far as I will go" does the same thing. 02 bullet 2 asks for Anglo-Saxon and frankly obscene language for appetite, and for the Latinate/plain collision to be the voice. As drafted, the chapter has almost no collision sentence. **Fix:** name it once, plainly, for example: "Lower down I was wet, and I noted it in the same hand…". Then give the bared forearms one long, greedy sentence where anatomy meets want, for example: "the flexor tendons standing up under the skin of his wrist when he flexed against the tape, and I wanted my mouth on them." Leave palmaris longus for Chapter 17.

4. **Medium.** "Here is the rest, because you are not him." This address introduces a paragraph of backstory, so it is direct address used to explain (01 bullet 1). **Fix:** make the address implicate the reader, for example: "Here is the rest, since you've been reading my drawer as well." Then trim the paragraph to the addressed sentence and the bus. "Not *she*, not *I*. *You*" is the part that earns its place.

5. **Medium.** "Eleven women had signed what I signed." It restates the realisation before the scene's Turn ("I had believed myself alone… the floor was a list") arrives. Para 87 has already laid the floor. Three statements of one thought is drift toward explanation (02 bullet 1). **Fix:** cut this sentence. Let "It came back now for the length of a clause and fell over" end the paragraph.

6. **Low.** Generic stock phrasing that any novel could contain: "The room had swallowed the corridor." / "My body got there before I did." / "He watched it land." / "Then I saw what else was on the table." **Fix:** cut the first. The preceding lift/ears image already does the work. For the second, use the specific action: "My left hand, the gloved one, went to my flank before I did." For the third, use "He watched it go in," which is a surgical register she would use. The fourth: "Beside his glass and the photograph, set square…" can stand without the lead-in.

7. **Low.** "longer than I needed to" appears twice (paras 45, 131), and anchors/ch25.md uses it at a key moment. **Fix:** keep it at the hands (131) and change the door line to "I stood by the door and let my body go over the room."

## What works

- Brandt's speech moves from fragments ("Jacket. Inside left." "Didn't fight it. The farm.") to complete sentences at "I would have done the same thing." That is 07 point 5 executed exactly. Do not smooth the early fragments.
- The joke line in Scene 3 is right for Act II: the conversion, the cheese course, the wine list he can't afford, "so well supplied with margin," the toe tags that "had ever helped," the "special effect." Keep the rate.
- The grading voice is present and in control ("Composure: excellent," "a mark for each," "highest mark I had given him yet"). The handoff to the anchor works: gloves off, nitrile in its box, record ending, sleeves. Leave it untouched.
