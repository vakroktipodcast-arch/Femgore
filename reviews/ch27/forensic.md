# ch27 — Forensic and procedural accuracy

- **Verdict:** REVISE

- **Findings:**

1. **"She had interviewed nine of the eleven. The two remaining were..."** An FBI agent telling a subject's colleague (who is also a witness, as prosector on Pell and assistant on Marchetti) the list's size, how far the interviews have got, and that Vale is one of the last two breaks the protective order she is working under (ledger, Objects: the list, "fixed and non-prejudicial sequence"; counsel's privacy brief). Any investigator would see a case being contaminated. The outline's Turn needs Iris to learn these facts, so keep the facts and make the leak deliberate: one line of Iris reading it as pressure, e.g. "She had said it to someone who would repeat it. She had said it to be repeated." That turns a procedural howler into Reyes doing what she does, closing on paper. Mara's flat "in the order" report can stay as it is.

2. **"My coffee was by the sinks where I had put it at eight."** No food or drink is allowed in an autopsy room under any bloodborne-pathogen regime, and a pathologist with 1,400 cases would not leave a cup by the scrub sinks. Move it to the anteroom or office ("on the office desk, where I had put it at eight"). Keep the skin on the coffee.

3. **"opening the coronaries in short cuts with the fine scissors"** Forensic practice is to cross-section the coronaries with a blade every two or three millimetres. Opening them along their length with scissors can push out exactly the fresh thrombus she is about to find. Fix: "cross-cutting the coronaries every few millimetres with a fresh blade". The thrombus line can stay as it is.

4. **"I put my wrist to my left side, through the scrubs"** Halfway through an open case she would have a gown and apron over her scrubs, and touching her body while gowned is a contamination breach she would notice. The gesture is in character (cast.md: she touches the wound when thinking), so keep it and make the breach register: "through the gown and apron, which I should not have done". At minimum, change "scrubs" to "gown".

5. **"Through the office door the break-room television was on"** Being able to read captions on a break-room set from a dissection table means the clean and dirty areas have no barrier between them. Put the set in the office or anteroom, seen through its glass ("through the observation window"), or have her read the captions as she passes on the way to the sinks.

6. **"Half of it was on the plate, jointed... I found the hip joint with my thumb"** A jointed leg has already been cut through at the hip, so what she finds on the plate is the knee, between thigh and drumstick. Then "both of them" oysters come from "the carcass" in the tin, but one oyster normally goes with a carved leg. Iris is the narrator who would get bird anatomy right. Fix: change "hip" to "knee" in the plate passage (the standing "pulled the other leg off" from the tin can keep the hip), and say she carved only the meat off one side, so the whole carcass with both oysters is still in the tin.

7. **"with the tin still too hot to touch"** She has sat until the plate went cold ("after a long time"), handled the page, gone to the desk and eaten half a bird. A roasting tin left on the hob would be cool to the touch by then. Fix: the tin went back into the switched-off oven to keep warm, which also explains why the kitchen is warm and the fat is "hot still at the bone". Or just cut "still too hot to touch".

8. **"OFFICIALS SAY SENATOR'S KILLER ACTED ALONE"** With no suspect and no description released, officials would not say it as fact. They would say "INVESTIGATORS BELIEVE ATTACKER ACTED ALONE" / "NO EVIDENCE OF WIDER PLOT". This keeps the outline's "lone extremist" while reading like real press-office language.

9. **"She said you were meant to see her last Wednesday."** The ledger allows Iris not to know about the Wed 4 Nov booking, but readers will ask how anyone misses an FBI appointment. Half a clause from Mara would cover it: "It went through the Chief's office." Optional.

- **What works:**
  - The finding at the gardener: the hand callus read as a history, a plaque rupture in the proximal LAD, and "widow-maker" dictated by its proper name. It is exact and shows the work is real. Keep it.
  - The advice on telling a six-year-old about a death, and lower central incisors as the first teeth lost at six. Both are clinically right, and both are technique used as cover.
  - The drawer sequence (key turned to open, drawer closed, key left in the lock, not turned) and the hand that does not shake over the bin both match the ledger exactly. Do not touch them.
