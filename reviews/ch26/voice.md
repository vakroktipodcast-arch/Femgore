**Verdict:** REVISE. Light pass. The narrator is right, but the joke rate and some writerly flourishes have come back too soon after Chapter 25, and a few lines explain or could sit in any thriller.

**Findings** (most serious first)

1. **The joke rate has not thinned.** I count about ten wisecracks in ~3,100 words: "which in a sense it was", the hotel linen, "covered in soup", "filing a complaint", "the jacket were a rumour", "take an interest", "a dog in a coat; I mean the dog", "autopsies on the buildings", "absorption of a surgeon", "They'd had practice". That is the Act I–II floor of one a page. It breaks 02 (jokes thin in Act III, and the reader should feel the loss) and the outline's "Appetite: starved". The dog-coat gag is the worst offender, a full Act I comic beat one chapter after the numb pole. **Fix:** cut the dog-coat, soup, rumour and "take an interest" lines. Keep the ones that are professional reflex (linen/hospitals, holster/iliac crest, buildings that didn't let you out). Reflex jokes are defensive. Play jokes are not.

2. **"it was not hunger. I didn't name it."** (line 67). This names the absence of appetite, which 11 (last point) says to show through the body and never name. It also spends the Turn's "I was not hungry" forty lines early, so the Turn lands second-hand. **Fix:** cut "and it was not hunger". End on "the first thing my body had volunteered in weeks that was not a complaint. I didn't name it."

3. **"For the first time, I had the feeling the marking ran the other way."** (line 65). This drifts into explanation and tells the reader what the scene has already shown (01: direct address and commentary never explain). "I had the feeling" is also generic. **Fix:** cut the sentence. End the paragraph on "There was no page for her."

4. **Lines that could sit in any thriller:** "I wasn't sure that made us even." (line 63) and "It had a face now." (line 75). Both are stock beats. **Fix:** cut line 63's last sentence outright. For line 75, make it concrete and hers, for example "Now it had a callus," which ties back to the hands she just read.

5. **Writerly flourishes edging toward purple:** "each one alone in a different way" (Tolstoy riff), "walked down the middle of that knowledge like a pavement". These are performed cleverness, not the exact-and-dry register of 02. In a chapter written just past the numb pole they read as the author, not Iris. **Fix:** end the bus sentence at "three people in it". End the other at "and I knew it."

6. **"about the length of a held breath"** (line 27). This is a stock phrase, and she is counting everything else. **Fix:** give it a number, e.g. "a gap of four seconds".

7. **The "somewhere else" simile is used twice:** "a metronome somebody had left running in another room" (line 11) and "a tap running in another flat" (line 91), on top of Chapter 25's "a radio goes on talking in another room". Three uses in two chapters makes it a tic (06 texture). **Fix:** keep the tap and recast the metronome, e.g. "once for every tread, like a step counter".

8. **"I knew exactly who she was. I am telling you now, before anybody else can"** (line 75). Both halves are required by the outline Turn, so do not cut them. "before anybody else can" is close to explanation, though. **Fix:** "I am telling you now, before she does." This puts the reader ahead of the interlude, which implicates them (01).

**What works** (do not break)
- The Reyes-hands paragraph (line 59). It is the one long, greedy, anatomical sentence in the chapter, and the only appetite left is attention. The echo of Chapter 1's "start forgetting it" is earned.
- Line 65, "Something in me reached for a mark... It didn't find one." It handles the syllabus voice exactly right: it reaches for the voice and doesn't find it.
- The plain closing run from line 99 to the end (hand on the page, "I hadn't counted it"). Its numbness is shown in the body, in the Chapter 25 register.
