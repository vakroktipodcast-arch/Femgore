- **Verdict:** REVISE (light: two must-fix lines, both one-clause changes; no structural change).

- **Findings:**

1. **Must-fix. There is no daylight at six.** "It was still on at six, yellow and foolish against the daylight." The ledger header fixes US daylight-saving rules: the clocks go back on Sun 1 Nov, the setting has Labor Day, and Olivia is at Wellesley. On 5 October that puts sunrise in any mainland US city at roughly 6:45 to 7:20 a.m. Civil twilight starts about 25 minutes earlier. At six the window is full night, so the lamp would look necessary, not foolish. Fix: move the hour, e.g. "It was still on at half past seven, yellow and foolish against the daylight." That still leaves him time to change, make the coffee, reach the War Room, make the scene and be "home before eleven" (ledger, Timeline Mon 5 Oct). If six has to stay, keep the hour and drop the daylight: "yellow against the black of the window". The first fix keeps the better image.

2. **Must-fix. The bed cannot smell "not of him".** "The bed smelled faintly cold and clean. Not of him." Ch 13 (manuscript, S1) fixes his pillow as "dented and soft and faintly rancid with the oil of a scalp", and the halves were laid on that pillow. Iris was there for minutes, gloved, wearing unscented soap, and had left about ninety minutes before he got home. That does not take a man's smell off his own bed, and the heating is on, so the bed would not be cold either. A professional reader would stop here. Fix: turn it into the absence he is actually checking for, e.g. "The bed smelled of him, as it always did, and of nothing else." That keeps the beat: he is looking for a trace of her, and she has left none.

3. **Must-fix (sequence). He reads the quarter-inch after he has disturbed it.** "The licence lay a quarter-inch to the left of true." By this point he has sat on the bed, which tilts the mattress and pillow, lifted the halves from beside the licence, and picked the licence up. Nobody could judge a quarter-inch on a soft pillow after all that, and a fixer would know it. Ch 13 gives the right moment: "he came in and saw the pillow and stopped." Fix: keep the line where it is (ruling 7) but put it in the past perfect and tie it to the doorway, e.g. "It had lain a quarter-inch to the left of true; he had seen that from the door."

4. **Must-fix (ledger, flag for the reviser).** "When he lifted it, it had left a ring." The ledger (Objects, pillow copy of Lesson Two) says "the glass ring comes from the suite." The Ch 17 anchor ("using it as a coaster") is compatible with a ring made on Monday, but the ledger is not. Either put this ring in the beat sheet's `## New facts` so that line can be amended, or keep the glass on the page and cut the ring sentence. Optional, on the physics: neat rye at room temperature does not sweat. A ring needs spirit on the base of the glass. He poured at this counter, so a drip is easy to supply ("rye down the side of the glass") if the reviser wants it watertight.

5. **Optional. Night one's clock is at its limit.** From 12:26 to 12:40 he has to stand "a long time" in the kitchen, sit "a long time" on the bed, read the licence and the page, laugh, and reshelve the record. Ch 13 fixes both times and both "long time"s, so nothing has to go. Do not add any more duration words (no "minutes", no "an age") to the first two paragraphs.

- **What works:**
  - "Cut first, then snapped. Not temper. Care." The scored break read by thumb matches Ch 13's method (one scalpel pass, pressed over an edge) and is exactly how a layman would read a tool mark.
  - "He knew what the licence was, and sleeved it with the halves." He keeps a dead man's licence knowing what it is. That reads as his choice, not luck.
  - He listens to the Mingus "for anything that was not where he had left it, and found nothing", directly under the unit on the record wall. His suspicion goes to objects and people and never to the cameras, which is what Ch 14 needs. Keep it.
