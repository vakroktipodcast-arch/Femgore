# Continuity review: interlude-3-cam (rewrite, ends Mon 5 Oct 11:40)

- **Verdict:** REVISE. Two small must-fixes (the stated hour's light, one smell that contradicts Ch 13); everything else holds against Ch 13, 14, 16, 17 and the ledger.

## Findings

1. **MUST-FIX. Daylight at six.** "It was still on at six, yellow and foolish against the daylight." Mon 5 Oct falls under daylight saving (the clocks do not go back until Sun 1 Nov: ledger header). At any latitude a US city could have, sunrise on 5 Oct is about 6:45 to 7:10 and civil twilight begins about 6:15 to 6:40, so at 6:00 it is dark. Fix: move the hour ("still on at seven") or change the light ("against the window going grey"). Check that the later "against" is not repeated; "daylight" at 11:40 is correct and stays.

2. **MUST-FIX. "The bed smelled faintly cold and clean. Not of him."** Ch 13 has Iris smell this pillow: "the near one dented and soft and faintly rancid with the oil of a scalp"; the far one is the cool, unslept one (ledger: second pillow unslept since the divorce). A bed Cam sleeps in every night does smell of him, and not "clean". "Cold" would suit the far pillow, not the bed he uses. Fix: cut the two sentences, or give him a wrongness he can actually register without contradicting Ch 13 (the room a degree off, the pillow where nobody had put anything in two years). Do not use "clean" or "not of him".

3. **OPTIONAL. "did not ... look into the front room."** Ch 13: the front-room feed hears the front door at 12:26 and he "crossed the frame in charcoal ... and went out of the far side of it towards the kitchen". He walks through that room to reach the kitchen. The author's first-third ruling (does not open the fridge, the front room) stands; the line is true only if "look into" means "stop in". Fix: "did not stop in the front room" or "did not so much as turn his head to the records".

4. **OPTIONAL. Interval at the print.** Ch 13: front door 12:26, "a long time" in the kitchen, then the bedroom (sits "a long time"), glasses, licence, page, laugh, sleeve shelved, rye poured at 12:40. Fourteen minutes holds three "long time"s only if each is brief. The page has "a long time" twice and "a long time" again in the same page. Not a contradiction; if a beat needs shortening, trim one of them rather than the 12:40.

5. **OPTIONAL. "Her name was on the file."** At 12:26 a.m. he reads the print as coming "off the file" and attaches her name to the file. Defensible: Mara's 6:11 text makes Iris senior reviewer on Diane (Ch 7), the office statement went over her name on 29 Sep (Ch 10), and he has known who she is since 16 Sep (Ch 9). But he cannot see the system log (Ch 13: it logs every look). Keep, but "her name was on the file" should read as his inference from the sharper image, not as a fact he has checked. Fix if wanted: "and her name was on the file, if you knew where to look."

6. **OPTIONAL. Staging of the lamp and the page.** He sleeps in his clothes "the jacket buttoned over the page"; Ch 13 has the page in the inside breast pocket, left. Consistent. Only note: Monday at 11:40 he "opened it on the counter" and puts a ring on it; nothing says he refolds along her creases. Ch 16 has it "folded in quarters" under his glass. Add four words (refolded along her creases) if the reviser has room.

7. **OPTIONAL. Turn: "stood in the dark."** He was not home when she was there and cannot know the room was dark. Reads as his picture of her, so it can stay; if the reviser wants it exact, "stood where his head went" without "in the dark".
