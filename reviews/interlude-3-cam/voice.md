# Voice review: interlude-3-cam

- **Verdict:** REVISE

## Findings

1. **"She could have killed him in his sleep, and she had chosen"**: the last paragraph spells out the Turn. The record already showed this ("Not temper. Care."), and here it is said a second time as a conclusion. "Killed him in his sleep" and "he found, to his surprise" are generic thriller prose that could appear in any novel. This breaks 01 (the interlude's single Turn should be shown, not explained) and the anchor (interlude-2 ends on a concrete image, "twenty years of habit, turned for the first time on the room"). Fix: cut the "could have killed / chosen to warn" sentence and the "to his surprise" clause. End on something physical, such as the glass on the page or his hand on the pocket. Let the long final sentence carry the grammar of his honesty without naming the feeling.

2. **"The room took the sound of his feet and gave none"**: this lifts Iris's Chapter 17 sentence ("took the sound of the nitrile and gave none of it back"). **"the tone arm lifted and went home"** is also her Chapter 17 phrasing. Both are Iris-isms in an interlude (02, and the interlude register in anchors/interlude-2-cam), and they take the echo away from Chapter 17 before Iris gets there. Fix: write both plainly in Cam's register, e.g. "The room was so quiet he could hear the carpet under his shoes" and "the side ended and the arm came back to rest."

3. **"It takes a steady hand to break one so it"**: this is a stock aphorism, and "steady hand" belongs to Iris's own motif (ch01: "My hands were steady"). It also explains the line before it, which already lands. Breaks 06 (Cam's plain, lay register) and the any-novel test. Fix: cut it and end the paragraph on "Not temper. Care."

4. **"It was the first thing in twenty years he had done"**: the narrator tells us what the act means. "Twenty years" also appears three times (lines 13, 23, 33). Breaks the anchor's restraint, where meaning arrives through action ("The order of those two things was not quite a decision"). Fix: cut the sentence, or make it concrete, e.g. "He did not decide what to say first. He just said it." Keep "twenty years" in one place only.

5. **"he told Theo Akers he had leaked the photograph"**: the "he" is ambiguous on first read (Cam or Theo?), and the beat after it ("Cam knew where the print had come from") relies on the reader having parsed it. Plain close-third must be clear. Fix: "he told Theo Akers, in front of the room, that Theo had leaked the photograph."

6. **"The boy went white"**: a stock phrase, and an unmodified colour (06, Colour). **"a small red heart"** is also unmodified. Fix: "The boy's ears coloured," or another physical tell, and "the donor heart" for the licence.

7. **"Tuesday. Not the Tabard."** / **"The charcoal shirt."** / **"Every shut door pointed the same way. He knew the hand."**: from line 23 to line 39 the prose slides into beat-sheet shorthand (day, fragment, day, fragment). The anchor uses fragments inside a scene that holds still. Here they summarise a week, and "He knew the hand" is generic. Fix: keep the fragments, but put one full sensory sentence in each day (as "It tasted thinner in daylight" does), and replace "He knew the hand. Vale's." with the specific thing he recognises. One example is the reporter's message, worded the way he would have worded it.

## What works

- The dry jokes in Cam's register: "a drink after midnight belonged to the day before," "Holt did not look up, which took real skill," "a smile that was sorry about something." They are cool and not Iris's. Keep them.
- "Fair mark. He did not turn it over." This is a clean callback to the interlude-2 anchor without repeating it.
- "He noted the time the way he would note another man's." / "It tasted thinner in daylight." Plain, exact, layman's language. This is the register at its best.
