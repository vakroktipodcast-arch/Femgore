# Voice — interlude-3-cam

**Verdict:** REVISE. One small must-fix; the rest is optional polish. The register matches the interlude anchor: plain, cool, Cam's fragments, no Iris-isms, no direct address, no banned phrases, no em-dashes.

**Findings:**

1. **Must-fix.** "Sharper than the copy on his phone. Hers." and, in the next paragraph, "her name was on the file." The nearest woman named is Diane, and Vale is not named until "where Vale had squared it", twenty paragraphs later. So both pronouns read as Diane's. The close-third rule in `01` ("never head-hop") depends on the reader knowing whose head and whose hand. The anchor names people at once ("Theo Akers, junior digital"), and the ledger fixes that Cam calls her "Vale" in his head. Fix: change "Hers." to "Vale's." and the "her name" sentence will then resolve to Vale.

2. **Optional.** "He stood and looked at it a long time." and "He sat like that a long time". The same vague duration appears twice, from a man who otherwise tells the time to the minute (twenty-six past twelve, twenty to one, six, eleven-forty). That breaks the anchor's habit of measuring ("Eight minutes to midnight"; "His thumb stopped on the glass and stayed there"). It also makes these the two sentences that could appear in any novel. Fix: cut "a long time" from the first. For the second, give it a clock ("He sat like that until the lamp's timer clicked", or a time).

3. **Optional.** "The sum came out the same every time." The closing paragraph's "the other sum, the one he had left on the pillow" pays off a second sum the text never set up. Fix: add one plain sentence after the first sum, such as "There was another one. He left it where it was." Then the payoff lands instead of explaining itself.

4. **Optional.** "He knew what the licence was, and sleeved it". This repeats what "The heavy one, then. ... His own measure, given back. Even." has already worked out. It is drift toward explanation (`02`: exact, not explanatory). Fix: "He sleeved the licence with the halves."

5. **Optional.** "he heard himself accuse the Akers boy". "Heard himself" is a stock thriller construction. Fix: "In the War Room he accused the Akers boy, in front of everyone, of something the boy had not done, and he was home before eleven." The dry "home before eleven" already carries the loss of control. The single glancing sentence complies with author ruling 4. Do not expand it.

**What works:**

- The bookkeeping voice, kept plain: "a drink after midnight belonged to the day before" and "He noted the time the way he would note another man's." This is Cam's measure and his only joke, at the right rate for an interlude.
- "They met with a small tick ... Cut first, then snapped. Not temper. Care." These are anchor-register fragments, in layman's vocabulary with no anatomy.
- "what she had cut was a record." The plain sentence that sets up the fixed Turn. Keep it uninflected.
