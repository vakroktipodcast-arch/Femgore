- **Verdict:** REVISE. The chapter's clinical register holds, but its clock and one line of plan history contradict Ch 21–22 and the ledger's count. A reader who has stood on a protective-detail timeline will catch both.

- **Findings:**

1. **The clock can't hold Scene 1 and Scene 2.** Locator: "He did not hurry. Nobody was hurrying him." The ledger (Fixed numbers, "Ch 22–23 count") puts Ch 22's end at about 15 s and the door glance at 40 s. That leaves about 25 seconds for all of this: the name, him sitting, two passes over her face, two reads of the badge, the shaken head, a two-part apology with a pause between the parts, the hand held out "a moment longer" and lowered, "Iris", four sentences with ballroom gaps between them, "went on looking for a moment", a frown held "for a while", his correction line, and her reading his face. Done at the pace the prose describes, that is 45–60 seconds. Iris counts everything, and this book has taught its reader to count with her. Fix: drop the second badge read and "Nobody was hurrying him". Change "longer this time", "a moment longer" and "looked at me for a while with it" to beats measured in a breath. Put one bare count in Iris's voice at the S1 Turn (about "Twenty-five."), so the 40 in S2 reads as earned. Her own thoughts can run as long as they like, because thought takes no clock time. His actions can't.

2. **"I had built the night on forty" contradicts the plan as Ch 21–22 planted it.** Locator: "I had built the night on forty. I had walked it". Ch 21 says "My plan was built for sixty." Ch 22 says "I had allowed fifteen because I had sixty". She learned about forty from the 7:21 thank-you minute that same night (ledger, Timeline, Mon 2 Nov). "Walked it … with the detail" also suggests a volunteer pacing the corridors alongside the Service, which no detail would allow. Fix: "I had rebuilt the night on forty at twenty past seven, counting his first minute of the evening from the back corridor."

3. **At 40 her exit route closes, and she doesn't register it.** Locator: "It was forty, on my count." Ch 22 planted the rule: when the minute ends, "the post would step back to the landing, and the stair would be watched again, and the only door I had would open onto a man with an earpiece." Iris, who ran her exits like a differential four pages earlier, would note at 40 that the post has gone back to the landing and her exit is gone. Without that, the stakes from Ch 22 disappear. Fix: one clause after "forty", for example "Out in the corridor the post would be stepping back to the landing."

4. **The chair gap reads as luck.** Locator: "a room I have not worked before". Lesson Three as written is "immobilise him", and the long zip-tie is already flat in the blazer lining (ledger, Objects, Ch 24). As written, Iris only finds the anchor point now, which makes Ch 24's restraint depend on furniture she happened to get. A sightline problem compounds it: with Holt seated close to a skirted banquet table, the joint between arm and front leg sits at or below tabletop height and behind his knee. Fix: make the glance a confirmation, not a discovery. Something like "where its arm met the front leg, the gap the hire catalogue's photograph had shown me, there by his knee", and cut "a room I have not worked before".

5. **The courtroom vocabulary belongs to the wrong jurisdiction.** Locator: "nothing is left in it that a barrister can take hold of". The setting is American (FBI, Senate, Wellesley), and there are no barristers in US courts. A medical examiner also doesn't trim a report "for a jury". She writes it so it survives cross-examination. Fix: "the way you write a report you expect to defend on the stand, until nothing in it gives a defence attorney a handle." "Witness box" can stay if house spelling is British, but "stand" is safer.

6. **Holt couldn't know the door was sealed.** Locator: "come now, tonight, through a door that had been sealed". This line sits in the list of things Holt didn't ask. The seal is on the corridor side and only Iris knows it is there, so he couldn't have asked about it. Fix: "through a door he had not heard open".

7. **Optional: the damp carpet should hold her footprints.** Locator: "The carpet was damp." Shampooed nylon pile that is still damp keeps shoe impressions. Iris, who logs the label scrap as "Evidence control", would notice her own four paces in it. Fix: add "and held my four paces in its pile" here or in Ch 25. One clause, no more.

- **What works:**
  - Iris reads the face and it doesn't confirm anything. "The pupils did nothing" and "The face gave me nothing I could have sworn to" are exactly what a pathologist would and wouldn't claim. Keep that restraint.
  - The four sentences given "the way I give evidence" and "I have dictated longer findings on a hand." Testimony discipline used as a weapon is the chapter's professional spine.
  - The kit placement matches the ledger exactly: the vinyl roll and label scrap in the right outer pocket, and the hard case, marker, pin and page in the left inside pocket. The hand going to the wound below the ribs is anatomically right. Don't move anything.
