- **Verdict:** REVISE

- **Findings:**

1. **She never sweeps for a device or checks her surveillance kit.** Line 31, "the tablet propped against the fruit bowl, where it had stood", and line 71, "'The Architect,' I said to the flat". This woman runs two button cameras on the War Room (ledger, Objects, Surveillance: "paired to tablet and laptop"). The first thing she would ask is whether he left one of his own in her flat, and whether he saw the tablet or the clean laptop. If the tablet sat on the fruit bowl all day, Brandt could have watched his own War Room on it, and the ledger says he never finds the units (Who knows what, line 410). Then she watches the feed live and speaks a lesson title aloud in a room he has just been in. A professional reads that as a lapse she would never make. Fix: in S1, after the fridge and before the desk, give her one or two sentences. The tablet and clean laptop were with her all day (in her bag or in the car), or locked and untouched. She runs a quick sweep for the kind of thing she buys herself (smoke detector, sockets, lamp base, the desk's underside), knowing what to look for because she has fitted them. Keep it short and keep the kitchen as the tell. The sweep is procedure, not instinct.

2. **The drawer stack contradicts itself.** Line 11 gives the order as dossiers, photographs, NDA, then the sleeve (ledger, Objects, Drawer order). Line 19 says "so a man who wants the pages has to lift the dossiers as a block", and S3 (line 65) lifts only the dossiers and then "slid the sleeve out". That leaves the photographs and the NDA unaccounted for. Fix: make it "lift everything above it as a block" in both places, or have the sleeve slide out from under the photographs and NDA once the dossiers are off. Either way the block that comes back a quarter-inch left must include whatever was actually moved.

3. **The scent hangs where it couldn't.** Line 3, "at about the height of a standing man's chest". He left at about 3 p.m. and she gets in at 6:30, with the door open and street air coming past her. A soap trace doesn't stay layered at chest height for three and a half hours. It sits on what he touched. Fix: keep the smell faint and general in the hall, then let it be strongest at the desk (the drawer pull, the manila, the chair back) when she gets there. That also reinforces the drawer as the evidence. Keep "by the time I had shut it I knew" as knowing that someone had been in. Who it was should come from the dossier and the 12:58–3:06 gap, since she has never been close enough to smell Brandt before Ch 13 (ledger, Brandt: the vetiver "cellar" smell).

4. **She doesn't examine the point of entry.** Line 3, "The deadbolt turned with the weight it always turns with". She inspects the brass of the desk lock "the way I would look at the skin around an entry wound" (line 11) but never the front door, which is where entry happened. Her deadbolt was locked when she got home, so he either picked it and relocked it or had a key. A pathologist with evidence habits would look. Fix: one clause back at the door, e.g. the deadbolt's face is clean and the bolt throws true, so either a key or someone better with a pick than she would have guessed. Leave it unresolved. Nothing in the ledger fixes his method, so add nothing that does.

5. **The sheriff logic misstates what the police hold.** Line 39, "and that means telling them about the paper." The sheriff's office already has the paper in evidence (ledger, Objects: the shredded NDAs are left at the scene; the fragment shows "LC-03"). What Brandt can't do is tell them what the paper *is*. Fix: "and that means telling them what the paper is."

6. **The spleen simile is backwards.** Line 77, "packed in tight and dense, the way the spleen is when it has been working hard". A spleen that has been working (reactive, septic) is big, soft and often diffluent, not tight. A congested spleen is the heavy, firm one. A pathologist reader will stop on this. Fix: "heavy and firm, the way a congested spleen is", or "full, the way a spleen is when it has been taking in more than it was built for." Either keeps the image.

7. **The milk-ring check is wrong.** Line 7, "looked at the ring under the cap, and the ring was whole." The tamper ring breaks the first time the bottle is opened, and this is milk she uses. A whole ring would mean she has never opened it. Fix: check something that records her own last use, such as the level against a mark she would know, or the dry drip line on the neck. The kettle check (line 7) is also empty, since it would be cold after three hours anyway. The water level is the better tell.

8. **Fingernail for depth.** Line 87, "checked the distance with a fingernail, the way you check the depth of a cut". Nobody measures wound depth with a nail. She probes with an instrument (ledger, Ch 12: she confirms the track with the closed jaws of the forceps). A nail's width as a field measure for a margin or an abrasion is fine. Fix: "the way you size a margin against a fingernail when there is no ruler to hand."

- **What works:**
  - Line 11: "A man who had found that panel would have put it back exactly as it was. So would a man who hadn't." This is correct evidential reasoning, and an honest refusal to over-read. Keep it.
  - The 12:58–3:06 gap is found by scrubbing two fixed-angle units, and the screen is unreadable at that resolution while the thumb is not. This stays inside what the ledger says the kit can do.
  - The three walls are argued from what each party would have to disclose (the paper, the old files, what she has or who she has told), not from luck. Only finding 5's wording needs touching.
