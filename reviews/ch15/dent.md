# Dent review: Chapter 15, The Isolation

- **Verdict:** REVISE. Structure, Turns and Snapper are sound; the chapter is roughly 600 words over the cap and Scene 2 is mostly exposition until its last third.

**Word count:** estimate only (no shell available, so `scripts/lint.py` was not run). By hand: S1 about 1,380, S2 about 1,220, S3 about 1,160, total about 3,760 against 3,150 (about 19% over; beat sheet split was 1,050 each). Run lint to confirm.

## Turns, scene by scene

- **S1:** lands. "I had more time than I needed, and I didn't trust it, and so I used it to cook." Event follows (brine, stock, liver standing up). Not summarised.
- **S2:** lands. "He went willingly. He had read the syllabus." Preceded by the taxi, the bottle and the stair light going up, so it is earned by an event and then admitted.
- **S3:** lands. "On the way out, the hall mirror. I looked like someone going on a date." then "I didn't correct it." Snapper follows as a short coda.

## Findings

1. **Over length.** Whole chapter (about 3,760 words). Breaks style/10 point 1 ("Chapter 17 may run long; nothing else may") and strategy/03 ("roughly 1,000-1,100" per scene). Fix: cut about 600 words, roughly 330 from S1, 170 from S2, 100 from S3. S1 candidates: the stitch-removal mechanics ("drew the stitch out through its own track..."), the café description, one of the two Reyes/arithmetic paragraphs, and the brine sensory run before the liver. S3: the nail-filing paragraph can fold into the scrub sentence.

2. **S2 is exposition, not scene, until 5:30 p.m.** "With the feeds gone I had to herd him blind" (paragraph of about 270 words), then voicemail, car reasoning and Tabard. About 700 words of summary before Iris is anywhere. Breaks strategy/03 (Scene 2 should carry pressure, not just logistics) and the dent pressure point (Brandt felt in every scene; here he is only an inventory). Fix: cut the source-inventory in the herding paragraph to the three facts the reader needs (code from facilities mail, floor plan and spec from the card, suite left out of the complaint on purpose). Keep the voicemail scene, which is the only live moment in the stretch, and keep the Tabard paragraph.

3. **Free help from Holt reads as luck.** "That was a pressure I hadn't had to apply. So was the other one." Hero rule (strategy/03, "No luck, no rescue"; style/10 point 5). Two pressures arrive uncaused: the car freeze (inferred, never observed) and Holt's lockout. Both are pending Ch 14 facts, so not an error, but the chapter leans on them in the same breath as the inquiry succeeding early. Fix: make it her design. One clause: she chose the shell account for the car service because she knew it was paid through it; the Holt line stays as a dry aside, not as a gift. This also keeps the "distrust" thread from S1 reading as caution rather than as the author nodding at a coincidence.

4. **S2 Turn leans on an unglossed reference.** "his answer to the broken record" (last line of S2). The Dolphy record is a Ch 13 object; nothing in this chapter reminds the reader. The Turn is the line the scene exists for and it is carrying a callback it hasn't loaded. Fix: one clause earlier in S2 (for instance at the record mailer) such as "the way he had answered the broken record I left on his pillow", or give the mailer a half-line. Do not change the Turn wording itself.

5. **S3 Turn is stated twice.** "I looked like someone going on a date." then "You look like you've got a date," I said. The narration pre-empts its own spoken line, so the admission lands at half strength. Fix: cut the narrated sentence and let the spoken line carry it, with the hall mirror named in the lead-in ("On the way out, the hall mirror.") and "I didn't correct it." as before. Outline Turn survives (she says so, does not correct).

6. **Door "not quite pressed into its seals."** Last paragraph. Hero rule check: acceptable, since the stairwell is opened by her code and the unseated door is Brandt's invitation (beat sheet, Snapper note). But the prose gives the reader no way to know it is his doing rather than a convenient slip. Fix: at most a clause tying it to him ("left that way for me") or keep it as is and let Ch 16 answer. If you leave it, note it is relying on Ch 16 to close.

7. **First sentence is procedural, not load-bearing.** "The stitches came out at a quarter past six on the Monday..." It sets the date and the body but carries little weight; the real hook ("I keep appointments, even my own") is buried mid-sentence. Fix: restructure so the clause about appointments is the sentence's close or its own short opening line. The last sentence is strong and should not be touched.

## Checks that pass
