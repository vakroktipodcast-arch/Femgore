- **Verdict:** REVISE (light; two local fixes, nothing structural)

- **Findings:**

1. "I put the needle into his left deltoid through the sleeve" (scene 1, injection paragraph). This is the chapter's one act of violence, and it is told as pure procedure. Iris is missing from both sentences; her response only arrives a paragraph later ("my mouth wet"). That breaks `bible/style/04` point 7: never without her, and any violent sentence that could be lifted out without losing her should be cut. Fix: put her in the act's own line. For example, end the plunger sentence on her body: "and pressed, and my mouth was still full of it." Or have the muscle's "small resistance and then none" register in her as well as in him. Keep it to one clause, since this is the starved act.

2. "Orbicularis oculi closed hard around both eyes" comes before "the sharp first note of the other sweat". The sensory order runs backwards. The flinch is read by sight, anatomised in Latin, and only then does the smell of fear arrive. `bible/style/04` point 1 says smell first, then sound and texture, with sight last because she looks after she knows. Fix: let the apocrine note reach her as he flinches, or open the flinch on sound (the upholstery, the plastic pulling) and the smell. The orbicularis reading should come afterwards, as confirmation. The one taste in five chapters lands harder if her nose finds his fear before her eyes do.

3. "took the syringe out capped" / "the rest of the agent". The agent is never named, though the lock, the log and the lab around it are exact. Under `bible/style/04` point 2, anatomical and pharmacological exactness is how her competence reads as eroticism, and an unnamed drug from a pathologist reads as coyness. Fix: if `ledger/continuity.md` fixes the compound, name it once, at the cap or the plunger. If it does not, give its class and dose in her clinical register, and log it under New facts in the beat sheet. Do not invent it silently.

- **What works:**
  - The appetite is at the right stage of the arc. Fear-sweat makes her mouth water, "and I am reporting it", unapologetic and filed like a finding. Then the lost count ("I did not feel it go. I found it gone."), and then the snapper's "Nothing came into my mouth." Fed, noticed, starved, in a single chapter, with no moralising.
  - The body carries the page. The cold inside two pairs of gloves, the hand on the scar that she "didn't send", tears logged at thirty-seven degrees, and "black to the wrist, and clean" answering 17's red.
  - Holt's grief is rendered with clinical sincerity (corrugator, mentalis, "qualified to sign for it"). That keeps her crying from becoming victim-softness and makes her harder to look away from.
