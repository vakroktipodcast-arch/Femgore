# ch24 — Voice

- **Verdict:** REVISE

- **Findings:**

1. "the first thing I had tasted in five chapters" (Sc1, aftershave paragraph). This is drafting language that has leaked into the prose. Iris testifies to a reader; she does not count chapters, and the ch17 anchor never steps outside the frame. Rule: 01 point 1 (direct address implicates, it never explains the apparatus). Fix: "the first thing I had tasted since the bathroom floor".

2. "The first time in this book I had been touched" (Snapper, last line). Same frame break, and it lands on the Snapper, so it does the most damage of anything in the chapter. Rule: 01 point 1, and the anchor's frame ("I want it on the record"). Fix: "The first time in two years I had been touched by someone who didn't know who I was, and it was him." Check the time span against the ledger.

3. "The syllabus shattered from the inside, and I was crying too" (Sc2, close). "Shattered from the inside" is stock thriller and purple. It also explains the Turn the scene has already shown. Rule: 02 point 1 (exact, not generic), plus the brief's ban on drift toward explanation. Fix: cut "The syllabus shattered from the inside," and keep "I understood then that he would never be taught. There was no page that reached him. I was crying too, and he thought we were grieving the same woman."

4. "I abandoned the script." (Sc3, opening line). "Script" is not her word. She would say lesson, plan or protocol. As it stands, the sentence could appear in any other novel. Rule: 02 point 2 (her vocabulary). Fix: "I stopped following the lesson." Or cut it and open on the next paragraph's "There was no next step."

5. "I am telling you plainly, because there isn't another way to tell it." (Sc3). This is filler direct address that implicates nobody. The anchor's version gives a reason ("because I want it on the record"). Rule: 01 point 1. Fix: cut the clause after "plainly", or replace it: "I am telling you plainly, so that you can't say later you weren't told."

6. "'You knew her,' he said. Not a question." (Sc2). "Not a question" is a stock genre tag, and it explains the line. Rule: brief (sentence that could appear in any novel); 07 (dialogue should carry itself). Fix: cut "Not a question." Holt's level delivery already does that work.

7. "on the worst day of their lives" (Sc3, long Holt paragraph). This is a stock phrase inside an otherwise exact sentence. Rule: 02 point 1. Fix: name the real occasion, e.g. "who has sat with a great many widows in a great many front rooms".

8. "I am qualified to tell you what it was, and I will be exact, because it deserves that much." (Sc2, the weeping). This nearly copies ch17's "I am going to be exact about them, because they deserve it." Here it reads as self-pastiche, not as a motif. Rule: anchors (same narrator, not an imitation of her). Fix: "I am qualified to tell you what it was." Then go straight to the face.

9. "the chin puckered over mentalis like the skin on cooling milk" (Sc2). This reuses ch17's peritoneum simile "like the skin on hot milk". It is the second milk-skin in two set pieces. Rule: 06 point 3 (exact, fresh to the body in front of her). Fix: replace it with a dimpled image she would know from work, e.g. "puckered over mentalis like orange peel".

10. "beside the capped red marker" (Sc3, pin paragraph). The colour is unmodified. Rule: 06 point 1 (never red unmodified). Fix: "the capped oxblood marker", or whatever shade the ledger has for the ch17 marker.

11. "the thing I had spent two years on had never failed to see women" (Sc2, finding paragraph). Here the thesis is stated, not found. "The thing" is vague for a narrator this exact. Rule: 02 point 1, plus the brief's ban on drift toward explanation. Fix: "the syllabus had never failed to see women. It had seen Diane down to the stammer. It had chosen which ones." The last two sentences stay as they are.

- **What works:**
  1. The aftershave/apocrine paragraph: one greedy sentence that calls back "fear, recent onset" and lets appetite return as saliva. It is the ch17 register, used once and on purpose. Keep it intact apart from finding 1.
  2. The grading reflex is shown failing ("it found no hand to hold the pen with"), and "Manubrium… went no further than that." This is how 02 point 4 should be handled in 22–25: the voice absent, and the absence felt on the page.
  3. Holt's em-dashes are confined to Sc2 ("Her daughter — Olivia — I was at the —"), and the Act III jokes thin to almost nothing after Sc1 ("Counting is what offices are for", "We had both done the reading"). Do not add any jokes after Sc1.
