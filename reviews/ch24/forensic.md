- **Verdict:** REVISE (light). The procedure, anatomy and evidence handling hold up. Three moments need a clause each so they don't read as luck or as a slip Iris would never make.

- **Findings:**

1. **A free-handed man gets a long, unexplained grace period.** "And there it was, coming up through everything else in the room" / "I looked last, the way I always do." After the flinch, a full sensory paragraph and an observation paragraph come before "I put the needle into him". All that time Holt is conscious and undrugged. His right hand is free and the detail is just outside. Before that, he says nothing at all while he is tied and she opens the case and uncaps the needle. A protection or law-enforcement reader will ask why he neither grabs nor calls out. The chapter's own explanation comes too late ("He didn't shout. Even then his syntax held."). This breaks the agent brief: physical action must be physically possible, with nothing reading as luck. **Fix:** make the time explicit. One clause is enough: the smell and the look happen inside a second, "before he had a breath to use". Or put the needle in on the flinch and make the sweat and orbicularis paragraphs her reading of him as it goes in. Also give him one polite, puzzled beat at the tie ("He looked at the tie as if it were a lanyard someone had handed him"), so his silence reads as his character and not as a gap in the plot.

2. **Skin contact on a body the federal government will autopsy.** "His fingertips found the gap at my wrist... and rested there." His bare fingertips are on her bare skin. This is a contact-transfer event, and the victim's fingernail scrapings and hand swabs will be taken at Holt's autopsy with two federal observers present (ledger Timeline, Tue 3 Nov). Her profile is on the ME office's elimination register (ledger People, Iris, Ch 12 line). Iris's skills are listed as "knowledge of what investigators look for" (cast.md, Iris). A pathologist who double-gloved to the wrist would register that inch. **Fix:** do not move his hand. The Snapper needs it. Add half a clause of registration and no action, e.g. "the one inch of wrist it didn't cover, which I knew the name for at the bench, and I left it there." That makes the touch cost her something, and the forensic risk is now her choice and not the author's oversight. If the author wants no transfer, put the contact on the glove cuff. Either way, flag it to the continuity agent for Ch 25 to 27 and the sequel.

3. **A wooden top under a skirted table.** "The black nitrile made a small dry sound against the wood". The ledger gives "skirted banquet table as dressing table" (ledger Places, Alder Suite). Hotel banquet tables are dressed with a top cloth as well as the skirt, and anyone who has worked hotel advance knows it. **Fix:** "against the cloth", and change the sound to match (a soft one, or none: "made no sound on the cloth as I let go").

4. **How a lab logs controlled stock.** "out of a locked cabinet with a log on its door". Controlled stock is logged in a bound or electronic register that is signed in and out, not on a sheet on the cabinet door. The detail is small but a lab professional will notice it, and it is what makes Reyes's paper trail credible (cast.md, Reyes: "builds her case on paper"). **Fix:** "out of a locked cabinet with a signed register beside it". Keep the line about the log not agreeing with the shelf. Do not add anything about what the stock is (ledger Objects, syringe rule).

5. **A pathologist overstating.** "that peaked, unmistakable shape nobody has ever produced on purpose". The grief brow (medial frontalis with corrugator) is hard to make voluntarily, but a minority of people can. A pathologist who is "qualified to tell you" would hedge. **Fix:** "that almost nobody can produce on purpose".

- **What works:**
  - The syringe handling is right and matches the ledger: capped out of the case, cap to case, case to pocket; withdrawn with the barrel part-given; set down open with the fifteen-year rule broken on the page. It never names or explains the contents. Holt's observed state (heavy shoulders, slowed speech, conscious, crying) stays observation only.
  - The anatomy is spoken naturally: orbicularis in the flinch; corrugator, frontalis and mentalis in the grief; apocrine "other sweat" from the axillae; tear tracks reasoned from head position; her own tears read by temperature first. It reads as a person who has done this, not a manual.
  - The tie choreography (two fingers and a thumb like a pulse, under the arm at the front-leg gap, drawn tight "before his face had caught up with his arm") is physically sound for one woman of her build and matches the ledger's chair and gap.
