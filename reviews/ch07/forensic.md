- **Verdict:** REVISE

- **Findings:**

1. **The scene kit is missing from the inventory (lines 7–15).** Ch 4 (l. 69, l. 79) and Ch 5 put heavy plastic sheeting on the floor with its edges taped, foam weatherstrip round the door frame, a moving blanket hung inside the door, a rubber wedge and a cord loop. There is also the kit row along the far wall. The ledger's Ch 6 ruling (Objects) says "all other kit go[es] with her". Ch 7's "What went with me" never touches any of it. The sweep then reads bare concrete, "the dust was the station's", which is a floor Iris had already swept and covered with sheeting. A professional reader will see a taped plastic floor and a blanketed door left behind at a homicide scene. **Fix:** add a short paragraph before the sweep. She cuts the cord loop, pockets the wedge, strips the weatherstrip and blanket, then lifts the sheeting, folding it in on itself from the far wall towards the door around where Diane sits. She reads the concrete it covered, so the oblique-light sweep is reading a floor that has just been uncovered. If the sheeting was under Diane's body, the continuity agent has to rule on that. Raise it there; don't settle it in the prose.

2. **She takes off her overshoes inside the scene and then stands there in trainers (l. 13), before the sweep (l. 15).** Ch 4 has her sweeping the floor and bagging the sweepings, so the trainers would leave sole prints in what dust remains. The order is also backwards. Any scene examiner would do the oblique-light sweep while still in the suit, then undress at the sill, stepping each foot out over the threshold as its overshoe comes off. Her sole-reading at l. 13 shows she is already thinking about soles, which makes the slip worse. **Fix:** put the sweep first, then the doffing. "Just inside the door" becomes "at the sill, one foot over it".

3. **She leaves the lantern on the floor and then goes back for it (l. 15 against l. 17).** "You don't walk back across a floor you have just read" is followed by her switching off the lantern and packing it. For its beam to lie flat across the floor it has to be inside, so she crosses the floor she has just read to fetch it. **Fix:** "laid the lantern on its side at the threshold, at my feet". This keeps the oblique light and the rule.

4. **"Every scrap of that paper carried the number" (l. 11).** The footer is one thin strip per page (ledger, Objects: "Every NDA page footer carries only the series matter number"). Eight pages cross-cut to fingernail size leave only a handful of pieces with the number on them, and the lab reconstructs three (Fixed numbers). A pathologist would not say "every scrap". **Fix:** "every page that went into that bag had carried the number under its thin rule".

5. **The rigor paragraph ends wrong (l. 59).** Rigor does not "let go of everything at once". It passes off gradually, in roughly the order it set in. At four degrees it also lasts well beyond a day and a half, and Iris has just placed the body in a fridge. The rest of the paragraph is correct: jaw first, height at about 18 h, lividity fixed. **Fix:** "...it holds longer in the cold, and then lets go, a muscle at a time, in the order it came."

6. **The cyclist reads as convenience, and Iris does not treat him as a witness (l. 25).** Tyres on gravel crunch. Silence is more believable on packed earth, or from a rider freewheeling downhill whose hub ticks only once he has passed her. More important: the rider passed her parked car at the lay-by within forty minutes of the kill. Someone with Iris's training would note at once what he could give an investigator: a woman in a fleece, a slate-grey hatchback, the time. As written, she logs her pulse and nothing else. **Fix:** change "packed gravel" to "packed earth", and add one clause of inventory, e.g. "What he could give them: a woman, a fleece, a grey car at a lay-by. I added it to the schedule's debit column." The Turn stays as it is.

7. **Minor: "adrenaline, a flood of it, for hours" (l. 49).** Adrenaline's half-life is a couple of minutes. A pathologist giving herself the lecture would say what is actually sustained. **Fix:** "adrenaline, a flood of it, topped up for hours", or "the sympathetic drive, for hours".

- **What works:**
  - The surgical-scrub detail. Water runs off the elbow and never back onto the hands, which is correct, and it shows she has done it.
  - Onglet as the crura of the diaphragm, and the sequence of rigor (jaw first, height around 18 h, fixed lividity in a body that died sitting). This is accurate, and it is told in her voice rather than as a lecture.
  - The inner gloves stay on until the car and come off inside out. Diane's own phone goes back into her pocket powered off, and the reasoning is given ("A missing phone writes a robbery"). Keep both.
