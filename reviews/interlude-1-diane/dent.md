# Dent review: interlude-1-diane

- **Verdict:** PASS. Two minor findings, neither blocking.

## Checks

- **Length and shape:** one scene, one Turn, about 790 words by hand estimate (target 600-900; run the lint to confirm). Correct for an interlude (strategy/03 point 5: single Turn, no three-scene count).
- **Turn (outline):** lands at "Because it's the one hour of my life I'm not reachable." It arrives as spoken admission, then is tightened by "To not be reachable for one hour of my life. That's all I want. One." It is an event in dialogue, not a summary. The narration does not state that Iris has found the profile, so the reader holds the gap as the beat sheet requires.
- **Scene 1 in the trouble in the first page:** not applicable (Diane's POV, Iris is off the page). The "trouble" is Diane's managed calm: the opening paragraphs (61 unread, "Eight weeks and six days", the dated tape) set it up inside the first 150 words.
- **Act pressure (certainty that nothing is coming):** felt throughout. Everything is labelled, scheduled and "managed", and the alarm tone closes the routine.
- **Hero rule:** no luck, rescue or convenient door in the scene. Iris is not present. The only open Diane-side facts (never-private profile, Sunday habit) are things Diane did or said herself.
- **Snapper (strategy/03 point 3, style/10 point 8):** an image, not a cliffhanger. "a small green light going round its face as it filled" is correct and does not name a watcher.
- **First and last sentences:** last is load-bearing. First is serviceable but light (finding 2).

## Findings

1. **Turn is mid-scene, not last.** After "...hour of my life. That's all I want. One." about 230 words follow (the "list" exchange, the Viking-style sign-off, the DND exposition, the closing routine) before the Snapper. This breaks style/10 point 3 ("every scene ends on its Turn") only in the letter, since the Snapper image is doing the closing work and the beat sheet planned it this way. The risk is dilution. Fix: keep the structure, but trim the narration line "On the walk the phone was on Do Not Disturb, and only one number rang through it." That is the one place the prose explains rather than shows, and it lowers the Turn's echo. Move the fact into Diane's dialogue ("You're the list" already carries it) or cut it, and trust ch05 to pay it off.
2. **Opening sentence carries no name and little weight.** "She was home by five past ten, which for a Wednesday was early." Diane is not named until "Diane sat down" in the sixth paragraph. Style/10 point 8 wants the first sentence load-bearing. Fix: name her in sentence one, and let the detail that matters ride on it, for example "Diane Pell was home by five past ten, which for a Wednesday was early, and nobody had noticed yet." Keep it dry. Do not add anything that sounds like foreboding (beat sheet: Diane must not sense Iris).

## What works

- The Turn's two-step delivery: the plain first answer, then "Then, because she edited everything, she said it again, tighter."
- The stutter held to one beat, resolving on "Tired.", with Olivia's silence as the contrast.
- The Snapper image: the green light on the charging watch, no watching-verb, no announcement.
