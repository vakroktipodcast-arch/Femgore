**Verdict:** REVISE (light). The register is mostly right for an interlude, but the narrator steps in with wit and explanation a few times, and two lines could sit in any novel.

**Findings:**

1. "so that at least something in her life could be thrown away on schedule". This is an aphoristic joke delivered by the narrator. It has the shape of an Iris line, like the white-bread/childhood riff in ch01, and it nudges the reader toward Lesson One. Interludes must be plainer and cooler, with no Iris-isms (task brief; 01 point 2; compare the interlude-2-cam anchor, where wit stays inside Cam's own head: "Both of them would get what they wanted, Cam thought."). Fix: end the sentence at "in her own hand." The tape and dates already make the point.

2. "She opened the fridge and stood in its cold breath." The fridge is personified, and this is a stock image that could appear in any novel. The Cam anchor keeps its similes functional and rare. Fix: "She opened the fridge and stood in the cold from it," or simply "She stood at the open fridge."

3. "then a fire door sighed shut behind her". This is the second soft personification within four sentences, and it is generic. Fix: "then a fire door closed behind her". The register change is already carried by "the whisper became her ordinary voice."

4. "Then, because she edited everything, she said it again, tighter." Two problems. The narrator explains her motive (drift toward explanation), and the restatement that follows ("To not be reachable for one hour of my life. That's all I want. One.") is longer, so "tighter" is inaccurate. Fix: cut the clause and the adjective, e.g. "Then she said it again." Alternatively, actually tighten the line ("Not reachable. One hour. That's all.") and let the reader see the editing.

5. "It was good advice, and kind, and the same sentence she would have used at the office." The last clause points at the Gatekeeper parallel that the reader is meant to make for themselves ("process" versus "protection"). It is explanation, not observation. Fix: keep "She believed it. It was good advice, and kind." and cut the office clause. "The process is how she closes the window" has already done the work.

6. "There was the old catch from childhood, a beat in which the word stood..." The stutter is correctly non-phonetic (07 point 4). However, "the old catch from childhood" is a backstory gloss, and the hesitation is followed by a word substitution, so it is not the single hesitation the rule asks for. Fix: cut "from childhood", and render it as one beat: "the word stood in the doorway and would not move. Then a different one came out from behind it."

7. "and they both laughed". This is generic warm-family shorthand and could be in any novel. Fix: give one specific, e.g. "and Olivia laughed, and Diane heard herself laugh," or cut it and go straight to "and Diane told her to go to bed".

**What works:**

- The closing image, "a small green light going round its face as it filled". It is cool, plain and sourced light, and it ends on the Turn without comment.
- "She listened the way she listened at work, one ear on what was being said and one on what was wanted." Together with the pale band under the dress watch, this does in plain terms what Iris would do with Latin.
- The fragments of free indirect speech ("Buy a second blanket; she would pay." / "Mother at four.") follow the Cam anchor's cadence exactly. Keep them.
