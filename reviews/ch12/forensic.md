- **Verdict:** REVISE

- **Findings:**

1. "keyed the midline into his panel on my way out" — Iris arms an intrusion alarm over a moving, bleeding man tied to a rack upright and a corpse in a motion-lit hall. The survivor will trip the PIRs within seconds and bring a monitored response to a floor with her blood on it. That undoes her own reasoning ("He would clean it better than I could"). Anyone who has run a scene will stop here. It breaks ledger Fixed numbers (Lantern alarm code; PIR) and the Ch 12 rule that Brandt does the cleaning. Fix: give her one clause of reasoning. Either she leaves the panel unset on purpose ("I left it open for whoever he sent"), or she arms it knowing it rings only Brandt's phone, not a monitoring company. Either way she should notice the problem, not repeat the routine without thinking.

2. "twisted my torso into the lamp until the wound came into the light" — the wound sits below the tip of the twelfth rib, which is posterolateral (ledger, Fixed numbers, wound). Nobody can see that spot directly well enough to place three buried fascial sutures and seven everted skin stitches, sitting on a bath edge. A surgeon reading this will ask where the mirror was. Fix: add a hand or shaving mirror propped on the cistern. Let her register the mirror reversal once as a cost ("everything backwards, which I corrected for at every pass"). That keeps the steadiness and makes it earned.

3. "Peritoneum intact." / "At home I found I was right." — she cannot confirm the peritoneum, or which layer the blade stopped in, on herself at home without a CT or a wound exploration. The negative signs (no guarding, no rebound, no Kehr's sign, clear urine) give her an inference, not a finding. A pathologist would phrase it as one. Fix: "Peritoneum, on the evidence, intact." Support "I found I was right" with one concrete act, for example the base of the irrigated track seen in the mirror, or the depth gauged against the needle driver's jaw.

4. "to just short of flexor carpi ulnaris" — the ulnar artery and nerve lie immediately radial to the FCU tendon. A cut that stops "just short of FCU" has gone through the ulnar artery, which contradicts "left him both arteries" (ledger, Minor, Contractor B: both radial and ulnar arteries spared). Fix: end the stroke "just short of the ulnar artery" or "short of the ulnar bundle". Leave the rest as it is.

5. "the first metatarsal, where the bone runs long and thin" — the first metatarsal is the shortest and thickest of the five. An anatomist would wince. Fix: cut "long and thin". Keep "close under the skin", which is true and is the point.

6. "took the dead man's wallet from his back pocket with two gloved fingers" — the man is heavy, supine and lying in his own blood, so his back pocket is under his weight. Two fingers will not get the wallet out cleanly, and that reads as luck. Fix: one clause of effort, for example "worked it out from under his hip with two fingers", which still keeps her touching him only with two fingers.

7. "one round his good wrist and one round the post" — once his good hand is tied, the survivor can no longer press on the cut wrist, and Brandt's collection is hours away. Iris has just called the bleed "survivable". She would think about compression, and the consideration is in character (see "I would like it noted"). Fix: she tucks his cut wrist into his armpit or between his knees before she ties him, or she says he can still clamp it against his thigh.

8. "Blood loss perhaps three hundred millilitres, a generous donation" — a standard whole-blood donation is about 450–500 ml, so 300 ml is less than one. Fix: "most of a donation" or "two-thirds of a donation".

9. "Lidocaine lives in a cabinet at work." — a medical examiner's morgue does not routinely stock local anaesthetic. The next sentence also makes it sound as if the drug is there but withheld from her. Fix: "Lidocaine lives in hospitals." Keep the joke that follows.
