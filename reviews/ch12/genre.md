# ch12 — Genre contract

- **Verdict:** REVISE

- **Findings:**

1. **The kill is the coolest moment in the fight, when it should be the hottest.** At "Then I opened the side of his neck. Anterior triangle" the anatomy is there, but nothing after it shows what she feels: no pulse, no heat, no hunger. She steps back, takes her hands away and counts. The bible says her pleasure is on the page in every kill except the one in Chapter 25 (style/04, point 3), and that every explicit line is also a line about what Iris is feeling (style/04, last point). The excitement peaks earlier, at the wrist cut and when the lights come on, and then falls away at the death itself. That reverses the shape Chapter 12 needs (style/04, Ch 12 point; strategy/10, Act II). **Fix:** in the same sentence as the spray, put the carotid's pulse against her wrist or face and set it against her own racing pulse, then give the pleasure its plain name. Keep "I counted", but make the counting something she enjoys, not a way of cooling down.

2. **"it was fast and it was ugly and the fans drowned"** This is a summary of the violence, not a rendering of it, and it borrows the outline's own words. It also leaves sound out completely and goes straight from anatomy to temperature (style/04, point 1: render, don't imply; smell, sound, texture, temperature, weight, sight last). **Fix:** cut the phrase. Add one texture or weight detail (how the vessel wall gives, the drag of the blade against the sternocleidomastoid), and one sound she hears under the fans because she is close enough (the wet change in his breathing is already there, so move it up).

3. **She never looks at him.** After the spray, the dead man is reported ("gone in under a minute") but never seen. The bible says sight comes last but does come: "she looks after she knows" (style/04, point 1), and the reader is promised "to see her see it" (strategy/02, point 3). **Fix:** before "He was dead on the warehouse floor", add one or two sentences of her looking at him under the white LED light: the blood across the white-painted floor, his beard. Pair the look with her reaction in the same line.

4. **"I did not call it pleasure. I called it an advantage"** This is Act I behaviour. In Act I she files the feeling under "the grade" (strategy/10, Act I). Act II says "She stops pretending", and the fight in Chapter 12 "is a pleasure she describes as such". Later lines ("I was delighted", "the most fun I had had") repair some of this, but the first naming moment still steps back. **Fix:** have her drop the cover story here, on the page. For example: "I could have called it an advantage. I didn't bother." Then keep the pulse and the warm face as findings.

5. **"He was the man who had said my name."** This hands the reader a motive two paragraphs before the Turn says she killed him "for no reason except that I could". It gives the reader a way to dismiss her: provoked, a reaction to being violated (style/12). It also weakens the Turn in the outline (outline/ch12, Scene 2 Turn). **Fix:** cut the line, or turn it against itself, for example "He was the man who had said my name, which was not a reason, and I knew it was not."

- **What works:**
  - The stab told layer by layer, "the kidney, the colon, the spleen and your opinion of yourself", and "I was delighted". This is exactly what Chapter 12 needs: funny, exact, and frightening.
  - The self-suture paragraph, where pain and "the deep plain pleasure of doing a thing properly" share one sentence, and the steady hands. Also the raw tartare narrated as physiology ("Haemorrhage wants iron"). Do not trim either.
  - The sex is brief and unsentimental, and its centre is the stranger's soft fingertips and the toothbrush taken "because it had me on it". It is never the point of the scene, as the outline's editorial note asks.
