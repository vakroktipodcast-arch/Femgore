**Verdict:** REVISE (light: two line-level fixes; the chapter is otherwise right for its stage)

**Findings:**

1. **The appetite reads as gone, when it should be starved.** Quotes: "Then it was gone. I noted that it had gone", and "It had been a rule about wanting. Now it was a rule." Both lines are good on their own. Together, and with nothing to answer them, they say the hunger is dead. That breaks strategy/10, Act III: she "enters the suite hungry for the one meal the whole curriculum was for". Chapter 25's nothing only lands if the hunger for Holt is still alive here, unfed and with no pleasure in it. At the moment Chapter 20 spends the payoff five chapters early. Fix: at "I thought about Monday." (para 6), or right after "Now it was a rule.", add one clinical line. In it she notes that the hunger is still there, aimed at one man only, and that she feels no pleasure in it, like an empty stomach logged as a finding. Don't explain it and don't warm it. Keep "Now it was a rule" and let the new line push against it.

2. **The wound pulls one chapter too early.** Quote: "The wound pulled on the turn of the landing". style/05 (The wound) says it is "a presence" that she touches when thinking (already done well at "the ridge of the scar"), and that "It pulls in 21". Pulling it here takes away 21's beat. Fix: make the stair beat presence, not pain. She feels the scar under the waistband on the turn and logs it, with no pulling. Or cut the clause and let the count carry the stair.

3. **Minor: the chicken's link back to Chapter 17 is too oblique.** Quote: "waiting too long for a reason". style/09 marks this meal as "planned, not taken". This is where the genre reader watches that meal get thrown away, and "a reason" leaves a reader who has forgotten 17 without the link. Fix: add one clause that names what the brine was for (the meal after, in her flat language), so throwing it away reads as the appetite being starved, not as housekeeping.

**What works (do not break):**

- The click at the seal: "the old click came… Then it was gone. I noted… the time." This is appetite noticed in its absence, reported as a finding. It is exactly the Act III register, and she doesn't moralise.
- Humour still carries a chapter with no violence: "Fail, for reasons beyond the candidate's control", "She knew when to stop", "Most of them mention the cat", "a senator's last night". The deadened ribs joke ("it didn't come out funny. It came out as a number") marks the starvation through the humour itself.
- No apology anywhere: "Any sentence that could be read as sorry. I wasn't". The body stays on the page throughout (toner warmth as the only warm thing, cold flat, the stale heel of bread, the belt that needs a new hole, the hand finding the pocket). She is harder to look away from, not easier to dismiss.
