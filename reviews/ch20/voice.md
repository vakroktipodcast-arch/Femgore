# Voice review: ch20

- **Verdict:** REVISE. This is a light revision. The narrator is recognisably the same one, and the joke rate thins correctly for Act III. The weak spots are explanation and procedural drift in Scenes 1 and 3, plus a Turn pasted in from the outline.

## Findings

1. **"The green room was a converted meeting suite with a service door…"** (l.27). This is the outline's Turn copied almost word for word. It restates what lines 21–25 have just shown, so it is a summary sentence (02, register; 01, point 1: "never to explain"). It also produces the clash "service door the Service". Fix: cut the first sentence. End the scene on the line before ("I noted that it had gone, and I noted the time.") followed by "The seal was a sticker." on its own line. That is the beat the Turn needs.

2. **"The abuse desk had restored the line after its review"** (l.7). The whole paragraph recaps the plot mechanics: tap, uplink, abuse desk, farm. It drifts into thriller exposition, with "because nobody but Brandt had known… and nobody but me knew" explaining the logic to the reader (02; 01, point 1). Fix: cut it to two sentences. Keep "copying the campaign to itself for a dead man under the signature of a dead woman", which is the voice. Drop the abuse-desk and review clauses.

3. **"I didn't need to write anything; I only needed to print."** and **"bought from the uniform supplier for cash because of the wordmark"** (l.13, l.65). These are generic heist logistics, and each one stops to justify itself ("because… because…"). The sentences could sit in any procedural novel. Fix: in l.13, cut "I changed nothing in their database… only needed to print" and "Hers was a real name… for the door". Let the warm card and the three lines do the work. In l.65, cut "because of the wordmark" and shorten "because on Monday nothing would pass the magnetometer but me, a badge, a bag, and a page" to "On Monday the magnetometer would get me, a badge, a bag, and a page."

4. **"The belt went to its last hole and was loose on it."** (l.73). The Latinate/plain collision is close to absent from the whole chapter (02, Vocabulary: Latinate "for anatomy, always"). The flank is "the ridge of the scar" (l.15), and the weight loss is reported as a layman would report it. Starved appetite should still be documented by a pathologist. Fix: add one clinical finding about herself here, for example estimating the loss in kilograms against the belt, in dictaphone register. Make the l.15 scar anatomical as well (e.g. "the ridge of the scar below the costal margin").

5. **"found every gap in my coat"** (l.67). This is a stock phrase that could appear in any other novel. Fix: tie it to her body, e.g. the coat now sitting away from her the way the blazer does in l.73, with the cold getting in where she has lost weight.

6. **"in the same order as before, move for move, the way you do a procedure"** (l.71). The simile is circular: it compares a procedure to a procedure and uses "before" twice. It also leaves the Ch15 ritual unnamed, though the outline needs the reader to feel it hollowed. Fix: "in the same order as the night of the suite, move for move" and drop the simile. Or use a precise surgical comparison, such as the scrub sequence.

7. **"pressing each strip… until it had decided to stay"** (l.61), together with l.45 "until it had decided to stay". Using the Ch1 motif twice in one chapter weakens it. Fix: keep it at l.61, where the gesture repeats with no pleasure. At l.45 cut to "June's sticker, and my thumb."

8. **"I traced the route with the red from the pen tray"** (l.17). This is unmodified "red" (06, point 1). Fix: name the colour of the marker, or say "the red marker" as the anchor does in ch01 so that it names the object, not the colour.

9. **"It was probably somewhere in the voicemail; I wouldn't know."** (l.51). The line is muddy. It reads as though she is unsure of her own nickname, which weakens the Turn just before it lands. Fix: cut it, or make it exact: "She would have said it in the voicemail. I hadn't played it."

Banned-phrase check: clean. Dialogue: clean, with "Thanks" and "You don't eat before" both properly clipped. Grading voice: present, which is allowed before Ch22. Direct address: used once (l.43), and it implicates the reader. That is fine.

## What works

- "Twelve pairs, I thought, and it didn't come out funny. It came out as a number." This line makes the Act III thinning of the jokes visible. Do not touch it.
- "It had been a rule about wanting. Now it was a rule." This handles the absence of appetite through the body and the rule, without naming it (11). Keep it, along with the trussing-needle line in l.73.
- "For a moment the old click came… Then it was gone. I noted that it had gone, and I noted the time." This is the clinical self-report at exactly the right temperature.
