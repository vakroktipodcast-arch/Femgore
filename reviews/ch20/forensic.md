- **Verdict:** REVISE. The procedure here holds up, but four links in the Election Eve plan rest on luck or on assertion rather than on research, and Ch 21–26 depend on them.

- **Findings:**

1. **Ruth Calder on the door list is assumed.** "it would already be on any list they printed for the door". Nothing establishes why a no-show phone-banker would be on the Election Eve back-of-house list. A professional reads this as the plan's single point of failure, papered over. Ch 21's checker works from a laminated list by surname (ledger, Minor: credential checker), so the name has to be on it. That is the outline's whole thesis (outline/ch20.md S1: "the vulnerability is … the bureaucracy"). **Fix:** add one clause showing she has seen how the list is made. For example, the door list for the night is an export of every name marked active, and that export is on the card. It could go with the existing "marking people inactive had also been Compliance". The same clause, or the run-of-show, should let her know the desk checks the name against the list and asks for no ID. Then Ch 21 reads as research paying off.

2. **The seal is in the walk-through photographs too early, and the "Service's advance plan" is in campaign mail.** "found the walk-through photographs, and found that corridor, and that door". The card was collected Sat 24 Oct (ledger, Timeline). The Service sweeps a hold room and seals its doors on the day, after the sweep, not nine days ahead. Its own advance plan would also not circulate in campaign email. And "showed it with one door" sits badly beside the same Service having sealed the second door. **Fix:** make the source the campaign's copy of the advance site diagram or the security annex. Have it list the pantry door as *to be sealed, corridor side* (hatched, one word). The photograph then shows that make of label on another door on the walk-through, or the annex shows the label stock. The Turn line stays exactly as written.

3. **The closet is unlocked by luck, and nothing covers Monday or the sweep.** "The closet was unlocked; it was set-up day." Hotel linen rooms are normally locked, because linen walks. "It was set-up day" does not explain how she gets back in on Election Eve. A sceptic will also ask whether the sweep reaches a closet one floor below the hold room. **Fix:** give two clauses of cause. First, why the door opens on event days: it is propped for banquet linen runs, or it has a lever latch the hotel doesn't lock during functions. The par sheet, already in the scene, could carry this. Second, one line from the annex limiting the sweep to floor three and the principal's route. Both reuse sources the chapter already has.

4. **The vendor gate: fire code and the floor plan's "19 S".** "that fire code requires to open from inside on a button". Egress codes call for a push bar, or a manual release that holds the door unlocked for longer than 19 s. Hotel floor plans also don't record relock timers. A facilities person would stop here. **Fix:** cut the fire-code attribution and the plan note. Make it "a green exit button inside, on a timer". Let S3's count ("Nineteen") be where she learns the number, which is the stronger beat anyway. If a document must show it, use a door schedule in the security annex.

5. **"Hundreds" of letters out of coat pockets is too many.** "I have taken hundreds of letters out of coat pockets on my table." Over about 1,400 autopsies, the notes that reach her number in the dozens, and most are recovered at the scene, not from pockets at autopsy. A pathologist would wince. **Fix:** "I have read a great many of these: most as photocopies in a scene file, a few out of coat pockets on my table." "More of these than anyone I know" stays.

6. **A caterer's branded jacket bought for cash.** "bought from the uniform supplier for cash because of the wordmark". Suppliers embroider branded uniforms to the company's order and don't sell them over the counter. **Fix:** "off the supplier's seconds rail" or "second-hand", for cash. That keeps "bought for the wordmark" (ledger, Objects: catering jacket).

7. **The cable mats are the wrong way round.** "so that the networks' cables could run under them". Cables are run first and the protectors go over them. **Fix:** "laid over the runs from the multbox to the riser", or "laid ready for the networks' cables". One phrase.

- **What works:**
  - She checks the plan physically rather than trusting paper: 31 s timed on the stair, the gate counted back to locked, the button pressed with a knuckle. Keep all of it. The fix in finding 4 should lean on it more, not less.
  - The staging logic is right: "nothing would pass the magnetometer but me, a badge, a bag, and a page". The research is right too: the par sheet tells her the Wednesday-wedding cloths won't be touched.
  - The professional detail in the letter scene: the clear plastic property bag; the letter written to be read first "with gloves on and a camera"; June's costume with ten ribs on one side and twelve on the other. The clock-change arithmetic ("what was now four … knowing it was five") is also correct.
