# Continuity review: Chapter 13

**Verdict:** REVISE. One real factual clash (the pork's timing) and a few small repairs. Dates, times, counts, the wound, the hand-offs to Ch 12 and Interlude 3, and Iris's knowledge all hold.

## Findings

1. **Medium. The pork is braised "that afternoon" but already carved "at lunch".** Quote: "Braised that afternoon with the fennel and the garlic... a few slices gone at lunch." Lunch comes before the afternoon, so the dish cannot be braised after the slices are taken. Braised at about 3 p.m., it also could not be "set cold in its own jelly" by 10 p.m. (ledger, Brandt: braised pork shoulder, Sun 4 Oct; "one end carved at lunch"). The same "afternoon" appears in the smell paragraph ("Fennel and garlic from the afternoon"). Fix: braise it "that morning" and change the smell line to "from the day". That keeps the ledger's Sun 4 Oct, lunch and jelly, and Brandt is still at the War Room from the afternoon.

2. **Low-medium. The deadbolt reasoning is garbled.** Quote: "nobody with a key leaves a door looking that innocent." A key leaves no marks at all, so the clause argues backwards. It also overstates Ch 9, where Iris "left that open" (a key, or a pick). The ledger ruling (14 Nov) lets her treat it as a pick by Ch 13, so the conclusion is allowed but the logic is not. Fix: drop the clause and write something like "He had come through mine without a mark, and by now I had decided it was a pick." The "even" payoff stays.

3. **Low. Wrapper versus worn soap.** Quote: "I did not read the wrapper." The bar is later "worn to a lozenge", so it has no wrapper (ledger, Brandt's kitchen: a bar of vetiver soap by the tap). Fix: "I did not look for the maker's name", or cut the wrapper clause.

4. **Low. Her DNA discipline slips in the kitchen.** She bares the right hand, eats with her fingers, smooths the jelly with a bare fingertip, then uses his tap and soap bare (ledger, People: she takes a toothbrush home for DNA in Ch 12; Who knows what: Brandt never learns what she ate). Brandt cannot test it, and the later Brandt-house search by anyone else is not in play. But "I would have touched nothing of his but the food" is not literally true, since the tap and soap are touched too. Fix: either drop "nothing of his but the food", or have her wipe the tap handle with the towel she pockets. The towel is already pocketed, so this costs one clause.

5. **Low. "I can't tell pitch."** In the same paragraph she names the A above middle C, but by counting keys (fine). In Ch 14 she hums the Mingus recognisably enough that Mara asks what it is (`manuscript/ch14.md`, "I was humming."). Fix: "I can't name a pitch by ear" would stay true to both.
