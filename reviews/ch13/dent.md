# Dent review: Chapter 13 (manuscript/ch13.md)

- **Verdict:** REVISE. The structure and all three Turns are sound, but the chapter runs about 4,100 words against a ~3,150 target.

## Turn map

- **Scene 1:** The Turn lands at "Then the licence. I had taken it off his…". It runs through the invoice paragraph ("I laid it beside the halves. It was an invoice…") and the quarter-inch slide. The scene closes on "I didn't say why. There was nobody to say it to." It is an event followed by an admission, not a summary. The flirting is shown and never named.
- **Scene 2:** The Turn lands at "I know one note." and then "It went out into the warm house…". It closes on "I closed the fallboard over it while it was still sounding, and left the room before I could play more." The key is in tune and she leaves, as the outline requires.
- **Scene 3:** The Turn lands at "Then he laughed." It carries through the drink and the Black Saint ("I sat in the dark… listened to the whole side with him"). The Snapper follows: "At the end of side one the tone arm lifted…". It is an image and an admission, with no cliffhanger and nothing after it.

## Findings

1. **Length.** Scene 1 is about 1,600 words, Scene 2 about 1,250 and Scene 3 about 1,250. The total is roughly 4,100 against a 3,150 target, with scenes meant to be ~1,050 each.
   - Rule broken: style/10 point 1 ("nothing else may [run long]" except Ch 17). Strategy/03 says 1,000–1,100 per scene.
   - Fix: cut Scene 1 by about 500 words. Compress the smell inventory ("Coffee grounds, a day old…"). Trim the Diane-print paragraph. Merge the wardrobe, glasses and pillow handling into fewer beats. Cut either the "restaurant table" simile or the "library dying contentedly" aside. Cut about 150 words each from Scenes 2 and 3. In Scene 2, shorten the taste paragraph. In Scene 3, merge the car-park paragraph with the feed set-up.

2. **Scene 1 delays the Turn.** The licence arrives only after the print, the walk-through, two unit placements, the record choice and the break. The quoted line "Then I went through his house." opens a long catalogue before any pressure beyond atmosphere.
   - Rule broken: strategy/03 ("Scene 1 grabs") and style/10 point 2.
   - Trouble is present on page one: she is inside his back door with the pick in her hand. But the grab slackens between the print and the break.
   - Fix: after the cuts in Finding 1, keep the break and the licence closer together. The camera placements can each be one sentence.

3. **Scene 2 ends its Turn past its peak (minor).** The key lands strongly, but "Full marks, I thought." and the quiet fallboard sentence soften the cut-off slightly.
   - Rule broken: style/10 point 3 (end on the Turn).
   - Fix: keep the fallboard closing as the last line. Move "Full marks" earlier, or drop it, since the "He kept it true for nobody" line already carries the grade.

4. **Competence and luck check (no violation).** Every door is hers: she picks it and relocks it. "No alarm and no camera" is justified in-line ("A man who knows what logs are for keeps none of his own"). Brandt's return at 12:26 is not a threat she has to escape. This is flagged only because the ease of the whole visit leaves the Brandt pressure resting on atmosphere rather than risk.
   - Optional fix: the one line about egress timing ("Egress begins when placement ends") could carry one clock reference. A single "he'd be back at midnight" would keep the danger live in Scene 2.

## What works

- Both the first sentence ("His house was two degrees warmer than mine") and the last (the tone arm and "neither of us got up to turn it over") carry weight. Do not touch the Snapper.
- "I did not time it." as a one-line paragraph, then the afterwards-noticing paragraph. It is the best tell in the chapter.
- Brandt is felt in every scene through his things, his food and his body on the feed. Scene 2's "I took off my right glove." stays unflagged.
