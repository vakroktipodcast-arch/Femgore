**Verdict:** REVISE

**Findings:**

1. "I watched the walls go up around Cam Brandt in real time" (sc. 1, final para). "Walls go up" and "in real time" are stock thriller phrasing; the sentence could appear in any novel. Breaks the reviewer brief's generic-prose test and 02 point 2 (no Latinate/plain collision). Fix: keep it clinical, e.g. "I watched the rejection run its course in Cam Brandt, a procedure I had only ever read in a dossier…", which ties back to the kidney image.

2. "Counsel's reply was a masterpiece of the form." (sc. 2). This is a reviewer's cliché, not Iris. The grade that follows ("I gave him an A") does the job better. Fix: cut the sentence and open on the quoted phrases, or give it her register: "Counsel's reply had been embalmed before it was sent."

3. "She looked at me a beat longer than a junior should." (sc. 3). "A beat longer" is a generic default. Fix: measure it the way she measures things: "She looked at me for about as long as it takes to read a label twice."

4. "I'm telling you because… / I'm telling you about the egg so you'll… / I'm telling you what she did. I'm not telling you…" (sc. 3). That is four "I'm telling you"s inside eight lines. The tic wears out, and "so you'll notice the rest of the column" explains the device to the reader instead of implicating them (01 point 1: direct address never explains). Fix: keep "nothing is also a menu" and the Mara pair, and cut the second egg sentence ("On Thursday I ate an egg… rest of the column") completely. The column already shows the reader the gap.

5. "one egg, boiled, eaten out of the cup with a teaspoon" (sc. 3). The egg and the half-slice of toast get no taste, but everything she eats must be described (06 point 4). The tap water gets one; the egg doesn't. Fix: give it one flat, exact taste, e.g. "the yolk gone chalky, sulphur at the back of the tongue", in a short plain sentence so the dip in appetite shows through the body.

6. "so that the footers would be there for whoever opened it, pointing at the people who keep the index" (sc. 2, LC-0311 para). The sentence re-explains an earlier act, and the tap/plumbing paragraphs before it ("headers, and the bodies of mail up to a couple of kilobytes…"; "So I read the letter's words in the body of an email. I never saw its letterhead.") lean the same way. This is drift toward explanation (02 point 1; brief). Fix: cut "pointing at the people who keep the index" and "So I read the letter's words… letterhead." The toe-tag and "already opened" lines carry the point without them.

7. "I didn't enjoy it as much as I'd expected to. I noted that." (sc. 2). The appetite is dipping, which is right for the arc, but here it is named outright, and "I noted that" is the Ch. 25 numb-pole tic ("I noticed that") arriving eleven chapters early (11, last point, in spirit: show the absence through the body). Fix: cut both sentences and let "reading a colleague's results over his shoulder" stand. Or swap them for a body finding, e.g. her mouth dry and the coffee untouched.

8. "went white to the hairline… a clean pallor going up under his hair" (sc. 1). This says the same thing twice, and the second version is weaker. Fix: keep one, preferably "a clean pallor going up under his hair as if a blind had been drawn", and drop "went white to the hairline".

9. "My thumb had stopped on the glass over the signature." (sc. 2). This is nearly word for word the interlude anchor's "His thumb stopped on the glass." In Iris's narration it reads as Cam's cooler register leaking into hers. If the rhyme is meant, keep it but make it hers ("My thumb was on the signature, the way you keep a finger on a pulse"). If it isn't meant, change it.

10. Joke rate (02 point 3; Act II floor of one a page): scenes 1 and 2 clear it easily. Scene 3's "My reasons" paragraph and the leave-booking paragraph run about a page on logistics with only "Third, which was the second again" to carry them. Fix: one more dry line in the leave paragraph, e.g. on the blank *Reason for absence* box.

**What works:**
- The clinical metaphors for being frozen out: the ward giving a bed a wide berth, the rejected kidney, "how a hospital handles a death it would rather not certify", the password typed "the way the bereaved ring a number". This is the voice at full strength. Don't touch them.
- The humming beat and the snapper ("A phone has no sides… I stopped it there myself"): short declaratives, no explanation, the Turn carried by the body.
- The "body's version" finding list ("Patient reports her mood as good. Noted. Unexplained. Not discarded."). The grading voice works as self-deception here, which is right for Act II.
