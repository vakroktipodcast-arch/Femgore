# Ch 14 — Forensic and procedural accuracy

- **Verdict:** REVISE. The surface procedure is sound. Three plausibility gaps would make a professional reader stop, and each can be fixed with a clause.

## Findings

1. **"I had not pictured anyone following them."** (S2). Medium-high. This breaks `bible/cast.md` (Iris: "knowledge of what investigators look for"). A pathologist who has fed fragments to evidence for fifteen years leaves a matter number in a victim's mouth so that it points at "the people who keep the index". She cannot then never have pictured a records request. A professional would laugh at it, and it makes the Turn look like her oversight when it should be the world moving. Fix: she did picture it, and pictured it stopping at counsel, "where paper goes to wait". What she did not picture is someone with a name pressing past that wall. The Turn ("something I did not write") survives and grows sharper.

2. **"He had not locked him out of the plumbing"** (S2). Medium. Reads as luck, measured against `ledger/continuity.md` (Lantern holds Brandt's standing replica of campaign mail; the lockout of Thu 8 Oct). An off-book mirror usually syncs on its owner's credentials. Revoking Brandt's login at 7:51 would kill the feed, and any campaign-ops reader will ask why it didn't. Fix: one clause giving the mechanism. The mirror could run on a journaling rule or service account set up under the compliance director's authority (Diane, already the officer of record), not on Brandt's login. "Holt couldn't close a door he had never been told existed" then becomes literally true.

3. **"I booked Wednesday the fourteenth off through the leave system."** (S3). Medium. Breaks `bible/cast.md` (she knows what investigators look for and removes it). A timestamped leave request for the morning after a planned homicide, filed five days ahead, is exactly the line an investigator pulls. Iris files it as "administration" and never weighs it. Fix: half a sentence of her weighing it. A planned day off reads as ordinary in a personnel file, and a sick call on the morning itself does not. Her "reporting it as administration" can then stay as the deflection it is.

4. **"under my scrub top to the dressing … the nylon tails stiff"** (S2). Low-medium. Breaks `ledger/continuity.md` (Ch 12: dressing taped with micropore; seven 4-0 nylon, tails long). If the dressing is on, her fingertips meet tape, not tails. A pathologist straight from the suite would also not put a bare hand under her scrubs onto a day-six suture line. Fix: "pressed the dressing, the tails a ridge under the tape". Optionally, have her note she washed her hands first, or not.

5. **"Mara and I scrubbed side by side … went back to her nails."** (S3). Low. Breaks morgue routine (`ledger` places the sinks; it does not say a surgical scrub). Prosectors gown and glove before a post. Nail-brushing is a theatre habit, and the thorough wash comes after the case. Fix: "washed up side by side" and drop "her nails", or move the sandwich offer to the post-case wash. The ledger fixes only "at the sinks", so either works.

6. **"His clothes came in bagged, and when I opened the bag"** (S3). Low. Breaks intake practice. A natural death from an armchair arrives dressed in the body bag, and clothing comes off at the external exam. Fix: "When we unzipped the bag his room came up out of it." The smell, the radio and the two days all stay.

7. **"bodies of mail up to a couple of kilobytes"** (S2). Low, reads as luck. Breaks `ledger/continuity.md` (Lantern tap: ~2 KB, no attachments). A law-firm email carries a confidentiality footer, an internal note, a reply and an FBI letter "quoted in full". That is tight for 2 KB, and the letter arriving complete looks convenient. Fix: one clause. The digest cut off at the foot, mid-way through the firm's disclaimer, with the letter just inside the limit. Or she reads it "to within a line of the cut".

8. **"It's what you write on the toe tag before the family arrives."** (S2). Low. Breaks practice: an unidentified tag carries a case number and "unknown", not an initial. Fix: "It's what goes on the toe tag before anyone knows the name: a number." Her point survives.

9. **"anything quieter than a dropped instrument goes into it"** (S3). Low. Physically inverted. She hears her own hum by bone conduction under any fan noise, but Mara, a metre off, would struggle to hear it at all. "I felt it before I heard it" is right. The fans should be swallowing it for Mara, not for Iris. Fix: put Mara close, leaning in at the scale, so she catches it at that range, or drop "That's why I didn't hear it at first" in favour of not *noticing* it.

## What works
- The 480 g heart: firm, a thick left ventricle palpable before sectioning, cold from the cooler, years of blood pressure read in one figure. It is correct and economical.
- The lockout as procedure: the reader refusing twice, an IT man badging him in, three logins at falling speed, and Holt's chair moves. It is credible campaign and IT practice, and it matches Interlude 3.
- The letter reaching her inside counsel's covering email with no letterhead, and counsel's stall language. The paper process reads as real.
