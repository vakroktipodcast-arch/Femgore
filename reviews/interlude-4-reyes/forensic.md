**Verdict:** REVISE. The procedure holds and the Turn is earned. A handful of details would make an agent or examiner wince, and each is a one-clause fix.

**Findings:**

1. "The same pressure both times, each circle closed" (para 2). You can't read pen pressure off a photocopy. Pressure is a property of the original: indentation, ink pooling, the reverse of the sheet. She has said a line earlier that she has only copies. This breaks the draft's own setup and the ledger (Reyes, "Works from photocopies of the two pinned pages"). Fix: give her what a copy does show, e.g. "The same stroke both times", or attribute the pressure to the questioned-documents note on the originals ("the lab's note said the same pressure").

2. "every agent in the building was on a rope line" (para 12). FBI agents don't work rope lines. That is Secret Service work, and the ledger keeps the Service distinct (Ch 21 pat-down, Ch 22 seal, Ch 26 "no Service pin"). A field-office reader would stop here. Fix: "every agent in the building had been pulled onto election week" or "was on the election command post".

3. "Someone had put the right paper in the right mouth" (para 10). As written, the match is circular. She asked for LC-0311 in the Wed 7 Oct letter because she had already read it off the footers (ledger, Objects: FBI letter). A list produced under that matter will carry that number in its header. Nothing has been learned, so the "pleasure" is unearned. Fix: make what's new the names, not the number. For example: "The number she had been carrying since September now had eleven names under it." That keeps the ledger fact (three reconstructed fragments match the header) and gives her a real gain.

4. "every scrap of footer carried the same matter number" (para 3). This contradicts para 10, where only three wet-damaged footers had to be pieced back together by the lab before they could be read. The ledger's Ch 6 note says one fragment shows only "LC-03". Fix: "every footer the lab could piece together carried…".

5. "She wrote *reviewer* in the margin… and stopped her pen" (para 14). Her restraint is right, but a professional would notice an omission. An agent who finds that a signatory reviewed the victim's autopsy has a problem with the integrity of her own evidence, separate from any suspicion. Her first thought is whether to take it to the judge, not only to stay patient. Without that thought she reads as passive. Fix: add one procedural clause, e.g. "Taking one surname to the judge, on a weekend, would tell counsel which name she wanted." This keeps her at ten for a procedural reason, and it stays inside the beat sheet's limit: no further thought about her, no "pathologist" set beside "practised".

6. "The eleventh gave a disconnected number" (para 12). She can't know a number is disconnected on a Saturday evening without dialling it, and the beat sheet keeps her calling no one on the page. Fix: "a phone number and an address, both with a last-contact date years old". The list already carries a last-known-contact column, so the date does the work.

7. Minor, optional: "The courier envelope was sealed with red tamper tape." A return under a court order would normally go to the prosecutor, not by courier to the agent's desk. One clause fixes it ("the AUSA's office had sent it straight across") if the reviser wants it. Leave it out if the word count is tight.

**What works:**
- The protective order holding Vale at tenth, and the woman abroad "logged anyway, because the paper was what would be read later". This is how a case agent actually protects a file.
- The profile is built from sources: the autopsy files, the matter number and counsel's own brief. Each term has a document behind it, and nothing comes from intuition.
- "closed again, roughly" agrees with Ch 17's sutureless close. Brandt's suite and the edit desk agree with the ledger. Don't add autopsy detail here.
