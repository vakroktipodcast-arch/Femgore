# Stitch review — whole manuscript

Read in the order of `outline/00-reading-order.md`: 27 chapters and 5 interludes, 96,048 words by the lint count. Nothing in `manuscript/` was edited. `scripts/lint.py` passes on all 32 files.

How the numbers were made:

- **Dates** are worked from the text against a Tuesday 3 November election.
- **Phrase counts** are mechanical (regex over the manuscript files).
- **Motif counts** are beats counted on the read, except Hands and the Grade, which are word hits.
- **Joke counts** are a hand tally from a single read. Treat each chapter figure as accurate to about ±2.
- A "page" is 300 words.

Chapters 1, 17, 25 and Interlude 2 are approved anchors. Where a seam fails against an anchor, the fix belongs in the neighbouring unit.

## 1. Headline findings

1. **Interlude 3 spends Chapters 14 and 15 before they happen.** In 899 words it tells the reader that Holt freezes Brandt out, the card fails twice, the farm goes dark, the reporter's message lands, the car is frozen, he skips the Tabard and he is already in the suite with a bottle and a record. Chapter 14 scene 1 then retells the same Monday-to-Thursday, some of it in the same words. Chapter 15 scene 2 asks whether he will choose the Tabard or the suite, and its Turn ("He went willingly") arrives as confirmation.
2. **The seam from 24 into the Chapter 25 anchor does not fit in five places.** She "stood up" when she never sat; the room is cold in 25 and warm in 22–24; the list is certain in 20–24 and hedged in 25; Olivia's name is placed differently; the zip-tie vanishes for a chapter. Details in section 2.
3. **"The syllabus assumed he would recognise me" is stated eight times across Chapters 22–24.** It is the one idea in Act III the book does not trust the reader to hold. Three of the ten weakest pages are restatements of it.
4. **The eleventh name reads as Nora Wade, and it is not.** Interludes 4 and 5 describe "the eleventh name, the one after Vale" as a disconnected number and an old address. Every reader will take that for the dead woman (W after V). The ledger says Nora was struck from the list and the eleventh is alive and came forward on 6 November. Nothing on the page says so, and Chapter 27's "nine of the eleven, two left" then looks like an arithmetic error.
5. **The reader never sees Brandt found.** Diane's discovery gets a whole chapter and an interlude. Brandt's body (found Thursday 15 October per the ledger) is never discovered on the page, and the campaign never reacts. The first acknowledgement is "for a dead man" in Chapter 20.
6. **The simile shape "the way you…" appears 313 times, about once a page.** It is the voice, and it is also the book's most audible tic. Chapters 1, 16 and 17 run at 18–23 each.
7. **The appetite arc is on spec in every chapter.** No banned state-words, the meal after lands at 7, 12, 17 (planned) and 27 exactly as the bible lists, and Chapter 18's sentence is the one the strategy asks for. The one thing to weigh: appetite already fails three times in Act II (10, 14, 15), which slightly softens the drop into Act III.
8. **Joke rate: Act I 0.98 per page, Act II 0.92, Chapter 18 0.44, Act III 0.34.** The shape is right. Both early acts sit just under the one-per-page floor, pulled down by Chapters 5, 9, 10 and 14. Act III does not feel thinner until Chapter 21, because 19 and 20 still run at about 0.65.
9. **Motifs are all present and placed as the bible specifies**, with two things to rule on: three mirror beats the bible does not list (7, and two in 18), and "I have never owned a record" used in Chapter 8 before the Chapter 17 anchor line.
10. **Dates and elapsed times hold throughout.** Every "days since" I could check against the calendar is right.

- **Acts I and II are at the floor, not above it.** Given the margin on the tally, both acts pass, but four chapters are clearly under: 5 (the wait and the capture), 9, 10 and 14. Chapter 5 is tension and can carry it. Chapters 9 and 10 are the two that open Act II, where the bible wants the reader seduced.
- **The loss is not felt until Chapter 21.** Chapters 19 and 20 run at about the same rate as 9 and 10. The reader is meant to "feel the loss before they can name it"; as drafted the drop is from 0.7 to 0.4 to nothing across 20, 21 and 22, which is abrupt.
- **The book names the loss twice, and both are good.** The skeleton's ribs in Chapter 20 ("it didn't come out funny. It came out as a number") and the network joke in Chapter 22 ("I didn't make the joke. I am only showing you where it was").
- **Chapter 26 rebounds to six.** Hotel linen, fire code and the barman arrive within minutes of the kill. The bible only requires the syllabus voice to be gone from 22 to 25, so this is permitted. Whether she should be that fluent on the stairs is the author's call.

## 7. The ten weakest pages

Ranked worst first. Each is about a page, located by its opening words. The test applied is the book's own: does this make the reader more or less able to look away from Iris?

1. **Interlude 3, middle.** From "Monday, in front of the room, he told Theo Akers" to "Each door shut with the care of the groove, and pointed out of the house." Nine days in 250 words. It is a synopsis, and it is the page that spends Chapters 14 and 15 (headline 1). The interlude's first and last thirds are scenes and are good.
2. **Chapter 14, scene 1.** From "I had been taking the history for three days" to "Brandt came back nearly two minutes after him and did not sit." The reader has just read all of it in Interlude 3, in some cases in the same words.
3. **Chapter 23, scene 3.** From "The syllabus had not anticipated this" to "I knew what was in every pocket I had on." The sixth statement of the recognition point, followed by the third inventory of her pockets. "I had not prepared for manners" is the only line the page needs.
4. **Chapter 22, scene 3, opening.** From "I stood across the table from him and waited. Every rehearsal I ever ran started here" to "I had never once written a line for the half-second going on." Restates the end of scene 2, which had already landed on "He saw me. He did not recognise me."
5. **Chapter 24, end of scene 2.** From "The syllabus had assumed he would recognise the lesson" to "The syllabus shattered from the inside, and I was crying too." The Turn is the outline's sentence set down nearly whole. It is the one place in Act III where the narration names what happened instead of showing it, and it sits directly after "It had chosen which ones", which is the best line in the act and has already done the work.
6. **Chapter 5, scene 1, second and third paragraphs.** From "The dark had stopped being dark hours before" to "a loop of cord on the inside was how I would shut it." Re-explains rhodopsin, the bracket, the recorder and the hinges from the last page of Chapter 4. The reader is waiting for Diane and is handed the manual again.
7. **Chapter 15, scene 2, second paragraph.** "With the feeds gone I herded him blind…" One 250-word paragraph of consultancy, lease, alarm, cameras, facilities mail, keypad, fit-out drawing and acoustic specification. Iris is present only in the last two clauses. Interlude 3 has already told the reader where he ends up.
8. **Chapter 21, scene 1.** The badge paragraph ("The badge on my lanyard said RUTH CALDER…") and the scene's closing paragraph ("I passed because the checker was a campaign volunteer…"). Both repeat Chapter 20 scene 1 almost phrase for phrase: July, one phone bank, eleven calls, marking people inactive had been Compliance.
9. **Chapter 20, scene 1.** From "The Calloway files floor plans" to "I wrote that down and didn't use it." Two paragraphs of stairs, doors, paces and seconds. The scene's real find, the seal that is only a sticker, is strong and comes after it.
10. **Chapter 8, scene 3, opening.** From "There are two units in that room" to "They knew only that it was bad." The third explanation of the camera placement, then a room the Interlude 2 anchor is about to show again from the inside. The scene comes alive when Brandt walks in.

Runners-up: the dock-level geography paragraph in Chapter 26 scene 1 ("Back of house, street level…"); the tap-and-plumbing explanation in Chapter 14 scene 2; the off-stage week summarised through captions in Chapter 27 scene 1.

## 8. Questions for the author

These need a ruling before anyone revises. None is fixed here.

1. Interlude 3: keep it running to the Tuesday and accept the pre-telling, or stop it earlier (for instance at the first drink before noon) and let 14–16 carry the freeze-out and the choice of room?
2. The 24-to-25 seam: Chapter 25 is approved. Should 24 be brought into line (seat her, cool the room, tie handling), or do you want to touch the anchor on the hedged list and the window?
3. The eleventh name: should a line on the page tell the reader she is alive and is one of the three who came forward, so that Nora stays the twelfth?
4. Brandt's discovery: is it meant to stay offstage?
5. "I have never owned a record" in Chapter 8, and the fogged mirror in Chapter 7: keep as early notes, or reserve for 17 and 15?
6. Chapter 26's jokes: intended return of the voice, or too soon?
