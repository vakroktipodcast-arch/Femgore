- **Verdict:** REVISE. The asphyxia sequence, timings and scene-composition logic hold. Five small fixes are needed where a pathologist reader would stop.

- **Findings:**

1. **The bite that never comes.** "With two gloved fingers of my right… packed the paper in past the incisors". Two fingers are pushed to the oropharynx of a conscious, blindfolded woman, and the push brings on a hard gag. Nothing holds the jaw open, because Iris's left hand is on the larynx. Clenching or biting during that gag is close to reflex. Without it, a professional reads luck, and Iris (ledger: careful about her own DNA, double gloves) would have planned for a bitten glove. Smallest fix: one clause showing she knew the bite was coming and was quicker than it, e.g. "out before the bite came; her teeth met on paper and the outer glove." The ledger already puts double nitrile on her for this kill, so the outer glove can take it.

2. **Fresh urine is not ammonia.** "Warm ammonia rose into the cold air". The callback "after the ammonia it was almost festive" repeats it. Freshly voided urine smells of urine. Ammonia comes from bacteria breaking down urea in stale urine, which is something Iris meets in old scenes and would never confuse with a fresh void. This is the kind of slip a pathologist reader catches at once. Fix: "warm urine" or "the warm salt-and-urea smell". In S3, "after the urine". The beat sheet's sensory lead already says "urine".

3. **One hand short at the tape.** "I shut her jaw over the bolus with the heel of my left hand". Next, the palm goes on the occiput to tip the head forward. The left hand cannot hold the jaw and the occiput at once, and the right holds the roll. As written, the jaw is free before anything holds it shut, and the bolus can come out. Fix: one clause putting the first pass across the mouth before she lets go, e.g. "drew the first pass across her mouth under my hand, and only then let the jaw go".

4. **The stated reason for the larynx grip is wrong.** "so that she could not swallow or retch the paper away". Holding the thyroid cartilage can limit laryngeal elevation and so make swallowing harder. It does nothing against a retch, which is driven by the diaphragm and abdomen. The ledger's own reason is steadying the head (ledger, Objects, "Diane's industrial tape", the thyroid-border bruises). Fix: cut "or retch", or recast as "to steady the head and pin the larynx so a swallow couldn't carry it down".

5. **The laryngoscope comparison is the wrong action.** "with the pressure I would give a laryngoscope blade". A laryngoscope lifts the tongue forward and up. It does not push into the oropharynx. An intubator would notice, and a forensic pathologist has seldom held one. Fix: take the figure from her own bench, e.g. "the pressure I'd use to seat packing in a cavity at the end of a case".

6. **Minor: the investigator would not use tweezers at the scene.** "with a torch, with tweezers, picking a wet scrap off a dead woman's lip". Iris, of all people, knows that material at the mouth stays on the body for the ME and is recovered at autopsy (as Ch 8 does: "each NDA fragment bagged separately"). Fix: let the investigator read the number by torchlight without touching it, "because you don't touch it". That keeps the "understanding, slowly" beat and gains a professional reflex.

7. **Minor: the pin line pins the wrong property.** "Through skin that has stopped bleeding a pin goes in like a pin through a hem." Fresh dead skin resists a pin exactly as living skin does. Bleeding governs what comes out, not how the pin goes in. Fix: let the ease belong to the thin skin over bone and the absence of a flinch ("nothing flinched, and nothing came"), and keep "Nothing came" for the bleeding.

- **What works:**
  - S2's asphyxia order and clock: still under the tape for 20–30 s, CO2-driven inspiratory effort (nares dimpling, suprasternal and intercostal recession), struggle, loss of consciousness, convulsion, void, apnoeic pause, agonal gasps, then the carotid fading through irregularity to nothing in seven minutes. Also the honest hedge that smothering "doesn't promise" petechiae. All of it is textbook and reads as seen, not looked up.
  - S3's evidence logic: ties and tape bagged for their lot numbers, furrows left as "evidence of nothing I owned", the marker cap held in the mouth and not the glove, and a post-mortem pin with no vital reaction. Each one sets up a Ch 8 finding cleanly.
  - The cooling paragraph: core plateau, periphery first, "from the far end". Accurate and in character. Do not trim it.
