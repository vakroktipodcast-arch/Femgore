- **Verdict:** REVISE

- **Findings:**

1. **The marker cap leaves her DNA on the scene.** "I held the cap in my teeth, which is where it would leave" (S3). This is the line a professional would laugh at. Saliva on the cap goes onto her gloves, and the same gloves have just handled the page, the pin and the shirt. Iris is defined by "knowledge of what investigators look for and therefore what to remove" (bible/cast.md, Iris, skills). The double gloves are in the ledger for a reason (ledger, Kit). Fix: she pushes the cap onto the back of the marker, or holds it in her other gloved fist. Keep the clause about it leaving with her.

2. **Her uncovered face is never mentioned.** "my face hot, actually hot" / "My mouth was dry under the hood". The kit lists a coverall, hair cover and hood but no mask (ledger, Kit). She talks at close range for most of S1 and S2, her face is hot and sweating, and she leans over the body for nine minutes. Every forensic reader will ask where her breath and sweat went. Fix: one clause in S1 putting her back out of arm's reach while she talks, and one for S2–S3 (she keeps her face turned away and above the body). Or add a mask as a new fact in beats/ch06.md, not silently in the prose (CLAUDE.md rule 3).

3. **Diane talks and fights as if the agent was never given.** "She said it quietly, and fluently" / "drumming, the bound ankles lifting". The ledger gives her a partial IM dose at capture, about 8:28, and fixes its effect as heavy, weak, slow limbs and slow speech (ledger, Objects, agent syringe). Two hours later it may well have worn off, but the page never says so. As written, the dose reads as forgotten. Fix: one clause in S1, e.g. her speech had come back up to its own speed and her hands with it, which is why the ankles were tied.

4. **The fingertip hold is anatomically false.** "where the head can be held still without any argument from the neck" (S2). The larynx moves freely and gives no grip on the head. Pressure at the thyroid borders makes a conscious person cough and pull away. The hold itself is ledger-fixed and causes Ch 8's bruises (ledger, Diane's industrial tape), so keep the action and change the reason. Her occiput is already against the pump housing, so the housing holds the head. The fingertips fix the larynx so she cannot swallow or retch the paper away from where Iris is seating it.

5. **One gag, then "spent", reads as luck.** "the gag reflex fired once, early, and was spent". In a conscious woman packed to the oropharynx, the gag fires again and again and can bring up vomit. She ate porridge three hours earlier, and Ch 8 finds it in the stomach (ledger, Diane, gastric contents). The paper being aspirated with vomit would also change Mara's findings. Fix: keep "once" as Iris's intended result and give her the method. She seats the bolus in one firm push and holds it, rather than feeding it in piece by piece, so she gets one hard gag instead of a series. She also names the vomiting risk in a clause: she tips the head forward and waits it out.

6. **Nothing stops Diane spitting the paper out before the tape.** "I tipped her head forward with my palm on the occiput and went round". Between packing and the first turn of tape her mouth is open, nothing is holding it, and her tongue is free. Industrial tape also needs its free end anchored before a one-handed wrap. Fix: the heel of her hand or a thumb closes the jaw over the bolus, the tape's end goes down on the cheek first, then she wraps.

7. **The phases are out of order.** "Then the convulsion ... and then the loss of consciousness". In the textbook asphyxial sequence, consciousness goes at or just before the convulsive phase. Then come a pre-terminal pause and the gasps. A pathologist "checking against the book" would not put them in this order. Fix: "the loss of consciousness, which you can see in the hands ... then the convulsion". Or "the convulsion, and somewhere early in it the loss of consciousness". Change the order only; the beat sheet's other content stands.

8. **Petechiae are certain here, but not in smothering.** "I knew what was blooming in the conjunctivae". Petechiae are classic in neck compression and come and go in smothering. Ch 8 does find them (beats/ch08.md), so the outcome stands, but Iris should not claim to know. Fix: make it a bet, "I'd have put money on petechiae", which also matches her liver bet in S3.

- **What works:**
  - The breathing sequence: tape dimpling over the nares, the suprasternal notch and intercostal spaces drawing in, and "afraid of its own exhaust". It is correct and sounds like someone who has seen it.
  - The cooling-curve paragraph, with the plateau, early loss of heat from the fingertips and nose, and reading cooling curves "from the far end". It is accurate and it carries the character.
  - The timings: 10:40 tape, 10:47 arrest, nine minutes on her heels, the furrows left and the ties bagged for lot numbers. They are plausible and match the ledger.
