# Chapter 18 — The All Is Lost Moment

- **Act:** Act II — The Practicum (Chapters 10–18)
- **Act pressure:** Pressure: Brandt, until Chapter 17; then her own knowledge. Appetite: named, indulged, and finally seen.
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense
- **Target:** ~3,150 words, three scenes
- **Manuscript file:** `manuscript/ch18.md`

**Scene 1 — The Collapse.** The car. Then home. No forty-minute shower. The bathroom floor for six hours. The cold since Chapter 1 cracks and I break — not theatrically, completely. The only scene in the book where I cry. And under the crying, the thing I can’t stop seeing: how happy I was, exactly, while he said it.

> **Turn:** When I can stand, the first thing I reach for is my phone. Not to call anyone.

**Scene 2 — The Truth Confirmed.** The archive folder. The message, three years old, a woman’s hesitant voice: ‘I think we may have known the same person. I just wanted to talk.’ Dead eight months later. Beside it, two weeks old, Tessa Quinn asking for the pathologist in the news. I did it twice. The designed failure included me, and I’ve been running it, and I was eating well the whole time.

> **Turn:** I have been the Gatekeeper. My page would read: Confused process for protection. I write it on a page. I don’t pin it to anything.

**Scene 3 — The Choice.** The syllabus is not justice. It is a way to never face that I could have helped before — and a way to have the thing I actually wanted, which was this, all of it, the suite and the record and his skin. A clean exit exists: stop, turn myself in, let the story surface, let the other ten be believed. I make tea. I don’t eat. I decide to continue. Not because the philosophy holds. Because I want to. I tell you that, and it is the most honest sentence in the book.

> **Turn:** Mara texts. I don’t reply.

> **Snapper:** The two voicemails in the folder, side by side, both marked as read. The page with my own name on it face-down on the desk. End of Act II.

> *Editorial note:* The femgore All Is Lost: she sees the appetite, keeps it, and says so. Nothing is renounced. That is what makes Act III a horror novel rather than a redemption arc.
