# Interlude 3 — Cam

- **Placement:** after Chapter 13
- **POV:** Cam, close third, past tense
- **Target:** 600–900 words, one Turn
- **Manuscript file:** `manuscript/interlude-3-cam.md`

Brandt comes home, sees the print on the counter and the broken record on his pillow, and sits on the bed holding the halves for a long time. A man who has arranged consequences for others for twenty years, arranging them for himself. He finds the page, reads it, keeps it, and laughs. He plays side one of the Mingus and sleeps in his clothes. In the morning he accuses a junior staffer of leaking, goes home, and drinks before noon for the first time in nine years. The interlude ends there, at his kitchen counter. Everything after that Monday morning (telling Holt there is ‘a problem from the old files’, Holt’s ‘I thought Diane closed those’, the quiet removal of his access, the soundproofed room) is carried by Chapters 14–17 and is not shown here.

***Turn:*** *He understands she could have killed him in his sleep and chose to warn him instead. She wants him to understand first. He finds, to his surprise, that he wants to.*
