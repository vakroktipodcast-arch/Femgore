# Chapter 24 — The Stripping of Armor

- **Act:** Act III — The Final Exam (Chapters 19–27)
- **Act pressure:** Pressure: Reyes, closing on paper; the knowledge from 18 in every room. Appetite: starved.
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense
- **Target:** ~3,150 words, three scenes
- **Manuscript file:** `manuscript/ch24.md`

**Scene 1 — Provocation.** I begin the prescribed method — Lesson Three as written: immobilise, keep conscious, pin the page, leave him alive for eleven women to finish. The agent from my toxicology access. He flinches. He is, finally, frightened. The moment is, briefly, satisfying — the first thing I’ve tasted in five chapters.

> **Turn:** He asks what I want. Not what I’m going to do — what I want. It is the first question anyone in the campaign has ever asked me, and I don’t have an answer any more.

**Scene 2 — The Crack.** He understands this is about the campaign and reaches for the only loss he can feel. ‘Is this about Diane?’ His voice breaks. ‘She was family. I haven’t slept since. Her daughter — Olivia — I was at the —’ He is weeping. Real grief, for the woman who filed me into a cabinet. None for me, because none is available. The syllabus assumed he would recognise the lesson. He is grieving the Gatekeeper.

> **Turn:** I understand he will never be taught. There is no page that reaches him. The syllabus shatters from the inside — and I am crying too, and he thinks we are grieving the same woman.

**Scene 3 — The Rupture.** I abandon the script. The third page is in my hand and I don’t pin it. Whatever happens next is not curriculum. You know what it is before I do.

> **Turn:** I put the page on the table, beside the Marine. He reaches for my hand, to comfort me.

> **Snapper:** His hand on mine. The agent I never finished giving him. The first time in the book I am touched by someone who doesn’t know who I am, and it is him.

> *Editorial note:* The hinge as decided: Holt grieves Diane. The man who cannot see Iris sees Diane perfectly, which means the system did not fail to see women; it chose which ones. His grief must be entirely sincere.
