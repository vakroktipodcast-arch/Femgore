# Chapter 9 — Plot Point One — The Hunter Hunted

- **Act:** Act I — The Prerequisite (Chapters 1–9)
- **Act pressure:** Pressure: the campaign’s certainty that nothing is coming; from Chapter 8, Brandt. Appetite: present and unnamed.
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense
- **Target:** ~3,150 words, three scenes
- **Manuscript file:** `manuscript/ch09.md`

**Scene 1 — The Entry.** I come home to find my apartment has been entered. Nothing taken; the dossier in the drawer a quarter-inch left of where I left it. I know. I check the kitchen first, then the drawer, and tell the reader which of those is the tell.

> **Turn:** He has read the syllabus. He knows there are two pages left and whose names are on them. Mine is a name to him now.

**Scene 2 — The Three Walls.** Through my access, I watch Brandt not make three calls. Not the police — he’d have to explain how he found me. Not Holt — I’ve watched Holt cut men loose for less. Not a contractor with a clean brief — he doesn’t know what I have or who I’ve told. I narrate his reasoning for him, correctly, and enjoy being understood.

> **Turn:** He is going to come for me himself, with no one’s permission. We are the same kind of alone.

**Scene 3 — The Inversion.** I update the syllabus. The Architect is hunting the student. I write in the margin a single word.

> **Turn:** Excellent.

> **Snapper:** I leave the dossier a quarter-inch to the left and sleep nine hours. End of Act I.

> *Editorial note:* Brandt’s constraint is dramatised through Iris watching him fail to act, which keeps it in her voice and gives her the first flicker of kinship.
