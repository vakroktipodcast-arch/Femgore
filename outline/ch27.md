# Chapter 27 — Resolution & Aftermath

- **Act:** Act III — The Final Exam (Chapters 19–27)
- **Act pressure:** Pressure: Reyes, closing on paper; the knowledge from 18 in every room. Appetite: starved.
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense
- **Target:** ~3,150 words, three scenes
- **Manuscript file:** `manuscript/ch27.md`

**Scene 1 — The Morgue.** A week later. I’m at work. The public story: a lone extremist. The party has named a replacement candidate and the polls have barely moved. The machine is intact; only the man is gone. Some of the eleven have come forward — not all. Mara watches me the way you watch a colleague you’ve begun to be afraid of, and I let her, and I grade her for it: full marks.

> **Turn:** Reyes has interviewed nine of eleven. The two left are a woman who has left the country, and Dr. Vale, postponed by Election Eve and rescheduled for Thursday. I find this out from Mara, who was asked to confirm my shift pattern.

**Scene 2 — The Sister.** Hannah calls. I answer. I say I’ve been busy. I ask about the kids. The most painful conversation in the book because it is so ordinary, and because I am good at it, and because I tell you how good.

> **Turn:** Saying goodbye I realise I don’t know when I’ll be able to call back — and that Hannah heard it.

**Scene 3 — The Table.** The kitchen table. The folded page on the wood. A plate I made and haven’t touched. I try to throw the page away. I can’t. I slide it into the drawer beneath the writing desk and close the drawer. I come back to the table. I eat. All of it. Then I look at my hands for a long time.

> **Turn:** The drawer doesn’t lock. I don’t lock it. Thursday is in three days and I have just eaten for the first time in a week.

> **Snapper:** End on my hands. Do not tell the reader what to feel. The reader already knows what I am going to do on Thursday, and so do I, and neither of us says it.

> *Editorial note:* The appetite returns in the last scene. That is the genre’s refusal of redemption and the sequel’s first line.
