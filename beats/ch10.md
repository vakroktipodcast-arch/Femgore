# Beat sheet: ch10, Escalation of Trouble

- **Unit:** ch10 (`manuscript/ch10.md`). First chapter of Act II, The Practicum.
- **POV:** Iris Vale, first person, past tense. The outline's Turns are in present tense, so render them in past tense on the page.
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Three scenes of ~1,050 each. No syllabus insert at the chapter head.
- **Act pressure:** Act II. The pressure is Brandt, until Ch 17. In Ch 9 he read her drawer. Here he makes his first move, and it is paper, not hands: a lure and a decoy built in the same trade he used on her three years ago. He has to be felt in every scene: his craft in S1, his stillness on the feed in S2, and in S3 the margin of his page, where her name for him sits above Tessa's. Appetite pressure: Act II, "named, indulged, and finally seen". This chapter does the naming, and the reader should feel that the naming has a cost Iris refuses to look at.
- **Appetite at entry:** It is turning from competence into identity. In Ch 9 she was found, read correctly, and fed by it. She graded the man who broke into her flat *Excellent.* and slept nine hours. She has not called the feeling anything yet.
- **Appetite at exit:** Named and indulged. She says plainly in S1 that she enjoys being hunted by someone good, and she enjoys his craft even when it is the same craft that was used on her. She eats a whole lunch sitting down, after the delete, and it tastes as good as it did the night before. That is the horror, and she reports it straight. The first crack is opened and not looked at: a cost that falls on someone else. She names her refusal and keeps refusing. Ch 18 sends her the bill.
- **Body temperature:** Act II warmth begins here (Ch 9 kept Act I cold). It is late-summer heat that the city hasn't let go of. The warmth is in her body as well as the weather, and she likes it. No cold anywhere in the chapter, not even at the voicemail. The absence of cold at the voicemail is the point. Do not flag it.
- **Knowledge constraints (from the ledger, ch09 beats and later outlines):**
  - Iris believes she is the only woman the campaign silenced. She learns of the eleven names only in Ch 16. Brandt's profile must therefore **not** be about NDAs, silenced women or a pattern. It is about one unstable volunteer with a personal grievance against Diane. That is also Brandt's own logic: wall one, nothing that points at the paper.
  - Her access is still the War Room feed only, with picture and sound. She cannot read his phone screen. Anything she knows about the profile comes from public sources and her own clean machine.
  - She does not know about the shell company, the server farm or the off-book surveillance (Ch 11). She may notice that the account was run from machinery somewhere, and want it, but she must not find it.
  - The old voicemail from three years ago is in the office archive. She does not think of it, play it or recall it here. The ledger has her learning of its significance in Ch 17. The only seed allowed is the archive folder itself, unopened (S2 beat 9).
  - Calling-card details (the tape, the page, the circled role) have not been released to the public (see New facts). The internet knows only "killed at the reservoir pump station" and, after Brandt's leak, "knew anatomy".

## Scene 1: The Bait

1. Monday 14 September, 6:40 a.m., the ME's locker room. Iris is half into scrubs when she opens *The Cloakroom* on her phone, as she has every morning for three years, the way you check an old fracture on a cold day. It's the anonymous political gossip site where the blind items about her ran. There is a new item, posted at 6:00 a.m., and by its second sentence she knows whose hand it is. That is the chapter's trouble, on the first page.
2. The item: a "former campaign volunteer", let go by Diane Pell last spring for "erratic conduct", has been posting about Pell for weeks under an anonymous account. Then a line from "a source close to the investigation" that whoever killed Pell "knew anatomy". Iris recognises Mara's word *practised*, paraphrased and taken out of the file. He has the file. Below the item there is a photograph and a link.
3. She doesn't tap the link. It runs through a shortener, and she reads that as a door with someone standing behind it, counting. The item has two jobs and she names both. It gives the investigation and the internet somebody to chase. And it lures: anyone who knows the profile is false, and has a reason to look closely, will look closely. She puts the phone in her locker with the link unopened and carries it through the day like a sweet in a pocket, deferred on purpose. That deferral is the appetite, and she knows it is.
4. The working day, briefly, for texture and for the body. A man in his seventies has come off a ladder while clearing gutters, and has a subdural haematoma. At lunch she checks the War Room feed. Staff are delighted, because a crank volunteer is a gift seven weeks out. Comms is drafting "we do not comment on online speculation". Brandt stands at the glass and says nothing, and he is the only person in the room who is not surprised.
5. Evening, the flat, the windows open on a warm night. She reads the account on her clean laptop through a cached archive copy, and never touches the live link. Its first post is dated Thursday 3 September, the day after he read her drawer. It is eleven days old and aged like a starter: fed at regular hours, the misspellings placed like seasoning, the grievance getting louder in a believable curve. It reads as a whistleblower nobody would believe. That is the craft, and it is his old craft. It is what he did to her with blind items three years ago. He built a woman whose accusations sound like symptoms.
6. She grades it, and the grade is high. Then she names the appetite, the first time in the book she does so plainly. She is enjoying this. Being hunted by somebody good is the most interesting thing that has happened to her in three years. She reports it warm, in the body, and does not apologise.
7. The photograph. At first she takes it for a composite or a stock face, the kind a careful man would use. Then she looks at it as a pathologist looks. The pores, a slight asymmetry at the alae of the nose, a healed laceration through the lateral third of the left eyebrow with the hair growing back white along it. Nobody invents a scar like that. It is a real face.
8. A reverse image search on the clean machine resolves in seconds, which tells her he wanted it to. It finds a public social profile: a veterinary technician, a grey greyhound, a sister's wedding, a birthday cake with candles reading 31.
9. **[TURN]** The profile had a real face. It is a woman who looks like her: the same jaw, the same widow's peak, the parting on the same side, a few years younger. Brandt chose her for that. Her name is Tessa Quinn.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell. The locker room at 6:40 a.m. smells of floor disinfectant, the clean-laundry steam of fresh scrubs, and her coffee in its paper cup. In the evening, the warm flat with the windows open: hot tarmac cooling, someone's barbecue two streets over. Light by source: the phone's white in a dim locker room, the laptop's blue in the evening flat. Texture: the cold glass of the phone and the deliberate not-tapping thumb.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "He'd been growing her for eleven days, the way you grow a sourdough starter: fed at the same hours, kept somewhere warm, and thrown out the moment it's done its job."
  - On *The Cloakroom*: "I read it every morning the way you press on an old fracture when the weather turns. Not because it helps. Because you want to know it's still there."
  - On the face: "Nobody puts a scar like that on a composite. Composites are drawn by people who want you to remember them, and scars are what you get from the people who don't."
- **Body:** Hands. The thumb hovers over the link and stays off it. She notes the restraint as a finding, the way Ch 9 noted the hover over his dossier. She is warm (Act II begins): her neck in the locker room, her forearms bare at the open window in the evening. She eats standing in the evening while she reads, because she is planning: bread torn from a loaf, a tomato salted over the sink. She mentions it only in passing. Her face against Tessa's on the laptop screen, though she does not hold up a mirror. Mirrors belong to 15, 19 and 20.
- **Motifs:** The grade (Brandt's profile, graded high). Hands (the thumb). The paperwork thread (Mara's word leaking out of the file, the first time the file is used against anyone). Mirrors are deliberately withheld. The resemblance is read off a screen, not a glass.

## Scene 2: Collateral

1. Tuesday 15 September. By 9 a.m. the internet has her name. He left the image findable and it was found. Between cases Iris follows it on her phone, from the public side only, where she is one of thousands of people looking and therefore no one.
2. The catalogue inside forty-eight hours, kept forensic and brief. Her home address. Northgate Animal Hospital, the clinic where she works, closed for the day after its phones are flooded. A man livestreaming from the clinic car park. *KILLER* sprayed on her garage door. Strangers "analysing" her hands in a photograph of her holding a spaniel puppy. Iris corrects their anatomy silently and cannot stop herself.
3. The office's public information officer brings Iris a one-line statement to approve, because she was senior reviewer on Pell and the Chief wants Mara kept out of it. The line answers the "knew anatomy" claim by saying nothing: the office does not comment on active investigations. It goes out under her name. By the afternoon a wire story quotes "Dr. Iris Vale, the senior pathologist who reviewed the autopsy". The office does not release photographs of its pathologists. Her name is in the news and her face is not.
4. Tuesday evening, the flat. She braises a lamb shoulder low and slow while the War Room feed runs on the tablet against the fruit bowl. Theo Akers shows the room that the decoy is trending. Someone cheers. Brandt says, "Don't share it." He watches it on his own phone for a long time with nothing on his face. She can't read his screen and doesn't need to.
5. She narrates his reason for him, as she did in Ch 9, and gets it right. The bait only works if nobody cuts the line. Whoever moves to help Tessa Quinn is the person who knows she didn't do it. He is not watching Tessa. He is watching the water around her.
6. Then her own reason, as the outline requires, honestly. First she lays out the tactical reason: any contact would be a rise to his fly. She tells the reader that is true, and that it isn't the reason. The reason is that Tessa Quinn is not on the syllabus. Three names. There is no fourth. She says it plainly and means it, and the reader should hear how tidy it sounds.
7. Wednesday 16 September, mid-morning. Mara stops at Iris's office door, upset that her word has left the file. "They're saying a vet tech. Iris, the adhesive alone..." Iris stops her kindly, in short spoken sentences. Iris does not grade Mara, aloud or in narration. Keep the gap visible.
8. Iris is in the suite until noon. At 12:20 she sits down at her desk with lunch in its glass container, and the desk phone's message light is on. One new message, left at 11:38 a.m. She plays it on the handset. It is forty-seven seconds long. Tessa gives her name, then says she is sorry, twice. She is looking for "the pathologist in the news, the woman", because she didn't write the name down. She has never met Diane Pell and never been to the reservoir. The police have asked her where she was that Sunday. There are men outside her house. She thought that someone who actually knew what happened could just say so. "I'd just like to talk to someone who knows. If you can." She leaves a number. In the background a dog shifts its claws on a hard floor.
9. Iris presses 7. The automated voice says the message has been moved to the archive. She knows, as everyone in county service knows, that nothing in a public office is ever deleted. It is only moved to a folder nobody opens. She has never opened hers. **Do not let Iris notice that this rhymes with the Lesson One page ("the cabinet into the room no one enters"). The reader notices, or the second read does.**
10. **[TURN]** She listened to it once. She deleted it. She ate lunch: the lamb cold, the fat set white and melting on the tongue, the salsa verde sharp with caper and anchovy and too much lemon. She eats all of it, sitting down, with the fork she keeps in her desk drawer, and it is as good as it was last night. Report the taste in full. No commentary.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound. Tessa's voice through the cheap handset is compressed and close, with a breath before each sentence. Then the dog's claws on lino, a car door somewhere outside her house, the automated voice of the county system, and the small electronic chirp of the 7. Then taste, at the Turn: cold lamb fat, caper brine, anchovy, lemon. Smell on Tuesday evening: the braise, with garlic, rosemary and wine reducing, filling the flat. Temperature: the office warm because the building's air conditioning has been turned down for autumn and autumn hasn't come.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "I was in the news for a sentence that said nothing, which is the most accurate thing ever printed about me."
  - On the internet's forensic experts: "They had found a killer's grip in a photograph of a woman holding a spaniel puppy, which is a thing you can do if you have never held a scalpel or a spaniel."
  - On Brandt: "He wasn't watching her. Nobody fishing watches the worm."
- **Body:** Eating. On Tuesday she cooks, standing (planning). On Wednesday she eats lunch sitting down, which is how she eats after a kill. Do not say so. Let the posture be the only signal. Hands: her index finger on the 7. She watches it press, the way she watches her hands everywhere, and reports that it is steady. Warm all through the scene, including at the delete.
- **Motifs:** Voicemail (the second of the three unreturned. Set it up so Ch 18 can lay it beside the first. It is played once, and the archive is real). The meal (not the meal after, but eaten in that posture). The grade (Brandt's reasoning graded correct; Mara pointedly not graded). Hands. Tape, through Mara's unfinished sentence about the adhesive.

## Scene 3: Detachment

1. Wednesday night, the flat, after ten. Every window is open and the heat is sitting in the rooms like a guest who won't take the hint. She stands at the counter with the heel of the loaf and the end of the salsa verde and thinks about where an account that regular is run from. He has machinery somewhere, and she would like to see it. She doesn't go looking tonight. (Seed for Ch 11. No address, no company, no names.)
2. Detachment is the first principle, and she gives it its morgue form. You meet the body before you meet the name. You read the history after the findings, never before, because history contaminates. The syllabus is the same rule widened: three names, and everybody else is history that isn't available.
3. She maintains it, mostly. She does not search Tessa Quinn tonight. One tab is still open on the laptop from Monday, and she closes it without looking. Then she admits she looked long enough to know what it was: the greyhound.
4. The inventory, involuntary. She lists what she knows about Tessa without ever having tried to learn it. The eyebrow scar, the white hairs. The clinic. Her age. The greyhound's name, which is Pilot. The colour of the garage door under the paint. A pathologist cannot unsee a finding, and she presents the list as proof of training, not as care.
5. Direct address, the voice doing what the plot can't yet. There is something here. She can see its outline the way you see a shadow on a scan before anyone has measured it, and she is not going to measure it. She tells the reader she is refusing, and asks the reader to watch her refuse.
6. The drawer, the brass lock. Brandt's dossier is still a quarter-inch to the left, where she left it in Ch 9. She takes out the Lesson Two page, her drawer master, with *Excellent.* in red in the margin beside the subject line.
7. Below *Excellent.* she writes *Tessa Quinn*, in black ink, in the small upright hand she uses for case numbers. Her stated reason is administrative. A name that isn't written down goes on following you about. Once it is written it is filed.
8. **[TURN]** She draws a line through the name, ruled against the edge of Brandt's dossier, one stroke, straight and level. She has never struck through a name before. She doesn't say that the care she took is the tell. The line is the crack. Then the page goes back into the drawer, squared to its own edge, and the drawer is locked.

- **Sensory lead:** Texture and temperature. The heat of the flat on her skin. The paper softened at the corners from two years of handling, the nib dragging slightly on the dossier's edge as she rules. Smell: the black ink (not the red marker; she does not uncap it tonight), lamb fat on her fingers that she has washed off and can still smell. Light: the desk lamp only, the same lamp as in Ch 9. Sound: the street through open windows, a car alarm two blocks away that stops on its own.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "We are taught to read the history after the findings, because a history is a story somebody else wants you to believe, and the body has never once tried to win me over."
  - "Three names. I had been very clear with myself about the number. It was the only thing in the syllabus I had never had to redraft."
  - On the inventory: "I knew the dog's name. I hadn't asked for it. Nobody ever asks me for anything, and I know everything anyway." (The trap here is any line that apologises for the knowledge or the appetite. The banned list forbids it. She reports, never regrets.)
- **Body:** Hands. She rules with the pen and holds the dossier flat with her other palm, both steady. Make it the steadiest she has been all chapter, so the steadiness reads as effort. Warm skin. She is not tired, unlike Ch 9's Snapper, and this carries into the Snapper. She is full from lunch, still, and notes that at ten at night she isn't hungry. That is the only time in Act II's opening that the body declines food. It is reported, not explained.
- **Motifs:** The grade (*Excellent.* above, a struck name below. The page now holds his grade and her deletion, one on top of the other). Hands. Voicemail, by implication (the line is the delete key done in ink. Do not say so). The quarter-inch, kept. Vinyl, offstage: through the open window somebody is playing music on a stereo, which she can't name and doesn't try to.
