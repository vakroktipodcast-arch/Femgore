# Beat sheet — ch01: The Hook & the Syllabus

- **Unit:** ch01 (`manuscript/ch01.md`)
- **POV:** Iris Vale, first person, past tense
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Three scenes of ~1,050 each.
- **Act pressure:** Act I, The Prerequisite. The pressure is the campaign's certainty that nothing is coming, and it has to be felt in every scene: the break-room television in 1, Hannah's sticker in 2, the debate in 3. Brandt doesn't come in until Chapter 8. Running under everything is the fifth pressure, appetite. It's present and unnamed, and the reader should feel it as a danger before Iris does.
- **Appetite at entry:** appetite as competence. She loves the morgue and doesn't call it love. The pleasure is already there in the cut, the weight and the naming, but she files it under craft.
- **Appetite at exit:** the appetite has come up three times (Mara's remark, the second sandwich, the hunger at the drawer). Each time she noticed it and misfiled it. At the drawer it is physical hunger, reported straight, and she doesn't connect it to the kit on the desk. The reader does.
- **Chapter head:** the Lesson One syllabus page (text as in `bible/style/08`) is reproduced as a document above Scene 1. Scene 3 calls back to it. Don't re-quote it in full there.
- **Grading rule for this chapter:** Iris grades Hannah's lie, the debate, Holt and herself. She does **not** grade Mara, out loud or in narration. The gap should be visible, because Scene 1 is the first place the reader learns she grades everything.

## Scene 1 — The Suite

1. First paragraph, first page: Iris has the Y-incision open on a Jane Doe. She is naming each organ for Mara as it comes out, and the voice is set at once: exact, funny, in love with the work. Mara stands across the table and watches the way people watch a chef.
2. The Jane Doe in brief. A woman in her sixties, found in a bus shelter, no ID, a hospital bracelet cut off long ago and the pale band of skin where it was. Iris reads the body's biography from the outside before she reads it from the inside. *Reading order: smell first, then sound, texture, temperature, weight. Sight last.*
3. The liver. Iris lifts it out and weighs it in her hands before she weighs it on the scale (1,840 g). It's a long, greedy sentence about the smooth capsule, the fatty cut surface the colour of putty and old butter, the heft. It's written as pleasure, and she never uses the word.
4. Mara asks a technical question about the heart (hypertensive, 520 g). Iris answers in short spoken sentences while the narration runs long and hungry underneath. The gap is the joke, and she's in on it.
5. On the break-room television, glimpsed through the office door: a poll graphic, Marchetti up, the anchor calling the race "his to lose". Mara mentions the debate tonight. Iris says she might watch it and makes it sound like she means the weather.
6. The clean cut: a single stroke through the brain on the slicing board, and a sentence about how small and particular the satisfaction is. She doesn't call it joy. She calls it "a good day's work, early".
7. Closing up. Surgical tape on the specimen labels. Iris's hands are steady as always, and she notes that they have not shaken since her second year of residency. That line exists for Chapter 2.
8. [TURN] Mara, stripping her gloves, says quietly that Iris works like she's taking apart something she built. Iris lets it pass without answering. To the reader, a beat later: she liked hearing it. No analysis.
9. Out: she scrubs in at the sink and the suite's smell stays on her anyway. The morgue on her skin is the image that carries into Scene 2.

- **Sensory lead:** smell. Under the extraction fan the suite smells of cold iron, a faint sweetness and chlorhexidine, with the faecal note she has stopped minding and still names. Sound: the hose, the scale settling, the saw kept off-page or brief. Temperature: the suite's cold, which she likes. Texture: the liver's capsule, slick and taut.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - Mara watches her like a chef, "though in fairness to chefs, they rarely have to weigh the customer first."
  - On the liver at 1,840 grams: "which is roughly what thirty years of a good time weighs, give or take the good time."
- **Body:** hands. They're steady, wet and gloved, and the liver lies in both palms. The suite cold on her forearms is pleasant, the cold she chooses. No eating yet. She notes she skipped breakfast, which is a seed.
- **Motifs:** hands; tape (surgical tape in the morgue); the grade (withheld from Mara, noticeably); cold (the chosen kind).

## Scene 2 — The Sister

1. Noon, in a city park in late-August heat. The temperature cuts hard against the suite. Hannah has spread a blanket under a plane tree with a cool bag and her two daughters, Astrid (8) and June (5), who are wet from the sprinkler.
2. Hannah calls her "Ris" and hugs her. Then, pulling back, she says "you smell like a hospital" and Iris doesn't correct her. Under the sunscreen the morgue is still on her skin.
3. The sandwiches are Hannah's: roast chicken and tarragon mayonnaise on white, cut into triangles for the girls. Iris eats two whole ones while Hannah eats half of one and the crusts June refused. Iris clocks the ratio and puts it down to skipping breakfast.
4. June has a campaign sticker on her T-shirt, MARCHETTI in navy and gold, handed out at the library. Hannah says, warmly and in passing, that she likes him and that the veterans thing seems real. Iris says "mm" and peels the sticker off June's shirt when it starts to lift, and smooths it back down. Nobody notices her thumb.
5. Hannah starts gently on Iris's life: the flat, the hours, whether she's taking any holiday. Iris turns each question aside with practised warmth. She is very good at this and lets the reader see how good.
6. Astrid asks what Iris does all day. Iris gives an answer that is true, child-safe and very funny to the reader ("I find out why people stopped"). Hannah gives her a look.
7. A third sandwich is left in the cool bag. Iris wants it and doesn't take it. It's the first time her body asks for more than it gets, and she reports it as a fact about lunch.
8. [TURN] Hannah asks if she's seeing anyone. Iris lies fluently and kindly: "Sort of. Early days. He works weekends." She gives just enough detail to stop the next question, and no name. Then she tells the reader exactly how good the lie was, grades it (it lost marks only for being kind), and adds the private truth: strictly, she was seeing three people, and she had photographs.
9. She leaves on time. Hannah says "call me on Sunday". Iris says she will.

- **Sensory lead:** temperature, the August heat after the suite's cold. Then taste: tarragon, the mayonnaise going warm, white bread gone soft from the cool bag. Smell: cut grass, sunscreen, and under them the morgue on her own skin.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - Hannah "ate like a mother: half a sandwich and the edges of other people's."
  - On the lie: it was a B-plus at most, docked for compassion, "which is the only thing that ever gives a good lie away."
- **Body:** eating, in the chapter's first meal. Two sandwiches to Hannah's half, and the third declined. Heat on her neck. Her thumb on the sticker.
- **Motifs:** tape (the sticker: an adhesive, peeled and pressed down, prefiguring the sticker seal on the service door); the grade (the lie); the meal, pre-echoed (she eats seriously and blames physiology before there is anything to blame it on).

## Scene 3 — The Glass and the Drawer

1. Evening, the flat. The debate is on her tablet, propped against the fruit bowl, and the tablet's blue light is the only light. She is eating nothing and grading both candidates on structure. Holt is winning on syntax and she says so, fairly.
2. Holt answers a question on veterans and mentions Mark. It is sincere and it works. She grades that too, and gives it full marks, which is the worst grade she can give.
3. At the close of an answer Holt adjusts his left cuff: thumb and forefinger of the right hand, a small quarter-turn of the cufflink, his eyes going down to it for two seconds. Name the gesture exactly. Don't say what it is to her. It is the gesture she last saw from much closer than a tablet. Nothing more is confirmed.
4. Cold. It's real and physical, the body's report, and specific: the forearms first, then the backs of the hands, then the jaw. Not a metaphor. (Keep the metal taste back for Chapter 2's Turn. This is cold only.)
5. An hour without moving. The debate ends, a panel takes over, the panel ends. She knows it's an hour because the tablet tells her, and she reports it with the forensic interest she'd give a stranger's lividity.
6. She gets up. The drawer of the writing desk, the key. Inside: three dossiers (Pell, Brandt, Marchetti), photographs, her own signed copy of the NDA, and THE BUTCHER'S SYLLABUS, printed, three pages, two years old. The paper has gone faintly soft at the corners from being handled and not used.
7. She reads Lesson One aloud to the flat and hears what her voice does to it: it sounds like a judgement and it sounds like her. It's a brief callback to the chapter-head page, not a re-quote. One phrase at most ("You will be evaluated accordingly").
8. From the drawer's back compartment she takes the kit and lays it on the desk in a row, as she would instruments: the red marker, the black vinyl tape, the zip-ties. Name the uncapped marker's solvent smell, the vinyl's dull sheen, the zip-ties' ratchet when she tests one.
9. [TURN] She looks at the row and notices she is hungry. Plainly, in the stomach, and at once. Report it as a finding, as physiology, the way she reported the sandwiches, and don't connect it to the kit. Let the reader connect it.
10. She doesn't eat. She puts on her coat. (Carry into the Snapper.)

- **Sensory lead:** sound and light. The tablet's tinny applause, the fridge hum, a debate moderator's voice talking to no one. Then temperature (the cold). Then the drawer's smell: paper, the marker's solvent.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Televised debates are autopsies performed by people who have never been told where anything is."
  - On the hour without moving: she has sat still for longer, "but on those occasions the other party was dead, and it was their stillness we were documenting."
- **Body:** cold (Act I cold, the unchosen kind, the opposite of the suite). Hands: still on her knees for an hour, then steady laying out the kit. Hunger at the end, unfed.
- **Motifs:** hands; tape (black vinyl); cold; the grade (Holt full marks for Mark; Lesson One read aloud); the Marine's photograph, by implication only. Mark is mentioned in the debate, but no photograph is shown.

## Snapper

She leaves the tablet on when she goes. Holt is still talking as the door closes, to a room with nobody in it, and she has decided to stop being one.

*(Sharpened from the outline: "nobody in it" keeps "empty" off Iris's state, per the banned list. The admission is the last clause and it's the last thing she says. It's not a cliffhanger, and her destination isn't given.)*
