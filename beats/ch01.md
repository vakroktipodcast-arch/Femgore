## Scene 1 — The Suite

1. First paragraph, first page: Iris lifts the liver out of a Jane Doe and doesn't put it on the scale at once. She holds it, across both palms. The voice is set at once: exact, funny, in love with the work. ("You are allowed to hold it"; reads the manual "for what it forgot to forbid.") Scale: 1,840 g.
2. Mara's "Heavy." Iris's "Fatty. Look at the edge." Mara watches her work the way people watch a chef through the pass. One long cut: putty-and-old-butter surface, greasy under the blade.
3. Smell first, stated as her rule: cold iron, chlorhexidine, a faint sweetness, the low faecal note she stopped minding in her first year and has never stopped naming. "The smell of things that are exactly what they are."
4. The Jane Doe in brief. A woman in her sixties, small, good teeth, bad feet, found in a bus shelter upright with her bag on her knees and her eyes open, there most of a morning by the driver's reckoning. No ID. A pale band a centimetre wide on the left wrist where a hospital bracelet was cut off. Iris reads her from the outside before the inside. *Reading order: smell first, then sound, texture, temperature, weight. Sight last.*
5. Mara asks about the heart (520 g, hypertensive, left ventricle 2 cm). Iris answers in short spoken sentences ("Headaches. Maybe.") while the narration runs long and hungry underneath: a fist clenched so long it forgot how to open; muscle answers resistance by growing. The gap is the joke and she says she's in on it. She had held the heart too and hadn't mentioned it to Mara.
6. On the break-room television, glimpsed through the open office door past the coat stand, sound down: a two-colour map, a bar chart with his bar longer, a man in a navy suit smiling "with his whole face", MARCHETTI, the ticker's *his to lose*. Mara follows her eyes: "Debate's tonight. Are you going to watch?" Iris: "Might." Said like an umbrella.
7. The brain comes last ("It's the dessert"). One pull of the long knife on the slicing board, front to back, no sawing; it opens like a book on a stand. She tells the reader how small the satisfaction is so they won't make it bigger: a door closing without being slammed, the click of a good pen. "I called it a good day's work, early." Small bleed, left basal ganglia, raisin-sized, oxblood on cream: cause of death. Mara writes it down.
8. Closing up together. Specimen pots labelled, surgical tape, three strips each, pressed with the thumbnail for the sound of it staying. Hands steady; not shaken since her second year of residency, "I mention it now so you'll know what it means later." Mara strips her gloves inside out, the way Iris taught her.
9. [TURN] Mara, not looking up, quietly, as if telling her the time: "You work like you're taking apart something you built." Iris says nothing and lets it go past like a car she waits to cross behind. Then one line on its own: "I liked hearing it." No analysis. **Scene ends here.**

- **Sensory lead:** smell (stated as her rule). Then texture (the capsule drawn taut as a drum skin, wet-stone slickness), weight (the liver across both palms), temperature (the suite's cold, chosen). Sound: the scale "thinking about it", the extraction fans, the tape deciding to stay. Saw kept off-page.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - In prose: Mara watches like a chef through the pass, "though in fairness to chefs, they rarely have to weigh the customer first."
  - In prose: reads the manual "the way a lawyer reads a contract, for what it forgot to forbid."
  - In prose: the brain is "the dessert"; "the scale thought about it."
  - Unused, available if the drafter needs a trim-in: on the liver at 1,840 g, "roughly what thirty years of a good time weighs, give or take the good time."
- **Body:** hands, wet and gloved, the liver across both palms; thumbnail pressing tape. The cold of the suite is a cold she chooses. No eating. (The skipped breakfast is **not** raised in Scene 1; the prose first has it at the sandwiches. Keep it there.)
- **Motifs:** hands; tape (surgical tape on labels); the grade (withheld from Mara, noticeably); cold (the chosen kind); the television (the campaign's certainty).

## Scene 2 — The Sister

1. Noon, a city park, late-August heat "hot enough to lean on". The contrast with the suite is physical: her hands have been in things at four degrees, by choice, and the heat goes for the back of her neck before the gate. Hannah has the blanket out under the big plane tree by the sprinklers, with the cool bag and the girls: June (5), still in her wet clothes, running at the jets and shrieking; Astrid (8), in a towel at the blanket's edge, watching with a lifeguard's patience.
2. Hannah gets up, hugs her, holds on, holds her off with her nose wrinkled: "You smell like a hospital." Iris doesn't correct her; the suite is on her skin under the soap, and "people like the word hospital. It has hope in it." **[Hannah's form of address on greeting: prose "Ris", ledger "Iris". See Conflicts. Do not resolve in this sheet.]**
3. Hannah's sandwiches: roast chicken (from Sunday) and tarragon mayonnaise on soft white, cut into triangles ("nobody has ever been sad holding a triangle"). Iris eats one, then another, faster. Exact taste: salt, fat, liquorice, the soft bread where it touched the ice pack; "the comfort of being a child."
4. Hannah eats half of one, and the crusts June refused, without seeming to notice. Iris clocks the ratio, two to a half, and puts it down to having skipped breakfast: "which was true, and which is the kind of true that is very convenient." (This is the chapter's first mention of the skipped breakfast.)
5. June's navy-and-gold MARCHETTI sticker from the library, wavy from the sprinkler. Hannah, warmly and in passing: she quite likes him, the veterans thing seems real, his brother. Iris: "Mm." June climbs into her lap; the sticker lifts; Iris peels it back a centimetre and smooths it down with her thumb until it "decided to stay." Nobody looks at her thumb.
6. Hannah starts on her life, from the side "the way you approach a horse": sleeping, holiday (none last year), the morgue and "nobody but the, *you know*". Iris answers everything warmly and a little too fully with a funny story as the door out (the pathologist before her who sang under the fans, and nobody knew for years); promises a week in October she won't take. Refusing is information. She tells the reader to look at how good she is.
7. Astrid, pretending to watch the sprinklers: "What do you actually do. All day." Iris: "I find out why people stopped." Astrid nods as if it were a reasonable job. Hannah gives Iris a look over her head; Iris files it.
8. A third sandwich in the cool bag, visible through the gap in the lid, a triangle and its other half in the paper. Iris wants it, specifically, in the stomach. She doesn't take it. "A fact about lunch," and she tells the reader the facts about lunch. First time the body asks for more than it gets.
9. [TURN] Hannah leans back on her elbows: "So. Are you seeing anyone?" Iris, fluently and kindly: "Sort of. Early days. He works weekends." The anatomy of the lie: three facts, one forecloses the next question, none to look up; no name, "because a name is a thing people repeat to their husbands"; a slightly shy smile on *early days*. Hannah squeezes her ankle, says *good*, and *call me on Sunday*, and means both ("the only part I minded"). Iris grades it B-plus, docked for compassion, "the only thing that ever gives a good lie away." Last line: "And I wasn't, strictly, lying about the seeing. Strictly, I was seeing three people. I had photographs." **Scene ends here.** (Prose has no leave-taking; do not add one after the Turn.)

- **Sensory lead:** temperature (heat after the suite's cold), then taste (tarragon gone anise-sharp overnight, mayonnaise, soft white bread, firmed-up sweet chicken), then smell (the suite on her own skin under soap, against the park). Sound: sprinklers, June's shrieking.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - In prose: Hannah "ate the way mothers eat, half a sandwich and the edges of other people's."
  - In prose: the lie "lost marks for compassion."
  - In prose: "I find out why people stopped." (Child-safe, true, funny to the reader.)
  - In prose: "People like the word *hospital*. It has hope in it."
  - In prose: "Nobody looked at my thumb. Why would they."
- **Body:** eating, the chapter's first meal, two to Hannah's half, and the third declined. Heat on the back of her neck. June's weight in her lap. Her thumb on the sticker.
- **Motifs:** tape (the sticker: an adhesive peeled and pressed down, prefiguring the sticker seal on the service door; "decided to stay" echoes the surgical tape in Scene 1); the grade (the lie); the meal, pre-echoed (she eats seriously and blames physiology before there is anything to blame it on); the photographs (planted in the last line, paid off in the drawer).

## Scene 3 — The Glass and the Drawer

1. Evening, the flat. The debate on the tablet, propped against the fruit bowl on the kitchen counter; its blue the only light in the flat. She has not eaten. She grades both men on structure ("content is opinion, structure is anatomy"). Holt wins on syntax: his sentences land with room to spare "the way a good surgeon's hands are never quite touching the thing they are about to touch"; the other man tests each rung like a ladder he doesn't trust. She gives Holt a distinction for syntax, fairly.
2. Asked about veterans, Holt mentions Mark: quick, almost apologetic, the only true thing he knows on the subject. Sincere, and it works; she watches a long soft exhalation go through the hall. She gives him full marks, "the worst grade I know how to give."
3. At the end of the answer, as the moderator turns away: Holt's right thumb and forefinger take his left cufflink and give it a quarter-turn, as if loose (it isn't). His eyes go down to it. Two seconds. Up again, listening with his whole face. Name the gesture exactly; don't say what it is to her. One line: "It looked smaller on a tablet." Nothing more is confirmed.
4. Cold. It's a finding, not a figure of speech: the volar forearms first, as if lowered into a sink of water left overnight; then the backs of the hands on her knees; then up the sides of the neck into the hinge of the jaw, which goes tight and stays tight. Not the suite's cold, "the kind that comes to you." (Cold only. Keep the metal taste for Chapter 2's Turn.)
5. An hour without moving. She knows because the tablet told her: debate over, panel, a woman says *his to lose*, panel over, something else begins. She charts it as she would a stranger's stillness ("no voluntary movement observed over approximately sixty minutes… Jaw held. Eyes open"), noting she has sat still longer but "the other party was dead."
6. She gets up. The walnut writing desk, the only thing she overpaid for; the drawer locks with a small brass key on the ring with her car key, so she carries it every time she drives, "which I have always thought was the point." It slides out with a long wooden sigh. Inside, top to bottom: three manila dossiers with string and button (Pell, Brandt, Marchetti), photographs, her own signed copy of the NDA (three years old, signed in the blue ink they gave her, rounder than her signature now), and in a clear sleeve the three printed pages, two years old, with a typed title page: THE BUTCHER'S SYLLABUS. Soft at the corners, "handled and not used."
7. She stands in the blue light and reads Lesson One aloud to the flat and hears what her voice does to it: a judgement; her. One phrase only, *You will be evaluated accordingly*. She wrote it at this desk at about this hour two years ago and had never heard it said. She sounds like someone who has marked things for years and has finally been handed the right paper.
8. From the back compartment (a panel that comes away if pressed in the right place) she lays out the kit in a row, left to right in order of use, as she lays out instruments: the red marker (uncapped to check; solvent smell, a classroom whiteboard on the first day of term; recapped), the black vinyl tape (a full roll, dull sheen like the back of a beetle), the zip-ties (a bundle; one drawn through its own head to test the ratchet, a sound like a watch wound very fast; it holds). She looks at them for a while.
9. [TURN] "I was hungry." Plainly, in the stomach, all at once, like coming in from the cold and smelling someone else's dinner. She charts it: no food since the sandwiches, nine hours, "an entirely ordinary finding. It was physiology. I want you to have that the way I had it." Not connected to the kit; the reader connects it.
10. *(run-in to the Snapper, not a second Turn)* She doesn't eat. She puts the three things in her coat pocket, puts the coat on, "turned off nothing."

- **Sensory lead:** sound and light first: the tablet's blue as the only light, the tinny hall, the moderator's voice, the fridge hum. Then temperature (the cold, in order of body parts). Then the drawer's smell and sounds: wooden sigh, paper, the marker's solvent, the zip-tie's ratchet.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - In prose: "Televised debates are autopsies performed by people who have never been told where anything is."
  - In prose: on the hour without moving, "the other party was dead, and it was their stillness we were documenting."
  - In prose: she carries the key on the car ring because carrying it is the point.
  - In prose: she wrote the title page, was embarrassed a week later, and a week after that stopped being embarrassed.
- **Body:** cold (Act I cold, the unchosen kind, the opposite of the suite). Hands: palms down on her knees for an hour, then steady laying out the kit. Jaw held. Hunger at the end, unfed. Things put down squared in a row.
- **Motifs:** hands; tape (black vinyl); cold; the grade (Holt: distinction, then full marks; Lesson One read aloud; her own stillness charted); the Marine, by name only (Mark mentioned in the debate, no photograph shown); the meal (the unfed hunger); the drawer and its key.
