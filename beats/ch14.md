- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Three scenes of ~1,050 each. No syllabus insert at the chapter head (inserts are Ch 1, 6 and 17 only).
- **Act pressure:** Brandt, in the last week before Ch 17, and here he is a pressure because he is coming apart. A man the campaign has walled off has nothing left to lose, and he holds the one thing she can't control: the list. A new pressure arrives in S2 and stays for the rest of the book: Reyes, as paper. She has stopped pretending the gifts were tactics. She wants the next meeting and knows it.

The body is already on the "you don't eat before" footing of Ch 15, and the reader should recognise it before she does. Still warm. **Do not give her the Act I cold at the Reyes letter.** The cold returns from Ch 18. Here the menace is registered in appetite (the lunch she doesn't eat) and in hands (the wound), not in temperature. ## Scene 1: The Fracture

1. Thursday 8 October, 7:20 a.m., the flat. She is not due on the table until ten. The tablet has the War Room feed live (as it did for the debate in Ch 1); the bezel unit has been feeding her for six weeks. Beside it, against the fruit bowl, is a black coffee she made and isn't drinking, gone cold and smelling of the cup. She opens on the door reader's two notes at 7:51, then goes back to take the history first ("You don't start a chart at the last line"). She has watched three days of the wobble, and she gives the case history in brisk clinical order. On Monday morning, in front of the room, he told Theo Akers, the boy in digital, that he had leaked the photograph. The boy said he'd deleted it as he was told, and a clean pallor went up under his hair. Brandt went home before eleven. Since then he has stood: at the end of the table, at the window, at the urn, carrying a coffee he doesn't drink (echoing ch09's undrunk urn coffee).
2. The room has noticed before anyone has said anything. Iris reads the staff as a body: a ward knows which bed's cultures have come back before the consultant has read them, and the nurses' routes go a step wider round the foot of it. In three days nobody has stood within an arm's length of him. She was counting.
3. Diane would have managed this: a side room, a hand at the elbow, one pause of the kind her staff mistook for thoughtfulness, and fixed before lunch. Diane is not available. Iris knows the exact reason, and the time (10:47, by her watch).
4. On Wednesday evening, at 9:14 by the feed's timestamp, Brandt bent to Holt's ear. Holt stood and put a hand on his shoulder, and the two of them left the glass room together, out of the lens. She could not hear it. It is the one conversation of the week she wanted, and the camera gave her the doorway, a rectangle of corridor light with nobody in it. Holt came back alone four minutes later and was charming about something on the wall screen. Brandt came back nearly two minutes after him and did not sit.
5. She has read the file. Holt's dossier in her drawer has a section for the men he has stopped knowing (ch09), and read in a row they are a clinical course, given as transplant rejection: first the meetings stop including you, then the doors, then the systems; nobody says the word, any more than a kidney is told it is being rejected; the host just stops sending it blood, a vessel at a time. Nobody had the authority to dismiss Brandt, so they stopped letting him in. At 7:42 a.m. the huddle starts without him. On his way to the head of the table Holt touches the deputy communications director on the shoulder, and the deputy sits at the end of the table, in Brandt's chair. That is the only literal chair in the scene.
6. At 7:51 a.m. Brandt comes into frame outside the glass in charcoal, holds his card to the reader, gets the two notes and the brake-lamp light, looks at the card, turns it over, and tries again. At the head of the table Holt does not look up, and Iris notes that it takes years of practice not to look up through glass. A man from IT comes along the corridor, lets him in with his own card, and smiles a smile that is sorry about something.
7. He goes to the laptop at the end of the table. Somebody is sitting at his place, so he logs in standing, one hand flat on the table. The units can't read a screen, so she reads his hands, as in ch09. The password goes in once at speed, again a little harder on the keys, and a third time slowly, a key at a time, each finger lifting clear, the way you type when you've begun to suspect it isn't the keyboard.
8. **[TURN]** His hands stop on the trackpad, right over left, fingers a little flexed. He looks up the table at Holt, and Holt is laughing, head back, at something the deputy said. She watches the rejection run its course in him, the procedure she had only read in a dossier, performed by Holt himself, by the book, without his ever being seen to hold the book. She gives it full marks. Something moves under her sternum, a small warm displacement, which she refuses to call sympathy. She files it as the feeling of watching a colleague receive the diagnosis you would have given him yourself, and she does not let the sentence go any further. Her left hand has gone under her shirt to the dressing on her flank, and it stays there. (Holt walls him off by Holt's order; Iris never sees the order, only its effects.)

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first: the feed's compressed audio and the door reader's two-tone refusal, thinned by the speaker into something like a microwave finishing. Then the HVAC hum of the room, the clatter of the huddle, and his silence inside it. Temperature: the coffee going cold at her elbow, and her own warmth, which is Act II and stays put. Light: the tablet's blue in a morning kitchen, the glass room's fluorescents, and the flat white of an overcast morning behind the glass (the sun is already up at 7:20; no sodium, no dawn).
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Nobody in that building had the authority to fire Cam Brandt, and so nobody did. They simply stopped letting him in, which is how hospitals handle a death they would rather not certify."
  - On the password: "A man types his password a third time the way the bereaved ring a number they know is disconnected. Not to get through. To hear it not answer."
  - (Held in reserve, use only if the scene wants a third: on Holt not looking up, "It takes years of practice not to look up through glass. I've seen consultants manage it at a nurse's station. He did it with his whole face.")
- **Body:** Cold coffee untouched. This is the first missed intake of the chapter, and it goes unremarked here and is counted in S3. Her hands are flat on the counter either side of the tablet, the way you rest your hands on a table during a case conference. Once, at the Turn, her left hand goes under her shirt to the dressing on the flank (six days old, the nylon ends a stiff line under the tape, itching) and stays there.
- **Motifs:** The grade (she gives Holt's procedure full marks, and reads the room's body language as a diagnosis). Hands (Brandt's on the trackpad, hers on the counter, the wound). The coffee rhymes with the ch09 undrunk coffee. Keep it unpointed.

## Scene 2: The Name

1. Thursday, 1:10 p.m., the ME's car park, her car, engine off. It is lunch, and she has brought none. The canteen is on the far side of the car park and she came out the other way; crossing the tarmac she checked her coat pocket the way you check for a wallet, and it was empty, as she knew it would be. She takes the tablet out of her bag and opens the tap's digest, as she has twice a day since she fitted it (Ch 11). Over Brandt's own uplink it sends headers and the bodies of mail up to a couple of kilobytes, nothing attached: "a patient's observations instead of the patient". (She does not open it inside the building.)
2. The joke the scene is built on: Holt locked Brandt out of the house that morning but not out of the plumbing, because officially there is no plumbing. The farm is Brandt's private copy of the campaign, three companies deep, with a dead woman's name on the paperwork, and its copy of the mail runs under Diane's compliance authority, not his login. Holt cannot close a door he has never been told exists. Brandt is out. Iris, through his drains, is still in.
3. At 11:12 counsel sent an email to Holt's office, not to Brandt, who had been copied on every email of counsel's she had read for a week (S1's walling, now in paper). Its first line is the instruction: *Hold until after the third.* Under it is counsel's reply, sent at 11:05, and under that the FBI letter, received Wednesday 7 October and quoted in full. The digest cuts off a line into the firm's disclaimer, with the letter just inside the limit, so she has its words and no letterhead. It requests all agreements under matter LC-0311, with associated signatory records, "in connection with the death of Diane Pell". It is signed Special Agent E. Reyes.
4. Counsel's reply is a masterpiece of the form: the campaign is "reviewing the scope of the request and attendant questions of privilege" and "will respond in due course". Iris gives counsel an A; he has the form by heart. *In due course* is what an insurer writes on a cause-of-death enquiry, meaning after the body is cold. Stalling is the one bodily function a law firm never loses, even on the table.
5. She checks the matter number. She knows LC-0311 the way she knows the timestamp on a voicemail she deleted (ch10): it sits under a thin rule in the footer of every page of her agreement. She cut those pages to fingernail size and packed them into Diane's mouth so the footers would be there for whoever opened it (ch07); Diane's teeth closed at the last on the tip of her glove, and she can still find that pressure in the fingertip. She had pictured the footers followed as far as counsel, embarrassing a man in a good suit. She had not pictured anyone with a name pushing past him, and someone has. All agreements under the matter: staff, vendors, donors, bus contracts, and hers among them, eight pages and a signature.
6. She searches the name (on the tablet, the device in her hands; no phone in the scene) the way she would pull a chart: name, rank, office. There is nothing she can use. The Bureau does not publish its people, which she respects, and an initial and a surname turn up a hundred strangers. She has a dossier on everyone who matters, in a locked drawer, with string and a button; she knew the boy in digital by his hairline. Reyes has a signature block and an initial, and this person has no file.
7. Out of habit she opens the War Room on the tablet. At 1:40 p.m. counsel goes into the glass room and speaks to Holt with his head inclined, like a registrar to a consultant at a bedside, and Holt nods twice. Brandt is at the far end of the table, ten feet away, standing with a coffee, and nobody calls him over. He may not have known. For the first time that week she knows something about his campaign before he does; she waits for it to feel the way it should, and it feels like reading a colleague's results over his shoulder, and her mouth is dry.
8. Her hand goes to the dressing through her scrub top, and she is thinking, so it stays there. The car is warm (the sun has been on it all morning). She notes the warmth, and notes that it doesn't change.
9. **[TURN]** She reads the letter a second time, all of it, from the signature block up to the first line, the way she reads a chart, latest entry first. There is no page for Reyes: no subject line, no function, no Evaluation typed two years ago at her desk. She makes the Ch 12 distinction herself: the warehouse the Friday before was off the syllabus, but that was a thing she did; this is a thing she is in. For the first time something is happening that she did not write.

- **Sensory lead:** Texture and temperature: the car seat hot through her scrubs at the backs of her thighs, like a bath someone else has run. Smell: the morgue on her skin inside a closed car, which is formalin and the unscented soap that doesn't shift it and, under both, a sweetish note fifteen years of corridors have set into her skin like a watermark; in the hot car it is like being shut in with a colleague. Sound: the shut-in quiet of a parked car. Light: midday through the windscreen, the tablet's glare. Taste: none. She has no lunch, and the drafter should let the absence show by the pocket she checks and the canteen she doesn't walk to, not by naming it.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Holt had locked Cam Brandt out of the house. He had not locked out the plumbing, because officially there was no plumbing, and so counsel's correspondence came to me the way everything in that building eventually comes to a pathologist: through the drains."
  - "I had a file on everyone. I had a file on the boy in digital. Reyes had a signature block and an initial, and an initial is not a person. It is what you put on the toe tag before the family arrives." (Held in reserve: "'Will respond in due course.' I have seen that phrase on a cause-of-death enquiry from an insurer. Both parties understood it to mean after the body was cold.")
- **Body:** No lunch, and that goes unnamed in this scene (the empty coat pocket, the canteen she walks away from). Hand to the dressing through her scrub top (left flank, the tails a ridge under the tape, itching the way healing skin does), held there. Mouth dry at 1:40. Still warm. No cold.
- **Motifs:** The paperwork thread (strategy 09, the matter number coming home). The grade (counsel an A; Reyes cannot be graded, and that is the menace). Hands (the wound; the remembered pressure of Diane's teeth in the fingertip). Voicemail, sideways: she knows LC-0311 "the way I know the timestamp on a voicemail I deleted". One clause, no more.

## Scene 3: Acceleration

1. Friday 9 October, 5:10 a.m., the flat, at the desk lamp (it ticks as it warms). She unlocks the drawer and takes out Lesson Two, her master: *Excellent.* in red beside the subject line, *Tessa Quinn* in black beneath it, ruled through once (per ch09 and ch10). She does not reread the Evaluation. She does arithmetic.
2. The original plan had the Architect in the third week of October, close enough to the election that the campaign could not absorb him: you can bury a Chief of Staff in September with a statement at 5:58, but nobody can bury the man who writes the statements a fortnight from the vote. She tells the reader the plan was good, that she liked it for two years, and that she gave it up in the time it takes a kettle to boil.
3. Her reasons, given as strategy, in order, crisp and correct. Counsel can stall, and stall well, but paper wins in the end (she works for it). A man Holt has cut loose has nothing left in the building and a reason to walk into a federal office and trade what he knows for himself, and what Brandt knows is her name. Brandt walled off is Brandt with a reason to talk to E. Reyes. Third, which is the second again: inside the week. She gives the logic full marks. The real reason sits under the stated ones and the reader hears it: while squaring the page to its own edge she thinks, for no reason she puts into words, of a letter with an initial at the foot of it reaching him before she does. She does not say more. She puts the page back and locks the drawer.
4. At half past nine, at work, she books Wednesday 14 October off through the leave system, one line. Her reasoning: a day booked five days ahead is wallpaper in a personnel file; a sick call on the morning itself is the line an investigator underlines. She leaves the optional *Reason for absence* box blank ("On my side of the building nobody gets to"). She reports it as administration.
5. The admission to the reader, plainly, in the manner of Ch 1's "I tell you the facts about lunch": she ate less that week, and nothing is also a menu. Thursday: a black coffee made at seven and left to go cold beside the tablet, no lunch, and in the evening one egg, boiled past soft to chalk, eaten out of the cup with a teaspoon at the counter, and half a slice of toast, the other half left on the board. Friday so far: water from the kitchen tap at five, stood in the pipes all night, flat and mineral.
6. She gives the body's version in its own notation, without explaining it: appetite reduced, onset Thursday; no nausea; no pain; afebrile; weight not taken; patient reports her mood as good; noted, unexplained, not discarded. The body knows something the plan does not, and she doesn't ask it what. Do not let her name what it is (Ch 15's "you don't eat before" is where the reader's recognition gets confirmed).
7. Before the case, Mara and Iris wash up side by side, a basin apart. Mara tells her about her lunch, an egg-and-cress sandwich she has had in the fridge since eight and plainly means to eat the minute they are done, and offers half across the sink. Iris pictures it by one o'clock (white bread sweating in clingfilm, the egg's sulphur through the mayonnaise, wet peppery cress). Iris says "No, thank you, Mara." in four words. Mara looks at her for about as long as it takes to read a label twice, then goes back to the soap. Iris does not grade her ("I'm telling you what she did. I'm not telling you what it was worth"), and the reader should notice it: everyone else in the chapter has been marked.
8. The case, at a quarter past eleven: a man of seventy-one, found in his armchair with the radio on, there about two days; his room comes up out of his clothes when the bag is unzipped. The extraction fans run at the pitch of an aeroplane cabin at altitude and become the air, so she doesn't hear herself at first.
9. Iris takes the heart out (heavy, firm, four degrees, the left ventricle thick as the wall of a cistern) and the scale says 480 g: blood pressure, years of it, unremarked, and then an armchair. She feels a vibration behind her sternum before she hears it, and realises she is humming. It is the Mingus she has been hearing all week through the unit on the top shelf of Brandt's record wall, side one, every night since the page, from the chair by the piano, and that she first heard in September through laptop speakers (ch08) and turned off. She has never hummed at work, not once in fifteen years. She doesn't know how long she has been doing it. The fans have been keeping it for her.
10. **[TURN]** Mara has leaned in over the scale to read the weight; her pen stops on the form and she looks up: "What is that?" Iris tells her. "Mingus. *The Black Saint and the Sinner Lady.*" Title and artist in full, given freely. The scene ends on her telling. No narration of regret, and nothing pointing at Ch 17's suite. The reader should feel the slip as a danger to her: she has just given the name of the record to the woman who wrote "practised". Iris does not feel it.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound. In the flat, the desk lamp's tick and the drawer runners. In the suite, the extraction fans and, under them, her own humming, which she hears in her chest before her ears. Taste: water at 5 a.m., the flat mineral taste of a tap that has stood all night, and the absence of anything else, reported once. Smell: the decedent's room still on his clothes: dust cooked onto the back of a radio, the sourness of a chair that has held one man's shape for years, a close sweet unaired smell like the inside of a biscuit tin. Texture: the heart in her gloved hand, firm and heavy and cold at four degrees, the opposite of what Ch 17 will put in her bare hand. Do not point it out.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "I promised to tell you everything I ate. On Thursday I ate an egg. I'm telling you about the egg so you'll understand I'm being thorough, and so you'll notice the rest of the column."
  - On moving the date: "Nobody revises a syllabus in the middle of term unless the examiner is coming early. I told myself that was the reason, and gave myself full marks for the logic." (Held in reserve, on the decedent: "He died with the radio on. Somebody had been playing to him for two days, which is more than most of my patients get.")
- **Body:** Not eating, counted out loud. Hands steady with the heart and the scale. She hums without deciding to, so the body is ahead of the person, which is the beat's whole point. The wound has been quiet today and is not touched in this scene. Keep it out so S2's touch lands as the chapter's thinking beat. Warm in the suite despite the cold room.
- **Motifs:** Vinyl (Mingus heard, never owned, now coming out of her own mouth). The meal (its absence counted, against the meal after). The grade (she grades her own logic, grades no one in the suite, and does not grade Mara). Hands. Tape: surgical tape on the specimen labels, one passing mention at most.

## Snapper
