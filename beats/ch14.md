# Beat sheet: ch14, The Architect's Panic

- **Unit:** ch14 (`manuscript/ch14.md`). Act II, The Practicum.
- **POV:** Iris Vale, first person, past tense. The outline's Turns are in present tense, so render them in past tense on the page.
- **Dates:** S1 and S2 Thursday 8 October; S3 and the Snapper Friday 9 October. Four days before the suite night (Tue 13 Oct) and five before Ch 17's Wednesday (14 Oct).
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Three scenes of ~1,050 each. No syllabus insert at the chapter head (inserts are Ch 1, 6 and 17 only).
- **Act pressure:** Brandt, in the last week before Ch 17, and here he is a pressure because he is coming apart. A man the campaign has walled off has nothing left to lose, and he holds the one thing she can't control: the list. A new pressure arrives in S2 and stays for the rest of the book: Reyes, as paper. Appetite runs under both. It is named and indulged in Act II, and in this chapter it shows up as the opposite of eating.
- **Appetite at entry:** Appetite as identity, mid-flirtation. In Ch 13 she stood in his kitchen in the dark eating his food, touched one key of his piano, and sat in her car listening to a whole side of a record with him three miles away. She is warm (Act II). She has stopped pretending the gifts were tactics. She wants the next meeting and knows it.
- **Appetite at exit:** Sharpened and jealous, though she never uses that word. She moves Brandt up the timetable for reasons she gives as strategy (S3 beat 3). The reader hears the real reason under them: she does not want anyone else to get to him first, whether Holt, Reyes or the paper. She is eating less and tells the reader so. The body is already on the "you don't eat before" footing of Ch 15, and the reader should recognise it before she does. Still warm. **Do not give her the Act I cold at the Reyes letter.** The cold returns from Ch 18. Here the menace is registered in appetite (the lunch she doesn't eat) and in hands (the wound), not in temperature. Never name the state as empty, hollow or numb (style 11); show it by the pocket she checks, the plate, the toast.
- **Knowledge constraints:**
  - **Iris must not hear Holt say "I thought Diane closed those."** In the Ch 17 anchor she asks Brandt "What did he say?" and learns it from his mouth, so the Holt and Brandt conversation (Wed 7 Oct, 9:14 p.m., in a small room off the War Room per Interlude 3's beats) happens off the feed, out of the lens's reach. She sees the two men leave the glass room together and come back separately. She sees the consequence the next morning. She never hears the words or the change of subject ("the numbers in the suburbs").
  - **Do not pre-empt the anchor's image.** In Ch 17 Brandt says "Your furniture gets moved, a little at a time." Iris's narration of Holt cutting Brandt loose must use a different figure: a known clinical course, read in stages (see S1). No furniture, no chairs being taken away as a metaphor. A literal chair is allowed once (beat 5).
  - **Do not reuse the anchors' set images.** "Louder than God" (extraction fans), "putty" (Ch 1 and Ch 17 both use it for a colour), "tallow", the "ironing board" amplifier smell and "like a mouth that has decided to" belong to the anchors. Find other figures.
  - Iris still believes she is the only woman the campaign silenced (the ledger puts the eleven names in Ch 16). The FBI request covers all agreements under matter LC-0311. She thinks of the other agreements under that matter as staff, vendor and donor paperwork. She does not imagine other signatories like her. She checks that LC-0311 is the matter on her own NDA's footer, and that is all. Individual control numbers are not in the letter and not on her pages.
  - Interlude 3 content (the drinking, "nine years", his private reasoning) is not hers. She can report only what the feed shows. Safest: leave the 11:40 a.m. drink out and let the reader hold it.
  - Her access, as established upstream: the War Room feed (picture and sound, from Ch 1/8, which can't resolve text on a screen, per ch09); the passive tap in Brandt's server farm (Ch 11; full capture on its card, a thin encrypted summary relayed over the building's uplink); and the cameras in Brandt's house (Ch 13: record wall, and the wardrobe facing the bed).
  - Reyes is "E. Reyes" to Iris. She does not learn the first name in this chapter or any other.
  - The Thursday 8 Oct rally (convention centre) is the source of "footage one" that she studies in Ch 19. She does not watch it, or look for a minute alone, in this chapter. The "sixty seconds" comes to her from Brandt in Ch 17.
- **Distinction for the drafter:** the S2 Turn ("for the first time something is happening that I did not write") must not read as a repeat of Ch 12's "the first thing in the book that isn't on the syllabus". Ch 12 was something she did off the syllabus. This is something done to her by a person who has no page. If needed, Iris can make the distinction herself in half a sentence: one was a thing she did, the other a thing she is in.

## Scene 1: The Fracture

1. Thursday 8 October, 7:20 a.m., the flat. She is not due on the table until ten. The tablet leans against the fruit bowl with the War Room feed live (as it did for the debate in Ch 1), and a black coffee she isn't drinking sits beside it. She has watched three days of the wobble, and she gives the reader the case history in brisk clinical order: on Monday he accused Theo Akers, the junior digital staffer, of leaking, in front of the room, and the boy went white to the hairline. He has stopped sitting at the table. He brings coffee to his place and doesn't drink it (echoing ch09's undrunk urn coffee).
2. The room has noticed before anyone has said anything. Iris reads the staff as a body: they angle away from him the way a ward angles away from a bed whose cultures have come back, making room without admitting why. Nobody stands within an arm's length of him.
3. Diane would have managed this. She would have walked him into a side room, paused once in the way that staff mistook for thoughtfulness, and fixed it before lunch. Diane is not available. Iris knows the exact reason, and the time (10:47).
4. The evening before, at 9:14 p.m. by the timestamp, Holt put a hand on Brandt's shoulder and the two of them left the glass room together, out of the lens. She could not hear it. It is the one conversation of the week she wanted, and the camera got the empty doorway. Holt came back alone four minutes later and was charming about something on the wall screen. Brandt came back nearly two minutes after him and did not sit.
5. She has read the file. Holt's dossier in her drawer has a section on men he has cut loose (ch09), and she narrates what follows as a disease with a known course, the stages in order: first the meetings stop including you, then the doors, then the systems, and nobody ever says the word. At 7:42 a.m. the morning huddle starts without him. His seat at the end of the table goes to the deputy communications director, and Holt touches the deputy's shoulder on the way past. That is the only literal chair in the scene.
6. At 7:51 a.m. Brandt arrives outside the glass in charcoal, holds his card to the reader, gets the red light, and tries again. Holt does not look up, and Iris notes that it takes real skill not to look up through glass. An IT staffer lets him in with a smile that is sorry about something.
7. He goes to the laptop at the end of the table and logs in. She can't read the screen at that resolution, so she reads his hands, as in ch09. The password goes in once, twice, and a third time slower, a key at a time, the way you type when you've begun to suspect it isn't the keyboard.
8. **[TURN]** His hands stop on the trackpad. He looks up through the glass at Holt, and Holt is laughing at something the deputy said. She watches the walls go up around him in real time, the whole procedure she had only read about, performed correctly and by the book. She feels something she refuses to call sympathy. She files it as the feeling of watching a colleague receive the diagnosis you would have given, and she does not let the sentence go any further. (Holt walls him off by Holt's order; Iris never sees the order, only its effects.)

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first: the feed's compressed audio and the door reader's two-tone refusal, thinned by the speaker into something like a microwave finishing. Then the HVAC hum of the room, the clatter of the huddle, and his silence inside it. Temperature: the coffee going cold at her elbow, and her own warmth, which is Act II and stays put. Light: the tablet's blue in a morning kitchen, the glass room's fluorescents, and the flat white of an overcast morning behind the glass (the sun is already up at 7:20; no sodium, no dawn).
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Nobody in that building had the authority to fire Cam Brandt, and so nobody did. They simply stopped letting him in, which is how hospitals handle a death they would rather not certify."
  - On the password: "A man types his password a third time the way the bereaved ring a number they know is disconnected. Not to get through. To hear it not answer."
  - (Held in reserve, use only if the scene wants a third: on Holt not looking up, "It takes years of practice not to look up through glass. I've seen consultants manage it at a nurse's station. He did it with his whole face.")
- **Body:** Cold coffee untouched. This is the first missed intake of the chapter, and it goes unremarked here and is counted in S3. Her hands are flat on the counter either side of the tablet, the way you rest your hands on a table during a case conference. Once, at the Turn, her left hand goes to the wound on the flank (six days old, nylon still in, itching under her shirt) and stays there.
- **Motifs:** The grade (she grades Holt's procedure as competent, textbook, and grades the room's body language as a diagnosis). Hands (Brandt's on the trackpad, hers on the counter, the wound). The coffee rhymes with the ch09 undrunk coffee. Keep it unpointed.

## Scene 2: The Name

1. Thursday, 1:10 p.m., the ME's car park, her car, engine off. It is lunch, and she has brought none and does not go to the canteen. She opens the tap's relayed summary from the server farm on her phone, as she has twice a day since Ch 11.
2. The joke the scene is built on: Holt has walled Brandt off from the campaign's front door, but the back door Brandt built himself is still running. His off-book farm still carries the campaign's own traffic, including counsel's inbox. Holt cannot close a door he has never officially been told exists. Brandt is out. Iris, through his plumbing, is still in.
3. In counsel's inbox there is a letter on FBI letterhead, received Wednesday 7 October and forwarded to Holt's office and not to Brandt (S1's walling, now in paper). It requests all agreements under matter LC-0311, with signatory records, "in connection with the death of Diane Pell". It is signed Special Agent E. Reyes.
4. Counsel's reply, drafted and already sent, is a masterpiece of the form: the campaign is "reviewing the scope of the request and attendant questions of privilege and will respond in due course". The internal note to Holt's office is one line: hold, nothing goes before the third. Iris grades counsel generously. Stalling is the one bodily function a law firm never loses, even on the table.
5. She checks the matter number. She knows LC-0311 by heart, the way she knows a voicemail's timestamp (ch10): it sits under a thin rule in the footer of every page of her NDA. She left those footers in Diane's mouth to point at the people who keep the index (ch07). She had pictured them embarrassing someone. She had not pictured anyone following them, and someone has.
6. She searches the name the way she would pull a chart: name, rank, office. There is nothing she can use. The Bureau does not publish its people, and an initial and a surname turn up a hundred strangers. She has a dossier on everyone who matters in this book, kept in a locked drawer, and this person has no file.
7. Back on the War Room feed, which she opens on the phone out of habit, it is 1:40 p.m. Counsel goes into the glass room and speaks to Holt with head inclined, and Holt nods twice. Brandt is at the far end of the table, ten feet away, and nobody calls him over. He may not know. For the first time this week she knows something about his campaign before he does, and she doesn't enjoy it as much as she would have expected.
8. Her hand goes to the wound under her shirt, and she is thinking, which is how the reader knows. The car is warm in the midday sun. She notes the warmth, and notes that it doesn't change.
9. **[TURN]** She reads the request a second time, the whole letter, signature to letterhead. There is no page for Reyes. For the first time something is happening that she did not write.

- **Sensory lead:** Texture and temperature: the car seat hot through her scrubs, the phone warm in her palm. Smell: the morgue on her skin inside a closed car, which is formalin and the unscented soap that doesn't remove it, and which in the sun is stronger than usual. Sound: the car's small ticks as it cools, the feed's audio thin through the phone speaker, and the shut-in quiet of a parked car. Light: midday through the windscreen, the phone's white glare. Taste: none. She has no lunch, and the drafter should let the absence show by the pocket she checks and the canteen she doesn't walk to, not by naming it.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Holt had locked Cam Brandt out of the house. He had not locked out the plumbing, because officially there was no plumbing, and so counsel's correspondence came to me the way everything in that building eventually comes to a pathologist: through the drains."
  - "I had a file on everyone. I had a file on the boy in digital. Reyes had a letterhead and an initial, and an initial is not a person. It is what you put on the toe tag before the family arrives." (Held in reserve: "'Will respond in due course.' I have seen that phrase on a cause-of-death enquiry from an insurer. Both parties understood it to mean after the body was cold.")
- **Body:** No lunch, and that goes unnamed in this scene. Hand to the wound under her shirt (left flank, healing, itching), held there. Her thumb stops on the phone screen over the signature, a callback to her thumb on the trackpad in ch08 and Brandt's thumb stopped on the glass in Interlude 2 (the reader's rhyme, never Iris's). Do not point it out. Still warm. No cold.
- **Motifs:** The paperwork thread (strategy 09, the matter number coming home). The grade (counsel graded; Reyes cannot be graded, and that is the menace). Hands (the wound, the thumb). Voicemail, sideways: she knows LC-0311 "the way I know a timestamp". One clause, no more.

## Scene 3: Acceleration

1. Friday 9 October, 5:10 a.m., the flat, at the desk lamp. She unlocks the drawer and takes out the Lesson Two page (her master, with *Excellent.* and Tessa's struck name in the margin, per ch09 and ch10). She does not reread the Evaluation. She does arithmetic.
2. The original plan had the Architect in the third week of October, close enough to the election that the campaign could not absorb him. She tells the reader the plan was good and that she is abandoning it.
3. Her reasons, given as strategy, crisp and correct. Counsel can stall, but paper wins in the end. And a man Holt has cut loose is a man who might walk into a federal office and trade a list for himself. Brandt walled off is Brandt with a reason to talk to E. Reyes. The real reason sits under the stated ones and the reader hears it: she will not have him taken from her by a letterhead. She does not say it. She moves him "inside the week" and puts the page back squared to its own edge.
4. Later that morning, at work, she books Wednesday 14 October off through the leave system, one line, no reason given. She reports it as administration.
5. The admission to the reader, plainly, in the manner of Ch 1's "I tell you the facts about lunch": she ate less this chapter, and she is telling you because she promised to tell you everything she put in her mouth, and nothing is also a menu. Thursday: a black coffee left to go cold, no lunch, and in the evening one boiled egg and half a slice of toast, the other half left on the board. Friday so far: water.
6. She gives the body's version without explaining it. The body knows something the plan does not. She reports it the way she would report a finding that doesn't fit the history: noted, unexplained, not discarded. Do not let her name what it is (Ch 15's "you don't eat before" is where the reader's recognition gets confirmed).
7. Friday, late morning (about 11:15), the autopsy suite. A routine case with Mara across the table: a man of seventy-one found in his armchair with the radio still on, cardiac by the look of the coronaries. Iris is taking the heart out. The extraction fans drown small sounds, so she doesn't hear herself at first (find a fresh figure for the fans; the anchor's is spent).
8. Mara's lunch comes up before the case does, as the two of them scrub: an egg-and-cress sandwich she has had in the fridge since eight and plainly means to eat the minute they are done, half offered across the sink. Iris says "No, thank you, Mara." in four words. Mara looks at her a beat longer than a junior should. Iris does not grade her, aloud or in narration, and the drafter should make the reader notice it: everyone else in the chapter has been marked.
9. Iris is weighing the heart (about 480 g, enlarged, which is this man's history in one figure) and realises she is humming. It is the Mingus she has been hearing all week through the bedroom feed at Brandt's house, where he has played side one every night since the page, and that she first heard thinly through a laptop in ch08. She has never hummed at work. She doesn't know how long she has been doing it.
10. **[TURN]** Mara, over the scale: "What is that?" Iris tells her. "Mingus. *The Black Saint and the Sinner Lady.*" Title and artist in full, given freely. The scene ends on her telling. No narration of regret, and nothing pointing at Ch 17's suite. The reader should feel the slip as a danger to her: she has just given the name of the record to the woman who wrote "practised". Iris does not feel it.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound. In the flat, the desk lamp's tick and the drawer runners. In the suite, the extraction fans and, under them, her own humming, which she hears in her chest before her ears. Taste: water at 5 a.m., the flat mineral taste of a tap that has stood all night, and the absence of anything else, reported once. Smell: formalin and the decedent's room still on his clothes, of radio dust and an old armchair. Texture: the heart in her gloved hand, firm and heavy and cold at four degrees, the opposite of what Ch 17 will put in her bare hand. Do not point it out.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "I promised to tell you everything I ate. On Thursday I ate an egg. I'm telling you about the egg so you'll understand I'm being thorough, and so you'll notice the rest of the column."
  - On moving the date: "Nobody revises a syllabus in the middle of term unless the examiner is coming early. I told myself that was the reason, and gave myself full marks for the logic." (Held in reserve, on the decedent: "He died with the radio on. Somebody had been playing to him for two days, which is more than most of my patients get.")
- **Body:** Not eating, counted out loud. Hands steady with the heart and the scale. She hums without deciding to, so the body is ahead of the person, which is the beat's whole point. The wound has been quiet today and is not touched in this scene. Keep it out so S2's touch lands as the chapter's thinking beat. Warm in the suite despite the cold room.
- **Motifs:** Vinyl (Mingus heard, never owned, now coming out of her own mouth). The meal (its absence counted, against the meal after). The grade (she grades her own logic, grades no one in the suite, and does not grade Mara). Hands. Tape: surgical tape on the specimen labels, one passing mention at most.

## Snapper

Mara, that night, texted me the album. I didn't reply. I played it.

Sharpened, for the drafter to take or leave: the phone face-up on the kitchen counter beside the bread board and its half slice of Thursday's toast, gone stiff at the edges, playing the album through its small speaker. When it reached the place where a needle would have lifted at the end of side one, nothing lifted, because a phone has no sides, so she stopped it there herself. End on her stopping it, or on "I played it". This keeps side two unplayed for Ch 17 ("side two never played") and quietly answers the Ch 13 Snapper (the needle lifting in two silent rooms). She owns no record. Do not explain it. No reply to Mara, and do not call it a voicemail. It is an unreturned message that rhymes with that motif without joining the three.
