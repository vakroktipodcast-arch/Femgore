# Beat sheet: ch07, The Cleanup

- **Unit:** ch07 (`manuscript/ch07.md`)
- **POV:** Iris Vale, first person, past tense. The outline's Turns and Snapper are in the present tense. The prose puts them in the past tense, keeping their content and order.
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Three scenes of ~1,050 each.
- **Act pressure:** Act I, The Prerequisite. The pressure is the campaign's certainty that nothing is coming. Brandt only arrives as a pressure in Ch 8, so here it is the system's machinery, still confident. Scene 1: the control numbers she trusts to point "up" at counsel. Scene 3: the campaign statement already out before six, and the Chief already managing the optics by choosing "a face that's never been near politics". The fifth pressure, appetite, carries Scene 2. The reader should stop believing her about physiology in this chapter (style 04: "the reader stops believing her around Chapter 7").
- **Appetite at entry:** appetite as competence, just fed for the first time and not yet fed in the body. The ch06 Snapper is "I am ravenous." She has killed, it felt good, and she has filed the feeling under "the grade".
- **Appetite at exit:** still present and still unnamed, but now on the record. The reader has three pieces of evidence against her account. She bought the meal on Saturday, before the kill. She explains it as adrenaline while her hand shows no adrenaline tremor. She is saving the War Room feed "the way you save the last bite". She reports all three straight and connects none of them. At the end she is dressed an hour early for a body she made. That is appetite as competence in its last fully deniable form.
- **Grading rule:** she grades her own egress, the campaign's statement and the meal. She does **not** grade Mara, out loud or in narration, even when Mara's text practically asks for it ("Hope that's ok??").

## Scene 1: The Egress

1. The first line puts her inside the task. Diane is dead, and Iris is working the exit checklist she wrote in her head two years ago. She walks through it out loud to the reader, item by item, in the syllabus voice. The trouble is on the first page: she is in a pump station with a body, and the trail outside is a public one.
2. **What she removes.** She peels the industrial tape from Diane's mouth slowly, the way you take a dressing off a graft, so the packed paper stays in place and shows. She cuts the zip-ties and pockets them, because they are hers and they have a lot number. She takes the planted phone (ch05), battery out. She does not consider adhesive (see Conflicts).
3. **Diane's own phone.** She takes it out of the foil sleeve (the sleeve is kit, so it goes with her) and leaves it powered off in Diane's coat pocket. Her reasoning: a missing phone writes a robbery, and she will not have anyone else's story on top of hers. Olivia's unread text is still on it. Iris knows and doesn't look.
4. **What she leaves, and why.** The black vinyl over the eyes. The page pinned over the sternum, GATEKEEPER circled. The shredded NDAs in the mouth, gone soft and grey at the edges. She explains control numbers to the reader: legal's numbering in the footer of every agreement, an index that points at the people who keep the index. She is pleased with this. It is the campaign's own filing, aimed back at it.
5. Hindsight, one sentence at most (see Conflicts). Numbers point both ways. She had thought about who sat at one end and not about who sat at the other. Then straight back to the present of the scene, with no lingering.
6. **Her own trace.** Coverall, hair cover, two pairs of gloves, overshoes. Off in reverse order of putting them on, outer gloves last but one, everything into a bag inside a bag. Then a final sweep with the torch held at floor level, because oblique light finds what overhead light forgives. She has watched scene examiners do this for a decade and has opinions about how most of them hold the torch.
7. She looks at Diane once more, from the door, as she would check a body laid out for a family viewing. She grades her own presentation. She listens for a moment longer than she needs to and hears nothing (a faint echo of the ch06 stutter Turn, which isn't named). She notes the lingering as a time cost: forty seconds over schedule.
8. Out, dressed as a Sunday walker in fleece, trainers and a daypack with the bagged kit in it. The door is shut behind her. It's the same door as the three claps in ch04, and the trail can't hear anything through it. The trail in late-morning September is empty in both directions, as it has been after nine on three Sundays of counting. It is about 900 m of trail to the road where her car is parked in a lay-by, not at the trailhead lot where Diane's car is.
9. She walks at the pace of someone with nowhere to be. That is a skill and she is good at it, and she tells you so.
10. **[TURN]** As she reaches the road, a cyclist's bell rings close behind her on the trail. A rider passes her on the left in a flash of hi-vis and a slipstream of air, and is gone up the road without looking back. She didn't turn her head in time to see a face, and the rider didn't see hers. The rider was coming from the direction of the pump station. She does not know how long the bike was behind her. She was not alone out there as long as she believed.

- **Sensory lead:** smell. Inside: damp concrete, iron-stained water standing in the old sump, the bleach-and-starch smell of wet paper, and the urine of a body that has voided at the end, which she names without fuss. Outside: pine resin, reservoir water, cut bracken, the air cooler than the room. Sound: the industrial tape coming off in a long tearing note, the snip of the zip-tie cutters, gravel under trainers, and then the bell. Texture: the paper going soft as felt where it was wet. Temperature: sweat cooling inside the coverall, then the September morning on her neck.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - Locard's exchange principle, every contact leaves a trace, "which is also true of family Christmas, and which nobody has ever successfully cleaned up after either."
  - On control numbers: "A control number is a number that controls something. I should have asked what."
  - On the walk out: she had practised looking like a woman with nowhere to be, "which for a woman alone on a trail is a costume with very few pieces and none of them optional."
- **Body:** hands, gloved, doing fine motor work (peeling, snipping, the sleeve). Sweat, then the cool. Hunger present from the ch06 Snapper and reported once, flatly, as a fact about the schedule. Her pulse at the bell, which she counts.
- **Motifs:** tape (industrial tape off, black vinyl left on); hands; the grade (her own egress, her presentation); paperwork thread (control numbers, strategy 09); voicemail by near-proximity (Olivia's unread text on the dark phone, which she leaves).

## Scene 2: The Meal

1. Home just after eleven. Procedure first. The bag inside the bag goes where it goes. Disposal is not shown on the page and stays her procedure. Trainers into a bag. Clothes on the hottest wash.
2. Forty minutes in the shower, timed, hot as the boiler allows. She scrubs as for theatre: nails brushed, the backs of the hands, between the fingers, up to the elbow. The pump station's concrete smell won't leave her hair. The morgue never leaves her skin either, she notes, so this is just a new tenant.
3. No tears. She checks for them the way she'd check a decedent for a pulse: procedure, not hope. None. She reports it in one line and doesn't make it mean anything.
4. She lies on the bed in a towel, wet-haired, "for an hour", to let her heart rate come down. She wakes at 1:40 a.m. with the pillow damp and the flat dark. She slept through four o'clock. From the dossier, Diane calls her mother on Sundays at four. Iris states it once: somewhere a woman waited by a phone, and Iris was asleep. Then she leaves it.
5. Hunger woke her. That has never happened before and she notes it as new data: she was ravenous at the pump station door and slept through it, which is the first thing that day she can't account for.
6. She cooks properly, by the cooker-hood lamp only. The marrow bones and the onglet were bought on Saturday at the butcher's. She states that plainly and doesn't comment on what Saturday was the day before. Oven as hot as it goes for the bones, canoe-cut beef femur. The steak comes out of the fridge to lose its chill. Shallots. The heel of a sourdough loaf.
7. **Course one, ~2:40 a.m.** Roasted marrow on toast with flat-leaf parsley, lemon and flaky salt. A long, greedy sentence. Yellow marrow, adipocytes gone translucent and trembling, scooped out of the medullary cavity of a femur with the handle of a teaspoon. It is anatomy and appetite in the same clause.
8. **Course two, 3 a.m.** Onglet seared hard and left rare, shallots in the pan butter, the bread to mop. She eats all of it at the counter on the stool. (She sits after a kill, per style 05.) She tells the reader, with no change of tone, that onglet is the crura of the diaphragm, the muscle that does the breathing. She watched one stop that morning. She salted it.
9. The lecture. Fight or flight, adrenaline, glycogen depleted and asking to be replaced, cortisol, gluconeogenesis. It is all true and she's fluent and funny about it. Planted in the lecture: adrenaline produces a fine tremor of the hands, eight to twelve hertz. The reader does not believe her. Neither, quite, does she. She gives it one clause ("which is all true, and which I said to myself in the voice I use for families") and moves on.
10. **[TURN]** Her left hand lies on the counter beside the empty plate, fork in her right, and it is perfectly still. No tremor at all, not eight hertz, not anything. She notes it as she would note a finding, in the dictation voice: *left hand, at rest, no tremor observed.* She doesn't interpret it. The reader holds it up against beat 9.

- **Sensory lead:** taste, the chapter's first full meal. Marrow is warm salted butter with a mineral note like licking a spoon that has been in a stock. Parsley is green and bitter against it, lemon cuts through. The onglet's crust is salt and char with a faintly ferrous, liverish depth at the centre, the rare part the colour of oxblood. Then sound: fat spitting in the pan, the oven fan, the fridge, the 3 a.m. silence of the building. Temperature: the shower's heat, then the cooling flat, the oven's breath on her face.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "The literature is clear that the post-adrenergic body wants to replace its glycogen. The literature is silent on whether it wants bone marrow. I have read the literature."
  - On the shower: forty minutes "is how long a surgical scrub would take if the surgeon had nothing to operate on afterwards and simply wanted to be clean."
  - On onglet: the French call it the butcher's steak because the butcher kept it for himself. She lets that sit with no comment. (It echoes the title; don't explain it.)
- **Body:** eating as the scene's main action, seated, all of it. Hands: scrubbed, pruned from the water, then the left one dead still. Sleep: long and dreamless, the "well" half of her alternation. The full stomach, reported.
- **Motifs:** the meal after (its first appearance, and the template for 12, 17 and 27); hands; the grade (she grades the onglet's sear and her own lecture, and gives the lecture a lower mark than the steak).

## Scene 3: The Morning

1. 5 a.m. onwards. She washes up and dries the pan. The marrow bones go into the bin double-bagged out of a habit she notes is professional and very funny. (She does **not** mention what she is wearing. The Snapper holds it back.)
2. Tea: the small daily gesture, which survives from v3. Builder's strength, milk, standing at the window as the dawn comes up the colour of weak tea itself, with sodium streetlight going off block by block.
3. She knows the body's timeline without wanting to: lividity fixed by now; rigor at or near its height about twenty hours in; the jaw first. She narrates Diane's present condition as a pathologist would, with real pleasure in the accuracy. It is her own work, still going on without her.
4. Phone: a news alert at 5:58. The campaign statement ("Our hearts are with Olivia and the whole Pell family. We ask for privacy at this time."). She grades it: C-minus. It spells grief correctly. It mentions Olivia by name. This is where the act pressure shows itself: the machine, certain and fast.
5. She has not opened the War Room feed. She is saving it "the way you save the last bite", and says exactly that. (It opens in ch08 S3. This keeps ch08's scene intact.)
6. **Text from Mara, 6:04 a.m.:** "You up? Unexpected one in overnight. Woman, 50s, off the reservoir trail. Chief rang me at home." Iris knows whose body it is. She reads the text twice and admires the shorthand.
7. **Second text, 6:11 a.m.:** "He's given it to me. Says he wants 'a face that's never been near politics'. You're senior reviewer. Hope that's ok??" Iris notes that the Chief already knows it's political. The system has decided what kind of body this is before anyone has opened it. She doesn't grade Mara or the double question mark. Make the absence visible.
8. She types a reply, deletes it, types another, deletes it. Her thumb is steady.
9. **[TURN]** She wasn't asked. She will be in the room anyway, on the other side of the table, standing where Mara stood last week, watching someone else take apart something she built. (Let the ch01 S1 Turn, "like she's taking apart something she built", sound under this. Don't quote it.)

- **Sensory lead:** taste and temperature. The tea is strong, tannic and too hot, and the first mouthful goes down over a stomach still full and warm from 3 a.m. The cool of a window left open a hand's width. Sound: the phone's buzz on the counter, loud as a dropped instrument in the quiet; the first bus. Light by source: dawn, sodium going out, the blue of the phone.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - On rigor: "Rigor mortis is the body's last attempt to hold a position, and like most last attempts, it lasts about a day and a half and then lets go of everything at once."
  - On the campaign statement: "They had used her daughter's name in the second sentence. Somebody in that room was very good at their job, and I hoped it was someone I'd met."
  - On the Chief's phrase: a face that's never been near politics, "which in this city narrows it down to the dead and Mara, and the dead were busy."
- **Body:** a full stomach, warm hands around the mug, a steady thumb on the glass. Cold is present but slight: the window, the hour. She is rested for the first time in weeks and says so as a finding.
- **Motifs:** the grade (the statement graded, Mara conspicuously not); hands (the thumb); the meal after, by residue (the stomach, the bagged bones); tea (the daily gesture).
