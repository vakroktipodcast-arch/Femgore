## Scene 1: The Egress

1. The first line puts her inside the task. Diane is dead, and Iris is working the exit checklist she wrote in her head two years ago. She walks through it out loud to the reader, item by item, in the syllabus voice. The trouble is on the first page: she is in a pump station with a body, and the trail below the spur is a public one.
2. **What goes with her.** The Ch 6 bag already holds the industrial tape, the four zip-ties (wrists cut at the bracket, ankles) and the ankle tape; she checks it against the lot-number logic and closes it. Added to it: the planted phone from Ch 5, battery out (the battery in a separate pocket); the clear zip bag that held the paper; the red marker, and the glassine envelope with its cap, which belong to the same bag as everything else from this lesson. She does not consider the vinyl's adhesive (see Conflicts).
3. **Diane's own phone.** She takes it out of the foil sleeve (the sleeve is kit, so it goes with her) and puts it back, powered off, in the left inside pocket of Diane's jacket, under the waxed cotton. Her reasoning: a missing phone writes a robbery, and she will not have anyone else's story on top of hers. Olivia's text is still the last thing the screen showed. Iris knows and does not power it on.
4. **What she leaves, and why, in a paragraph, not a lecture.** Diane on the concrete floor, against the pump housing. The black vinyl over the eyes. The page pinned over the manubrium, GATEKEEPER circled. The shredded NDA in the mouth, and the few fragments on the jacket and the floor, gone soft and grey at the edges. A one-line callback to LC-0311 under the thin rule (already explained in Ch 6): the campaign's own filing, aimed back at it. She is pleased with this.
5. **Hindsight, one sentence at most** (see Conflicts), then straight back to the present of the scene with no lingering. Numbers point both ways. She had thought about who sat at one end and not about who sat at the other.
6. **Her own trace.** Coverall, hair cover, outer gloves, overshoes, off inside the door in reverse order of putting them on, trainers underneath checked on the soles, everything into a bag inside a bag. The inner nitrile pair stays on until the car. Then a final sweep with the blue-white lantern laid on its side on the floor, because oblique light finds what overhead light forgives. She has watched scene examiners do this for a decade and has opinions about how most of them hold the torch. The lantern goes out last, into the pack.
7. She looks at Diane once more from the door, as she would check a body laid out for a family viewing, and grades her own presentation. She listens a moment longer than she needs to and hears nothing (a faint echo of the ch06 stutter Turn, which is not named). She logs the lingering as a time cost: forty seconds over schedule. It is about 11:25.
8. Out, dressed as a Sunday walker in fleece, trainers and a daypack with the bagged kit in it, inner gloves under the fleece cuffs and her hands in the pockets. She draws the door to and snaps her padlock on the hasp, the key into the pack. (Proposed; see New facts.) It is the same door as the three claps in ch04, and the trail cannot hear anything through it. The light outside is overcast and flat after the lantern's blue, and her eyes need a moment. The trail is empty in both directions, as it was on the Sundays she watched. Down the sumac spur, then about 900 m along the water-authority service track to the gravel lay-by where her car sits out of sight, not the south car park where Diane's car is.
9. She walks at the pace of someone with nowhere to be. That is a skill, she is good at it, and she tells you so.
10. **[TURN]** As she reaches the lay-by, near the county road, a cyclist's bell rings close behind her on the track. A rider passes her on the left in a flash of hi-vis and a slipstream of air, and is gone down toward the road past the MARCHETTI lawn sign without looking back. She did not turn her head in time to see a face, and the rider did not see hers. The rider came from the direction of the pump station. She does not know how long the bike was behind her. She was not alone out there as long as she believed.

- **Sensory lead:** smell. Inside: damp concrete, the iron of the pipework, the warm toner smell of the wet paper (ch06 prose: "toner... faintly electrical"), and the urine of a body that has voided at the end, which she names without fuss. Outside: sumac, leaf litter, reservoir water, air cooler than the body heat she has been carrying but warmer than the eleven-degree room. Sound: the snip of nothing (the ties are already off), the crackle of the bag inside the bag, the padlock's click, gravel under trainers, then the bell. Texture: the paper gone soft as felt where it was wet. Temperature: sweat cooling inside the coverall, then flat overcast air on her neck. Light: the lantern's blue giving way to grey daylight.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - Locard's exchange principle, every contact leaves a trace, "which is also true of family Christmas, and which nobody has ever successfully cleaned up after either."
  - On the matter number (this is the hindsight sentence): "A matter number tells you which matter. It does not tell you whose. I should have asked."
  - On the walk out: she had practised looking like a woman with nowhere to be, "which for a woman alone on a trail is a costume with very few pieces and none of them optional."
- **Body:** hands, gloved, doing fine motor work (the sleeve, the bag, the hasp). Sweat, then the cooler air. Hunger carried from the ch06 Snapper and reported once, flatly, as a fact about the schedule. Pulse at the bell, which she counts but does not give as a number. Hands in the fleece pockets because of what is on them.
- **Motifs:** tape (industrial tape bagged and carried out, black vinyl left on); hands; the grade (her own egress, her presentation); paperwork thread (the matter number, strategy 09); voicemail by near-proximity (Olivia's text on the dark phone, which she leaves).

## Scene 2: The Meal

1. Home about 12:15 p.m. Procedure first. The bag inside the bag, with the Ch 6 marker, its envelope, the ties and tape, goes where it goes. How it is disposed of is not shown, and it is done before she showers. Trainers into a bag. Clothes on the hottest wash. The inner gloves go in last.
2. Forty minutes in the shower, timed, hot as the boiler allows, unscented soap. She scrubs as for theatre: nails brushed, the backs of the hands, between the fingers, up to the elbow. The pump station's concrete smell won't leave her hair. The morgue never leaves her skin either, she notes, so this is just a new tenant. The bathroom mirror fogs over and she does not wipe it (no mirror beat).
3. No tears. She checks for them the way she would check a decedent for a pulse: procedure, not hope. None. She reports it in one line and does not make it mean anything.
4. She lies on the bed in a towel, wet-haired, "for an hour", to let her heart rate come down. It is about 1:15 p.m. She wakes at 1:40 a.m. with the pillow damp and the flat dark. She slept through four o'clock. From the trade-magazine profile, Diane calls her mother on Sundays at four. Iris states it once: somewhere a woman waited by a phone, and Iris was asleep. Then she leaves it.
5. Hunger woke her. That has never happened before and she notes it as new data: she was ravenous at the pump station door and slept through it, which is the first thing that day she can't account for.
6. She cooks properly, by the cooker-hood lamp only. The marrow bones and the onglet were bought on Saturday at the butcher's. She states that plainly and does not comment on what Saturday was the day before. Oven as hot as it goes for the bones, canoe-cut beef femur. The steak comes out of the fridge to lose its chill. Shallots. The heel of a sourdough loaf. She cooks standing, as she plans standing.
7. **Course one, ~2:40 a.m.** Roasted marrow on toast with flat-leaf parsley, lemon and flaky salt. A long, greedy sentence. Yellow marrow, adipocytes gone translucent and trembling, scooped out of the medullary cavity of a femur with the handle of a teaspoon. It is anatomy and appetite in the same clause.
8. **Course two, 3 a.m.** Onglet seared hard and left rare, shallots in the pan butter, the bread to mop. She eats all of it seated on the stool at the counter (she sits after a kill, per style 05). She tells the reader, with no change of tone, that onglet is the crura of the diaphragm, the muscle that does the breathing. She had watched one stop the morning before. She salted it.
9. The lecture. Fight or flight, adrenaline, glycogen depleted and asking to be replaced, cortisol, gluconeogenesis. It is all true and she is fluent and funny about it. Planted in the lecture: adrenaline produces a fine tremor of the hands, eight to twelve hertz. The reader does not believe her. Neither, quite, does she. She gives it one clause ("which is all true, and which I said to myself in the voice I use for families") and moves on.
10. **[TURN]** Her left hand lies on the counter beside the empty plate, fork in her right, and it is perfectly still. No tremor at all, not eight hertz, not anything. She notes it as she would note a finding, in the dictation voice: *left hand, at rest, no tremor observed.* She does not interpret it. The reader holds it up against beat 9.

- **Sensory lead:** taste, the chapter's first full meal. Marrow is warm salted butter with a mineral note like licking a spoon that has been in a stock. Parsley is green and bitter against it, lemon cuts through. The onglet's crust is salt and char with a faintly ferrous, liverish depth at the centre, the rare part the colour of oxblood. Then sound: fat spitting in the pan, the oven fan, the fridge, the 3 a.m. silence of the building. Temperature: the shower's heat, then the cooling flat (heating off, September), the oven's breath on her face.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "The literature is clear that the post-adrenergic body wants to replace its glycogen. The literature is silent on whether it wants bone marrow. I have read the literature."
  - On the shower: forty minutes "is how long a surgical scrub would take if the surgeon had nothing to operate on afterwards and simply wanted to be clean."
  - On onglet: the French call it the butcher's steak because the butcher kept it for himself. She lets that sit with no comment. (It echoes the title; don't explain it.)
- **Body:** eating as the scene's main action, seated, all of it. Hands: scrubbed, pruned from the water, then the left one dead still. Sleep: long and dreamless, the "well" half of her alternation. The full stomach, reported.
- **Motifs:** the meal after (its first appearance, and the template for 12, 17 and 27); hands; the grade (she grades the onglet's sear and her own lecture, and gives the lecture a lower mark than the steak).

## Scene 3: The Morning

1. About 3:30 a.m. onward, compressed. She washes up and dries the pan. The marrow bones go into the pedal bin double-bagged out of a habit she notes is professional and very funny. The hours to five go by in one line (the dark window, the lamp off). She does **not** mention what she is wearing, her feet, a towel or a dressing gown. The Snapper holds it back.
2. Tea: the small daily gesture, which survives from v3. Builder's strength, milk, standing at the window. It is still full dark at five; the grey comes up slowly toward six and the sodium streetlights go off block by block.
3. She knows the body's timeline without wanting to: she was brought in overnight and lies at four degrees in the cold room; lividity fixed by now; rigor about eighteen hours in, at or just past its height, the jaw set round the bolus. She narrates Diane's present condition as a pathologist would, with real pleasure in the accuracy. It is her own work, still going on without her.
4. Phone: a news alert at 5:58. The campaign statement ("A senseless act. Our hearts are with Olivia and the whole Pell family. We ask for privacy at this time."). She grades it: C-minus. It spells grief correctly. It mentions Olivia by name. This is where the act pressure shows itself: the machine, certain and fast.
5. She has not opened the War Room feed. She is saving it "the way you save the last bite", and says exactly that. (It opens in ch08 S3. This keeps ch08's scene intact.)
6. **Text from Mara, 6:04 a.m.:** "You up? Unexpected one in overnight. Woman, 50s, off the reservoir trail. Chief rang me at home." Iris knows whose body it is. She reads the text twice, the second time for what it leaves out. (She does not admire it; she does not grade Mara.)
7. **Second text, 6:11 a.m.:** "He's given it to me. Says he wants 'a face that's never been near politics'. You're senior reviewer. Hope that's ok??" Iris notes that the Chief already knows it's political. The system has decided what kind of body this is before anyone has opened it. She does not grade Mara or the double question mark. Make the absence visible.
8. She types a reply, deletes it, types another, deletes it. Her thumb is steady.
9. **[TURN]** She wasn't asked. She will be in the room anyway, on the other side of the table, standing where Mara stood last week, watching someone else take apart something she built. (Let the ch01 S1 line "like she's taking apart something she built" sound under this. Don't quote it.)

- **Sensory lead:** taste and temperature. The tea is strong, tannic and too hot, and the first mouthful goes down over a stomach still full and warm from 3 a.m. The cool of a window left open a hand's width. Sound: the phone's buzz on the counter, loud as a dropped instrument in the quiet; the first bus. Light by source: the lamp, then the blue of the phone, then grey, with the sodium going out.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - On rigor: "Rigor mortis is the body's last attempt to hold a position, and like most last attempts, it lasts about a day and a half and then lets go of everything at once."
  - On the campaign statement: "They had used her daughter's name in the second sentence. Somebody in that room was very good at their job, and I hoped it was someone I'd met."
  - On the Chief's phrase: a face that's never been near politics, "which in this city narrows it down to the dead and Mara, and the dead were busy."
- **Body:** a full stomach, warm hands around the mug, a steady thumb on the glass. Cold is present but slight: the window, the hour. She is rested for the first time in weeks and says so as a finding.
- **Motifs:** the grade (the statement graded, Mara conspicuously not); hands (the thumb); the meal after, by residue (the stomach, the bagged bones); tea (the daily gesture).
