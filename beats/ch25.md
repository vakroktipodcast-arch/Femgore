# Beat sheet — ch25: The Final Lesson (Execution Three)

- **Unit:** ch25 (`manuscript/ch25.md`)
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense. The outline's Turn and Snapper lines are in present tense; render them in past tense ("It was over before the minute was. I checked for the taste. There wasn't one.").
- **Target words:** ~3,150. Suggested split, inverting 17: Scene 1 ~350 (three paragraphs, no more), Scene 2 ~1,300, Scene 3 ~1,500. The kill gets the least page time in the book; the aftermath and the door get the rest.
- **Act pressure:** Act III. Reyes closing on paper; the knowledge from 18 in every room. In 25 Reyes is felt through paper: the page Iris can't pin, the eleven names the prescribed lesson relied on, a body with no Evaluation. The knowledge from 18 is the page with her own name on it, face-down in the locked drawer at home under the third page's master, which she now carries a twin of.
- **Appetite at entry:** Starved. The one taste in five chapters was his flinch in 24, Scene 1, and his grief for Diane and his hand on hers took it back. She comes into the chapter checking for hunger the way you check a pocket (23 Snapper).
- **Appetite at exit:** Gone, and she doesn't name it. She checks for the taste and there isn't one. No meal is planned. Show the absence through the body (the mouth, the hands, the pocket), never with "numb", "empty" or "hollow" (style 11).
- **Register (sentence-level inverse of 17):** 17 was lush, slow, funny and anatomical, and smell came first. 25 is plain, fast, numb and lay-worded, and sight comes first. Smell arrives late or not at all. Short declaratives only, with no long greedy sentence anywhere in Scene 1. No Latin, no anatomy, no named colours in Scene 1, no jokes in Scene 1, no "beautiful", no syllabus voice and no grading anywhere (style 02: the syllabus voice is gone in 22–25). Dialogue stays minimal. No em-dash cut-offs (that licence belonged to Holt in 24 only).

## Scene 1 — The Kill

1. First sentence: his hand on mine. It is warm, and it is the hand of a man comforting someone. (Her hands are double-gloved, pale nitrile under black, so the warmth is felt through two layers; do not mention gloves on the page in 25.) It carries straight on from 24's Snapper, with no gap and no scene-setting.
2. He says, slowly (the agent is partly in him), "Take your time." He means it kindly.
3. I take my hand back. I stand. Nothing has been decided. Something arrives that is rage and has no heat in it, and I notice it isn't hunger.
4. Paragraph two is the act. My hands, his weight, the chair going over, the dressing table knocked. Plain words only: hands, neck, floor, weight. No procedure and no counting.
5. The dressing table rocks and the Marine's photograph goes off it onto the carpet, face-down. One clause, not lingered on. The reader should hardly register it until the Snapper.
6. He fights less than Diane did, because of the agent and because he doesn't understand what is happening. I report the absence of each thing I reported in 6 and 17, in short form: no sound I want to keep, no heat. I notice the nothing while I'm doing it, and that noticing is the horror.
7. The left flank pulls hard. It is the only thing in my body that reports anything (style 05: the wound is the only part of her that hurts in 25).
8. Paragraph three opens with one line on its own: he was supposed to live.
9. **[TURN]** It is over before the minute is. I check for the taste. There isn't one.

- **Sensory lead:** texture. His hand on mine, warm, then the carpet under my knee. Sight follows. Smell is withheld on purpose, the reverse of 17's smell-first rule.
- **Jokes / dry observations:** none. This is ruled out by style 04 ("no jokes"). The missing joke should be felt in the cut rhythm, not explained.
- **The body:** hands (his on mine, then mine doing it); the left-flank wound pulling; the mouth checked for taste and finding none; cold already in place since 18, not commented on.
- **Motifs:** hands; the Marine's photograph (falls, unremarked); the meal after (the taste check stands in for it); the grade (absent, so no Evaluation forms in her head).

## Scene 2 — The Aftermath

1. He is on the floor. Sight first and sight only. I don't kneel. It is the first body in fourteen hundred-odd that I don't examine.
2. Reflex starts without me: I begin estimating, the way I have done every time, and I stop partway. I don't note a time of death. I don't look at a clock. (Do not state a time on the page.)
3. Smell arrives late and isn't catalogued: the shampooed carpet from 22, and under it him, in one flat clause. In 17 smell was a paragraph. Here it is one clause.
4. The page is on the table, opened along its creases (24), beside the empty place where the photograph stood until it fell. I pick it up. I don't read it. Nothing on it is circled; nothing shows through. Never reproduce or quote the page (style 08).
5. The kit is in my pocket: the red marker and the pin. I try to pin it. I hold the page over his chest.
6. My hands won't. They aren't shaking. They just don't. I watch them the way I watched my left hand in the lift in 2, as if they belonged to someone on my table.
7. The black vinyl tape is in the kit too (the roll in the right outer pocket). I don't take it out. His eyes stay as they are and his face stays uncovered. Have her register this in one plain line, with no irony drawn: the lesson was exposure, and he is exposed, and it isn't the lesson.
8. I fold the page back along its creases, in thirds, and put it in the inside breast pocket of the navy blazer. My hand stays flat on the pocket a moment longer than it needs to.
9. Pressure line, kept short: the prescribed lesson needed him alive and the eleven names in an agent's hands. Now there is a body and no page, and someone will have to file that. I know who files things.
10. **[TURN]** The folded page is the first thing in two years I have not been able to make myself do, and I didn't want to anyway.

- **Sensory lead:** smell, arriving late (shampooed carpet, then him). It is the deliberate weak echo of 17.
- **Jokes / dry observations (thin, Act III ratio):**
  - "I have written a time of death for every body I have ever stood over. I did not look at the clock. It was right there on the wall."
  - "It was a single sheet of paper. I have folded more difficult things. Bodies, for one."
- **The body:** hands that won't pin (watched, not shaking); the palm flat on the inside pocket; standing, not kneeling; wound quiet now; no hunger, with the mouth not mentioned again.
- **Motifs:** hands (the lift in 2, echoed); tape (vinyl tape left in the kit, unused); the syllabus page (unpinned, refolded, never reproduced); the grade (no Evaluation written); the knowledge from 18 (her own page face-down in the drawer at home, mirrored by this one, folded. One line at most, implied rather than explained).

## Scene 3 — The Door

1. Sound comes through the main door: the corridor filling. Voices, a hotel radio, and the ballroom through the walls, warm-up music and a crowd being told to get ready.
2. The clock only I am counting: the forty seconds went long ago. Someone outside says the time. The minute becomes two.
3. A knock. His name: "Senator Marchetti." A pause. A second knock. "Senator. We're at time."
4. I don't move. I watch the handle. The privacy latch he threw holds it. The service door behind me has a torn seal sticker that someone will eventually see (22).
5. **[TURN]** The detail doesn't open the door. Standing instruction: not to be disturbed. The system protects him for one more minute, and I use it.
6. Using it: competence, exhausted, muscle memory (hero rule, so nothing unlocks itself). I cap the syringe holding the rest of the agent and pocket it in its hard case. I close the kit. I check the carpet and the table for anything that is mine.
7. One dry beat, the only real joke of the chapter, and it should land like a dropped tray: the Service is excellent at standing instructions, which is what they are for.
8. I go to the service door and the torn sticker. My hand on it, with no wish this time about whether it's steady (contrast with 21's Snapper). The knock comes again behind me, harder.
9. I look back once, from the service door.

- **Sensory lead:** sound. The corridor filling, the knock, his name said by someone who respects him, the ballroom through the wall.
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - "The Service is very good at standing instructions. It is what they're for." (Use this one.)
  - Reserve: "Somewhere below me a room full of people was being told to welcome a man who was not coming down." (The ballroom is on the same level as the green room; if used, change "below me" to "along the corridor" or "through the wall".)
- **The body:** cold felt as the hands and face, not named as a feeling (the room itself is hotel-warm); hands doing small competent tasks (capping, closing, checking); the palm returning once to the inside pocket; standing very still while the handle doesn't move.
- **Motifs:** the Marine's photograph (Snapper); tape (the torn sticker on the service door); hands; the minute; the meal after (absent, and not mentioned. Its absence carries into 26).

## Snapper

Outline minimum, in past tense: *The Marine's photograph face-down on the carpet where it fell. I didn't turn it over. I thought about it.*

Sharpened option for the drafter (author's call): *The Marine face-down on the carpet where he fell. I didn't turn him over. I thought about it.* For one line, the pronoun leaves it unclear whether this is the photograph or the man on the floor, which ties the brother and Holt together without saying so. If the doubling reads as too clever, keep the outline's line exactly. Either way it is the chapter's last sentence. The photograph must be face-down (ledger and motif 09), and she must not turn it.
