# Beat sheet — ch25: The Final Lesson (Execution Three)

- **Unit:** ch25 (`manuscript/ch25.md`; approved anchor prose, also `anchors/ch25.md`)
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense. The outline's Turn and Snapper lines are in present tense; render them in past tense ("It was over before the minute was. I checked for the taste. There wasn't one.").
- **Target words:** ~3,150. Suggested split, inverting 17: Scene 1 ~350 (three paragraphs, no more), Scene 2 ~1,300, Scene 3 ~1,500. The kill gets the least page time in the book; the aftermath and the door get the rest.
- **Act pressure:** Act III. Reyes closing on paper; the knowledge from 18 in every room. In 25 Reyes is felt through paper: the page Iris can't pin, the eleven names the prescribed lesson relied on, a body with no Evaluation. The knowledge from 18 is the page with her own name on it, at home under the third page's master (see Conflicts 4 for where the prose puts it).
- **Appetite at entry:** Starved. The one taste in five chapters was his flinch in 24, Scene 1, and his grief for Diane and his hand on hers took it back. She comes into the chapter checking for hunger the way you check a pocket (23 Snapper).
- **Appetite at exit:** Gone, and she doesn't name it. She checks for the taste and there isn't one (S1 Turn, then the tongue against the teeth in S2: "tasted of nothing at all"). No meal is planned. Show the absence through the body (the mouth, the hands, the pocket), never with "numb", "empty" or "hollow" (style 11).
- **Register (sentence-level inverse of 17):** 17 was lush, slow, funny and anatomical, and smell came first. 25 is plain, fast, numb and lay-worded, and sight comes first. Smell arrives late or not at all. Short declaratives only, with no long greedy sentence anywhere in Scene 1. No Latin, no anatomy, no named colours in Scene 1, no jokes in Scene 1, no "beautiful", no syllabus voice and no grading anywhere (style 02: the syllabus voice is gone in 22–25). Dialogue stays minimal. No em-dash cut-offs (that licence belonged to Holt in 24 only).
- **Drug rule (ledger, Objects, the syringe):** the agent is never named. No drug name, dose, class, route, injection site or pharmacology, in beats as in prose. The syringe is only "the syringe" / "the agent". What Iris may observe of Holt: limbs heavy and slow, speech slowed, conscious, crying. Observation only, never explanation.

## Scene 1 — The Kill

1. First sentence: his hand on mine, warm and dry, the hand of a man comforting someone. His thumb moves once across my knuckles, the way you would do it at a funeral. (Her hands are double-gloved; do not mention gloves on the page in 25.) It carries straight on from 24's Snapper, with no gap and no scene-setting.
2. What I see of him, observation only: the agent has reached his face and his eyes are slow to follow anything. He says, slowly, "Take your time." He means it kindly.
3. I take my hand back. I stand. I have not decided anything. Something comes up that is rage and has no heat in it. I wait for it to turn into the other thing. It doesn't. I notice that.
4. Paragraph two is the act. My hands on him; he is heavier than he looks; the chair goes over and we go with it. One knee on the floor, my hands on his neck. Plain words only: hands, neck, floor, weight. No procedure and no counting.
5. The dressing table rocks when his shoulder hits it and the Marine's photograph goes off the edge onto the carpet, one clause, not lingered on. The reader should hardly register it until the Snapper.
6. He fights little, because he is slowed and does not understand what is happening. He looks up at me as if I had asked him a question and he wanted very much to get it right. I report the absence of each thing I reported in 6 and 17, in short form: no sound I want to keep, no heat. I wait for the heat the way you wait for a stair in the dark that isn't there, and it isn't, and I go on. The noticing is the horror.
7. The left flank pulls hard, where the knife had been. It is the only thing my body tells me (style 05: the wound is the only part of her that hurts in 25).
8. Paragraph three opens with one line on its own: he was supposed to live. That was the lesson as written: kept alive, found, finished by eleven women with paper.
9. **[TURN]** He stops. I take my hands away. It was over before the minute was. I checked for the taste. There wasn't one.

- **Sensory lead:** texture. His hand on mine, warm and dry, then his weight, then the carpet under my knee. Sight follows. Smell is withheld on purpose, the reverse of 17's smell-first rule.
- **Jokes / dry observations:** none. This is ruled out by style 04 ("no jokes"). The missing joke should be felt in the cut rhythm, not explained.
- **The body:** hands (his on mine, then mine doing it); the left-flank wound pulling; the mouth checked for taste and finding none; cold already in place since 18, not commented on.
- **Motifs:** hands; the Marine's photograph (falls, unremarked); the meal after (the taste check stands in for it); the grade (absent, so no Evaluation forms in her head).

## Scene 2 — The Aftermath

1. "He was on the floor." Sight first and sight only: on his back between the chair and the dressing table, one arm under him, the other out across the carpet, palm up. Tie still knotted, flag pin on the lapel turned sideways, one shoe half off, left cuff open and the cufflink on the carpet by his hand. I look at the cufflink for a while, then at the rest of him. (Items touching his jacket and left wrist: see Conflicts 8.) Eyes half open, mouth a little open: a man who had been interrupted.
2. I don't kneel. Fourteen hundred bodies, give or take, on tile, gravel, a kitchen floor in August, a river bank; kneeling is how it starts. I stand with my hands at my sides. He is the first one I don't examine.
3. I take stock of myself instead, because somebody had to: knee sore from the floor, the side quiet again as soon as my hands came away, breathing slow, face dry, cold and has been for weeks, which is not news.
4. The reflex starts without me: "The body is that of," aloud, in the dictaphone voice, and it sounds like someone reading from a card. I don't finish. The rest of the list runs on in my head like a radio in another room, and I turn that off too. Then the one dry line: I have written a time of death for every body I have ever stood over; I did not look at the clock, right there on the wall. (No time stated on the page.)
5. Smell arrives late and is one flat clause: the carpet cleaner they used on the whole floor, and under it, him. Then the echo: he was the second man who had told me to take my time. The tongue against the back of the teeth: nothing; a mouth that tastes the way a glass does when it has just come out of the dishwasher. I check the outside jacket pocket the way you check for keys: marker, pin. I don't know what else I thought would be there. Then Olivia's name and the question "what do you want", which I still can't answer; it wasn't down there either (see Conflicts 10 for the order of his saying her name).
6. The page is on the table, where I put it down beside the square of bare polished wood where the photograph stood. I pick it up and don't read it. I typed it two years ago; the words have become a shape. I hold it wrong way round and see it from the back (Conflicts 2 for whether anything shows through). I take the pin out and stand over him with the page above his chest, where it goes.
7. My hands don't. "I want to be clear about this": they aren't shaking, they have been steady most of my life, and they hold the page flat a hand's width above his shirt and the pin and do not move down. I watch them the way I watched my left hand in the lift in 2, as if they belonged to someone on my table. After a while the forearms ache from holding still, and that is all.
8. The tape stays in the kit and I don't take it out. His face is turned a little toward the dressing table and I leave it. One plain line, no irony drawn: the lesson was that he would be seen; he was going to be seen now by everyone; it was not the lesson. At work we cover the face, for the family and for ourselves; I left his uncovered and didn't decide that either. The pin goes back in my pocket.
9. I fold the page (fold and jacket: see Conflicts 1 and 3) and put it in the inside breast pocket, left, against my ribs. My hand stays flat on the pocket longer than it needs to. At home there is a page with my name on it that I haven't pinned either (placement: Conflicts 4). Pressure line, kept short: the lesson as written needed him alive, a man who could be asked questions, eleven names in an agent's hands, a story that came out in the right order, paper first and body nowhere. Now a body on a hotel carpet and a hole where the reason should have been, and somebody will have to file that. I know who files things. A woman with a list of eleven names will go down it and get to the end of it (Conflicts 7); I think about that the way you think about weather in another city.
10. **[TURN]** The folded page was the first thing in two years I had not been able to make myself do, and I didn't want to anyway.

- **Sensory lead:** sight, then one clause of smell, arriving late (carpet cleaner, then him). It is the deliberate weak echo of 17. Then taste (nothing), and the ache in the forearms.
- **Jokes / dry observations (thin, Act III ratio):**
  - In the prose: "I have written a time of death for every body I have ever stood over. I did not look at the clock. It was right there on the wall."
  - In the prose: "It was a single sheet of paper, and I have folded harder things."
- **The body:** hands that won't pin (watched, not shaking; forearms aching from holding still); the palm flat on the inside pocket; standing, not kneeling; sore knee; wound quiet now; dry mouth that tastes of nothing; no hunger, and the mouth is not mentioned again after the check.
- **Motifs:** hands (the lift in 2, echoed); tape (vinyl tape left in the kit, unused); the syllabus page (unpinned, refolded, never reproduced); the grade (no Evaluation written); the knowledge from 18 (her own page at home, one line); the Marine's photograph (the empty square of wood); the dictaphone voice.

## Scene 3 — The Door

1. Sound comes through the main door: the corridor filling, footsteps, low quick voices, a radio close to the door. Further off, the ballroom: warm-up brass and drums, a man on a microphone telling a crowd it won't be long now. One line for the wife, in the shadow of the curtain "where they always put the wife", and I don't think about her for long. (Direction of the ballroom: Conflicts 9; either way, "along the corridor" or "through the wall" is the safe wording.)
2. The count only I am keeping: the time he was allowed went a long while ago, before he took my hand. In the corridor, not loudly: "We're over." "Give him a second." The minute becomes two. I can't hear myself breathe; the band comes round to the start of the same song and plays it again.
3. I stand still between him and the door. Behind me, across the room, the service door with the sticker across the seam where I tore it. Someone is going to see that sticker. Not yet. Brandt's line, "That's your door. It's the only one" (17), and I put it down. (Room temperature: Conflicts 5. Keep the cold to the backs of the hands and the neck.)
4. The knock: two knuckles, polite, someone who respects the man on the other side. "Senator Marchetti." I don't move; I watch the handle, a long brass lever, and it stays level. I have opened a great many doors other people did not want opened; I had never stood on this side of one.
5. A pause. The ballroom music goes louder; the man says a name; the crowd answers with a long rising noise like a wave over shingle. The second knock, firmer: "Senator. We're at time." Nobody tries the handle. Two or three of them shift their weight; one says "Standing instruction." Another: "How long's he been?" "Too long." "Your call." "It's not my call. It's his."
6. I know what they are thinking, because I have stood outside enough doors: a man who lost his brother, the one minute of his day that is his, nobody wanting to walk in on that. They are good at their jobs and they are doing them.
7. **[TURN]** The detail doesn't open the door. Standing instruction: he was not to be disturbed. The system protects him for one more minute, and I use it.
8. Using it: I don't hurry, because hurrying is how you leave things behind. I recover the syringe with the rest of the agent still in it (where it lies: Conflicts 6), cap it, pocket it, close and fasten the case. I go back over the room in strips, as at work: carpet first, then surfaces, the table where the page was, the chair arm, the edge of the dressing table where my hip hit it. I don't touch him again. Whatever of mine is on him stays there; I know exactly what it is. I check myself the same way: lanyard, hair put up by feel, jacket straightened (Conflicts 3). Down one floor, the closet, the other jacket, the dock, run once in order in my head, and I don't feel anything about that either. I touch the inside pocket on the way past; the page is there. One dry beat, landing like a dropped tray: "The Service is very good at standing instructions. It is what they are for."
9. I go to the service door and put my hand flat on it beside the torn sticker. I don't look to see if my hand is steady; it doesn't matter to me whether it is (contrast 21's Snapper). The stairwell beyond is colder and silent. The knock comes again behind me, harder, no longer polite; somebody says his first name, not the title. I look back once, from the service door: the chair on its side, him between chair and dressing table palm up, the cufflink, the table with nothing on it, the bare square of wood.

- **Sensory lead:** sound. The corridor filling, the knock, his name said by someone who respects him, the ballroom through the wall, the same song twice.
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - In the prose (use it): "The Service is very good at standing instructions. It is what they are for."
  - In the prose, kept lighter: "I have opened a great many doors that other people did not want to open. I had never stood on this side of one."
- **The body:** the backs of the hands and the neck feel moving air (this is the prose's cold; Conflicts 5); hands doing small competent tasks (capping, closing, checking, re-pinning hair); the palm returning to the inside pocket twice; standing very still while the handle doesn't move; breath not audible to herself.
- **Motifs:** the Marine's photograph (Snapper); tape (the torn sticker on the service door); hands; the minute; the meal after (absent, and not mentioned; its absence carries into 26); Brandt's "your door"; the page (touched, not read).
