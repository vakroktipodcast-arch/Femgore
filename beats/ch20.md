# Beat sheet — ch20: The Desperate Plan

- **Unit:** ch20 (`manuscript/ch20.md`)
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense. The outline's Turns and Snapper are in present tense, so render them in past tense on the page ("The seal was a sticker." / "I preferred the date.").
- **Target words:** ~3,150. Suggested split: S1 ~1,100 / S2 ~950 / S3 ~1,100. Scene 2 is the shortest because the letter itself is never reproduced. Only its omissions are.
- **Act pressure:** Act III. Reyes is closing on paper, and Iris can see the paper move: campaign counsel's email in S1 says the signatory list goes out Saturday, and she is one of eleven names on it. The knowledge from 18 is in every room: her own page is in the drawer she locks at the end, the letter is a message to someone whose voicemail she hasn't played, and the hole she exploits (Compliance is dead, so nobody checks) is a hole she made.
- **Appetite at entry:** Starved. Ch 19 ended with the fridge opened and closed, and the replacement in the mirror had lost weight. She has mapped the fortress three times and felt nothing. Paperwork.
- **Appetite at exit:** Starved, and now on purpose. "You don't eat before" is kept as a rule after the thing it was a rule *for* has gone. The one flicker in the chapter is the S1 Turn, where she recognises the sticker. It's the old click of a finding, there and gone, and she doesn't feed it. Show the absence through the body (the belt, the fridge, the coffee left to go cold, the pocket her hand keeps going to), never with "numb", "empty" or "hollow" (style 11).
- **Register:** Jokes thin on purpose (Act III ratio, style 02). Use about one per scene, dry and procedural, and let the reader feel there should be more. The syllabus voice can leak in S1, where she is still in control of a plan: she grades the bureaucracy, the dead audit, counsel's email. It must leak less in S3, and not at all at the mirror. No "beautiful", no "perfect", no "lucky". No em-dash cut-offs.
- **Calendar (proposed; see New facts):** S1 is Friday 30 October, evening. S2 is Saturday 31 October, night (Halloween). S3 runs from Sunday 1 November (the clocks go back overnight) to Monday 2 November, late afternoon, Election Eve. This follows the election on Tuesday 3 November (ch01 beats) and Election Eve on a Monday (ch25 beats).

The ch25 drafter should treat that as one fold more, or as folding it back along its creases, rather than a first fold.

## Scene 1 — Reconnaissance

1. Friday 30 October, night. The kitchen table. The hotel's floor plans, warm from her printer, are laid out edge to edge like films on a lightbox. Open on the task, first page: the weak point isn't the Secret Service, it's the bureaucracy. The Service guards the man. Nobody guards the paperwork about the people standing near him.
2. How she sees it. Brandt's off-book mirror of the campaign network is still syncing. Holt cut Brandt's logins in October, but nobody ever cut the tap, because nobody but Brandt knew it existed, and Brandt is dead. Brandt's access is hers now. She reaches it through the Lantern terminal she has walked into before (ch11: the cloned badge, the midline code). One clause, no re-run of the break-in.
3. The credentials. The campaign controls back-of-house press and volunteer credentials, printed on its own badge printer in the volunteer office from one template. The credential audit was Compliance's job. She opens the audit spreadsheet. The file header reads *Last modified by D. Pell*, dated the Friday before Diane died, and nobody has opened it since. Compliance has been dead two months.
4. The template sits in the mirror's copy of the volunteer drive. So does the roster. She finds a no-show: Ruth Calder, signed up in July, worked one phone bank, made eleven calls, never came back, still marked active, because marking people inactive was also Compliance. Iris changes nothing in the database. She only prints. The badge reads RUTH CALDER / VOLUNTEER / PRESS ASSISTANT, with her own photograph. It comes out of a secondhand card printer she paid cash for, onto the same white PVC stock from the supplier on the campaign's own invoice. The name is already on any list they print.
5. Pressure. In the same mirror is counsel's email to the War Room, sent that afternoon: the subpoena fight is lost, and the signatory list is to be produced Saturday. Eleven names. She does the only arithmetic there is and touches the wound on her left flank. She doesn't think about the order the names will be read in. She thinks about Monday.
6. The venue. The hotel files event floor plans for its ballroom level, and the mirror has the advance team's copy. Read it like a body: the public rooms in front, the plumbing behind. A service stair beside the green-room corridor. A housekeeping closet one floor down. A loading dock at street level on the cross street, with a vendor gate. She traces the route with the cap of the red marker, not the tip.
7. The minute, written down as a protocol. Sixty seconds, from the footage (ch19). The door closes, the detail turns its back, five seconds for him to sit down with the photograph. After that she writes nothing, because what comes after is the lesson and the lesson is already written. Keep the third page closed, unquoted and unread on the page (style 08).
8. The green room is the Alder Suite, a converted meeting suite. The Service's advance plan shows one entrance. The hotel's plan shows two: the corridor door, and a service door from the suite's pantry onto the back corridor, for banquet staff. She finds the door in the advance team's walk-through photographs and enlarges it to four hundred per cent. A white label runs across the gap between door and frame.
9. She knows the label before she can read it. It's the same make of tamper-evident seal she puts on evidence bags at work, the kind that leaves VOID in the paint when it's lifted. For one beat the old click comes, a finding, and then it's gone. She notes that it went.
10. **[TURN]** The green room was a converted meeting suite with a service door the Service had sealed but the hotel floor plan still showed. The seal was a sticker.

- **Sensory lead:** smell and temperature. Hot toner and paper warm from the printer, the only warm thing in the flat (cold since 18). Sound: the laptop fan, the card printer's ratchet as it feeds a badge.
- **Jokes / dry observations (thin):**
  - "Lantern Data Services was still paying its electricity bill. Nobody had told it that both its officers were dead."
  - "Ruth had made eleven calls and gone home. I liked her. She knew when to stop."
  - Reserve: "Hotels are built like people. A face for the guests, and behind it the plumbing. I have spent my whole career in the plumbing."
- **The body:** eating standing up when planning (style 05), except she doesn't. A heel of bread on the counter, one bite, left. Coffee poured and gone cold beside the plans. Hands steady on the trackpad. The left-flank wound touched once, at the counsel email. Cold forearms.
- **Motifs:** tape (the sticker, a sibling of the evidence-bag seal and the black vinyl); the minute; the grade (she grades the dead audit and counsel's email); the red marker (capped, used as a pointer).

## Scene 2 — The Letter

1. Saturday 31 October, night, at the writing desk. The only paper in the flat is the stock she printed the syllabus on. She notices this and uses it anyway.
2. The phone lies face-down beside the paper, holding Hannah's unheard voicemail (ch19). She doesn't play it. She is writing a reply to a message she hasn't listened to, and she says so.
3. Halloween. Children go up and down the building's corridor, knocking, and the knocks reach her door and pass on. There's nothing in the flat to give them. Optional, if the author wants Hannah in the room: Hannah's text from earlier, a photograph of Astrid and June in costume, June as a skeleton. Iris counts the printed ribs, finds the wrong number on one side, and the correction doesn't arrive as a joke.
4. The genre problem. She has taken hundreds of letters out of coat pockets on her table and knows what they all say, in what order. She grades the form in one dry line. Her letter is falling into it and she can't write it out again.
5. What she does put in, described and not quoted: the practical things. The spare key, the lease, the bank. An instruction not to let anyone hand Hannah the effects in a plastic bag, because Iris knows what those bags look like. One plain memory from childhood or from the park (the crusts June refused, ch01), set down and not underlined.
6. What she leaves out, which is the scene's real engine, each one reported as precisely as a finding: the NDA. Holt, whom Hannah likes, and June's sticker. Diane. Brandt. The two voicemails in the archive, and the third face-down at her elbow. Anything that could be called sorry. She reports appetite. She doesn't apologise for it, here or anywhere.
7. She writes it to be read twice: first by whoever searches the flat if she doesn't come home, and then by Hannah. So she redacts herself as she goes, the way she redacted the syllabus. Competence even here, and she hears that it is the same voice.
8. She leaves it on the desk, above the locked drawer, where it will be found if she doesn't come home. Then she reasons through who will open the drawer after that, and stops reasoning.
9. **[TURN]** I signed it with the name Hannah uses for me, which no one had used in two years, and had to put the pen down. (Name: "Ris". See the Conflicts flag for how to make the two years work.)

- **Sensory lead:** texture. The pen dragging on the heavy syllabus stock, which takes ink slower than writing paper. Sound: the knocks along the corridor, children's voices through the door, a building that is having a party she isn't at.
- **Jokes / dry observations (thinner still):**
  - "I have read more of these than anyone I know. Most of them mention the cat."
  - Reserve: "I was writing back to a voicemail I hadn't played. It is a thing people do at the end, I'm told. I'd always assumed it was the bereaved."
- **The body:** hands, and the pen put down because the hand, not the mind, decides it. No food. The fridge isn't opened. Cold. The wound isn't touched in this scene, and the drafter should leave its absence unremarked.
- **Motifs:** voicemail (the third, unheard, face-down); hands; the grade (the genre of letters, graded); tape (only if the optional costume beat is used, a child's costume fixed with tape, one clause); the drawer.

## Scene 3 — Staging

1. Sunday 1 November. The clocks went back in the night. Her body wakes her at what is now four. Everyone in the country got an extra hour. She doesn't know what to do with hers and goes early. Black coffee, nothing else.
2. Set-up day at the hotel. Volunteers are routed in through the loading dock because the lobby is being dressed. She walks in on Ruth Calder's lanyard, past the dock office (a glass booth, a receiving supervisor eating, a hotel radio in its charging cradle on the desk), and walks her own escape route in reverse on the way in. Nobody checks the badge against anything. It's a lanyard, and a lanyard means someone else has already checked (ch02).
3. Work: she lays rubber cable mats and gaffer-tapes them down along the press riser. Tape again. She is good at it and is thanked for it.
4. On a break she takes the service stair down one floor from the ballroom level. She times it: from the stair door on three to the housekeeping closet door on two, thirty-one seconds at a working walk.
5. The closet. A par sheet is taped inside the door with the restock schedule, and she reads it like a chart. The round ivory banquet cloths on the bottom shelf aren't due out until a Wednesday wedding. She puts the catering jacket at the bottom of that stack: a black server's jacket with a slate collar, carrying the outside caterer's wordmark, bought from the uniform supplier for cash. Starch, bleach and warm lint from the laundry chute.
6. Out through the dock. The vendor gate opens from inside on a push button and closes itself on a timer. She counts it down from inside the gate: nineteen seconds, and nobody watches it close.
7. Monday 2 November, afternoon. The ritual from Chapter 15 runs move for move, and each move now has nothing in it: shower, dress, lay out the kit. No tasting menu. She makes a new hole in her belt with the trussing needle from the kitchen drawer. The press-assistant jacket, a navy blazer, sits looser than it did in the shop. Optional, if the ch18/19 beat sheets haven't dealt with it: the chicken from 17, a month in brine, the brine gone cloudy, goes to the chute in a bag, and she reports the smell and nothing else.
8. "You don't eat before." She says it out loud, as in 15. In 15 it was a rule about wanting. Now it's only a rule. She doesn't eat.
9. She unlocks the drawer, takes out the third page (never read on the page, never quoted), folds it in thirds and puts it in the blazer's inside breast pocket. Her own page, from 18, stays in the drawer. She locks it.
10. **[TURN]** In the mirror this time I didn't look like someone going on a date. I looked like someone going to work. I preferred the date.

- **Sensory lead:** smell. The back of house: cardboard, chafing-fuel gel, diesel from the dock, then the closet's starch, bleach and warm dryer lint. Temperature: the dock's cold November air, then the flat's cold shower on Monday. Do not use the hotel's carpet cleaner, which is reserved for 22 (style 05).
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - "The clocks went back that night. The country gave everybody an hour. I used mine to be early."
  - "Gaffer tape is the only adhesive more trusted than a lanyard."
  - Reserve: "I put a new hole in my belt with the trussing needle. It was the first thing I'd used it for in a month."
- **The body:** not eating on Sunday or Monday. The belt and the loose blazer (weight lost, never weighed). Hands taping, steady. Her palm returns to the inside pocket once the page is in it. The wound pulls lightly on the stair. Cold.
- **Motifs:** mirrors (15 the date, 19 the body, 20 work); tape (gaffer, the par sheet taped in the door, the sticker held in mind); the meal after (its absence as the meal *before*); hands; the minute (the thirty-one seconds and the nineteen are timed against it); the drawer.

## Snapper

Outline, in past tense: *The letter squared to the desk edge, the drawer beneath it locked, the third page in my jacket where my hand keeps finding it.*

Sharpened option: *The letter squared to the edge of the desk. The drawer beneath it locked. The third page in my jacket, where my hand kept finding it, the way a tongue keeps finding a tooth.* The tooth simile brings in the mouth, which is where the appetite used to be. If it reads as one image too many, cut after "finding it". The squaring should echo ch09's dossier moved a quarter-inch. She squares the letter because she is the kind of person who notices a quarter-inch, and the reader should remember who noticed.
