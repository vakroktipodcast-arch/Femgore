## Scene 1 — Reconnaissance

1. Friday 30 October, night. The kitchen table. The hotel's floor plans, warm from her printer, are laid out edge to edge like films on a lightbox. Open on the task, first page: the weak point isn't the Secret Service, it's the bureaucracy. The Service guards the man. Nobody guards the paperwork about the people standing near him.
2. How she sees it. Brandt's tap on the Lantern uplink is still relaying; the abuse desk restored Lantern's service after its review, one clause. Holt's lockout cut Brandt's logins, but nobody ever cut the tap, because nobody but Brandt knew it existed, and Brandt is dead. Brandt's access is hers now. She reaches it through the Lantern unit she has walked into before (ch11: the cloned badge, the midline code). One clause, no re-run of the break-in. **[See Conflicts 2: mechanism unsettled.]**
3. The credentials. The campaign controls back-of-house press and volunteer credentials, printed on its own badge printer in the volunteer office from one template. The credential audit was Compliance's job. She opens the audit spreadsheet. The file header reads *Last modified by D. Pell*, dated Friday 11 September, the Friday before Diane died, and nobody has opened it since. Compliance has been dead two months (campaign shorthand; about seven weeks).
4. The template sits in the mirror's copy of the volunteer drive. So does the roster. She finds a no-show: Ruth Calder, signed up in July, worked one phone bank, made eleven calls, never came back, still marked active, because marking people inactive was also Compliance. Iris changes nothing in the database. She only prints. The badge reads RUTH CALDER / VOLUNTEER / PRESS ASSISTANT, no photograph. It comes out of a secondhand card printer she paid cash for, onto the same white PVC stock from the supplier on the campaign's own invoice. The name is already on any list they print. (Not CLARE WHITLOW, ch02.)
5. Pressure. In the same mirror is counsel's email to the War Room, sent that afternoon: the subpoena fight is lost, and the signatory list is to be produced Saturday. Eleven names. She does the only arithmetic there is and touches the wound on her left flank. She doesn't think about the order the names will be read in. She thinks about Monday.
6. The venue. The Calloway files event floor plans for its ballroom level, on three, and the mirror has the advance team's copy. Read it like a body: the public rooms in front, the plumbing behind. Stair C beside the green-room corridor, its door about ten paces from the green-room door and about eight more along the passage to the seal (about twelve seconds, a figure she writes down and does not yet use). A housekeeping closet one floor down, on two. A loading dock at street level on the cross street, with a vendor gate. She traces the route with the cap of the red marker, not the tip. (Her desk red pen, not a lesson marker: ledger ruling.)
7. The minute, written down as a protocol. Sixty seconds, from the footage (ch19). The door closes, the detail turns its back, five seconds for him to sit down with the photograph. After that she writes nothing, because what comes after is the lesson and the lesson is already written. Keep the third page closed, unquoted and unread on the page (style 08). "Count to five" is seeded here for ch21.
8. The green room is the Alder Suite, a converted meeting suite. The Service's advance plan shows one entrance. The hotel's plan shows two: the corridor door, and a service door from the suite's pantry onto the back corridor, for banquet staff. She finds the door in the advance team's walk-through photographs and enlarges it to four hundred per cent. A white label runs across the gap between door and frame at shoulder height, corridor side.
9. She knows the label before she can read it. It's the same make of tamper-evident seal she puts on evidence bags at work, the kind that leaves VOID in the paint when it's lifted. For one beat the old click comes, a finding, and then it's gone. She notes that it went.
10. **[TURN]** The green room was a converted meeting suite with a service door the Service had sealed but the hotel floor plan still showed. The seal was a sticker.

- **Sensory lead:** smell and temperature. Hot toner and paper warm from the printer, the only warm thing in the flat (heating off since 18). Sound: the laptop fan, the card printer's ratchet as it feeds a badge.
- **Jokes / dry observations (thin):**
  - "Lantern Data Services was still paying its electricity bill. Nobody had told it that its officer of record was dead."
  - "Ruth had made eleven calls and gone home. I liked her. She knew when to stop."
  - Reserve: "Hotels are built like people. A face for the guests, and behind it the plumbing. I have spent my whole career in the plumbing."

## Scene 2 — The Letter

1. Saturday 31 October, night, at the writing desk. The only paper in the flat is the stock she printed the syllabus on. She notices this and uses it anyway.
2. The phone lies face-down beside the paper, holding Hannah's unheard voicemail (ch19: Sun 25 Oct, 4:00 p.m., 1:14). She doesn't play it. She is writing a reply to a message she hasn't listened to, and she says so.
3. Halloween. Children go up and down the building's corridor, knocking, and the knocks reach her door and pass on. There's nothing in the flat to give them. Optional, if the author wants Hannah in the room: Hannah's text from earlier, a photograph of Astrid and June in costume, June as a skeleton. Iris counts the printed ribs, finds the wrong number on one side, and the correction doesn't arrive as a joke.
4. The genre problem. She has taken hundreds of letters out of coat pockets on her table and knows what they all say, in what order. She grades the form in one dry line. Her letter is falling into it and she can't write it out again.
5. What she does put in, described and not quoted: the practical things. The spare key, the lease, the bank. An instruction not to let anyone hand Hannah the effects in a plastic bag, because Iris knows what those bags look like. One plain memory, set down and not underlined: the crusts June refused at the park, which Hannah ate without noticing (ch01, line 69).
6. What she leaves out, which is the scene's real engine, each one reported as precisely as a finding: the NDA. Holt, whom Hannah likes, and June's sticker (Iris pressed it back down with her thumb in ch01; she does not mention that either). Diane. Brandt. The two voicemails in the archive, and the third face-down at her elbow. Anything that could be called sorry. She reports appetite. She doesn't apologise for it, here or anywhere.
7. She writes it to be read twice: first by whoever searches the flat if she doesn't come home, and then by Hannah. So she redacts herself as she goes, the way she redacted the syllabus. Competence even here, and she hears that it is the same voice.
8. She leaves it squared on the desk, above the locked drawer, where it will be found if she doesn't come home. Then she reasons through who will open the drawer after that, and stops reasoning.
9. **[TURN]** I signed it with the name Hannah used for me, and had to put the pen down. (The ledger records the signature as "Ris". The page does not print it unless the author settles Conflict 1 that way; the past tense carries the loss without a stated span.)

- **Sensory lead:** texture. The pen dragging on the heavy syllabus stock, which takes ink slower than writing paper. Sound: the knocks along the corridor, children's voices through the door, a building that is having a party she isn't at.
- **Jokes / dry observations (thinner still):**
  - "I have read more of these than anyone I know. Most of them mention the cat."
  - Reserve: "I was writing back to a voicemail I hadn't played. It is a thing people do at the end, I'm told. I'd always assumed it was the bereaved."
- **The body:** hands, and the pen put down because the hand, not the mind, decides it. No food. The fridge isn't opened. Cold. The wound isn't touched in this scene, and the drafter should leave its absence unremarked.
- **Motifs:** voicemail (the third, unheard, face-down); hands; the grade (the genre of letters, graded); tape (only if the optional costume beat is used, a child's costume fixed with tape, one clause; Hannah's clear-tape habit is the better echo if Hannah is mentioned at all); the drawer.

## Scene 3 — Staging

1. Sunday 1 November. The clocks went back in the night. Her body wakes her at what is now four. Everyone in the country got an extra hour. She doesn't know what to do with hers and goes early. Black coffee and a heel of bread (the same heel as the Tuesday before 17, ch15, if the drafter wants the echo, unremarked). It is the last thing she eats (until ch27); the page need not say so.
2. Set-up day at the Calloway. Volunteers are routed in through the loading dock because the lobby is being dressed. She walks in on Ruth Calder's lanyard, past the dock office (a glass booth, a receiving supervisor eating, a hotel radio in its cradle on the desk), and walks her own escape route in reverse on the way in. Nobody checks the badge against anything. It's a lanyard, and a lanyard means someone else has already checked (ch02).
3. Work: she lays rubber cable mats and gaffer-tapes them down along the press riser. Tape again. She is good at it and is thanked for it.
4. On a break she takes Stair C down one floor from the ballroom level. She times it: from the stair door on three to the housekeeping closet door on two, thirty-one seconds at a working walk.
5. The closet. A par sheet is taped inside the door with the restock schedule, and she reads it like a chart. The round ivory banquet cloths on the bottom shelf aren't due out until a Wednesday wedding. She puts the catering jacket at the bottom of that stack, folded over a plain black tote, the kit in its inside pocket: a black server's jacket with a slate collar, carrying the outside caterer's wordmark, bought from the uniform supplier for cash. Starch, bleach and warm lint from the laundry chute. Only "the kit" is named on the page, never itemised and never described pharmacologically (ledger rule). What is staged is whatever she could not carry past a screening point on Monday; it is collected about 6:15 (ch21).
6. Out through the dock. The vendor gate opens from inside on the green request-to-exit button and closes itself on a timer. She counts it down from inside the gate: nineteen seconds, and nobody watches it close.
7. Sunday night, home: the chicken from 17, three weeks in brine, the brine gone cloudy, goes down the chute in a bag, and she reports the smell and nothing else. Tea and water only from here. Monday 2 November, afternoon (she has taken the day as leave). The ritual from Chapter 15 runs move for move, and each move now has nothing in it: shower, dress, lay out what she will carry (lanyard, black gloves, flat zip-tie for the lining, black vinyl roll). Nothing to lay out beside it, because the rest of the kit is already in the closet; the tasting menu is gone. She makes a new hole in her belt with the trussing needle from the kitchen drawer. The press-assistant jacket, a navy blazer, sits looser than it did in the shop.
8. "You don't eat before." She says it out loud, as in 15. In 15 it was a rule about wanting. Now it's only a rule. She doesn't eat.
9. She unlocks the drawer with the brass key, takes out the third page (never read on the page, never quoted), folds it in thirds and puts it in the blazer's left inside breast pocket. Her own page, from 18, stays in the drawer, face-down. She locks it.
10. **[TURN]** In the hall mirror this time I didn't look like someone going on a date. I looked like someone going to work. I preferred the date.

- **Sensory lead:** smell. The back of house: cardboard, chafing-fuel gel, diesel from the dock, then the closet's starch, bleach and warm dryer lint. Temperature: the dock's cold November air, then the flat's cold shower on Monday. Do not use the hotel's carpet cleaner, which is reserved for 22 (style 05).
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - "The clocks went back that night. The country gave everybody an hour. I used mine to be early."
  - "Gaffer tape is the only adhesive more trusted than a lanyard."
  - Reserve: "I put a new hole in my belt with the trussing needle. It was the first thing I'd used it for in weeks."
- **The body:** coffee and a heel of bread on Sunday morning, then nothing on Sunday or Monday (tea and water). The belt and the loose blazer (weight lost, never weighed). Hands taping, steady. Her palm returns to the inside pocket once the page is in it. The wound pulls lightly on the stair. Cold.
- **Motifs:** mirrors (15 the date, 19 the body, 20 work; hall mirror); tape (gaffer, the par sheet taped in the door, the sticker held in mind); the meal after (its absence as the meal *before*); hands; the minute (the thirty-one seconds and the nineteen are timed against it); the drawer.
