# Beat sheet: ch05, The Trap

- **Unit:** ch05 (`manuscript/ch05.md`)
- **POV:** Iris Vale, first person, past tense
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. The outline holds Scene 2 to a short capture (three paragraphs, then a fourth), so the weight shifts: Scene 1 ~1,250, Scene 2 ~700, Scene 3 ~1,200.
- **Act pressure:** Act I, The Prerequisite. The pressure is the campaign's certainty that nothing is coming, and here it lives inside Diane. Her campaign phone is muted for the hour (S1). The lock screen fills with campaign notifications nobody is reading (S2). She is sure that anything can be negotiated if you find the right currency (S3). Brandt is not yet a pressure. He comes into the chapter only as a name Diane offers. The fifth pressure, appetite, is present and unnamed, and the reader should feel the fourth paragraph of S2 as a danger before Iris does.
- **Appetite at entry:** Act I, appetite as competence. She found she liked the dark of the pump station (ch04 S3 Turn) and licked the container clean in the car (ch04 Snapper). The rug rehearsal left her "breathing hard and happy" (ch03 S3). All of it is filed under craft and preparation.
- **Appetite at exit:** It has been noticed once, plainly. The capture felt good, and she says so in the fourth paragraph of S2 as a finding about her own body. She files it straight back under the grade: the rehearsal paid off, under four seconds. She drank nothing after midnight and has not eaten, and her mouth has been dry all morning. When she says the Turn sentence of S3 her mouth is no longer dry. She reports that and doesn't explain it. Hunger is held back for ch06's Snapper ("I am ravenous"), so nothing in this chapter calls itself hunger.

## Conflicts

None. The outline agrees with the ledger: Sunday morning, the pump station by the reservoir, the bench and eleven minutes of reading, the foil sleeve in the kit, Diane 52, Olivia at Wellesley. The beats keep to these constraints:
- **Diane must not recognise Iris in this chapter.** The ch06 S1 outline gives the realisation to ch06 ("a small sound at the back of the throat"). In ch05 Diane only ever sees a red head-torch and a gloved hand, and hears a voice reading.
- **Diane's stutter must not surface.** ch06 S2 has it surface "once", and that once is the one Iris hears for the rest of the book. In ch05 Diane is fluent, unusually so for a woman on a floor, and Iris notes the fluency. That sets up ch06.
- **"Who else signed" must not tell Iris she was not alone.** The ledger has Iris learn of the eleven names in Ch 16, and the ch17 anchor has Brandt watch her learn "that I had never been alone". So Diane offers "a list" and "who else signed" with no number and no names. Iris takes it to mean who else signed off on her (counsel, witnesses, countersignatures) and cuts her off before it goes further. The reader can hear the other meaning on a reread. Iris doesn't ask, and the narration may mark that she didn't. Don't weaken the Turn to make room for any of this.
- **Scene 1 vs ch01:** in ch01 S3 she reads Lesson One aloud to the empty flat and hears that "it sounds like her". The ch05 S3 recognition has to be different and bigger: it is the first time she has said it **to its addressee**. The voice is neither the dictaphone voice nor the Hannah voice. It's a third one, and it's hers. Drafter: keep ch01's beat in mind and don't repeat its wording.

## Scene 1: Sunday Morning

1. 5:10 a.m., Sunday. Iris is already inside the pump station with the steel door pulled to within a hand's width of shut. The car is on the service road behind the building, out of sight of the trail. She has been in the dark long enough that it has gone grainy and then particular, and she likes it as much as she did in ch04. Put her in position in the first paragraph.
2. Inventory, done the way she'd lay out instruments. The bag holds industrial tape, zip-ties (ratchet tested), the foil sleeve, the red marker, the black vinyl tape, the Lesson One page in a clear sleeve, and a red-filtered head-torch, off. Two prepaid phones: the one in her hand and the one she put out at 4:50, face-down in the bent grass at the foot of the gravel spur, thirty metres off the trail where the spur meets the station door. The shredded NDAs are in the bag and are not described. They belong to ch06.
3. The body report. It's cold, Act I's unchosen cold: concrete through the soles of her shoes, the backs of her hands inside the nitrile. Her mouth is dry because she drank nothing after midnight, and she explains why in a joke (see below). Her hands are steady. After ch02's tremor she checks them, and they are.
4. Light comes up through the gap in the door: slate, then a thin bone colour off the water. Mist on the reservoir. A heron. The trail is empty, and the campaign is ten minutes' drive away and certain of everything.
5. From the fitness app (ch03) and two Sundays of watching (ch04), she knows Diane's numbers: out from the trailhead car park at 7:00, give or take four minutes, the bench by 7:35, eleven minutes of reading, the turn for home. Iris walks the route in her head and grades it. It's a sensible route, and in three years it has never once been varied. Brief callback to the ch04 abort (Olivia beside her). This Sunday the app showed one walker.
6. 7:56. Footsteps on gravel, the return leg. Through the gap, Diane in her olive waxed jacket, hands in pockets, walking like a woman who has been told walking is good for her. Iris's appraisal and her affection sit in the same sentence, as in ch04, and for the last time from outside.
7. Iris dials the planted phone from the phone in her hand. It rings in the grass. Diane stops, looks, and steps off the trail onto the spur. Iris narrates it the way a body moves on a table when you lift one limb: the whole of her following one sound. She bends and picks it up.
8. [TURN] Diane answers: "Olivia?" Iris hears it twice. First through the gap in the door, in the open air. Then a fraction of a second later in her own ear, through the earpiece, closer than anyone has spoken to her in years. Lay it out as a finding: it is the one name a mother says at a ringing phone during the one hour only her daughter is allowed to reach her. In the dark, Iris almost stops. She doesn't. She notes that she almost did, in the same flat register she'd use for a reading off a scale, and the scene ends there.

- **Sensory lead:** smell first. The station: wet concrete, iron gone to rust, an old sump smell of oil and standing water, and the green breath of the reservoir coming through the door gap. Then sound: the heron, gravel under Diane's feet, the ring in the grass, the name twice. Temperature: the concrete's cold, rising through her. Taste: her own dry mouth.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - On drinking nothing since midnight: she has catheterised enough of the dead to know exactly what three hours on cold concrete does to a bladder, "and I had no intention of leaving my DNA in a pump station for the sake of comfort."
  - Diane "walked like a woman who had been told walking was good for her by someone she paid, and believed them because she'd paid."
- **Body:** Act I cold in her feet, hands and jaw. Hands steady in black nitrile and checked, because of ch02. Dry mouth. Stillness: she hasn't moved in nearly three hours and reports it the way she reported the hour in ch01.
- **Motifs:** cold; hands; the grade (Diane's route, graded); the phone call as a cousin of the voicemail motif (a call answered for the wrong person). It's a seed only. It is not one of the three voicemails.

## Scene 2: The Capture

Four paragraphs, then the Turn. The capture itself is **three paragraphs, no more**. Short declaratives, entirely physical, and quick. The fourth paragraph is the confession.

1. **Paragraph one.** Iris ends the call. Diane says "Hello?" to a dead line and turns towards the door because it's the only thing there. Iris is already through it. A strip of industrial tape, pre-torn and folded at one end, hangs ready across the back of her left glove, exactly as on the kitchen floor in ch03. Her right hand goes over Diane's mouth from behind, and her weight goes down into the back of Diane's right knee (popliteal fossa, named) so the leg folds.
2. **Paragraph two.** Backwards over the threshold, into the dark. There's no dignified way to do it and she doesn't look for one. Diane goes down onto the concrete face-first. Her left cheekbone meets the floor, and Iris hears it and logs it as she would at the table: an abrasion over the left zygoma, about two centimetres, rushed. The tape goes across the mouth. She counts it, and it's under four seconds, as rehearsed. The nose is left clear on purpose, because Iris needs her breathing.
3. **Paragraph three.** A knee between the scapulae. Wrists behind her, one zip-tie, then a second through it to the iron bracket at the base of the old pump housing. Ankles zip-tied and a band of industrial tape over them. The door is shut with her heel. The soundproofing she tested with three claps in ch04 now does its job, and she hears the room go deaf. Diane's breath through her nose, fast and wet. Iris's own breath is the louder of the two.
4. **Paragraph four: what it felt like.** First person, so she tells you, and what it felt like was good. Say it plainly. The cold has left her forearms for the first time all morning, her face is hot, and she is breathing hard and happy, as she was with the rug, except that the rug had been more cooperative and far less motivated. It's the small particular satisfaction of the clean cut (ch01), but in the whole body. She files it at once under the grade: rehearsal, execution, under four seconds, pass. No apology, no "I shouldn't have". It's a finding.
5. Diane's own phone buzzes in her jacket's left inside pocket, against the floor and against Iris's knee. It's the only notification allowed through her muted hour.
6. Iris takes it out. The lock screen holds a stack of muted campaign notifications (staff channel, a poll alert, the comms director) and, on top of them, a text from **Olivia**: *are you on your walk? call me after, it's the roommate thing again.* Sent 7:59. Iris doesn't know about the roommate. The reader does, from Interlude 1.
7. A dry beat. A nineteen-year-old awake before eight on a Sunday is in trouble, in love or rowing. Iris doesn't grade the daughter.
8. [TURN] She holds the power button and slides the phone into the foil sleeve while the screen is still lit, and folds the sleeve shut. The text is still on the screen when it goes dark. She hears the screen go dark before she sees it. The sleeve is opaque and she never sees it go dark at all. The last thing she saw was the message. End on the fold of the foil.

- **Sensory lead:** sound and texture. The rip of industrial tape, the slap of a body on concrete, the ratchet of the zip-ties, the room going deaf. Then Diane's smell close up: walk-sweat, laundry soap, coffee on her breath behind the tape, the waxed cotton of the jacket. Temperature: Diane's heat under her, the first warm thing of the morning.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "The rug had been more cooperative. It had also been less motivated, which in fairness to the rug was the point of the rug."
  - Of the lock screen: the campaign had sent Diane fourteen things in the hour she had told it not to, "and her daughter had sent one, and only one of them was getting through."
- **Body:** hands at work, which is the only place in the chapter they move fast. A knee, weight, heat. Her pulse is up and she reports it. The cold leaves her forearms briefly, which seeds Act II's warmth. Diane's body is logged as findings (the zygoma abrasion, the breath through the nose) because it is Iris's work and ch08 will critique it.
- **Motifs:** tape (industrial: the rip, the seal, under four seconds); hands; the grade (she grades herself, pass); a message unanswered (Olivia's text). It sits beside the voicemail motif and is not one of the three.

## Scene 3: The Setup

1. A minute later. Iris switches on the red head-torch and angles it at the page, so her own face stays black and Diane sees a red light, gloved hands and nothing else. Iris sits on the concrete plinth of the old pump, an arm's length away. The foil-wrapped phone is on the floor between them, where she put it down.
2. She peels the tape from Diane's mouth. The adhesive takes the fine vellus hairs of the upper lip and leaves a residue, which she notes and which ch08 will notice. Diane doesn't scream. She has been told by the room, the way Iris was told in ch04, that nothing reaches the trail. She swallows, and asks for nothing.
3. Iris takes the Lesson One page out of its sleeve and reads it to her, the full text that appeared at the head of ch01. It is **not** reproduced again on the page. Quote one phrase at most, on the last line ("You will be evaluated accordingly").
4. The voice. She hears it as she reads. It isn't the dictaphone voice, and it isn't the voice she uses with Hannah. It's a third voice, and for the first time she recognises it as hers. The difference from ch01 is that the page has reached the person it was addressed to. Give this a long, greedy sentence. It's the only one in the scene.
5. Diane, fluent and composed, starts the negotiation. Her first move is process: "Whatever this is, it's a complaint. Complaints have a process." Iris tells the reader that Diane has just recited the page back to her without noticing. No em-dash interruptions. Cut her off with a full stop.
6. Second currency: Brandt. "I didn't run the press. Brandt did. I can give you Brandt." Iris notes that she opened with Brandt as if she'd opened with Brandt before. Iris already has Brandt. He's in the drawer under Pell.
7. Third currency: "There's a list. I can tell you who else signed." No number, no names. Iris hears it as a list of who signed off: counsel, the witness line, whoever countersigned her into the cabinet. She doesn't ask which list. One line of narration can mark that she didn't ask, without explaining why it matters.
8. Iris grades the negotiation, briefly and out loud in her head: strong structure, poorly sourced, wrong currency. Everything Diane is offering is information, and information is what a gatekeeper has. She is sure there's a price. That certainty is the campaign's, and the chapter's pressure, in one woman on a floor.
9. A dark aside, flat, as a fact about the schedule: at four o'clock Diane's mother's phone won't ring.
10. [TURN] Iris: "I don't want information. I want the grade." Diane goes quiet, and Iris watches her understand that this is not a negotiation, because there is nothing she has that Iris will take. Iris understands she has been waiting two years to say that sentence out loud, since the drawer and the syllabus. Report it with the same flat precision as the almost-stop in S1. Her mouth is no longer dry. She doesn't explain it.

- **Sensory lead:** sound, or its absence. The deaf room, where every sound stays: the paper of the page, the adhesive coming off skin, Diane's swallow, Iris's own voice coming back off the concrete close and unfamiliar. Light by source: red head-torch only, the page lit oxblood, everything else black. Temperature: cold returning to her hands as the heat of S2 leaves.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "She offered me a list the way the family offers the pathologist a history: helpfully, and some time after it could have made a difference."
  - On Diane's opening: "She led with process. I had written a whole page on that, and she had just read it back to me without having seen it."
  - Grade: "A strong opening. Poorly sourced. Wrong currency."
- **Body:** cold coming back into her hands; her pulse audible to her in the deaf room; the dry mouth, then not dry. Diane's body only as Iris sees it by red light: the abrasion, the adhesive residue, the wrists to the bracket. No eating. Hunger stays unnamed until ch06's Snapper.
- **Motifs:** the grade (Diane's negotiation; the sentence "I want the grade"); tape (residue); hands (holding the page); cold (returning). The syllabus voice leaks here at full strength, because she is fully in control.
