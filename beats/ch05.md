# Beat sheet: ch05, The Trap

- **Unit:** ch05 (`manuscript/ch05.md`, drafted and revised; this sheet is aligned to it)
- **POV:** Iris Vale, first person, past tense
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. The outline holds Scene 2 to a short capture (three paragraphs, then a fourth), so the weight shifts: Scene 1 ~1,250, Scene 2 ~700, Scene 3 ~1,200.
- **Act pressure:** Act I, The Prerequisite. The pressure is the campaign's certainty that nothing is coming, and here it lives inside Diane. She walks the same loop every Sunday and has never once left it, sure nothing on it can reach her (S1). The lock screen fills with campaign notifications nobody is reading (S2). She is sure that anything can be negotiated if you find the right currency (S3). Brandt is not yet a pressure. He comes into the chapter only as a name Diane offers. The fifth pressure, appetite, is present and unnamed, and the reader should feel the fourth paragraph of S2 as a danger before Iris does.
- **Appetite at entry:** Act I, appetite as competence. Hunger has been named once, plainly, as physiology (ch01 anchor: "I was hungry... It was physiology"). She found she liked the dark of the pump station (ch04 S3 Turn) and ate the oxtail cold in the car and licked the container (ch04 Snapper, Sat 12 Sep evening). The rug rehearsal left her "breathing hard and happy" (ch03 S3). All of it is filed under craft and preparation.
- **Appetite at exit:** It has been noticed once, plainly. The capture felt good, and she says so in the fourth paragraph of S2 as a finding about her own body. She files it straight back under the grade: the rehearsal paid off, under four seconds. She has had nothing to drink since midnight and nothing to eat since the Saturday oxtail, and her mouth has been dry all morning. When she says the Turn sentence of S3 her mouth is no longer dry. She reports that and doesn't explain it. (ch06 prose opens its last stretch with "My mouth had gone dry again under the hood. Then it was watering", so the dry, then wet, then dry again sequence has to hold.) Nothing in this chapter uses the word hunger or appetite. ch06's Snapper ("I am ravenous") is where it comes back.

## Scene 1: Sunday Morning

1. Open on Iris in position, inside the pump station, the steel door pulled to within a hand's width of shut. She has been there since 5:10 a.m. The dark has gone grainy and then particular (twenty minutes of dark adaptation, rhodopsin rebuilding), and she likes it better than she did in ch04, because now there is something to wait for in it.
2. Inventory, done the way she'd lay out instruments. She knows where everything is. The sheeting is taped at its edges. The pump housing stands on its concrete plinth, and at its base is the iron bracket she hung her weight from on Saturday, with **two wrist ties already threaded through it** and laid out long across the floor, cut to a length measured on the bench at home. **The kit sits in its row along the far wall, squared to the brick. At her feet the bag holds only the rest: two ankle ties, the head-torch with its filter (off), the Lesson One page in its clear sleeve, and the zip bag she isn't going to describe yet.** She suited by feel at about 5:15: white coverall, hair cover, overshoes, double nitrile, hood down for now. **Under the hair cover an earpiece runs on a wire to the prepaid in her hand, one of a pair.** The other prepaid she put out at 4:50, face-down in the grass beside the door at the top of the sumac spur, about 40 m up from the trail marker post. The post is bare: she took the recorder off it on Saturday after the third clap. The door opens inward on hinges greased that Saturday afternoon. Her padlock hangs open on the hasp outside, where anyone could see it and nobody would look. A rubber wedge holds the door off the frame, **the moving blanket is tied back to the hinge side, clear of the gap**, and a cord loop on the inside is how she'll shut it.
3. The body report. It's cold, Act I's unchosen cold (the station holds about 11 °C): concrete through the soles of her overshoes, the backs of her hands inside both layers of glove, the hinge of the jaw, which she has to remind to unclench. She has moved perhaps a handful of times in three hours, each a heel-to-heel weight shift, and she reports the stillness as she'd report anyone's. Her mouth is dry because she has had nothing to drink since midnight, and she explains why in a joke (see below). She has eaten nothing since the oxtail in the car and says nothing to herself about it. She checks her hands, as she has since Desmond's plaster. They're steady. She checks again, which is not the same as doubting them. **From 8 a.m. she works her fingers, fist and spread, twenty at a time, and tucks them under her arms between sets, because cold fingers are slow fingers.**
4. Light comes up through the gap in the door: slate by about ten to six, then a thin bone colour off the water. Mist on the reservoir like fat on cold stock. A heron on one leg in the shallows. By seven the sky has gone to ash and stays there. The trail is empty. Three years of Diane's Sundays sit in the app, public by default, and nobody at the campaign ever changed the setting.
5. From the fitness app (ch03) and the Sundays of watching (ch04), she has Diane's numbers: out of the south car park between 7:31 and 7:48, clockwise, the bench at 3.1 km by five or ten past eight, eleven minutes with the paperback, up at 8:20, on round the second half under the dam to the post at the foot of her spur at 8:33, give or take nine. She walks the loop in her head at Diane's pace and grades it. A sensible route: flat, open, well used, nothing on it that could reach her. In a hundred and forty-one Sundays she has **never once left the loop**, and that is the only consistency Iris needs her to have. Distinction. Brief callback to the ch04 abort, Olivia walking beside her. Olivia went back on the Monday train (Iris heard Diane say so, through a bezel, eating a peach), so she can't be here. Iris listens for two sets of footsteps anyway.
6. About 8:25. One set of footsteps on gravel, coming on from the bench side, brisk, short in the stride. Through the gap, Diane in her olive waxed-cotton jacket, slate collar, hands in pockets, six minutes early and inside the window. Iris's appraisal and her affection sit in the same sentence, as in ch04 (she liked her; she had her liver at 1,500 grams; she couldn't say which was warmer), and it is the last time she sees her from the outside.
7. Iris puts her hood up. At 8:27 she rings the planted phone in the grass. It goes off up the spur beside her feet, loud and cheap. Down at the post Diane stops. *She answers. She always answers.* (Iris had written it in her margin and watches it come true.) Diane looks up the spur, **which her track had never once climbed in a hundred and forty-one Sundays**, and then leaves the loop. Iris narrates it the way a body on the table follows when you lift a single limb: the shoulder comes, then the hip, then everything. Diane climbs the forty metres, her short stride gone long. On the fourth ring she is beside the door. On the fifth she bends, picks it up, and straightens with her back to the gap, an arm's length away, looking down the spur the way she came.
8. [TURN] Diane answers: "Olivia?" Iris hears it twice. First through the gap in the door, in the open air, small and level. Then a fraction later in her own ear, through the earpiece, closer than anyone has spoken to her in years. It is the name a mother says first at a ringing phone, before anyone has spoken. In the dark, Iris almost stops. She doesn't. She notes that she almost did, the way you note a reading off a scale, and the scene ends there.

- **Sensory lead:** smell first. The station: wet concrete, mineral cold like the underside of a paving slab, iron gone to rust (a cast-iron pan nobody has oiled), the old sump breath of oil and standing water from the dry wet-well, and the green, faintly metallic breath of the reservoir through the door gap. Then sound: gravel under Diane's feet, the cheap ring in the grass, the name twice. Temperature: the concrete's cold, rising through her. Taste: her own dry mouth, "like a stamp that wouldn't take".
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - On drinking nothing since midnight: she has drained the bladders of enough people who died of cold to know what three hours on cold concrete does to one, "and I had no intention of leaving my DNA in a pump station for the sake of comfort."
  - Diane "walked like a woman who had been told walking was good for her by someone she paid, and believed it because she'd paid."
- **Body:** Act I cold in her feet, hands and jaw. Hands checked and steady, because of ch02, then worked from 8 a.m. Dry mouth. Stillness at the door gap. No food in her, and she says nothing about it.
- **Motifs:** cold; hands; the grade (Diane's route, graded); the phone call as a cousin of the voicemail motif (a call answered for the wrong person). It's a seed only. It is not one of the three voicemails.

## Scene 2: The Capture

Four paragraphs, then the Turn. The capture itself is **three paragraphs, no more**. Short declaratives, entirely physical, and quick. The fourth paragraph is the confession.

1. **Paragraph one.** About 8:28. Iris ends the call and has the door open before the tone dies. Diane says "Hello?" to a dead line and looks down the spur for whoever left a phone in the grass. She doesn't turn round. Iris is already behind her. A strip of pewter industrial tape, pre-torn with one end folded under, hangs across the back of her left glove, exactly as on the kitchen floor in ch03. Her right hand goes over Diane's mouth from behind, her left forearm across the collarbones, and her weight goes down through her knee into the back of Diane's right knee (popliteal fossa, named) so the leg folds. **Coffee comes through the glove against her palm, Diane's breakfast on a hot breath, and Iris takes it as the first sample.**
2. **Paragraph two.** Backwards over the threshold, into the dark. There's no dignified way to do it and she doesn't look for one. Diane's left hand finds Iris's right wrist and drags at the glove, and the glove holds. Her right hand still has the phone and knocks once against the frame going past, and keeps it. Two steps inside Iris turns her and puts her down, and Diane goes down face-first onto **bare concrete** (the sheeting stops short of the plinth), her left cheekbone meeting grit, and Iris hears it and logs it as she would at the table: an abrasion over the left zygoma, about two centimetres. Rushed. The tape goes across the mouth from the back of her glove in under four seconds, as rehearsed, and the nose is left clear. Iris needs her breathing.
3. **Paragraph three.** A knee between the scapulae. **Iris takes the prepaid out of Diane's right fist one finger at a time and drops it in the bag.** Wrists behind her, one into each of the **two pre-threaded loops** waiting at the bracket, and the ratchets go twice, small and rapid. Ankles: **the two ties from the bag, then a band of industrial tape round both (four ties in all, two wrist, two ankle).** Then up: **the wedge kicked clear, the blanket let down, the cord**, the door drawn shut, her heel against the weatherstrip and her shoulder against the blanket to seat it, and she hears the room go deaf. She tested that with her hands on Saturday. It's better with Diane in it. Back down, knee between the shoulders again. Diane breathes through her nose, fast and wet. Iris's own breath is the louder of the two.
4. **Paragraph four: what it felt like.** First person, so she tells you, and what it felt like was good. Say it plainly. It feels good the way the clean stroke through the brain feels good (ch01), except that this time it's everywhere: the big muscles of her thighs and back where Diane's weight had been, her lungs, the heat in her face (warm, not hot), the cold gone out of her forearms for the first time since ten past five. She is breathing hard and happy, as she was with the rug. The rug had been more cooperative. She files it at once under the grade: rehearsal, execution, under four seconds. Pass. No apology, no "I shouldn't have". It's a finding.
5. Diane's own phone buzzes in her jacket's left inside pocket, between her and the floor, felt through Diane in Iris's knee. One buzz, then nothing. Iris reports only what she feels. (The ledger's Do Not Disturb, with only Olivia ringing through, is true but never stated on the page: Iris can't know it.)
6. Iris rolls Diane's left shoulder up on her knee and takes it out. The lock screen holds a stack of twenty-three notifications (the staff channel, a polling alert, the comms director twice) and, on top of them, a text from **Olivia**: *are you on your walk? call me after, it's the roommate thing again.* Iris doesn't know about the roommate. The reader does, from Interlude 1.
7. A dry beat. A nineteen-year-old awake before nine on a Sunday is in trouble, in love or in a row, and it's usually only one of them. Iris doesn't grade the daughter.
8. [TURN] **She holds the buttons down on the side with the message open on the glass, and the slider comes up over it and dims it, and she draws it across with a gloved knuckle.** It asks for no passcode. The text is still on the screen, dimmed and legible, when it goes dark. It doesn't go first and leave the light behind it; the words and the light go out together. Then the phone goes into the foil sleeve, and she folds the sleeve shut. End on the fold.

- **Sensory lead:** sound and texture. The rip of industrial tape, the slap of a body on concrete, the ratchets, the room going deaf. Then Diane close up: **coffee on her breath through the glove**, the waxed cotton of the jacket, the heat of her under Iris, the first warm thing of the morning.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "The rug had been more cooperative. It had also been less motivated, which in fairness to the rug was the point of the rug."
  - Of the lock screen: the campaign had sent Diane twenty-three things that morning, "and her daughter had sent one, and her daughter's was on top."
- **Body:** hands at work, the only place in the chapter they move fast, in a glove that must hold. A knee, weight, heat. Her pulse is up and she reports it, with no number. The cold leaves her forearms for a while, which seeds Act II's warmth. Diane's body is logged as findings (the zygoma abrasion, the breath through the nose) because it is Iris's work and ch08 will critique it.
- **Motifs:** tape (industrial: the rip, the seal, under four seconds); hands; the grade (she grades herself, pass); a message unanswered (Olivia's text). It sits beside the voicemail motif and is not one of the three.

## Scene 3: The Setup

1. A minute later. Iris takes Diane under the arms and sits her with her back against the pump housing, the wrist ties riding the bracket as measured, enough slack to sit and none for anything else, and she does it without a word. Then she sits on the concrete plinth of the old pump, a metre and a half away, out of reach of a kick, and switches on the red-filtered head-torch and angles it down at the page. With the hood up and the beam above and ahead of her face, her face stays black, and Diane sees an oxblood light, gloved hands and nothing else. The foil-wrapped phone is on the concrete between them, where she put it down.
2. While close, she peels the capture tape from Diane's mouth, folds it sticky-to-sticky and bags it (nothing of hers stays in the room). The adhesive takes the fine vellus hair of the upper lip and leaves a dull line of residue. Diane doesn't scream. She has already read the room (the shut door, her own breathing coming straight back at her off the brick) and understood what it was built for. That's an administrator's skill, and Iris credits it. Diane swallows, and for now asks for nothing.
3. Iris takes the Lesson One page out of its sleeve and reads it to her, all of it, from the subject line down, in the order she typed it. It is **not** reproduced again on the page. Quote one phrase at most, the last line (*You will be evaluated accordingly.*).
4. The voice. She had read it once before, to a flat with nobody in it. What she hadn't known is what it would do when it reached the person it was written for. Nothing in the room takes any of it away, so every word arrives at the weight she typed it, and she watches them land in Diane's face and shoulders like a weight settling on a scale. The warmth comes back up the insides of her forearms and stays while she reads. It isn't the dictaphone voice or the voice she uses for Hannah. It is the voice the page was written in. "It was mine, and I had never heard it arrive." One long, greedy sentence, the only one in the scene. See Conflicts: not "for the first time as mine".
5. Diane, fluent and composed, starts the negotiation. Her first move is process: "Whatever this is, there is a process. I can walk you through it." (Not "complaint", not "channels": ch06 uses both.) Iris gives her a line of credit for the fluency, with none of the pauses her staff took for thought. Cut-offs are full stops, no em-dashes.
6. Second currency: Brandt. "I didn't run the press. Brandt did. I can give you Brandt." Iris notes that she says it like a woman who has opened with Brandt before, in other rooms. Iris already has Brandt. He's in the drawer at home, under her.
7. Third currency: "There's a list. I can tell you who else signed." No number, no names. Iris hears it as a list of who signed off on her: counsel, the witness line, whoever countersigned her into the cabinet and initialled the drawer shut. She doesn't ask which list, and says so in one line without explaining why it matters. Iris also notes the one name Diane said that morning, once, to a ringing phone, and did not say again.
8. Iris grades the negotiation in her head: a strong opening, poorly sourced, wrong currency. Everything Diane offers is information, because information is what a gatekeeper has, and it has never once failed to buy her something. She is sure there is a price. That certainty, in a woman with her wrists on a bracket and a graze on her face, is the campaign's and the chapter's pressure.
9. A dark aside, flat, as a fact about the schedule: at four o'clock her mother's phone was not going to ring.
10. [TURN] Cold begins to come back into Iris's hands (peripheral vasoconstriction, "the oldest policy there is, and the one the campaign ran on"). Iris: "I don't want information. I want the grade." Diane goes quiet, and Iris watches her understand that this is not a negotiation, because there is nothing she has that Iris will take. And Iris understands that she has been waiting two years to say that sentence out loud, since the page went into the drawer. She reports it with the same flat precision as the almost-stop in S1. Her mouth is no longer dry. She doesn't explain it.

- **Sensory lead:** sound, or its absence. The deaf room, where every sound stays: the paper of the page, the adhesive coming off skin, Diane's swallow, Iris's own voice coming back off the concrete. Light by source: head-torch only, the page lit oxblood, everything else black. Temperature: cold returning to her hands as the warmth of S2 drains off.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "She offered me a list the way the family offers the pathologist a history: helpfully, and some time after it could have made a difference."
  - On Diane's opening: "She led with process."
  - The grade: "A strong opening. Poorly sourced. Wrong currency."
- **Body:** cold coming back into her hands; her pulse audible to her in the deaf room; the dry mouth, then not dry. Diane's body only as Iris sees it by the torch: the abrasion, the adhesive residue, the wrists tied to the bracket, **Diane on bare concrete, the sheeting stopping short of the plinth**. No eating. Hunger stays unnamed until ch06's Snapper.
- **Motifs:** the grade (Diane's negotiation; the sentence "I want the grade"); tape (residue, the capture strip bagged); hands (holding the page); cold (returning). The syllabus voice leaks here at full strength, because she is fully in control.
