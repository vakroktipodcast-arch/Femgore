## Scene 1 — Provocation

1. The first line is the method, begun. I took the gloves out of my jacket and put them on, left and then right, and snapped each cuff. Unlike 17's suite, this room gave the sound back, and the ballroom's warm-up music came through the wall over it. He watched me do it the way he watches staff do things, politely and waiting to be told what it was for.
2. Lesson Three as written, set down in one plain sentence without the syllabus voice: immobilise him, keep him conscious, pin the page, and leave him alive for eleven women to finish. Then, short, the knowledge from 18: eleven, less one who died suddenly at home, less the one standing in the room.
3. I took his left wrist the way I'd take a pulse, and he let me. He is a man whose wrists have been handled by staff for twenty years. The zip-tie went round his wrist and the arm of the chair, and it was through and tight before his face had caught up with his arm.
4. The syringe came out of the inside pocket, capped. A line of paper pressure: it came from the office's toxicology lab, and somewhere an inventory would be one short, and someone would count it, because counting is what offices are for. The agent stays unnamed on the page ("the agent", "it").
5. He saw the needle and flinched. It was a real flinch, orbicularis oculi closing hard and the head going back into the chair. He was frightened, finally, and nobody had ever made him be before.
6. The appetite comes back in the chapter's one long, greedy sentence, and smell comes first: through his aftershave and the hotel's carpet cleaner, the sharp first note of the other sweat, the one she named in Brandt (17). It is the first thing she has tasted in five chapters. Her mouth waters. She reports it as a finding and does not apologise.
7. Needle into the deltoid through his shirt sleeve, and her thumb on the plunger, pressing. While the taste is in her mouth she loses the count. She had been counting since the service door, and somewhere past forty there is no number. Nobody opens the door. She notices the door once and goes back to her thumb.
8. He doesn't shout. Even now his syntax holds, and he goes to the room's one move, the negotiator's move he has made with donors and with men like Brandt: "All right. All right." Then: "What do you want?"
9. **[TURN]** He asked what I wanted. Not what I was going to do: what I wanted. It was the first question anyone in the campaign had ever asked me, and I didn't have an answer any more. Her thumb stops on the plunger with the barrel part-given. She draws the needle out and stands holding the syringe, and the taste goes thin in her mouth.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell. Aftershave, the carpet cleaner (22), and then his fear-sweat rising under both. The smell returns to the lead position only for this scene, because the appetite briefly does. Sound behind it: the glove snap, the ballroom through the wall.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "He held his wrist out the way men hold out a wrist for a watch. I have taken the pulses of fourteen hundred people who couldn't refuse. He was the first who didn't think to."
  - Reserve, on the vial: "Somewhere a stock count would come up one short. Offices always find what they've lost, eventually. It's the only thing they find."
- **The body:** Hands (gloving, the wrist, the thumb on the plunger, and the thumb stopping). The mouth watering, then thinning. The pulse is not counted. She has lost one count and will not start another. The left-flank wound is quiet. The cold has been back since 18 and is noted once, in the gloves going on over cold fingers.
- **Motifs:** Hands. The Marine's photograph, face-up on the dressing table facing his chair, seen once over his shoulder. The minute (the lost count). Tape by contrast: the black vinyl is in the kit and is not used, because the prescribed lesson keeps his eyes and mouth open. The grade, by absence: the flinch is not marked.

## Scene 2 — The Crack

1. The agent takes the strength out of his arm, not his voice. He looks from the tie to me and works it through, visibly, the way he works a room, and he reaches the only campaign loss he can feel.
2. "Is this about Diane?" His voice breaks on her name. It is the first time his syntax has failed in the book.
3. Holt, in fragments, with the em-dash licence used here and only here: "She was family. I haven't slept since. Her daughter — Olivia — I was at the —" He doesn't finish it. Iris supplies "the funeral" silently and doesn't say it. She has seen the photograph in the papers, him beside the girl.
4. He is weeping. Iris reports it exactly: real tears, the face going in the way faces go, the free hand coming up to it. It is real grief, and she is the one person in the building qualified to say so.
5. He gives Diane back to her accurately. He says she stopped before certain words and people thought she was choosing them, and that she told him once, on a plane, about the stammer as a girl. Iris knew it from a dossier. He knew it because Diane told him. Let that sit with no comment.
6. Then Olivia's year and her school, and that Diane rang her mother on Sundays at four. He knows all of it. Iris has the same facts, every one, from a scope and a feed. She notes the overlap of their two files and goes no further.
7. Where she looks for herself in it, there is nothing for her, because nothing is available. He grieves the woman who filed me into a cabinet. One line, plain: the system had not failed to see women. It had chosen which ones (editorial note; let it surface as a finding, not a thesis). He doesn't say his wife's name. Sloane is downstairs in the ballroom.
8. The paper pressure, one line: "They still don't know who," he says. Iris knows they will, from a list, and that the list grieves no one. Nora Wade died suddenly at home and nobody from the campaign stood beside anybody at that funeral.
9. The syllabus assumed he would recognise the lesson. He is grieving the Gatekeeper. Iris almost grades it, and the reflex starts and finds no hand to hold the pen. Leave the refusal unremarked.
10. **[TURN]** I understood he would never be taught. There was no page that reached him. The syllabus shattered from the inside, and I was crying too, and he thought we were grieving the same woman. He says, through it, "You knew her," as a statement, kindly.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound. His voice going: the breaks, the wet breath between fragments, the ballroom's applause rehearsal coming through the wall at the wrong moment. Temperature second: her own tears, warm, which she notices the way she noticed Brandt's blood at thirty-seven degrees, but here it is her own heat, and it surprises her.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (one at most, before the Turn):**
  - "He knew she called her mother on Sundays at four. So did I. We had both done the reading."
  - Reserve: "Grief is a finding I can date to the hour. His was seven weeks old and had not been embalmed." (Use only if the drafter needs a beat of her old register before it breaks. Cut it if it grades him.)
- **The body:** Tears, hers, arriving without permission, the first since the bathroom floor in 18. Her hands hold the syringe needle-up with the thumb off the plunger. Her gloved left hand goes to the wound on the flank, through the jacket, and stays there while he talks. His body: weeping, the free hand at his face, the tied wrist turning in the zip-tie with no strength behind it.
- **Motifs:** Hands (his at his face, hers at the wound). The Marine's photograph (he has a dead brother and does not mention him; he mentions Diane). The grade (the reflex with nowhere to go). The meal after, by echo: Diane's Sunday, the mother's call at four.

## Scene 3 — The Rupture

1. She stops. No method, no next step. She tells the reader so: I abandoned the script.
2. She sets the syringe on the dressing table beside the photograph so that her right hand is free. She doesn't cap it. (25 caps it.)
3. The third page out of the inside pocket. It is folded once, in half, the way it has travelled since 20, and she opens it out. The crease holds. THE APEX was circled in red at home, before she left, and the circle shows through the back. Never reproduce or quote it (style 08). Never give its Evaluation.
4. The pin is in her other hand, the single steel pin of 6 and 17. The page is in my hand and I didn't pin it. She looks at his shirt where it would go, over the manubrium, and the word comes to her in Latin and goes no further.
5. The knowledge from 18, one line: the only other page she has written since the syllabus has her own name on it and is at home. Neither of them has been pinned to anyone.
6. The pin goes back into the kit, by her hand and not by decision, with the capped marker. Whatever happens next is not curriculum.
7. Direct address, once, to implicate: You know what it is before I do. (Style 01: to make the reader an accessory, not to explain.) Do not say what it is.
8. He watches her through the wet. He is slowed by the agent, half-finished in him, and still articulate in short pieces. He is the one trying to comfort, because that is what he is good at in small rooms.
9. **[TURN]** I put the page on the table, beside the Marine. Face-down, with the red ring faint through the back. He reached for my hand, to comfort me. His free right hand comes across, the same hand he offered her in 23, and this time it doesn't shake hers. It covers it.

- **Sensory lead:** Texture. The page (the crease, the weight of good paper, the slight drag of nitrile on it), the pin's point, and then his hand: warm and dry over the glove, his fingertips finding the one bare inch of wrist between cuff and sleeve, the inch that was clean in 17. Temperature closes it. He is warm. She has been cold since 18.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:** None after the S2 Turn. The absence should be felt in the rhythm. If the drafter needs one dry beat before the page comes out, it is this and nothing harder: "I had carried it folded for a week. Paper remembers. It's the one thing in an office that does."
- **The body:** Her right hand, empty, then covered. Her left holds nothing; the pin is gone into the kit. The tears have stopped or haven't, and she doesn't report which. Optional, and the author's call: the metal taste of the original injury surfaces once at his touch and is not named as such. If it crowds the Snapper, cut it. His body is warm, slowed, kind.
- **Motifs:** The syllabus page (unpinned, face-down, never reproduced). The Marine's photograph (the page beside it, face-up still). Hands (the handshake from 23 turned into comfort, and the bare inch of wrist from 17). Tape (the vinyl unused in the kit). The minute, by absence: still no knock.
