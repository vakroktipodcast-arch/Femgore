# Beat sheet — ch18: The All Is Lost Moment

- **Unit:** ch18. Manuscript file `manuscript/ch18.md`. Last chapter of Act II, The Practicum.
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense. The outline's Turns are written in present tense, so render them in past tense on the page.
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Suggested split: S1 ~1,100 / S2 ~1,050 / S3 ~1,000. No syllabus insert at the chapter head. The fourth page is written in this chapter and is **never reproduced** (style 08, ledger Objects).
- **Act pressure:** Brandt is dead and no longer a pressure. What replaces him is her own knowledge. It arrives as a body event in S1, as a document in S2 and as a decision in S3. Appetite: "finally seen". It has to be felt in every scene. In S1 she replays how happy she was. In S2 she remembers that she ate well after each delete. In S3 she wants the suite smell still in her hair, and says so.
- **Appetite at entry:** Consummated and elated (strategy 10, Act II). It is the best night of her life and she has said so twice. She is hungry. The chicken is in its brine, waiting, and she has told herself the hunger is physiology. She is still warm, and her hands are red to the wrist, which she has only just noticed (Ch 17 Snapper).
- **Appetite at exit:** Seen, kept, and stated. She understands exactly what she was enjoying while he told her. She does not judge the appetite wrong. She chooses to continue because she wants to, and tells the reader that in the plainest sentence in the book. The starving of Act III starts here: tea, no food, the chicken left in the brine. The cold is back for good (style 05: "cold again from 18").
- **Date and time (proposed, following beats/ch10.md):** Wednesday 30 September. S1 runs from about 2:15 a.m., in the car park, to about 8:50 a.m. on the bathroom floor. S2 is mid-morning. S3 runs from the afternoon into early evening. She booked Wednesday off weeks ago, because she plans the day after the way she plans the meal after. That is the only reason nobody comes looking for her.
- **Voice rules in force:**
  - S1 is the only scene in the book where Iris cries. Make it complete, not theatrical. Her clinical register tries to report it and fails partway. Let the failure show in the syntax: the long greedy sentences break into short ones. She doesn't announce the crying as a first and doesn't say "the only time in my life" (see Flags).
  - Jokes thin but stay above zero. This is still Act II, so the floor is one per page, but every joke now lands on her. The reader should feel the ratio falling before Act III.
  - Banned: empty, hollow, numb. Show the absence through the body: the fridge, the mug, the plate she doesn't take down. No "monster" and no "I'm not proud". She never apologises for the appetite. "Beautiful" is spent (Ch 17) and must not appear.
  - **No mirrors.** The bathroom has one above the basin. She is on the floor below it for six hours and doesn't look up into it. When she stands she faces the door. The mirror motif belongs to 15, 19, 20 and 27.
  - Cam's grammar is quoted only from memory, as complete sentences ("I'm sorry. I thought you should know."). Use no new Cam dialogue.
  - No em-dash cut-offs. Nora's hesitation is a pause, not a dash.

## Scene 1 — The Collapse

1. Open in the car, in the multistorey under the suite, at about 2:15 a.m. Her hands are on the wheel, red to the wrist, picked up straight from the Ch 17 Snapper. Now she looks. The blood on the backs of her hands has gone from cherry to oxblood and is setting in the creases of her knuckles. There are prints on the rim of the wheel and the gear knob. She reports it as a scene examiner would. It is the first time tonight she has seen herself as evidence.
2. Competence before collapse, because of the hero rule. From the door pocket she takes the tub of 70% isopropyl wipes she keeps in every car she has owned. She wipes the wheel, the knob, the indicator stalk and the inner door handle, and bags the wipes with the rest. Then she pulls a fresh pair of nitrile gloves on over the blood. She covers it rather than cleaning it, and she notes the difference.
3. The drive home, about thirty minutes, in sodium and then the blue-white of the ring road. She keeps to the limit exactly. The elation is still in her body like a drink, but it has stopped reaching her face. Under it, on a loop, his voice: *She died eight months later.* She can't hear the name. She can hear where the name was.
4. Home, about 2:45 a.m. She leaves the bags inside the front door. This is the first breach of procedure, and the reader should catch it against Ch 7's "procedure first. The bag inside the bag goes where it goes". She goes straight into the bathroom. The light is the cold fluorescent over the basin. She doesn't turn the shower on. No forty-minute shower. She sits down on the tile with her back against the side of the bath, still in her clothes, and doesn't get up again for six hours.
5. The cold comes back. The tile pulls the heat out through her thighs and her palms. This is the Act I cold, the unchosen kind, returning to a body that has been warm since Ch 10, and she knows it the way you recognise someone's step on the stair. She shivers. She reports it as rigor, then corrects herself, because rigor is the wrong word for the living. The correction is the last clean sentence for a while.
6. The cold cracks and she breaks. The register reaches for it: lacrimal glands, the diaphragm spasming, the glottis closing and opening on air the body pushes out on its own, a sound below decision. These are the words she used for Brandt's sounds three hours ago, and she hears that they are the same words. Then the anatomy stops. The sentences go short. She cries completely, for a long time.
7. Sound is what the scene is built on. His suite kept every sound and gave none back. Her bathroom is tile and enamel and gives back every one. She can hear herself from the walls, slightly late, the way the suite never let her hear him. For once there's a witness in the room, and it is her.
8. Under the crying is the thing she can't stop seeing: how happy she was, exactly, while he said it. She plays it back like footage, frame by frame and in order. She had the shears set to the fourth costal cartilage. Then came the celery sound, and his name for the woman went under it. Then "I'm sorry. I thought you should know." Then her thought, *three hundred and eighty grams, give or take*. Then the chicken. She was thinking about the chicken. She does not grade any of it.
9. The hours pass, measured by the body rather than the clock. The blood under the gloves has dried and cracks at the knuckles when she makes a fist, so her skin flakes the way the paint does on a wet-room ceiling. At some point she peels the gloves off inside out, the way she did the right one in the suite. Her hair is against her face and the suite is in it: vetiver, rye, warm dust, him. Grey morning comes into the frosted window. The mirror is above her the whole time and she never looks up into it.
10. **[TURN]** At about 8:50 a.m., when she can stand, the first thing she reaches for is her phone, still in its foil sleeve in her jacket pocket. Not to call anyone.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first. Her own crying returned by the tile, late and close. The tick of the cistern. A neighbour's shower through the wall at seven, someone else's ordinary morning. Then temperature: the cold through the tile, the cold enamel of the bath against her spine. Smell: the suite in her hair, with the isopropyl from the wipes sharp on top of it. Texture: dried blood cracking at the knuckle creases. Light by source: the basin fluorescent, then grey dawn through frosted glass.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (on herself):**
  - "I have attended a great many bathroom floors professionally. It is where the body goes when it wants to do something without a witness, and the body is always wrong about that."
  - "I had timed every shower of my adult life. If you want one finding for the night, it is that I didn't take this one."
  - "Tile is the only honest surface in a flat. It tells you exactly what temperature you are."
- **Body:** Hands first: red, then gloved over, then cracking, then bare. The cold returns and becomes shivering, then crying. Her pulse and breath are no longer counted, and the absence of counting is the finding. She is hungry, unfed, and doesn't mention it. The left flank wound pulls where she is folded against the bath. She touches it once with the heel of her hand and doesn't think anything, which the reader should feel as wrong. She is in her clothes from the suite.
- **Motifs:** Hands (red to the wrist, then gloved over the blood). Cold (the return). Voicemail (his spoken report of it, replayed). The meal after (the chicken in the replay, still not taken). The grade, by refusal: she grades nothing. No mirror, deliberately.

## Scene 2 — The Truth Confirmed

1. Before she opens anything, she sees her own prints in dried blood on the phone's glass, a thumb and the side of a forefinger. Procedure comes back in one piece. She washes her hands at the basin, nails and webs and wrists, without lifting her eyes, and dries them. She cleans the phone with a wipe. She resumes the protocol before the knowledge, and that order tells the reader something.
2. Mid-morning, at the writing desk, the locked drawer under her knees. The flat is cold. The late-summer heat of Ch 10 has finally broken overnight, and the heating isn't on yet. She opens the county voicemail app. Then the folder she has never opened: Archive.
3. It holds two messages. She keeps everything. In her years of county service she has returned every call or saved it, and she has pressed 7 exactly twice. She tells the reader this as the most damning statistic she has ever compiled.
4. The top message is Wednesday 16 September, 11:38 a.m., forty-seven seconds, marked as read. Tessa Quinn is asking for "the pathologist in the news". She doesn't play it again. She knows the dog's claws are on it.
5. The second is from three years earlier: 17 September, 4:52 p.m., thirty-four seconds, marked as read. She plays it. A woman's voice, young and hesitant: "My name's Nora Wade." That is where the name surfaces, in the woman's own mouth, and Iris hears it a second time under the sound of celery breaking. "I think we may have..." Then a single pause, the trochaic catch she heard in Diane at the pump station. Then "...known the same person. I just wanted to talk." Then a number. Don't explain the pause, and don't let Iris name Diane (ch06 carries out).
6. She knows the rest of that day without remembering it. She played the message once, the next morning, between cases. She pressed 7. She knows she ate lunch, because she always eats lunch. She doesn't know what it was, and for a woman who can tell you every meal in this book, that gap is the finding.
7. On the clean laptop, never the phone, she searches the name. She finds an obituary in a newspaper in another county: Nora Elizabeth Wade, 27, "died suddenly at home" on 21 May of the following year. That is eight months later, as he said. Iris knows what "suddenly" means at twenty-seven. It is one of two things, she has signed both, and she won't pick. She could request the file through professional channels, and doesn't, because a request is a document and documents are read.
8. She takes the Lesson Two master out of the drawer. *Excellent.* is in red in the margin, and below it *Tessa Quinn* in black, struck through in one level line. I did it twice. She puts the two messages side by side with the struck name and sees the pattern: the designed failure included her, she has been running it, and she was eating well the whole time. She names the meal after Tessa's message, the cold lamb and the salsa verde, sitting down, with all its taste. That is the bill Ch 10 promised.
9. Then the Lesson One text, from memory, because that page is on Diane's chest: *to convert the complaint into the file, the file into the folder, the folder into the cabinet, the cabinet into the room no one enters.* Key 7, the archive, the folder nobody opens. Ch 10 kept her from noticing this rhyme. Now she notices it, and says so once, flatly.
10. **[TURN]** I have been the Gatekeeper. My page would read: *Confused process for protection.* She takes a blank sheet from the printer tray. In black ink, in the small upright hand she uses for case numbers, she writes it on a page. The narration must not reproduce the page. It says only that her name is on it, and that she doesn't circle anything (the red marker stays capped). I don't pin it to anything.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound: the two voices through the phone's small speaker in a cold, quiet flat. Tessa's is not replayed, only remembered. Nora's has a breath before it, office noise behind it, and the catch. The chirp of the app. Texture: the master page soft at the corners, the slight drag of the nib on blank printer paper. Temperature: the first cold morning of autumn, her feet bare on the floorboards. Light: grey window light, then the laptop's white on her face.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (thin, self-directed):**
  - "Mara once said I'd archive a sneeze. In all those years I had pressed 7 twice. Both times I was busy."
  - "'Suddenly' is the word families use when the answer is one of two things. I have signed both, many times, and spelled them correctly."
  - "The county keeps a deleted message for seven years. It is the only promise the county has ever kept to me, and it kept it against me."
- **Body:** Hands washed now, raw at the knuckles where the blood cracked. They are steady on the pen, and the steadiness is the horror. She is hungry, with an audible stomach, and logs it as a finding without acting on it. She is cold from the feet up. Her hair is still unwashed and the suite is still in it. The wound is quiet.
- **Motifs:** Voicemail (the first two of three, together for the first time). The grade (she grades herself: the Gatekeeper's Evaluation, in her own hand). Tape, by absence (nothing over her eyes or mouth, and she leaves the page bare). Hands. The meal after, recalled as an indictment: Ch 10's lunch and the lunch three years ago she can't remember.

## Scene 3 — The Choice

1. Afternoon. She sits at the desk with the page face-up in front of her and lays out what she now knows the syllabus is. It is not justice. It is a way never to face that she could have helped before, twice, and both times pressed 7.
2. The second thing the syllabus is, stated without euphemism: a way to have what she actually wanted, which was this, all of it. The planning, the suite, the record, his skin, his heat through the nitrile, the heart in her bare hand. She lists them as she would list findings, in Anglo-Saxon for the wanting and Latin for the parts, and doesn't apologise for a word of it.
3. The clean exit, laid out the way she laid out the syllabus, in steps. Stop. Bag the kit and keep it as evidence instead of destroying it. Call the number on the FBI request she read in the feed in Ch 14 and ask for Special Agent E. Reyes. Let the story surface in a courtroom, where it can't be managed. Let the other ten be believed, the dead one included. She grades the plan honestly: it is the best plan she has ever written.
4. She makes tea. It is strong, with milk (the Ch 7 habit). Getting the milk means opening the fridge, and the chicken is there in its brine. The skin has gone white and puckered the way a hand does after hours in a bath, which the trade calls washerwoman's skin, and she knows to the hour how long a hand takes to get like that. She closes the door. Keep this to a single, purposeful opening. Ch 19's Snapper owns the open-and-close.
5. I don't eat. She drinks the tea standing at the window. It is too hot, then tannic, then cold, and she finishes it. Report the taste. There is no plate, and nothing is said about the absence of one.
6. She turns her head and the suite is in her hair: vetiver, rye, him. In Ch 17 she turned her head more than she needed to. Now she does it on purpose, slowly, the way you smell a glass, and she wants it. That is what decides her. Let the body decide before the sentence does.
7. I decide to continue. Not because the philosophy holds, which she has just watched fail. Because I want to. Candidate wording, which the drafter should keep this plain or plainer: "I was going to go on because I wanted to." Then she tells the reader it is the most honest sentence in the book, and that the reader should keep it next to the other word, the one from the suite.
8. Continuing is shown as procedure, resumed. The bags inside the front door go where they go. Disposal is not shown on the page and stays her procedure, as in Ch 7. Her clothes go on the hottest wash. The instruments are cleaned and rolled. She doesn't wash her hair. She grades the exit procedure: competent. She doesn't grade herself.
9. Early evening, about 6:40 p.m. The light goes to sodium outside. Hunger is present and refused, and she says so as a finding. She turns the page face-down on the desk. She doesn't lock it in the drawer. It isn't part of the syllabus, so it doesn't go with the syllabus.
10. **[TURN]** Mara texts. Something ordinary and kind, about work, ending in a kiss. Proposed: "Hope the day off was nice. Saved you a coffee, drank it for you. x". The phone lights up on the desk beside the face-down page. I don't reply. (This echoes Ch 14's unanswered album text, without comment. This time she doesn't play anything.)

- **Sensory lead:** Taste: the tea, from scald to tannin to cold. Smell: vetiver and rye in her own hair, chosen. Temperature: the cold flat at dusk and the mug's heat leaving her palms, the way his heat left his heart under her hand. Let the reader make that connection; she doesn't. Sound: the fridge's seal giving and closing, the washing machine starting, the single buzz of the text. Light by source: grey afternoon, then sodium, then the blue of the phone.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - On the clean exit: "It was the best plan I had ever written. It had only one flaw, which was that it ended."
  - On the chicken: "Washerwoman's skin. We see it on bodies pulled out of baths, and I know to the hour how long it takes."
  - On tea: "Tea is what you make when your hands need a reason to be holding something."
- **Body:** Hands round the mug, raw at the knuckles, steady. Hunger refused, the first deliberate starving (strategy 10, Act III begins in the body before the act does). The cold is settled now as the baseline, not a crisis. Her hair is unwashed on purpose. She touches the wound once, while she lays out the clean exit, because she is thinking. That is the one normal gesture in the chapter.
- **Motifs:** The meal after, refused: the chicken in the brine. The grade: the clean exit graded high, the exit procedure graded competent, herself not graded. Voicemail: a text unanswered beside the voicemails she never returned. Hands. Cold. Vinyl, by absence: she doesn't put anything on, and she has never owned a record.
