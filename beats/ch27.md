- **Act pressure:** Act III. "Reyes, closing on paper; the knowledge from 18 in every room." Reyes has to be felt in every scene. In S1 she is a phone call relayed by Mara, and a face Iris has already seen. In S2 she is Thursday, which Iris can't say to Hannah. In S3 she is the drawer Iris doesn't lock. The knowledge from 18 is the fourth page, face-down in that drawer, which the third page now joins.
- **Appetite at entry:** Starved and then gone. Her last food was black coffee and a heel of bread on the morning of Sunday 1 November (ledger); since then she has had tea and water and nothing else ("a week of tea and water"). The coffee made at 8 a.m. sits undrunk on her office desk.

The first real joke in the chapter arrives with the food, and the reader should feel the voice coming back and be frightened by it. She does not interpret it, apologise for it or plan with it. The last image is her hands.
- **Voice rules in force:**
  - The grade: the last grade in the book is Mara's, in S1, "full marks", for being afraid of her (style 09, ledger). After that beat Iris grades **nothing and no one**.   - No mirrors anywhere in the chapter. The reader supplies 27 (style 09). Her hands are the mirror. The window over the kitchen sink is "black, and beyond it the sodium of the street" and shows nothing of her; name no other reflective surface in S3.
  - Banned: empty, hollow, numb, beautiful, monster, "I'm not proud", "luckily". No em-dash cut-offs in dialogue.
  - Hannah's ch19 voicemail stays unplayed (ledger). Hannah must not summarise it on the call.
  - The name Hannah used for Iris ("Ris") is never said in Ch 27 (ledger). Hannah says goodbye without it, and Iris doesn't report it.

## Scene 1 — The Morgue

1. Monday 9 November, twenty to eleven, in the autopsy suite. First paragraph: leaf mould on a decedent's trouser cuffs, the one live outdoor smell in a room of cold iron and chlorhexidine. He is seventy-four, collapsed raking leaves in his own garden; his calloused palms and the polished right thumb web tell her so first. Heart out and on the board, coronaries cross-cut with a fresh blade: a fresh thrombus on a split plaque in the left anterior descending artery, "dark as the inside of a plum". She names the widow-maker once into the dictaphone and doesn't joke. She works well, and says it was only work.
2. Mara at the next table. When she comes round for the saw she stops a full stride short; Iris hears where, by the squeak of her clogs on the wet floor.
3. Out to the anteroom for fresh gloves. The break-room television through the office door, the same set as Ch 1, on mute, captions crawling under a podium: INVESTIGATORS BELIEVE SENATOR'S ATTACKER ACTED ALONE. Then SOURCES DESCRIBE A LONE EXTREMIST. NO EVIDENCE OF WIDER PLOT. NO DESCRIPTION HAS BEEN RELEASED. At "acted alone" she puts her wrist to her left side over the scar, through gown and apron, and takes it away again.
4. The finding behind the captions: there was no page on him because she didn't pin one. Two bodies that season carried paper and the third didn't, and a system handed a body with nothing pinned to it files it where it likes. "It had liked a lone extremist. It had one." (The story, not a suspect.)
5. The replacement. The result came in on the Tuesday within a point of the last poll, with a dead man's name still on the ballot. Do not say who won. On the Friday (6 Nov) a caption carried the replacement's name and she read it twice to keep it; she hasn't kept it. This is her memory of Friday's captions, not of this morning's. "The machine was intact. Only the man was gone."
6. Holt's autopsy. The Chief did it himself on the Tuesday, with two federal observers and Mara assisting. Iris was on shift, was not asked, and did not ask for the report. One line: she knows what is in it, because her hands wrote most of it. Don't render findings.
7. Friday's statement from three of the eleven, through a single lawyer, unnamed, "former campaign staff". She counts three the way she counts towels at the end of a case. She is not one of them. (Off the page: one of the three is the eleventh name, after Vale; Iris doesn't know that.)
8. Closing the gardener: surgical tape on the specimen labels, her initials, the date. Mara passes the needle holder handle-first at the full length of her arm; she hasn't hummed since October. When Iris's back is turned Mara looks at her hands; Iris knows by the clogs stopping while the hose runs over the same few inches of steel. "I let her." No weekend question, no easing joke ("there is always one available"). Grade, in narration only: Mara is watching exactly the right thing in exactly the right proportion and is afraid of precisely what there is to be afraid of. *Full marks.* No comment after. Then, one paragraph, the body: coffee on the office desk since eight with a skin on it, no lunch coming, belt on the trussing-needle hole and loose on that.
9. At the sinks, side by side, a basin apart. Mara reports in the flat order Iris taught her. Reyes rang at nine; the Chief is on the Senator's file and Mara has the rota this month. Reyes wanted Iris's shift pattern for Thursday; Mara said days. "She said you were meant to see her last Wednesday. Before." Water over Iris's wrists at the temperature she set, no colder than her flat. "Did you know?" "No." True, and one of very few true things she says that day. Then the face for the voice: flat shoes, trouser suit cut long on the right, short unpainted nails, biro mark on the right middle finger, a corridor half a second long. E. Reyes. She doesn't tell Mara.
10. **[TURN]** She doesn't ask what else the agent said. Mara tells her anyway, drying her hands a finger at a time, because it is in the order. Reyes was explaining the lost week: nine of the eleven interviewed, two left. "A woman who's left the country. And Dr. Vale. Postponed by Election Eve. Rescheduled for Thursday." End on the fact, no reaction from Iris. Whether Reyes said it on purpose stays unreadable.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell. Leaf mould first, then the old smell she no longer notices: cold iron, chlorhexidine, the faint sweetness of a fresh case. Sound: fans, hose, scale, clogs on the wet floor stopping a stride short. Temperature: the suite's cold, no colder than her flat. Texture: calloused metacarpal heads, the polished thumb web.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - Used in the prose: "He wanted, I assume, a face that had never been near politics; Mara's had now been over all three of its bodies."
  - Used: "The machine was intact. Only the man was gone."
  - Used: "I worked well. I would like that on the record, along with the fact that it was only work."
  - (Reserve, not in the prose) On Mara keeping the table between them: "She kept the table between us. That is what it's for."
- **The body:** Hands, gloved and steady on the case, watched by Mara the way Reyes watched them in the corridor (26). Iris doesn't watch them herself; that is saved for the Snapper. Hungry and doesn't notice. Coffee undrunk since 8 a.m. on the office desk. Belt on the needle hole, loose. Left-flank scar touched once, through gown and apron, at "acted alone".
- **Motifs:** The grade (Mara's full marks, the last in the book). Hands (watched). Tape (surgical, on specimen labels, one clause). Paperwork thread (nine of eleven, Vale on Thursday, the office's own rota delivering her). Voicemail by absence: Reyes rings the office, not Iris.

## Scene 2 — The Sister

1. About 6:30 p.m., the flat. The chicken is on the kitchen counter in its paper (bought on the walk home: about a kilo and a half, its weight in the crook of her arm, the man at the counter saying something about the cold and Iris answering him). She doesn't remember deciding to buy it. The heating is still off; she is standing at the writing desk with her coat on when the phone lights up on the wood: HANNAH. Under the name the notification is still there from two weeks ago: voicemail, 1:14.
2. "I answered." Its own short paragraph. After the calls she let ring out in Ch 19, the answering is the event.
3. Hannah's kitchen comes down the line: dishwasher churn, a tap, June singing four words from school over and over. Iris's flat has the fridge and her own breath. The phone is the only warm thing in the room; her other hand is flat on the desk, cold through the palm. "There you are," not unkindly. "I've been busy." She has never once said it and been lying in quite that way.
4. "Did you get my message?" "I did. Sorry." She tells the reader she hadn't played it; it is against her cheek while she says so. Hannah lets it go, because Hannah always lets it go. Hannah never says what is on it.
5. The technique, shown. Astrid's reading book (the title is not on the page); the small lift in Hannah's voice at being asked; then the second question that proves she listened: has June lost her first tooth yet (lower central incisors go first at six; she knows children by their teeth). "Only you," Hannah says, and laughs, and answers (answer not on the page). Iris laughs a half-beat late. Low, slightly slow voice for families, room left after each thing. She describes it the way she would a colleague closing: even bites, even tension, every knot the same. No mark given. One clause: her left hand a few inches from the letter, still squared, still unsent, the other things written out beside her hand; she doesn't look down.
6. The present. "She loved it, by the way. The microscope." Her own hair, a dead fly off the windowsill. Iris had known only what Hannah's uncaptioned photograph of 24 Oct showed (June with the microscope, the *from Iris* tag in Hannah's hand, in more tape than paper); she hadn't known what June would put under it. Hannah chose something in her register for a six-year-old, unasked, and was right. "I'm glad."
7. "Isn't it awful. About Marchetti." Said as people say it about weather. Iris: "Mm" (the Ch 1 "mm"). June asked whether the man on her sticker was dead.
8. Iris tells her: yes, his body stopped working and can't start again; don't say sleeping, don't say went away; answer only what she asks. Kept short so Hannah can repeat it. "I have said it to a great many families. Nobody has ever complained about the wording." Hannah: "That's good. That's really good. Thank you." June has stopped singing; the dishwasher goes into its rinse. No comment on it.
9. "You sound tired." "I am." The only thing she said all evening that wasn't technique. Goodbye in their family's order. "Call me Sunday?"
10. **[TURN]** She has the shape of "Sunday" ready like keys before a door, and there is no day to put there: she doesn't know when she can call back; the week has a Thursday in it that belongs to somebody else's diary. A gap, and she doesn't leave gaps with Hannah. Mouth dry; she swallows. "Soon." Two seconds' quiet, one more than Hannah has ever needed; the tap, and the fridge in her own kitchen. "Okay." "Love you." *She had heard it.* End there. Don't say what Hannah thinks. Hannah doesn't use her name for Iris.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound. Hannah's kitchen through the phone, warm, ordinary, slightly compressed, against the silence of the flat. Temperature: the phone's battery warmth at her ear, the only warm thing; her other hand cold on the desk.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (lands on herself):**
  - Used: "I have said it to a great many families. Nobody has ever complained about the wording."
  - Used (a dry observation, not quite a joke): "I have never once said it and been lying in quite that way."
  - (Reserve) "It was the only thing I said all evening that I hadn't prepared." (The prose has the plainer "wasn't technique".)
- **The body:** Standing in her coat, unsettled. One hand at the ear, one flat on the desk beside the letter. Unfed, unmentioned. Mouth dry; swallows before "Soon".
- **Motifs:** Voicemail (the third, unplayed, on the screen as she answers and "against my cheek"). Tape (June's present in Hannah's over-taped wrapping, one clause). Hands. The Marine by echo: the sticker man, "Mm". The grade by absence: technique described, no mark.

## Scene 3 — The Table

1. Twenty past nine. The kitchen table under its one bulb, her own table, where she told herself in October (Ch 17) she would sit for once. On the wood, where she put it down on coming in from the walk a week before: the folded page, folded once with the thirds still in it, unopened since. Beside it, a plate.
2. How the plate came: after the call she found herself at the counter with the bird out of its paper and patted dry, salt in her palm and then on the skin, the oven as hot as it goes, watching her hands as if she had come into the room late and they had started without her. No brine. Out of the oven she carved one side herself (leg, wing, breast), left the oyster on the frame, and plated it with bread for the juices. The rest of the bird went back into the tin in the oven, which she turned off. The oven was on an hour and a half; the kitchen is warm on her face and the backs of her hands, the first heat in the flat since the night she came home from the suite. She notes the temperature and nothing else.
3. Smell first, as a finding: rendered fat, salt, the crust the colour of a violin, the faintly sweet steam off the breast. Nothing happens in her mouth. She sits with hands in her lap while the steam stops, the juices at the thigh go from moving to still, the skin goes dull. Tongue dry against her teeth. This is the outline's "cannot eat it"; never name the absence.
4. When the skin has gone dull all over she picks up the page instead and doesn't unfold it. She carries it to the pedal bin, foot on the pedal, lid up, her hand over the opening holding the page by one corner. Her hand doesn't open. It doesn't shake either; the same not-doing as over a man's chest a week ago (Ch 25). She stands until the lid's hinge creaks under its own weight, takes her foot off, the lid comes down.
5. To the writing desk. One clause of inventory on the way: the letter, squared, there nine days; and against the side of the desk, sleeve to the room, a record in its cellophane. Unnamed, no account of how it came; she doesn't wonder aloud. She turns the brass key and opens the drawer.
6. The fourth page is where she left it, face-down, on the working masters and the dossiers. She slides the third page in on top of it, still folded: "the one I couldn't pin on the one I hadn't". She closes the drawer. The key stays in the lock. She doesn't turn it. No comment yet; the Turn line comes after the meal.
7. She comes back to the table and sits (she sits after). "And I ate." The long greedy sentence, one breath: the skin breaking under her thumb like the roof of a blister, salt before the meat, the fat still warm at the bone where heat keeps longest, the knee found with her thumb ("felt for the place, and it gave", an echo of the Ch 17 anchor, not an exact quote), the dark close-grained thigh with its faint iron at the bone, mouth flooding so fast it aches under the jaw, eyes shut, the drumstick's tendons like wet string, the wing she has never left on a plate, the breast in torn pieces through the juices. She doesn't stop to put anything down. Fingers only.
8. Bread through what's left until the plate shines. Then up, the tin out of the oven with a cloth, stood over for a moment at the hob, the other leg pulled off at the hip standing; then the whole tin back to the table and she sits, because she sits after. All of it. The oysters, both, dug out with her thumb after the joke. Bones go onto the rim of the plate as they come clean; the tin wiped with the last of the bread.
9. The body reported as a finding: stomach high and tight under the belt, hard as a fist, after a week of tea and water. The kitchen warm. The window over the sink black, the sodium of the street beyond it.
10. **[TURN]** "The drawer didn't lock. I didn't lock it. Thursday was in three days, and I had just eaten for the first time in a week." Its own paragraph. No interpretation after it, except the Snapper.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell first (roast fat, salt, warm kitchen), then taste, the first described taste in the book since Ch 18's tea and the one Act III has been missing: salt, crackling skin, fat, iron at the bone, bread soaked in pan juice. Temperature: the oven's heat in an unheated flat; the plate cooling; then the fat cooling on her fingers. Texture: skin, the joint giving, grease. Light by source: the one bulb, sodium at the window.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (one only, after she starts eating):**
  - Used: the oysters. "There are two small muscles on the back of a chicken ... every cook who knows about them eats them before the plate leaves the kitchen. I was the cook. Nobody was waiting."
  - (Before the food, not a joke, the bin beat, not in the prose) "I had disposed of the kit from three lessons without once looking back. I couldn't put one sheet of paper in a pedal bin."
- **The body:** First the absence of appetite (dry mouth, hands in her lap, the plate cooling), then the return. Warmth from the oven, not from her. Hands: they won't open over the bin, they turn the key one way only, then they are greasy. The wound is not touched in this scene. Stomach tight and full, reported as a finding.
- **Motifs:** The meal after (the Ch 17 chicken, eaten whole this time, unbrined). Hands (bin, key, fingers, Snapper). Vinyl (a record against the desk, unplayed, unnamed). The syllabus pages (third and fourth together in the unlocked drawer, never reproduced). Mirrors by absence. Cold, broken by the oven.
