# Beat sheet — ch27: Resolution & Aftermath

- **Unit:** ch27. Manuscript file `manuscript/ch27.md`. This is the last chapter of the book. There is no syllabus insert at the chapter head (style 08 puts inserts at 1, 6 and 17 only).
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense. The outline's Turns and Snapper are written in present tense. Render them in past tense on the page ("The drawer didn't lock. I didn't lock it.").
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Suggested split: S1 ~1,100 / S2 ~1,050 / S3 ~1,000. S3 can run slightly longer than S2 if the meal needs it. The meal is the aftermath, and aftermath gets the page time (style 04).
- **Day:** Monday 9 November, one week after Election Eve (Monday 2 November). The election was Tuesday 3 November. Thursday 12 November is "three days away". This follows the ledger's Ch 27 line and the dates proposed in the ch19, ch21, ch25 and interlude-4 beat sheets.
- **Act pressure:** Act III. "Reyes, closing on paper; the knowledge from 18 in every room." Reyes has to be felt in every scene. In S1 she is a phone call relayed by Mara, and a face Iris has already seen. In S2 she is Thursday, which Iris can't say to Hannah. In S3 she is the drawer Iris doesn't lock. The knowledge from 18 is the fourth page, face-down in that drawer, which the third page now joins.
- **Appetite at entry:** Starved and then gone. Since Sunday 1 November she has had tea and water and nothing else (ch21: "nothing since Sunday"; ch26: "I am not hungry"). She checked for the taste after Holt and there wasn't one (ch25). The cold has been the baseline since 18. The flat is unheated. The work at the morgue is only work.
- **Appetite at exit:** Returned, and never named. She eats all of it, at her own table, with her fingers. The long greedy sentence comes back for the meal and nowhere else in the chapter. The first real joke in the chapter arrives with the food, and the reader should feel the voice coming back and be frightened by it. She does not interpret it, apologise for it or plan with it. The last image is her hands.
- **Voice rules in force:**
  - The grade: the last grade in the book is Mara's, in S1, "full marks", for being afraid of her (style 09). After that beat Iris grades **nothing and no one**. That includes Hannah, herself, her own technique on the phone, and the meal. In S2 she tells the reader how good she is without giving it a mark. This is the first time in the book she grades Mara, and it is in narration, not out loud (style 07). The reader should notice the gap close.
  - Jokes: thin, one or two in S1, one in S2, which lands on herself. Then S3 holds none until the food, when one comes back. That return is the point.
  - No mirrors anywhere in the chapter. The reader supplies 27 (style 09). Her hands are the mirror. Don't let the dark window over the table reflect her, and don't name a reflective surface in S3.
  - Banned: empty, hollow, numb, beautiful, monster, "I'm not proud", "luckily". No em-dash cut-offs in dialogue.
  - Hannah's ch19 voicemail stays unplayed (ledger). Hannah must not summarise it on the call.
  - Neither the third page nor the fourth is ever reproduced or quoted (style 08). The third page is never unfolded on the page.
  - The Snapper must not tell the reader what Iris will do on Thursday. No plan, no kit, no Reyes in the last scene except as "Thursday".

## Conflicts

## Scene 1 — The Morgue

1. Monday 9 November, about 10:40 a.m., in the autopsy suite. First paragraph: Iris has a case open, an ordinary one. A man of seventy-four collapsed while raking leaves in his own garden. His heart did what hearts that age do: a fresh thrombus in the left anterior descending artery, the "widow-maker", which she names and doesn't joke about. She works well. Report the competence and give no pleasure. The smell is the old smell and it's only the smell.
2. The break-room television through the office door, the same set as Ch 1, on mute with captions. The public story: officials say the Senator's killer acted alone, a lone extremist, and the investigation continues. No description has been released. She notes, as a finding, why the story could be that simple: there was no page on him. The two earlier deaths had pages and the third didn't. The system had a body with nothing pinned to it and filed it where it wanted.
3. The replacement. The result came in on Tuesday within a point of the last polls, with a dead man's name on the ballot. On Friday the party named the man who will take what Holt won. She has to read his name twice to keep it, and doesn't give it to the reader. The machine is intact. Only the man is gone. Keep that sentence in some form. It is the outline's.
4. Holt's autopsy. The Chief did it himself, on Tuesday 3 November, with two federal observers in the room. Mara assisted. Iris was on shift that day and was not asked, and she didn't ask for the report. One line only: she knows what is in it, because she knows what her hands did. Don't render the findings. The reader has Mara's Ch 8 neck bruises and can do the rest.
5. Some of the eleven have come forward, and not all. On Friday a statement went out through one lawyer from three women, unnamed and described as "former campaign staff". Iris counts the three the way she counts towels. She is not one of them. She doesn't say what she would have said.
6. Mara is at the next table, or comes in to the sinks. She stands with the table between them, passes instruments handle-first and at full arm's length, and hasn't hummed since October. She watches Iris the way you watch a colleague you've begun to be afraid of: she glances at Iris's hands when Iris's back is turned, and Iris can feel the glance land.
7. I let her. Iris doesn't soften anything, doesn't ask Mara about her weekend and doesn't make the joke that would make it easier. Then the grade, in narration, never out loud, and it is the first time in the book she has graded Mara. Mara's watching is exact, proportionate and correct, and she is afraid of precisely the right thing. *Full marks.* It is the last grade in the book. Don't explain that, and don't follow it with any comment.
8. At the sinks, both scrubbing, Mara tells her plainly and in the flat way Iris taught her to report a finding. The FBI agent, Special Agent Reyes, rang the office at nine. The Chief is tied up with the Senator's file, and Mara keeps the rota this month, so the call came to her. Reyes asked Mara to confirm Dr. Vale's shift pattern for Thursday. Mara said days. "She said you were meant to see her last Wednesday. Before." Mara asks, "Did you know?" Iris says "No." It is true, and it is one of very few true things she says in the chapter.
9. Iris hears the voice behind Mara's report and puts a face to it: the flat shoes, the hands, the corridor (Ch 26). She doesn't tell Mara that. One line.
10. **[TURN]** Reyes has interviewed nine of the eleven. She told Mara, by way of explaining the lost week, that only two are left: a woman who has left the country, and Dr. Vale, postponed by Election Eve and rescheduled for Thursday. Iris finds this out from Mara, who was asked to confirm her shift pattern. End on the fact, and don't have Iris react. Whether Reyes let that slip or said it on purpose stays unreadable. Iris doesn't ask what else Mara told her.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell. The suite under the extraction fans: cold iron, chlorhexidine, the sweet note of a fresh case, and leaf mould on the decedent's trouser cuffs, which is the one live, outdoor smell in the room. Sound: the fans, the hose, the scale settling, and the soft squeak of Mara's clogs stopping farther from her than they used to. Temperature: the suite's chosen cold, which is now no colder than her flat. Texture: the man's calloused palms, a gardener's.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "The Chief took the case himself. He wanted, I assume, a face that had never been near politics. Mara's had now been near it twice."
  - "The machine was intact. I see that on the table most weeks: everything still in working order, and nobody home."
  - (Reserve) On Mara keeping the table between them: "She kept the table between us. That is what it's for."
- **The body:** Hands, gloved and steady on the case, and watched by Mara the way Reyes watched them in the corridor (26). Iris doesn't watch them herself in this scene. That is saved for the Snapper. She is hungry and doesn't notice. She has no lunch. A coffee goes cold untouched beside the sinks. She is five kilos down and more since Ch 19, and her belt is on the scalpel-punched hole. The left-flank scar is touched once, through the scrubs, when the television says "acted alone".
- **Motifs:** The grade (Mara's full marks, the last in the book). Hands (watched by Mara). Tape, by implication only (surgical tape on the specimen labels, as in Ch 1, one clause). The paperwork thread (strategy 09: nine of eleven, Vale on Thursday, and the system's own rota delivering her). Voicemail, by absence: Reyes rings the office, not Iris, so no fourth unreturned message.

## Scene 2 — The Sister

1. The same evening, about 6:30 p.m., in the flat. The heating is still off. She is standing at the writing desk with her coat still on when the phone lights up with HANNAH. The ch19 voicemail notification is still on the screen under the name, 1:14, unplayed. She answers. Give that its own short sentence. After the calls she let ring out in Ch 19, the answering is the event.
2. Hannah's voice, with the house behind it: the dishwasher, a tap, June somewhere singing something from school. Hannah says "There you are," not unkindly. Iris says she's been busy. She has never once said that to Hannah and been lying in quite this way.
3. Hannah asks if she got the message. Iris says "I did. Sorry." She never played it, and she tells the reader so in one line. Hannah lets it go, because Hannah always lets it go. Hannah must not say what was on the voicemail.
4. Iris asks about the girls, and the technique is shown. She uses names and details: Astrid's reading book, and whether June has lost her first tooth yet. She asks that because at six the lower central incisors go first, and she knows children by their teeth. She asks the second question that shows she listened to the first answer, and she laughs a half-beat after Hannah does. She tells the reader exactly how good she is at this, the way you'd describe a colleague's suturing. She gives no mark. The reader may hear Holt, who memorised every staffer's children's names. Iris doesn't make the connection.
5. The present. Hannah says June loved it, the microscope, and she's been looking at everything, her own hair, a dead fly from the windowsill. Iris didn't know what the present was until this sentence, because Hannah chose it and signed it *from Iris* (Ch 19). Iris says "I'm glad." Hannah chose something in Iris's register. That is how well Hannah knows her, and Iris notes it in one line and moves on.
6. Holt. Hannah brings it up the way people bring up the weather, because everyone is: "Isn't it awful. About Marchetti." Iris says "Mm," which is the Ch 1 "mm", and the reader should hear it. Hannah: June asked her whether the man on her sticker was dead, and Hannah didn't know what to say to a six-year-old.
7. Iris tells her. It is calm, true, child-safe and kind: what to say about a body stopping, what not to say about sleep, and to answer only the question June asks. It is the best advice anyone could give, and Iris has given it to a great many families. Hannah says, "That's good. That's really good. Thank you." This is the most painful beat in the chapter, so don't comment on it.
8. Hannah says Iris sounds tired. Iris says she is. Narration: it was the only thing she said all evening that wasn't technique. They begin to say goodbye. Hannah says, as she has for years, "Call me Sunday?"
9. **[TURN]** Iris begins to say "Sunday" and can't put a day on it. She doesn't know when she will be able to call back. There is a gap where the day should be, and Iris doesn't leave gaps with Hannah. She says "Soon." Hannah is quiet for one beat too long, then says "Okay," and "Love you," and it is plain that Hannah heard it. End on Hannah having heard. Don't say what Hannah thinks. She knows something has changed, not what (cast).

- **Sensory lead:** Sound. Hannah's kitchen through the phone, warm and ordinary and slightly compressed, set against the silence of Iris's flat. Temperature: the phone's warmth against her ear is the only warm thing in the room, and her other hand is cold, flat on the desk.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (this one lands on herself):**
  - On the advice about June: "I told her what to say. I have told a great many families. No one has ever complained about my wording."
  - (Reserve) "Hannah said I sounded tired. I said I was. It was the only thing I said that evening that I hadn't prepared."
- **The body:** Standing in her coat, so not settled. Hands: one holds the phone, and the other is flat on the desk a few inches from the letter to Hannah (Ch 20). The letter is still squared to the desk edge, still unsent, and she doesn't touch it or look down at it. One clause, near beat 4: she is saying the ordinary things to Hannah with the other things sealed in an envelope beside her hand. She is still unfed and doesn't mention it. Her mouth is dry. She swallows before "Soon".
- **Motifs:** Voicemail (the third, unplayed, visible on the screen as she answers, and still there after the call). Tape (one optional clause: June's microscope came wrapped in Hannah's double-taped paper, Ch 19). Hands (one at the ear, one beside the letter). The Marine, by echo only: June's sticker man, "Mm". The grade, by its absence: technique described, no mark given.

## Scene 3 — The Table

1. About 9:20 p.m. The kitchen table, her own table, where she promised herself in Ch 17 she would sit for once. On the wood is the folded page, where she put it down when she came in after the two-hour walk on Election Eve. It has lain there for a week, folded once, and it has never been opened. Beside it is a plate she made and hasn't touched.
2. The plate. She roasted a chicken this evening after the call, without deciding to: salted, the oven as hot as it goes, the skin. It is the Ch 17 recipe without the brine, cooked by her hands while she watched. Half the bird is on the plate, jointed, with bread for the juices, and the other half is in the roasting tin on the hob. The oven has warmed the kitchen, and it is the first heat in the flat since Ch 18. She notes the temperature and not the meaning.
3. Smell first, reported as a finding: rendered fat, salt, the Maillard crust, and thyme if she used it. Nothing happens in her mouth. She sits with the plate cooling for a long time and does not eat. This is the outline's "cannot eat it". Show it through the body (dry mouth, hands in her lap) and never name the absence.
4. The page. She picks it up and doesn't unfold it. She takes it to the kitchen bin, puts her foot on the pedal, the lid lifts, and her hand is over the opening with the page in it. Her hand doesn't open. This is the Ch 25 hands again, the same not-shaking, not-doing. She stands like that until the lid's hinge creaks. She takes her foot off the pedal.
5. The writing desk. One clause of inventory on the way: the letter to Hannah, squared, and against the side of the desk a record, still in its cellophane, sleeve to the room, never played. Don't explain it (style 09). Optional, author's call: let the reader see the sleeve's clock with too many hands (*Out to Lunch*, Ch 17) without the title being said. If that reads as explaining, show only "a record".
6. She turns the key, opens the drawer, and slides the third page in on top of the fourth, which is face-down where it has been since Ch 19, and on top of the Lesson Two master and the dossiers. The two pages lie together, the unpinned one on the unpinned one. She closes the drawer. The key stays in the lock. She doesn't turn it. Don't comment yet. The Turn line comes after the meal.
7. She comes back to the table and sits. She eats. The voice comes back with the food: the chapter's first and only long greedy sentence, the skin shattering and salt, the fat, the thigh joint found with a thumb, "find the place, and it gives" (an echo of the anchor's Ch 17 sternoclavicular line; the drafter should not quote it exactly). Taste, described with the precision of tissue (style 06). She eats with her fingers.
8. All of it. The half on the plate, then the other half from the tin, standing at the hob for one moment and then back to the table to sit, because she sits after. The bread through the juices. The oysters off the back. The one joke of the scene comes here, and with it the reader feels the voice returning and should be frightened by how good it sounds. The bones go on the plate, cleaned, in an order she doesn't remark on.
9. **[TURN]** The drawer didn't lock. I didn't lock it. Thursday was in three days, and I had just eaten for the first time in a week. Give it its own paragraph. There is no interpretation after it.

- **Sensory lead:** Taste, the first described taste in the book since Ch 18's tea and the one the whole of Act III has been missing. Salt, crackling skin, fat, the iron at the bone, then the bread soaked in pan juice. Before taste comes smell (roast fat and salt, the warm kitchen). Temperature: the oven's heat in an unheated flat, and the plate cooling, then the fat cooling on her fingers. Texture: the skin, the joint giving, grease. Light by source: the one bulb over the table and sodium at the window. The window does not reflect.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (one only, and only after she starts eating):**
  - On the oysters: "There are two small muscles on the back of a chicken, one in each hollow of the pelvis, and every cook who knows about them eats them before the plate leaves the kitchen. I was the cook. Nobody was waiting."
  - (Before the food, not a joke, for the bin beat) "I had disposed of the kit from three lessons without once looking back. I couldn't put one sheet of paper in a pedal bin."
- **The body:** First the absence of appetite (dry mouth, hands still in her lap, the plate cooling), then the return. She eats seated, with her fingers, all of it. Warmth for the first time since 18, from the oven, not from her. Hands: they won't open over the bin, they turn the key one way only, and then they are greasy. She doesn't touch the wound in this scene, because she isn't thinking. Her stomach after a week is tight and full, and she reports it as a finding.
- **Motifs:** The meal after (the return, and the Ch 17 chicken finally eaten, and eaten whole). Hands (the bin, the key, the fingers, the Snapper). Vinyl (the record against the writing desk, unplayed, unexplained). The syllabus pages (third and fourth together in the unlocked drawer, never reproduced). Mirrors, by absence (the reader supplies 27). Cold, broken by the oven.

## Snapper

Outline minimum: end on my hands. Do not tell the reader what to feel.

Sharpened option, past tense, for the drafter: *The fat on my fingers went white as it cooled. My hands were on the table either side of the plate, and they were steady. I looked at them for a long time.*

The fat setting is how she measures "a long time", by what matter does as it cools, which is her lifelong register (cf. Ch 19's butter set opaque). Do not add a sentence about Thursday, Reyes, the drawer or what the hands are for. The last word of the book should be on the hands or the looking, not on a meaning. No mirror, and no reflection in the window.
