# Beat sheet: ch15, The Isolation

- **Unit:** ch15. Manuscript file `manuscript/ch15.md`
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense. The outline's Turns are written in present tense, so render them in past tense on the page.
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Suggested split: S1 ~1,050 / S2 ~1,050 / S3 ~1,050. This chapter may not run long; only Ch 17 may.
- **Act pressure:** Brandt, who is now pressure as a destination rather than a threat. Every move in the chapter is aimed at him, and he is aimed back. Reyes is a faint second pressure, carried over from Ch 14 (one beat in S1). Appetite: indulged as anticipation. The planning is foreplay, and this chapter is where the reader should feel that the foreplay has started.
- **Appetite at entry:** Appetite as identity, but restrained. In Ch 14 she ate less and told the reader so ("the body knows something the plan does not"). She hums the Mingus at work. Mara texted her the album (Fri 9 Oct) and she played it. She is warm (Act II).
- **Appetite at exit:** Deliberately unfed and at its sharpest. She cooks lavishly on Monday for the woman she will be on Wednesday, and eats only the liver, standing up. On Tuesday she eats lightly by rule ("you don't eat before"). The appetite is named by the mirror ("someone going on a date") and not corrected. She is warm, her pulse is up, and she is hungry on purpose.
- **Voice rules in force:** No "beautiful": it is reserved for Ch 17. No "perfect" for the violence or the plan. No "luckily" or "fortunately": the inquiry working early is her competence, plus a suspicion, never luck. At least one joke per page. Every gate Brandt passes through is one she left open, which is the hero rule. The mirror motif belongs to this chapter ("the date"), so use it once, in S3 (the hall mirror), and nowhere else. Cam does not speak in this chapter: he is seen once, at a distance, and does not hear her.

## Scene 1: Resources Cut

1. Monday 12 Oct, 7:40 a.m., at a bus-station café on public wifi. She is on her clean laptop (the one kept apart from work and personal devices) with a burnt-tasting americano. She files an anonymous complaint with the state's data-privacy regulator, alleging unlawful interception by the shell company behind the server farm, and copies it to the abuse desk of the upstream provider that carries the farm's line. She attaches the incorporation filing from Ch 11. The officer of record is Diane Pell, deceased.
2. She is plain with the reader about the arithmetic. She doesn't need the inquiry to succeed. She needs him dark for forty-eight hours: no feeds, no cameras, no eyes in the warehouse district or anywhere his systems reach, starting before Tuesday night and running until after it.
3. The lever is Diane. Every compliance officer in the city has read about the dead woman at the reservoir, and none of them will want their company's name beside hers on a customer account for an hour longer than it has to be. She notes that the Gatekeeper is still keeping the Architect's door, this time by being dead on it.
4. She shuts the laptop, and a thought comes uninvited. She has just written to the government, and somebody else, E. Reyes, is writing to it too (from Ch 14). She considers which inbox her complaint might land in and decides it doesn't matter for forty-eight hours. Then she goes to work.
5. The morgue, a day shift. Mara is at the next table and asks, not looking up, whether she listened to it. Iris says yes, all of it, twice. Mara says nothing more and goes pink at the ears. Iris doesn't grade her, and the narration should make the reader notice that she doesn't.
6. Her own case is routine: an eighty-one-year-old man, natural causes, an enlarged heart. She weighs it on the hanging scale, 460 g, at four degrees out of the cooler. She registers the cold of it through the glove the way you register the weather, as something not yet worth a sentence.
7. 4:41 p.m. Her phone buzzes against her hip under the gown. The passive tap she planted in Ch 11 has stopped relaying its heartbeat. The upstream provider has cut the line; the server farm is dark. It has taken nine hours from the filing.
8. Blinding him has blinded her. Her tap went dark with his line, and she had not priced that in. For the next forty-eight hours she will have to see him with her own eyes, like a person. She touches the flank wound through the gown (stitches out today, day ten).
9. Distrust. Nothing on the syllabus has ever gone faster than she planned it. When things go early, in her experience, it is because someone at the other end didn't fight. She doesn't let herself finish the thought about who.
10. [TURN] She has more time than she needs, and she distrusts it, and she uses it to cook. On the way home she goes to the butcher's (Ch 7's) for a whole free-range bird with its giblets. The bird goes into a 3% brine with bay, peppercorns and lemon peel. The neck and wingtips go into a stock. She fries the liver in butter and eats it on toast, standing up at the counter, and it is the only thing she eats.

- **Sensory lead:** Taste first, since the chapter is about what she does and doesn't put in her mouth. The café americano is burnt, with the paper of the cup in it. The morgue smells of formalin and floor cleaner. Temperature: the heart at four degrees, the cooler's breath when it opens. At the stove that evening the sound is the brine salt hissing into hot water, and the smell is bay and black pepper, then the liver browning in butter, sweet and ferrous.
- **Candidate jokes:** "I had killed the officer of record, which in my experience is the fastest way to get a compliance department to return your email." / "Four hundred and sixty grams. He had a big heart. It is the commonest thing in the world, and it is almost always the thing that kills them." / "The liver was the only part of the bird I ate. I am a professional. You always taste the liver first."
- **Body:** The flank wound, touched once at 4:41; the stitches came out that morning, day ten. She remarks that the 460 g heart is cold, which is normal and is the baseline for Ch 17's thirty-seven degrees. Don't flag the comparison. Her hands at the stove are quick and happy. She eats standing up because she is planning (style 05). She eats very little, the liver only, which keeps faith with Ch 14's "I eat less".
- **Motifs:** Hands (the stove, the scale). The grade (the regulator's response time; she grades the upstream provider's abuse desk: "prompt, frightened, a B-plus"). The meal is set up here as the meal after: the chicken goes into its brine for Wednesday. Voicemail is not used in this scene.

## Scene 2: The Herding

1. Monday night. With the feed gone she has to herd him blind, from what she already knows: twenty years of a man's habits in the dossier, and eleven days of his traffic captured by her tap before it died. Herding, she tells the reader, is mostly a matter of which gates you leave open.
2. The gate she left open is the one place she did not put in the complaint. In the tap's traffic she had found every vehicle of his and named all of them except one: the media consultancy that owns the soundproofed editing suite. Its lease, its alarm contract and its building cameras are paid through a separate holding company, and the cameras route through Lantern, so they are dark now too. She left the suite out on purpose. It is the only door that hasn't closed.
3. 10:15 p.m. Monday. From a second burner she leaves a voicemail on his personal phone, posing as a business reporter from a wire service. She is calm and polite. She asks about Diane Pell's directorship of the shell company and says she'll "try you at the house tomorrow." Now his house is a place where someone might be standing on the step. She notes that this is the first message she has ever left that she knew would not be returned, which makes it the inverse of the voicemail motif.
4. Tuesday morning, before her shift. The freeze has reached the card that the shell company kept on file for his car service, so there will be no car. She counts this as a pressure she didn't have to apply, and a second one too: Holt walled him out of the War Room in Ch 14. "Holt was herding for me, for nothing. He has always been good with staff."
5. The one thing she leaves alone is the Tabard Club. It is Tuesday, his piano night. She will not touch the club, because she wants to know whether, given a choice, he picks the room where people listen to him or the room where nobody can.
6. A working day she gets through on competence: two cases, a report signed. Mara hums four bars of the Mingus at the sinks and stops when she realises she is doing it. Iris doesn't comment, which is the second time this chapter she has declined to grade her.
7. 5:30 p.m. She parks across the street from the building: a converted printworks east of the river, the consultancy on the third floor, a rear car park with a stairwell up to the suite corridor. The street is under sodium light. The stairwell door code and the suite's keypad code are both from the tap's traffic (a facilities email). She cannot run the three-clap test this time. For once she is relying on someone else's soundproofing and the acoustic contractor's spec sheet, which she has read twice, and she grades it.
8. 6:20 p.m., the hour he would leave for dinner before his Tabard set. A taxi pulls up, not his car. He gets out in charcoal, carrying a paper bag with the neck of a bottle showing and a record in a card mailer under his arm. He looks up and down the street once, not afraid, the way a man looks when he is early for a table and wonders whether she is too. He doesn't see her.
9. He lets himself in with his key. The stair light comes on behind the frosted glass, floor by floor, and goes out at the third.
10. [TURN] He went willingly. He had read the syllabus. He knew what a soundproofed room was for, and he went anyway, and she understood that this was his answer to the broken record.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound: the burner's dial tone, her own voice in a reporter's register, and on Tuesday evening the ticking of her cooling engine and the taxi's indicator across the street. Temperature: October dusk through the car window, still mild (the weather does not turn until overnight into Wednesday). Light by source: the sodium streetlight, the stair light behind frosted glass going up floor by floor. Smell: the car's cold upholstery, and her own skin, which smells of morgue soap.
- **Candidate jokes:** "Herding is mostly a matter of which gates you leave open. Sheepdogs know this. So does every HR department I have ever filed a complaint with." / On the soundproofing: "For the first time in my career I was trusting somebody else's paperwork. I read his acoustic consultant's report twice and gave it a B. The seals were good. The prose was not." / "He had a bottle. You don't bring a bottle to a place you are hiding in. You bring a bottle to a dinner."
- **Body:** Hands on the wheel, still, at ten and two, for fifty minutes. Her pulse rises when the taxi's indicator comes on, and she reports it as a finding. She has eaten nothing since the liver, which she mentions once and does not dwell on. The wound pulls slightly from sitting twisted to watch the door, and she touches it.
- **Motifs:** Vinyl (the record mailer under his arm, so the reader guesses the Mingus before the Snapper confirms it). Voicemail (she leaves one, inverted). The grade (the acoustic report; Holt as an unwitting sheepdog). Hands.

## Scene 3: Dressing

1. 7:00 p.m., home. She clears the kitchen table, lays a clean white towel on it and sets out the tools in order of use, as if they were a tasting menu: the small blade and handle first, then the small retractor, the toothed forceps, the larger blade, spare blades in their sleeve, the rib shears last. Off to the side, like cutlery set out for the table, go the kit: nitrile gloves (a box), the thin black leather gloves for the car and the suite door, the red marker, black vinyl tape, zip-ties, industrial tape, the foil sleeve for his phone, ten towels and the bags.
2. What is not on the table is a page. She has watched him on the feed reading his copy and keeping it (Ch 13). She is going to a lesson without the lesson, and she trusts him to bring it. She notices that she trusts him and doesn't take it back.
3. She rolls the instruments into their canvas roll the way you roll a good knife roll, ties it and sets it in the bag. Then she goes to shower. The shower is hot and short, because the forty-minute shower is for after (Ch 7). The flank wound is closed now, a pink seam whose stitches she took out herself yesterday, on the tenth day. She washes around it and then, deliberately, over it.
4. Hands: a surgical scrub with nail brush and soap, two minutes a hand, by the clock. Nails filed short and square. She sees, without remarking on it, that she has done what he does with his own (Ch 17: "filed square").
5. Food: "you don't eat before." She names it as a rule from a different kind of evening, and also, she notes, the rule for the patient, not the surgeon. About 7:30 she drinks a cup of Monday's stock, hot, salted, golden with fat on top, and eats one heel of bread. In the fridge the chicken sits in its brine, pale, waiting for Wednesday.
6. Dressing: a black silk shirt she can push to the elbow, black wool trousers, flat shoes with a good sole for stairs. The shirt hangs loose over the scar. Her hair is washed and pinned up, because she will be working. No jewellery, for the obvious reason.
7. Then lipstick, which she does not wear to work, in oxblood. She gives the reader a reason (the mouth is the part of a face that gives the least away, and a colour on it gives it something to do). The reason is plausible and she does not believe it, and she reports that she doesn't.
8. She goes through the bag one last time: the roll, the kit, the codes to the stairwell door and the suite keypad written nowhere because she has them by heart, her car keys. Her pulse is up and her face is warm. She reports both as findings and doesn't put them down to nerves.
9. [TURN] The hall mirror on the way out. In the mirror she looked like someone going on a date. She said so, out loud, to the empty flat. She didn't correct it.

- **Sensory lead:** Texture first: the towel's nap under the steel, the cold of the rib shears' handles, silk on skin that has just been scrubbed. Then taste: the stock, salt and fat and the faint sweetness of the neck bones, and the lipstick's plain waxy taste when she presses her lips together (unscented; she owns no scented products). Temperature: the hot shower, then the cool silk, then the warmth in her own face. Smell: the brine when she opens the fridge (bay, lemon peel, the raw bird), and her own clean skin, unscented soap and nothing else.
- **Candidate jokes:** "The rib shears were the cheese course: heavy, optional, and the real reason you book." / "Nil by mouth is an instruction to the patient. I had never in my life followed one." / "No jewellery. Rings tear gloves, and I was not going to a place where I could change them." (Draft in past tense; the joke is that she then does exactly that in Ch 17.)
- **Body:** Showered, scrubbed and hungry on purpose. The scar is touched under the water. Her hands are clean and steady, with nails filed short. She has eaten lightly (stock and one heel of bread), and the pull of the stomach against the waistband is reported as a pleasure. She is warm. Her pulse is raised at the mirror.
- **Motifs:** Mirrors, the first of three (15, the date; hall mirror). Hands (scrub, nails). Tape (the black vinyl roll on the towel, among the courses). The meal after, set up and waiting (the brined chicken). The grade (she grades her own preparation once and stops herself). Vinyl is held for the Snapper.
