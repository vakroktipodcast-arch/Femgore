## Scene 1: Resources Cut

1. Monday 12 Oct, about 6:15 a.m., the flat, bathroom basin. The stitches come out first: seven simple interrupted 4-0 nylon, tails long, snipped and drawn with the suture scissors and Adson forceps from the drawer under the sink. Day ten. The seam is pink and shut. She files it in one line and moves on (cheapest beat in the chapter; the wound gets its own mention at 4:41).
2. 7:40 a.m., a bus-station café on public wifi. She is on her clean laptop (the one kept apart from work and personal devices) with a burnt-tasting americano. She files an anonymous complaint with the state's data-privacy regulator, alleging unlawful interception by the shell company behind the server farm, and copies it to the abuse desk of the upstream provider that carries the farm's line. She attaches the incorporation filing from Ch 11. The officer of record is Diane Pell, deceased.
3. She is plain with the reader about the arithmetic. She doesn't need the inquiry to succeed. She needs him dark for forty-eight hours: no feeds, no cameras, no eyes in the warehouse district or anywhere his systems reach, starting before Tuesday night and running until after it.
4. The lever is Diane. Every compliance officer in the city has read about the dead woman at the reservoir, and none of them will want their company's name beside hers on a customer account for an hour longer than it has to be. She notes that the Gatekeeper is still keeping the Architect's door, this time by being dead on it.
5. She shuts the laptop, and a thought comes uninvited. She has just written to the government, and somebody else, E. Reyes, is writing to it too (from Ch 14). She considers which inbox her complaint might land in and decides it doesn't matter for forty-eight hours. Then she goes to work.
6. The morgue, a day shift. Mara is at the next table and asks, not looking up, whether she listened to it. Iris says yes, all of it, twice. Mara says nothing more and goes pink at the ears. Iris doesn't grade her, and the narration should make the reader notice that she doesn't. Her own case is routine: an eighty-one-year-old man, natural causes, an enlarged heart. She weighs it on the hanging scale, 460 g, at four degrees out of the cooler, and registers the cold of it through the glove the way you register the weather, as something not yet worth a sentence.
7. 4:41 p.m. Her phone buzzes against her hip under the gown. The passive tap she planted in Ch 11 has stopped relaying its heartbeat. The upstream provider has cut the line; the server farm is dark. It has taken nine hours from the filing. She grades the abuse desk: prompt, frightened, a B-plus.
8. Blinding him has blinded her. Her tap went dark with his line, and she had not priced that in. The War Room feed still runs but he is no longer in that room (Ch 14), so it is no use for him. The bedroom and record-wall units at his house are on his own broadband, which the farm carries, so they went dark at the same minute (proposed value; see New facts). For the next forty-eight hours she will have to see him with her own eyes, like a person. She touches the flank wound through the gown.
9. Distrust. Nothing on the syllabus has ever gone faster than she planned it. When things go early, in her experience, it is because someone at the other end didn't fight. She doesn't let herself finish the thought about who.
10. [TURN] She has more time than she needs, and she distrusts it, and she uses it to cook. On the way home she goes to the butcher's (Ch 7's) for a whole free-range bird with its giblets. The bird goes into a 3% brine with bay, peppercorns and lemon peel, in a lidded tub with a plate weighing it down. The neck and wingtips go into a stock. She fries the liver in butter and eats it on toast, standing up at the counter, and it is the only thing she eats.

- **Sensory lead:** Taste first, since the chapter is about what she does and doesn't put in her mouth. The café americano is burnt, with the paper of the cup in it. The morgue smells of formalin and floor cleaner. Temperature: the heart at four degrees, the cooler's breath when it opens. At the stove that evening the sound is the brine salt hissing into hot water, and the smell is bay and black pepper, then the liver browning in butter, sweet and ferrous.
- **Candidate jokes:** "I had killed the officer of record, which in my experience is the fastest way to get a compliance department to return your email." / "Four hundred and sixty grams. He had a big heart. It is the commonest thing in the world, and it is almost always the thing that kills them." / "The liver was the only part of the bird I ate. I am a professional. You always taste the liver first." / On the stitches: "Seven knots, tails left long so I could find them. I am the only surgeon I know who has ever had to read her own handwriting off her own flank."
- **Body:** The flank wound: stitches out at 6:15, touched once at 4:41. The 460 g heart is cold, which is normal and is the baseline for Ch 17's thirty-seven degrees; don't flag the comparison. Her hands at the stove are quick and happy. She eats standing up because she is planning (style 05). She eats very little, the liver only, which keeps faith with Ch 14's "I eat less". Warm, not cold.
- **Motifs:** Hands (the stove, the scale, the suture scissors). The grade (abuse desk B-plus; Mara not graded). Tape: none required (surgical tape on specimen labels at most, one clause). The meal is set up here as the meal after: the chicken goes into its brine for Wednesday. Voicemail is not used in this scene.

## Scene 2: The Herding

1. Monday night. With the feed gone she has to herd him blind, from what she already knows: twenty years of a man's habits in the dossier, and eleven days of his traffic captured by her tap before it died. Herding, she tells the reader, is mostly a matter of which gates you leave open.
2. The gate she left open is the one place she did not put in the complaint. In the tap's relayed summaries (set to carry mail from facilities and property senders in full) she had found every vehicle of his and named all of them except one: the media consultancy that owns the soundproofed editing suite. Its lease, its alarm contract and its building cameras are paid through a separate holding company, and the cameras route through Lantern, so they are dark now too. The same mail gave her the stairwell and keypad codes, the acoustic contractor's spec sheet and the floor plan with the cable grommets along the edit desk. She left the suite out on purpose. It is the only door that hasn't closed.
3. 10:15 p.m. Monday. From a second burner she leaves a voicemail on his personal phone, posing as a business reporter from a wire service. She is calm and polite. She asks about Diane Pell's directorship of the shell company and says she'll "try you at the house tomorrow." Now his house is a place where someone might be standing on the step. This is a message she means to go unreturned, which makes it the inverse of the voicemail motif. It is not a fourth of the three; don't number it with them.
4. Tuesday morning, before her shift. She cannot see whether the freeze has reached the card the shell company kept on file for his car service, so she reasons it: the account is paid through the shell, and a frozen shell does not pay for cars. She counts this as a pressure she didn't have to apply, and a second one too: Holt walled him out of the War Room in Ch 14. "Holt was herding for me, for nothing. He has always been good with staff." The inference is confirmed only by the taxi at 6:20 p.m.
5. The one thing she leaves alone is the Tabard Club. It is Tuesday, his piano night (set 9 to 11). She will not touch the club, because she wants to know whether, given a choice, he picks the room where people listen to him or the room where nobody can.
6. A working day she gets through on competence: two cases, a report signed. Mara hums four bars of the Mingus at the sinks and stops when she realises she is doing it. Iris doesn't comment, which is the second time this chapter she has declined to grade her.
7. 5:30 p.m. She parks across the street from the building: a converted printworks east of the river, the consultancy on the third floor, a rear car park with a stairwell up to the suite corridor. The street is under sodium light. She cannot run the three-clap test this time. For once she is relying on someone else's soundproofing and the acoustic contractor's spec sheet, which she has read twice, and she grades it. The tub of isopropyl wipes is in the driver's door pocket where it lives.
8. 6:20 p.m., the hour he would leave for dinner before his Tabard set. A taxi pulls up, not his car. He gets out in charcoal, carrying a paper bag with the neck of a bottle showing and a record in a card mailer under his arm. He looks up and down the street once, not afraid, the way a man looks when he is early for a table and wonders whether she is too. He doesn't see her.
9. He lets himself in with his key. The stair light comes on behind the frosted glass, floor by floor, and goes out at the third.
10. [TURN] He went willingly. He had read the syllabus. He knew what a soundproofed room was for, and he went anyway, and she understood that this was his answer to the broken record.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound: the burner's dial tone, her own voice in a reporter's register, and on Tuesday evening the ticking of her cooling engine and the taxi's indicator across the street. Temperature: October dusk through the car window, still mild (the weather does not turn until overnight into Wednesday). Light by source: the sodium streetlight, the stair light behind frosted glass going up floor by floor. Smell: the car's cold upholstery, and her own skin, which smells of morgue soap.
- **Candidate jokes:** "Herding is mostly a matter of which gates you leave open. Sheepdogs know this. So does every HR department I have ever filed a complaint with." / On the soundproofing: "For the first time in my career I was trusting somebody else's paperwork. I read his acoustic consultant's report twice and gave it a B. The seals were good. The prose was not." / "He had a bottle. You don't bring a bottle to a place you are hiding in. You bring a bottle to a dinner."
- **Body:** Hands on the wheel, still, at ten and two, for fifty minutes. Her pulse rises when the taxi's indicator comes on, and she reports it as a finding. She has eaten nothing since the liver, which she mentions once and does not dwell on. The wound pulls slightly from sitting twisted to watch the door, and she touches it. Warm car, warm face.
- **Motifs:** Vinyl (the record mailer under his arm, so the reader guesses the Mingus before the Snapper confirms it; the Dolphy is the broken one, so this is the other). Voicemail (she leaves one, inverted). The grade (the acoustic report; Holt as an unwitting sheepdog; Mara not graded). Hands. Tape: none.

## Scene 3: Dressing

1. 7:00 p.m., home. She clears the kitchen table, lays a clean white towel on it and sets out the tools in order of use, as if they were a tasting menu: the small handle and blade first, then the small retractor, the toothed forceps, the larger handle and blade, spare blades in their sleeve, the rib shears last. Off to the side, like cutlery set out for the table, go the kit: a box of nitrile gloves, the thin black leather gloves for the car and the suite door, the red marker, a steel pin, black vinyl tape, zip-ties, industrial tape, two foil sleeves (his phone's and her own), ten towels and the bags.
2. What is not on the table is a page. She has watched him on the feed reading his copy and keeping it (Ch 13). She is going to a lesson without the lesson, and she trusts him to bring it. She notices that she trusts him and doesn't take it back.
3. She rolls the instruments into their canvas roll the way you roll a good knife roll, ties it and sets it in the bag. Then she goes to shower. The shower is hot and short, because the forty-minute shower is for after (Ch 7). The flank wound is a closed pink seam, its stitches taken out by her own hand yesterday, on the tenth day. She washes around it and then, deliberately, over it.
4. Hands: a surgical scrub with nail brush and soap, two minutes a hand, by the clock. Nails filed short and square. She sees, without remarking on it, that she has done what he does with his own (Ch 17: "filed square").
5. Food: "you don't eat before." She names it as a rule from a different kind of evening, and also, she notes, the rule for the patient, not the surgeon. About 7:30 she drinks a cup of Monday's stock, hot, salted, golden with fat on top, and eats one heel of bread. In the fridge the chicken sits in its brine, pale, waiting for Wednesday.
6. Dressing: a black silk shirt she can push to the elbow, black wool trousers, flat shoes with a good sole for stairs. The shirt hangs loose over the scar. Her hair is washed and pinned up, because she will be working. No jewellery, for the obvious reason.
7. Then lipstick, which she does not wear to work, in oxblood. She gives the reader a reason (the mouth is the part of a face that gives the least away, and a colour on it gives it something to do). The reason is plausible and she does not believe it, and she reports that she doesn't.
8. She goes through the bag one last time: the roll, the kit, her own phone sealed in its foil sleeve, the codes to the stairwell door and the suite keypad written nowhere because she has them by heart, her car keys. Her pulse is up and her face is warm. She reports both as findings and doesn't put them down to nerves.
9. [TURN] The hall mirror on the way out. In the mirror she looked like someone going on a date. She said so, out loud, to the empty flat. She didn't correct it.

- **Sensory lead:** Texture first: the towel's nap under the steel, the cold of the rib shears' handles, silk on skin that has just been scrubbed. Then taste: the stock, salt and fat and the faint sweetness of the neck bones, and the lipstick's plain waxy taste when she presses her lips together (unscented; she owns no scented products). Temperature: the hot shower, then the cool silk, then the warmth in her own face. Smell: the brine when she opens the fridge (bay, lemon peel, the raw bird), and her own clean skin, unscented soap and nothing else.
- **Candidate jokes:** "The rib shears were the cheese course: heavy, optional, and the real reason you book." / "Nil by mouth is an instruction to the patient. I had never in my life followed one." / "No jewellery. Rings tear gloves, and a woman who is going to be elbow-deep in something should not be wearing anything she'd mind losing in it." / "I had laid a place for him at my own table, and he was not going to be there, which is the usual arrangement with my guests."
- **Body:** Showered, scrubbed and hungry on purpose. The scar is touched under the water. Her hands are clean and steady, with nails filed short. She has eaten lightly (stock and one heel of bread), and the pull of the stomach against the waistband is reported as a pleasure. She is warm. Her pulse is raised at the mirror.
- **Motifs:** Mirrors, the first of three (15, the date; hall mirror; 19 the body; 20 work). Hands (scrub, nails). Tape (the black vinyl roll on the towel, among the courses). The meal after, set up and waiting (the brined chicken). The grade (she grades her own preparation once and stops herself; Mara not mentioned). Vinyl is held for the Snapper.
