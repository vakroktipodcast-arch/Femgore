## Scene 1 — Egress

1. The first sentence is the service door closing behind her in the dark of the service passage: her left hand on it, the torn sticker beside her palm (corridor-side, per the ledger), the third knock still coming through two doors. No gap from 25. She counts as she goes, because the clock she has been keeping since 21 has not stopped.
2. Stair C: concrete treads, a painted steel rail, sodium-orange emergency lighting at each landing. She goes down at a walk, not a run. A run is a sound, and sounds carry in a stairwell. One flight, and the closet door is reached in thirty-one seconds, the figure she paced in Ch 20. The wound is quiet on the flat (as 25 left it) and pulls on the left only on the steps, in time.
3. The housekeeping closet, one floor down on two. Shelves of folded linen, the par sheet taped inside the door, a chemical smell. The catering jacket is where she put it back at about 6:15: folded at the bottom of the stack of round ivory cloths not due until a Wednesday wedding, over the plain black tote. Her Ch 21 bag and the campaign's box of press folders are where she left them. She doesn't feel relief. She notes that it is all there, the way she would note an instrument on a tray.
4. The change, done as muscle memory, in about ninety seconds she doesn't time. The gloves come off, both pairs, one turned inside the other, into the tote (narrated as "the gloves", not by hand). The navy blazer comes off. The third page, folded once down the middle and carrying its old thirds creases, moves from the blazer's left inside breast pocket to the inside left breast pocket of the catering jacket, empty since she took the kit out of it at 6:15, over the sternum. Into the tote, the blazer, the lanyard and the RUTH CALDER badge go first, face-in. Then the hard case with the capped syringe (out of the blazer's inside pocket), the red marker, the pin, the unused vinyl roll (from the right outer pocket), the scalpel (from the right inside pocket, guard on), the fingernail-sized scrap of seal label (right outer pocket) and, last, the Ch 21 bag from the shelf. No zip-ties: the long one is still on Holt. Her hair was put up by feel in 25; here she pulls it back lower with a band from her wrist, which is what catering staff do. The cloth stack is squared back the way she found it. The press-folder box stays on its shelf, squared, the only thing of hers left in the room.
5. A check of the closet the way she checked the green room: the shelf, the floor, the handle. She closes the door with the side of her wrist and her sleeve over it, out of morgue habit. You open doors with your elbow when your gloves are dirty, and her hands have been doing it for fifteen years without asking her.
6. Back into the stair and down a second flight. Above her, through the shaft, the sound changes: the knock stops, then a voice says his name in a different register (25 ended on "his name. Not the title."), then several voices at once, and a radio squalls and is answered. That is the breach. She is two floors below it. She doesn't speed up.
7. She comes out of the stair onto the dock level through a fire door with a push bar, opened with her hip and her left hand. It closes behind her on its closer. She keeps counting from the moment it latches, because counting is the only thing her body is still volunteering to do.
8. Back of house at street level: a concrete corridor painted to waist height, roll cages of linen, a stack of hotel pans, a time clock, the dock shutter half down onto the cold. The dock office is a glass booth on the left. A man in a hotel security fleece is inside with his back to the corridor, and the radio sits in its cradle on the desk.
9. **[TURN]** The radio, as she passes the booth glass: "Security, all stations. Lockdown, lockdown. Stairs and lifts are locked. Hold all staff where they are." She hears the maglocks on the stair door behind her thunk home. She was on the wrong side of it by eleven seconds. She kept walking.

- **Sensory lead:** sound. The knock through two doors. Her own shoes on concrete treads, kept soft. The shaft carrying the breach down to her, voices first and then the radio. The fire door's closer hissing and latching. The maglock's thunk. Then the radio's words, flat and procedural. Second: smell in the closet (laundered cotton, the bleach-and-citrus of housekeeping chemicals, rubber matting). Third: temperature. The stair is colder than the hotel-warm green room (25: "Through the door I could feel the stairwell, colder than the room"), and the dock corridor is colder again where the half-down shutter lets November in.
- **Jokes / dry observations (one, possibly two):**
  - On counting: "I had been counting since the service door. It was the only part of me still taking instructions."
  - On the closet, reserve: "Hotels keep their linen better than hospitals keep their dead. It is folded, labelled, and nobody has to sign for it."
- **The body:** the left-flank wound pulling on the steps down only (a carry-over from 25, where it was the only thing that hurt and stopped when her hands came away). A knee that remembers the green-room carpet, side unsaid, one clause at most. Hands doing the work by habit: left hand on the doors and bars, wrist and sleeve on the closet handle. After the jacket change her right hand drops out of the narration completely (see note 3). Do not mention it. Hands steady, reported once or not at all. No hunger, and food isn't mentioned. Cold rising as she goes down ("cold for weeks", 25's line, may echo in a clause). Breath steady. She is tired in the thighs and the backs of the knees, a tiredness she would recognise on a decedent from the lactate.
- **Motifs:** tape (the torn sticker beside her left palm in beat 1, the last time she touches it; the vinyl roll unused in the tote); hands (morgue-habit doors; the right hand's silence begins); the minute (her clock, still running, now counting doors); the syllabus page (moved, folded, not looked at, never reproduced); the meal after (absent, not mentioned).

## Scene 2 — The Corridor

1. Past the dock office the service corridor runs towards the vendor gate: a long straight run of painted block, fluorescent tubes, one of them flickering, a mop bucket against the wall, a laminated sign about manual handling. She walks at the pace of staff going off shift who don't want to be kept back. She carries the tote on her left shoulder. A porter and two servers with trays are coming along it towards her, in the same black jackets she is wearing, the wordmark on each breast, which is the point of the jacket; when the walkie-talkie voices carry down from the lift-lobby end they flatten to the wall, trays up, and she passes them without a look from either side.
2. Behind the three of them, a woman comes the other way from the service-lift lobby, heading towards the stairs and the radio. The lobby opens off the corridor ahead of Iris, and whoever is on a phone in it, at its far end, round the corner, is out of her sight; she does not pass him and does not know he is there. The woman is walking fast, a stride short of a run, which is a skill: the corridor has people in it and she does not yet know what she is running to. Mid-forties. Flat shoes. A dark trouser suit cut a little long on the right. A visitor lanyard turned round backwards, the way every lanyard turns itself round.
3. The walk, read in one clause and then in a long one, as Iris reads anything coming through the doors at work. The right arm swings less than the left. The right elbow stays close. The hips are faintly bladed, as if the right side is carrying something it has carried for twenty years and will carry tonight whether or not it's there.
4. No earpiece. No Service lapel pin. Not hotel, not campaign. Iris does the arithmetic before the woman is level with her: the only federal person in this building not on the Secret Service's channel is the one on the security annex distribution list and on page four of the run of show, the FBI liaison.
5. Half a second, level with each other. She looks at Iris's hands. Iris looks at hers. (Iris reports the look and does not say which of her own hands it went to. She doesn't know. Hold this.)
6. Reyes's hands, the long sentence, the reflex coming back. Short nails, unpainted. A callus in the web between right thumb and forefinger, the grip of twenty years of range qualifications. A biro mark on the inside of the right middle finger where the pen sits. On the left, no ring, and a faint pale line at the base of the ring finger (reported once, one clause, never again in this chapter or the next). They are the hands of someone who writes things down. The paperwork thread becomes flesh for one sentence. Iris feels it the way she would feel a finding.
7. Her face, briefly and without grading. Iris notes that her own face is in no file Reyes has. The ME's office doesn't release photographs of its pathologists, and the RUTH CALDER badge carries none. By Saturday's list Reyes has had a control number for her and, by inference, a surname; tonight she has the face and cannot attach it to either. Iris knows hers and isn't sure that makes them even.
8. She reaches for a grade and doesn't give one. One line: there was no page for Reyes, and for the first time Iris had the feeling the marking ran the other way.
9. **[TURN]** She didn't stop. Iris kept walking and didn't look back, and knew exactly who she was, and tells the reader before the interlude can: Special Agent E. Reyes. She had never known what the E stood for. She still doesn't. An initial had a face now. Reyes breaks into a run only after she has passed; Iris hears the flat soles change behind her and does not turn.

- **Sensory lead:** sound, then sight. Two pairs of flat soles on concrete, out of step and then in step for one stride as they pass; the servers' trays shifting against the wall. The fluorescent tube's tick. The radio, now behind Iris, repeating. Then sight, which is unusual for her: Reyes is read by gait and hands before smell. Smell comes last and small, as Reyes passes: cold air on a wool suit, and coffee. A campaign smell, urn coffee, and Iris recognises the brand of tiredness.
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - On the walk: "I have opened three police officers. All three had the same thickening of the skin over the right iliac crest, where twenty years of holster had been filing a complaint. I wasn't going to get to check. I'd have bet on it." (Use this one.)
  - On the lanyard, reserve: "Her lanyard had turned itself round, the way they all do. I didn't need the front. I had read the back of her for a month."
- **The body:** her legs keep the same pace, which is the competence in this scene: she doesn't slow, speed up or step aside more than staff would. The left hand stays on the tote strap. The right hand isn't narrated at all. Her mouth is dry. There is a pulse she notices in her neck, the first thing her body has volunteered since 24, and it isn't hunger. She doesn't name what it is. The wound is quiet. No tremor.
- **Motifs:** hands (the scene's whole exchange is four hands; hers stay unnamed); the grade (withheld from Reyes, and the marking felt to be reversed); the paperwork thread (the biro mark, the control number, the initial); the caterer's wordmark (the servers' jackets, the camouflage that works); voicemail, sideways, optional (in one clause, the woman three years ago also wanted "to talk", and Reyes will too. Use only if it stays one clause. Iris doesn't know of any interview booking in this chapter; she hears of the Wed 4 Nov slot only from Mara on 9 Nov, so keep any day out).

## Scene 3 — Out

1. The vendor gate at the end of the corridor: a steel pedestrian gate beside the dock shutter, with a card reader on the outside and a green request-to-exit button on the inside. She noted it on the floor plan in Ch 20, where fire code requires a way out. She presses it with the knuckle of her left hand, the cuff of the catering jacket pulled down over it, out of the same habit as the doors. The lock drops. She walks through a few minutes after nine (no clock read) and doesn't wait to hear it close. (The closing is held back for the Snapper.)
2. The fire-code line, dry, and the scene's one hard joke: a building has to let you out. She has done the autopsies on the ones that didn't.
3. The street. The cross street behind the hotel: bins, a catering van with its doors open, steam from a grate. Then the avenue. The city unchanged: traffic, a bus, two men arguing pleasantly outside a bar, a woman walking a dog in a coat. A woman in a catering jacket walking home at nine on a Monday is the least interesting thing in any city, and Iris knows it. It is the injury from Ch 2 working for her one last time, and she doesn't say so.
4. No cab. The reason is given as procedure, briefly: a cab is a record, a rideshare is a record with a map, and she knows what investigators look for because she has spent fifteen years telling them. So she walks. Just under two hours, about nine kilometres, by streets she picks for being ordinary.
5. One image of the night going on without him. Through a bar window there is a television with the ballroom feed: an empty lectern, flags, a crowd, and a banner along the bottom saying remarks delayed. Nobody in the bar is watching it. She doesn't stop either. (The city stays unchanged. The news hasn't broken, or it has and nobody here has looked up. Don't resolve which. This seeds Ch 27's "the machine is intact; only the man is gone.")
6. The walk in the body. She is cold in a thin jacket at about five degrees, a dry night, no coat, and doesn't do anything about it. The wound settles into a dull, regular ache. Her feet are in the flat black shoes she wore under the press outfit, which pass for catering shoes. She can feel a blister starting at the heel (optional echo of Ch 2's heel blister; if it does not reach the Ch 2 prose, drop the echo and keep the blister). One clause, with no comment.
7. Food going by as smell only: a kebab shop's vent (lamb fat on a spit, cumin), a bakery shut for the night breathing yeast through its grille. In Ch 2 she ate lamb cutlets in a stairwell. Here the smell arrives, is identified correctly, and goes. She reports it and nothing follows.
8. The knowledge from 18, at most one line: she is walking towards a locked drawer with her own page in it, face-down, and carrying the third page, folded once, still creased in thirds.
9. Halfway, at a crossing, waiting for the light, she looks down, and for the first time since the closet the right hand is narrated. It is flat across her chest, bare, over the inside pocket, pressing the folded page through the jacket. It has been there since the closet, as it was at the green-room door in 25, except that then she knew. She has held it the whole way, the way she held her left hand with her right in the lift in Ch 2, two fingers and then the whole palm.
10. **[TURN]** She realises it then, and leaves the hand where it is. She was not hungry. She notes that she would once have called that a finding.

- **Sensory lead:** temperature. The cold off the street when the gate opens, cold through the thin jacket for nearly two hours, the cold of the crossing-button's steel through the cuff over her knuckle. Then smell (vent, yeast, exhaust, a wet-leaf gutter), offered and declined. Sound: the city's ordinary noise, and the absence of sirens near her. If sirens come, they are far off and going the other way. Texture: the page's folds felt through two layers of cloth under her bare palm, at the Turn and not before (the middle fold, and the thirds under it).
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Fire code requires a building to let you out. I have done the autopsies on the buildings that didn't." (Use this one.)
  - On the bar television, reserve: "Somewhere behind me a room full of people were being asked to be patient with a man. They were very good at it. They'd had practice."
- **The body:** walking for nearly two hours. Cold, accepted. The wound aching regularly. A blister starting at the heel. Her stomach is not mentioned until the Turn, and then once. The right hand on the page is the scene's revelation. The left hand holds the tote strap and presses the gate and crossing buttons with a knuckle under the cuff. Hands steady, no tremor.
- **Motifs:** hands (the lift in Ch 2, made explicit; the last hands image before Ch 27's); the meal after (smells declined, then "not hungry"); the syllabus page (held, folded, still unread); the grade (absent; she gives the finding to herself and doesn't grade it); the minute (her clock stops without being mentioned, and the Snapper confirms it); cold (continuous, as in 25).

## Snapper

Taken (ledger: drafters take each sheet's sharpened Snapper), told in the past perfect after the Turn because it happened about an hour earlier: *Behind me, an hour back, the vendor gate had closed on its timer, unhurried. I hadn't counted it.*

The "hadn't counted it" closes the clock she has been keeping since Ch 21 (thirty-one seconds, eleven seconds, and the rest). The gate's timer is nineteen seconds and she knows it from the floor plan; she still doesn't count it. The first door in five chapters she didn't time is the one that let her go. It is an admission, not a cliffhanger. It is the chapter's last line, and the gate is closing *behind* her, not in front of anyone else.
