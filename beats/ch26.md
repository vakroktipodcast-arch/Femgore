# Beat sheet — ch26: The Escape

- **Unit:** ch26 (`manuscript/ch26.md`)
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense. The outline's Turn and Snapper lines are in present tense. Render them in past tense ("I was on the wrong side of it by eleven seconds. I kept walking.").
- **Target words:** ~3,150. Suggested split: Scene 1 ~1,100, Scene 2 ~900, Scene 3 ~1,150. Scene 2 is the shortest because the encounter lasts half a second. Its length comes from how Iris reads the half-second afterwards, not from slowing the corridor down.
- **Act pressure:** Act III. Reyes closing on paper; the knowledge from 18 in every room. In 26 Reyes stops being paper and becomes a body for the first time, in Scene 2. Scene 1 carries her as the system: the radio, the lockdown, and the RUTH CALDER volunteer badge Iris printed herself, a borrowed roster line under a real no-show's name, with nothing written to the database and no photograph. Scene 3 carries her as a face Iris can't stop seeing and the arithmetic of what each woman now has of the other. The knowledge from 18 is waiting at home: the fourth page, face-down in the locked drawer under the third page's empty place, which she is walking towards for two hours. Allow one line at most.
- **Appetite at entry:** Gone, unnamed. She checked for the taste in 25 and there wasn't one. No meal is planned. Her last food was black coffee and a heel of bread on Sunday 1 Nov in the morning (Ch 20 S3: "You don't eat before").
- **Appetite at exit:** Still gone, and now admitted once, exactly as the outline has it: "I was not hungry." She adds that she would once have called that a finding. No food is eaten in this chapter. Food passes her in the street and she reports it as smell and nothing more. This sets up Ch 27, where she eats "for the first time in a week."
- **Register:** The voice comes partly back, because she is doing what she is good at. Competent, exhausted, muscle memory (hero rule: every door she goes through is one she opens, and nothing unlocks itself for her). Sentences are still mostly plain and short. The long sentence comes back once, in Scene 2, for Reyes's hands, because Iris can't help reading a body, and it should feel like a reflex coming back. The jokes stay thin, the Act III ratio: one or two per scene, dry, short. There is no syllabus voice and no grade. In Scene 2 she reaches for a grade for Reyes and doesn't give one. No "numb", "empty", "hollow", "lucky" or "beautiful" (style 11).

## Scene 1 — Egress

1. The first sentence is the service door closing behind her in the dark of the service passage: her left hand on it, the torn sticker against her palm, the third knock still coming through two doors. No gap from 25. She counts as she goes, because the clock she has been keeping since 21 has not stopped.
2. Stair C: concrete, a painted steel rail, sodium-orange emergency lighting at each landing. She goes down at a walk, not a run. A run is a sound, and sounds carry in a stairwell. One flight. The wound pulls on each step down, on the left, in time.
3. The housekeeping closet, one floor down on two. Shelves of folded linen, the par sheet taped inside the door, a chemical smell. The catering jacket is where she put it back at about 6:15: folded at the bottom of the stack of round ivory cloths not due until a Wednesday wedding, over the plain black tote. She doesn't feel relief. She notes that it is there, the way she would note an instrument on a tray.
4. The change, done as muscle memory, in about ninety seconds she doesn't time. The navy blazer comes off. The third page, folded in thirds, moves from the blazer's left inside breast pocket to the inside left breast pocket of the catering jacket, empty since she took the kit out of it at 6:15, over the sternum. The black outer gloves come off, peeled inside-out one into the other; the pale nitrile stays on. The blazer, the lanyard, the RUTH CALDER badge and the balled black gloves go into the tote, face-in. The kit (the hard case with the capped syringe, the red marker, the pin, the unused vinyl roll, the zip-ties) goes under them. She pulls her hair back with a band from her wrist, which is what catering staff do. The cloth stack is squared back the way she found it.
5. A check of the closet the way she checked the green room: the shelf, the floor, the handle. She closes the door with the side of her wrist and her sleeve over it, out of morgue habit. You open doors with your elbow when your gloves are dirty, and her hands have been doing it for fifteen years without asking her.
6. Back into the stair and down a second flight. Above her, through the shaft, the sound changes: the knock stops, then a voice says his name in a different register, then several voices at once, and a radio squalls and is answered. That is the breach. She is two floors below it. She doesn't speed up.
7. She comes out of the stair onto the dock level through a fire door with a push bar, opened with her hip and her left hand. It closes behind her on its closer. She keeps counting from the moment it latches, because counting is the only thing her body is still volunteering to do.
8. Back of house at street level: a concrete corridor painted to waist height, roll cages of linen, a stack of hotel pans, a time clock, the dock shutter half down onto the cold. The dock office is a glass booth on the left. A man in a hotel security fleece is inside with his back to the corridor, and the radio sits in its cradle on the desk.
9. **[TURN]** The radio, as she passes the booth glass: "Security, all stations. Lockdown, lockdown. Stairs and lifts are locked. Hold all staff where they are." She hears the maglocks on the stair door behind her thunk home. She was on the wrong side of it by eleven seconds. She kept walking.

- **Sensory lead:** sound. The knock through two doors. Her own shoes on concrete treads, kept soft. The shaft carrying the breach down to her, voices first and then the radio. The fire door's closer hissing and latching. The maglock's thunk. Then the radio's words, flat and procedural. Second: smell in the closet (laundered cotton, the bleach-and-citrus of housekeeping chemicals, rubber matting). Third: temperature. The stair is colder than the hotel-warm green room, and the dock corridor is colder again where the half-down shutter lets November in.
- **Jokes / dry observations (one, possibly two):**
  - On counting: "I had been counting since the service door. It was the only part of me still taking instructions."
  - On the closet, reserve: "Hotels keep their linen better than hospitals keep their dead. It is folded, labelled, and nobody has to sign for it."
- **The body:** the left-flank wound pulling on every step down (a carry-over from 25, where it was the only thing that hurt). Hands doing the work by habit: left hand on the doors and bars, wrist and sleeve on the closet handle. After the jacket change her right hand drops out of the narration completely (see note 3). Do not mention it. No hunger, and food isn't mentioned. Cold rising as she goes down. Breath steady. She is tired in the thighs and the backs of the knees, a tiredness she would recognise on a decedent from the lactate.
- **Motifs:** tape (the torn sticker under her left palm in beat 1, the last time she touches it; the vinyl roll unused in the tote); hands (morgue-habit doors; the right hand's silence begins); the minute (her clock, still running, now counting doors); the syllabus page (moved, folded, not looked at, never reproduced); the meal after (absent, not mentioned).

## Scene 2 — The Corridor

1. Past the dock office the service corridor runs towards the vendor gate: a long straight run of painted block, fluorescent tubes, one of them flickering, a mop bucket against the wall, a laminated sign about manual handling. She walks at the pace of staff going off shift who don't want to be kept back. She carries the tote on her left shoulder.
2. A woman comes the other way, from the service-lift lobby, heading towards the stairs and the radio. She is walking fast without running, which is a skill. Mid-forties. Flat shoes. A dark trouser suit cut a little long on the right. A visitor lanyard turned round backwards, the way every lanyard turns itself round.
3. The walk, read in one clause and then in a long one, as Iris reads anything coming through the doors at work. The right arm swings less than the left. The right elbow stays close. The hips are faintly bladed, as if the right side is carrying something it has carried for twenty years and will carry tonight whether or not it's there.
4. No earpiece. No Service lapel pin. Not hotel, not campaign. Iris does the arithmetic before the woman is level with her: the only federal person in this building not on the Secret Service's channel is the one on the security annex distribution list and on page four of the run of show, the FBI liaison.
5. Half a second, level with each other. She looks at Iris's hands. Iris looks at hers. (Iris reports the look and does not say which of her own hands it went to. She doesn't know. Hold this.)
6. Reyes's hands, the long sentence, the reflex coming back. Short nails, unpainted. A callus in the web between right thumb and forefinger, the grip of twenty years of range qualifications. A biro mark on the inside of the right middle finger where the pen sits. A plain band, or no band (drafter's choice, and either way one clause; report it to the ledger). They are the hands of someone who writes things down. The paperwork thread becomes flesh for one sentence. Iris feels it the way she would feel a finding.
7. Her face, briefly and without grading. Iris notes that her own face is in no file Reyes has. The ME's office doesn't release photographs of its pathologists, and the RUTH CALDER badge carries none. Reyes has her control number. Now she has the face and not the name. Iris knows hers and isn't sure that makes them even.
8. She reaches for a grade and doesn't give one. One line: there was no page for Reyes, and for the first time Iris had the feeling the marking ran the other way.
9. **[TURN]** She didn't stop. Iris kept walking and didn't look back, and knew exactly who she was, and tells the reader before the interlude can: Special Agent E. Reyes. She had never known what the E stood for. She still doesn't. An initial had a face now.

- **Sensory lead:** sound, then sight. Two pairs of flat soles on concrete, out of step and then in step for one stride as they pass. The fluorescent tube's tick. The radio, now behind Iris, repeating. Then sight, which is unusual for her: Reyes is read by gait and hands before smell. Smell comes last and small, as Reyes passes: cold air on a wool suit, and coffee. A campaign smell, urn coffee, and Iris recognises the brand of tiredness.
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - On the walk: "I have opened three police officers. All three had the same thickening of the skin over the right iliac crest, where twenty years of holster had been filing a complaint. I wasn't going to get to check. I'd have bet on it." (Use this one.)
  - On the lanyard, reserve: "Her lanyard had turned itself round, the way they all do. I didn't need the front. I had read the back of her for a month."
- **The body:** her legs keep the same pace, which is the competence in this scene: she doesn't slow, speed up or step aside more than staff would. The left hand stays on the tote strap. The right hand isn't narrated at all. Her mouth is dry. There is a pulse she notices in her neck, the first thing her body has volunteered since 24, and it isn't hunger. She doesn't name what it is. The wound is quiet.
- **Motifs:** hands (the scene's whole exchange is four hands; hers stay unnamed); the grade (withheld from Reyes, and the marking felt to be reversed); the paperwork thread (the biro mark, the control number, the initial); voicemail, sideways, optional (in one clause, the woman three years ago also wanted "to talk", and Reyes will too. Use only if it stays one clause. Iris doesn't know of any interview booking in this chapter; she hears of the Wed 4 Nov slot only from Mara on 9 Nov, so keep any day out).

## Scene 3 — Out

1. The vendor gate at the end of the corridor: a steel pedestrian gate beside the dock shutter, with a card reader on the outside and a green request-to-exit button on the inside. She noted it on the floor plan in Ch 20, where fire code requires a way out. She presses it with the knuckle of her left hand. The lock drops. She walks through, shortly before 9:00, and doesn't wait to hear it close. (The closing is held back for the Snapper.)
2. The fire-code line, dry, and the scene's one hard joke: a building has to let you out. She has done the autopsies on the ones that didn't.
3. The street. The cross street behind the hotel: bins, a catering van with its doors open, steam from a grate. Then the avenue. The city unchanged: traffic, a bus, two men arguing pleasantly outside a bar, a woman walking a dog in a coat. A woman in a catering jacket walking home at nine on a Monday is the least interesting thing in any city, and Iris knows it. It is the injury from Ch 2 working for her one last time, and she doesn't say so.
4. No cab. The reason is given as procedure, briefly: a cab is a record, a rideshare is a record with a map, and she knows what investigators look for because she has spent fifteen years telling them. So she walks. Two hours, about nine kilometres, by streets she picks for being ordinary.
5. One image of the night going on without him. Through a bar window there is a television with the ballroom feed: an empty lectern, flags, a crowd, and a banner along the bottom saying remarks delayed. Nobody in the bar is watching it. She doesn't stop either. (The city stays unchanged. The news hasn't broken, or it has and nobody here has looked up. Don't resolve which.)
6. The walk in the body. She is cold in a thin jacket at about five degrees and doesn't do anything about it. The wound settles into a dull, regular ache. Her feet are in the flat black shoes she wore under the press outfit, which pass for catering shoes. She can feel a blister starting, at the heel, where Ch 2's was. One clause, with no comment.
7. Food going by as smell only: a kebab shop's vent (lamb fat on a spit, cumin), a bakery shut for the night breathing yeast through its grille. In Ch 2 she ate lamb cutlets in a stairwell. Here the smell arrives, is identified correctly, and goes. She reports it and nothing follows.
8. The knowledge from 18, at most one line: she is walking towards a locked drawer with her own page in it, face-down, and carrying its twin.
9. Halfway, at a crossing, waiting for the light, she looks down, and for the first time in the chapter the right hand is narrated. It is flat across her chest, over the inside pocket, pressing the folded page through the jacket. It has been there since the closet. She has held it the whole way, the way she held her left hand with her right in the lift in Ch 2, two fingers and then the whole palm.
10. **[TURN]** She realises it then, and leaves the hand where it is. She was not hungry. She notes that she would once have called that a finding.

- **Sensory lead:** temperature. The cold off the street when the gate opens, cold through the thin jacket for two hours, the cold of the crossing-button's steel under her knuckle. Then smell (vent, yeast, exhaust, a wet-leaf gutter), offered and declined. Sound: the city's ordinary noise, and the absence of sirens near her. If sirens come, they are far off and going the other way. Texture: the page's folds felt through two layers of cloth under her palm, at the Turn and not before.
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Fire code requires a building to let you out. I have done the autopsies on the buildings that didn't." (Use this one.)
  - On the bar television, reserve: "Somewhere behind me a room full of people were being asked to be patient with a man. They were very good at it. They'd had practice."
- **The body:** walking for two hours. Cold, accepted. The wound aching regularly. A blister starting at the heel. Her stomach is not mentioned until the Turn, and then once. The right hand on the page is the scene's revelation. The left hand holds the tote strap and presses the gate and crossing buttons with a knuckle.
- **Motifs:** hands (the lift in Ch 2, made explicit; the last hands image before Ch 27's); the meal after (smells declined, then "not hungry"); the syllabus page (held, folded, still unread); the grade (absent; she gives the finding to herself and doesn't grade it); the minute (her clock stops without being mentioned, and the Snapper confirms it).

## Snapper

Outline minimum, in past tense: *The vendor gate closing behind me, unhurried, on a timer.*

Sharpened option (author's call), told in the past perfect after the Turn because it happened about an hour earlier: *Behind me, an hour back, the vendor gate had closed on its timer, unhurried. I hadn't counted it.*

The "hadn't counted it" closes the clock she has been keeping since Ch 21 (forty seconds, thirty seconds, eleven seconds). The gate's timer is nineteen seconds and she knows it from the floor plan; she still doesn't count it. The first door in five chapters she didn't time is the one that let her go. It is an admission, not a cliffhanger. If the second sentence reads as too pointed, end on "unhurried, on its timer." and let the reader supply the rest. Either way it is the chapter's last line, and the gate is closing *behind* her, not in front of anyone else.
