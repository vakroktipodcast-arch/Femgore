# Beat sheet: ch09, Plot Point One: The Hunter Hunted

- **Unit:** ch09 (`manuscript/ch09.md`). End of Act I.
- **POV:** Iris Vale, first person, past tense. The outline's Turns are in present tense, so render them in past tense on the page.
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Three scenes of ~1,050 each. No syllabus insert at the chapter head (inserts are Ch 1, 6, 17 only).
- **Act pressure:** Act I, The Prerequisite. The campaign is certain nothing is coming, and that certainty is now a costume Brandt wears in the War Room while he is the only one who knows otherwise. From Ch 8 the pressure is Brandt, and here he steps out of the feed and into her flat. He has to be felt in every scene: his trace in S1, his body on the feed in S2, his name on the page in S3. Underneath runs the fifth pressure, appetite. The reader should feel her pleasure at being hunted as a danger to her before she does.
- **Appetite at entry:** Act I, appetite as competence, present and unnamed. She has killed once and eaten enormously (Ch 7). She has watched her own work on Mara's table and was proud of being in the file (Ch 8). She is interested in Brandt in a way she was not interested in Diane, and has not examined it. She has rewound his stillness three times (Ch 8 Snapper).
- **Appetite at exit:** Still unnamed, but it has turned towards a person. Being found does not frighten her. It feeds her, and she reports it as a professional courtesy ("enjoy being understood"). She grades the man who broke into her flat *Excellent*. She eats more than she meant to and sleeps nine hours. The reader sees the kinship and the hunger as one thing. Iris files them separately. Body temperature: Act I cold all chapter, with one small warmth at the S2 Turn that she puts down to the tea. Do not let it become Act II warmth yet (that arrives from Ch 10).
- **Knowledge constraints (from the ledger and later outlines):**
  - Iris believes she is the only woman the campaign silenced. She learns of the eleven names only in Ch 16, and that knowledge is the Ch 16 Turn ("I had believed myself alone"). So in this chapter she must **not** see the list, count names, or think "signatories" in the plural. She deduces that he found *her* NDA, her signature on his paper. "We are the same kind of alone" has to be sincere on the page and ironic in hindsight.
  - Iris does not yet know about the shell company or the off-book surveillance (Ch 11). Her S2 reasoning for the police wall has to rest on what she does know: the NDA itself, and how he would explain getting from shredded paper to a name ten weeks out.
  - Her access is the War Room feed from Ch 8 only, with picture and sound. She cannot see his phone screen or follow him outside the room. Brandt does not detect the feed (interlude-2-cam constraint).
  - Iris has not met Brandt at close quarters. The vetiver is not identified here (see S1 sensory lead).

## Scene 1: The Entry

1. Wednesday, early evening, the third day after Diane. Iris comes home from a day shift with a paper grocery bag on her hip and the morgue still on her skin. Her key goes in, the deadbolt turns the same weight it always turns, and the flat is as she left it. By the end of the first paragraph she knows it is not.
2. Smell first. The flat smells of itself: chlorine from the sink, the lemon in the fruit bowl, a radiator not yet on. Under that, at the level of a standing man's chest, something dry and green and expensive, like a cellar somebody keeps well. It is not hers. She owns unscented soap and nothing else. She does not name it. (Ch 17 names it.)
3. She does not go to the desk. She goes to the kitchen and puts the bag down. Knife block: full, every handle at its angle. Fridge: she checks the seal on the butter, the cling film on Monday's stock, the milk's cap ring unbroken. The kettle is cold. Nothing has been opened, added or swapped.
4. Then the desk and the locked drawer. The lock has no marks. She grades the lockwork in passing as entry competent, no scratches on the brass. Inside: three dossiers, her signed NDA, the two remaining syllabus pages (Lesson One went to the reservoir on Diane's chest). The kit in the back compartment lies exactly as she left it, and she cannot tell whether he found the compartment at all. It is the one thing in the flat she cannot grade.
5. The tell. She squares the dossier on top to a knot in the grain of the drawer's floor, left edge on the knot, every time. Tonight its edge sits a quarter-inch to the left of the knot. She measures bodies in centimetres and furniture in inches and does not know why, and says so.
6. She tells the reader which of the two checks was the tell. The drawer is the evidence. The kitchen is the tell, and the tell is hers: she checked the food before the files. A toxicologist knows the cleanest way into a body is through its fridge. Her own order told her what she is afraid of, and it told her he had not come to kill her.
7. Whose file was moved. The dossier on top is Brandt's, left there since Ch 8 when she read it for an hour. A man who opens a drawer holding three files reads his own first. His own file holds her note from two years ago, *the only one who'll understand it*. He has read that, in her handwriting, about himself.
8. She reconstructs his visit like an examination after the fact, from what was done to the room. He came in daylight while she was at the table with someone else's lungs. He read everything. He took nothing, because taking is evidence and he is a man who removes evidence for a living. He put it all back almost perfectly, and the quarter-inch is the size of a man whose hands are not quite as steady as he thought.
9. Grade, dry and fair: entry competent, restoration very nearly excellent, the quarter-inch the only error. She found the error because she has spent fourteen hundred cases finding the one thing somebody else put back wrong.
10. **[TURN]** He has read the syllabus. He knows there are two pages left, and the dossiers lying under them tell him whose names go on those pages: his and Holt's. And on the NDA in the same drawer, on his own template, is her signature. Mine is a name to him now.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell (her flat's own baseline, then the unplaced dry green note at chest height). Then sound: the deadbolt's familiar weight, the fridge seal unpeeling, the drawer runners. Texture: the drawer floor's grain under her fingertip, the knot. Temperature: the flat at the temperature she left it, the radiator cold, her own hands cold from the street. Light: she does not switch on the overhead light. Sodium from the street, then the desk lamp.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "I measure people in centimetres and furniture in inches. Nobody has ever asked me why, and the furniture has never complained."
  - On the kitchen: "Most women who come home to a burglary check the jewellery. I checked the butter. I'm a toxicologist; I know where the jewellery is."
  - On the restoration: he had put everything back "the way the bereaved tidy a room before the police come: lovingly, and a quarter-inch wrong."
- **Body:** Cold hands from the street. She keeps the grocery bag on her hip through the first beats and puts it down only in the kitchen. Her hand hovers over the moved dossier and **does not** square it. She notes the hover as a finding. She has not eaten since a mid-morning coffee, and the hunger is there under the inspection, reported once and left alone.
- **Motifs:** Hands (the hover, the fingertip on the knot). The grade (his entry graded like a student's paper). Tape (the black vinyl roll in the back compartment, lying exactly as left). Cold (Act I, the unchosen kind).

## Scene 2: The Three Walls

1. Same night. She cooks standing up, because she is planning: four eggs, soft-scrambled in too much butter, on toast spread with anchovy butter, the lemon zested over it. The tablet leans against the fruit bowl with the War Room feed on it, as it did for the debate in Ch 1. She scrubs back through the day and finds the gap. Brandt left the room at 12:58 p.m. and came back at 3:06 p.m., in the same charcoal suit, and went to the urn and did not drink the coffee.
2. Live feed, after nine. The room is quieter than on Sunday. Holt has given his statement on Diane. Staff are drafting a vigil. Brandt sits at the end of the conference table, not working, with his phone face-down by his hand. She eats and watches him decide, three times, not to do something. She narrates his reasoning for him, in her register and as a second person half-addressed to him.
3. **Wall one, the police.** The campaign security lead brings him a slip with a number: the county sheriff's investigator wants a liaison. Brandt holds the slip a long time, folds it in half and puts it in his inside pocket without dialling. Iris gives the reasoning. To give them her name he would have to explain how a man gets from shredded paper to a pathologist in two days. That means explaining the paper, and the paper is the thing the campaign cannot be seen to have made, ten weeks out. (She does not know about the shell company and must not cite it.)
4. **Wall two, Holt.** Holt comes in late, tie loosened, and is kinder in that small room than any of his staff. He has a hand on a weeping intern's shoulder, and he knows her parents' names. He stops beside Brandt and asks if there is anything he needs to know. "No, sir. Statement landed." Brandt watches Holt go. Iris has watched Holt stop knowing men; there is a section of his dossier for it. The moment Holt hears that someone has been reading the old files, the problem is the man who kept them.
5. Her body at Holt on the feed: Act I cold in the forearms, the same report as Ch 1, brief. She keeps eating through it, on purpose, and tells the reader that is a choice.
6. **Wall three, the clean brief.** Near midnight Brandt picks up the phone, scrolls a long way down past everyone he likes, and his thumb stops. She can't read the screen at that resolution and doesn't need to. She reads the thumb. He puts the phone face-down again. Her reasoning: he doesn't know what she has or who she's told. A dead pathologist with a dossier somewhere he can't reach is worse than a live one. She knows she has told no one and owns no safe-deposit box, and she is glad he can't know that.
7. She has made toast for the last of the eggs and eaten it, and she notes that she has eaten more than she meant to. It is not the meal after; there's been no kill. She reports it once, as appetite for supper, and moves on. The reader keeps it.
8. She tells the reader plainly that she enjoyed this: being read correctly and reading back, watching someone work her out in real time and get it right. Nobody has done that to her since she signed. It is reported as a pleasure, without apology.
9. **[TURN]** He is going to come for her himself, with no one's permission: not the police's, not Holt's, not a stranger's on a clean brief. He has walled himself in with her. We are the same kind of alone. The small warmth that comes with that sentence she puts down to the tea she has made and not yet drunk.

- **Sensory lead:** Taste (butter browning at the pan's edge, the salt-rot of anchovy against lemon zest, eggs barely set). Sound: the feed's compressed audio, the HVAC hum, the *tock* of his phone face-down on the table, which is also in interlude-2-cam. Light: the tablet's blue in a dark kitchen. Texture: toast crust, the warm plate she holds rather than sets down.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Three phone calls a man could have made, and I watched him not make each of them, which is the most intimate thing I had seen a man do in years."
  - On Holt and the intern: "He knew her parents' names. He knew mine once, too; he has a good memory for the first names of people he is about to make into paperwork."
  - "I have told no one. I don't own a safe-deposit box. I own a fruit bowl. He couldn't know that, and I found I wanted to keep it from him the way you keep the end of a book from someone who is enjoying it."
- **Body:** Eating standing up (planning). Four eggs and the toast, more than she meant. Cold forearms at Holt's entrance. The hand holding the plate is steady. Tea made and left to cool by the tablet. The one warmth at the Turn is misfiled.
- **Motifs:** The grade (she gives his reasoning full marks: "correctly" is her grade). Hands (his thumb on the phone, read like a finding). Cold (Act I, briefly, at Holt). The meal after, by contrast: this is explicitly not one, and she says so. Vinyl, offstage: she knows from his dossier that he will go home tonight and put on a record. She has nothing to put on.

## Scene 3: The Inversion

1. After one. Desk lamp only. She unlocks the drawer again and does not touch his dossier, which stays a quarter-inch to the left. She lifts out the Lesson Two page from beside it.
2. She reads the page through as he would have read it this afternoon: the redactions, the second person, *You see every room exactly as it is.* She hears it in his voice, because now she knows he has heard it in his. A brief callback only. Do not reproduce the insert (it heads Ch 17). One phrase at most.
3. She asks herself whether today changes the Evaluation. She considers it properly, as she would revisit a finding when new evidence arrives. *Mistook clarity for cruelty.* It holds. It reads better for his having read it. (This seeds the Ch 17 line "It still read well. It had held.")
4. The update. She says it out loud to the empty room in the syllabus voice: the Architect is hunting the student. The curriculum assumed she would set every lesson. It did not allow for the subject turning up to sit the exam early, in her flat, uninvited, and reading the marking scheme.
5. She notices she is not afraid, and treats the absence the way she would treat a missing organ: what should be here, and what is here instead. What is here is interest. She reports it, does not name it, and does not use any banned word.
6. The Apex page lies under Lesson Two in the drawer. She does not take it out or look at it, and the narration does not describe it. She only notes that he has read it, and that he is now the second person alive who has.
7. She uncaps the red marker. Solvent smell, the same as in Ch 1. The marker is for circling, and she has only used it on a body once. Tonight she uses it the way the colour was meant to be used, by a teacher.
8. **[TURN]** In the margin of Lesson Two, beside the subject line, in red, one word: *Excellent.*
9. She caps the marker and lays the page back exactly where it was, squared to its own edge, not to his. Then she lays her hand flat on the moved dossier without moving it, and takes it away.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell (the marker's solvent, the paper soft at the corners from two years of handling). Sound: the marker's squeak on the paper, small in a quiet flat. In Ch 17 it squeaks "like a mouse", so keep this one plainer for the echo. Texture: the paper's softened edges, the drawer floor, the knot under her palm. Temperature: the flat cold now, radiator off, her feet cold. Light: one lamp, the desk lamp. (This rhymes with "the one lamp in Brandt's suite". Do not point it out.)
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Most students wait for the exam. He had broken into the staff room to read the marking scheme, and then put it back nearly where he found it, which in my experience is the difference between a cheat and a scholar."
  - "I had only ever used the red marker on a woman's chest. It felt almost wholesome to use it on paper, the way it was designed."
- **Body:** Hands: the marker, then the flat palm on his dossier, touching and not correcting. Cold feet. Tiredness arriving all at once, the first time in the chapter her body asks for something other than food.
- **Motifs:** The grade (the word is a grade, and the first Iris has given in admiration rather than evaluation). Hands. Tape: none needed. The quarter-inch as an object now, a mark she chooses to keep.

## Carries in / carries out

**Carries in:**
- From interlude-2-cam: Brandt recognised his own NDA template (Century Schoolbook, the footer rule, half a control number) in Diane's mouth. He kept the only copy of the photograph on his phone, in his inside breast pocket, and told no one. He is already working alone. Iris does not know any of this. The reader does, and S2 lets the reader watch her get it right from the outside.
- From Ch 8: the War Room feed (picture and sound, recorded and live). Iris watched Brandt go still and rewound it three times. She read the Brandt dossier for an hour and left it on top in the drawer. Her note on it reads "the only one who'll understand it." Mara wrote "practised", and Iris is in the file.
- From Ch 7: the NDA fragments were left to point at campaign counsel ("the numbers lead to a list, and I am on it"). Ch 9 is that list arriving at a door, though Iris thinks of it only as her own NDA.
- From Ch 1: the drawer's contents (three dossiers, her NDA, the syllabus, the kit in the back compartment). The tablet propped against the fruit bowl. The red marker's solvent smell. The cold.
- From the timeline proposed in interlude-2-cam: Diane killed Sunday, War Room Sunday night, autopsy and feed Monday. Ch 9 is the Wednesday.

**Carries out:**
- To Ch 10: Brandt knows who she is and has told no one. His next move is to bait rather than strike: the fabricated whistleblower profile. Iris watches it land "and admires the craft". That only works because S2 here establishes her admiring him as a reader of people. Her "we are the same kind of alone" sets up her refusal to lift a finger for Tessa.
- To Ch 12: Iris's S2 reading is that "he is going to come for me himself". The Ch 12 contractors are briefed to take her, not kill her, which is still consistent with wall three (no clean kill). Ch 12 should let her register that he sent hands rather than coming himself, as a small surprise or a small hurt.
- To Ch 13: she enters his home as he entered hers, and leaves things instead of taking them. The quarter-inch is the precedent: he left one trace, and she leaves deliberate ones.
- **Flag for Ch 13 and Ch 17 (not a conflict):** the Lesson Two page in her drawer now has *Excellent.* in red in the margin. The Ch 17 anchor says the page pinned to Brandt is "his own copy, the one I had left on his pillow", and does not mention a margin note. Two ways to square it. (a) The pillow copy in Ch 13 is a fresh print and the annotated master stays in her drawer. The phrase "his own copy" supports this. (b) It is this annotated page, and the Ch 13 beats note it. The anchor is approved and does not mention the word, so (a) is the safer reading. Ch 13 should decide.
- To Ch 16: "We are the same kind of alone" is the belief that the eleven names break. The Ch 16 Turn ("I had believed myself alone. I have built two years on being the only one") lands harder if this chapter's line is sincere.
- To Ch 17: the dry green smell "like a cellar somebody keeps well" at the drawer is placed as vetiver in Ch 17 (the anchor: "which in fact smells of a good cellar"). Ch 17 does not need to call it back. The S3 beat on the Evaluation holding seeds "It still read well. It had held." The lone desk lamp rhymes with the one lamp in the suite.
- Body forward: nine hours' sleep, the "well" half of the bad/good alternation. Still cold, so the Act II warmth can start in Ch 10.
