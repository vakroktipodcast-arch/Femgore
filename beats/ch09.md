# Beat sheet: ch09, Plot Point One: The Hunter Hunted

- **Unit:** ch09 (`manuscript/ch09.md`; drafted, this sheet revised to match the revised manuscript). End of Act I. Wed 16 Sep.
- **POV:** Iris Vale, first person, past tense. The outline's Turns are in present tense, so render them in past tense on the page.
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Three scenes of ~1,050 each. No syllabus insert at the chapter head (inserts are Ch 1, 6, 17 only).
- **Act pressure:** Act I, The Prerequisite. The campaign is certain nothing is coming, and that certainty is now a costume Brandt wears in the War Room while he is the only one who knows otherwise. From Ch 8 the pressure is Brandt, and here he steps out of the feed and into her flat. He has to be felt in every scene: his trace in S1, his body on the feed in S2, his name on the page in S3. Underneath runs the fifth pressure, appetite. The reader should feel her pleasure at being hunted as a danger to her before she does.
- **Appetite at entry:** Act I, appetite as competence, present and unnamed. She has killed once and eaten enormously (Ch 7). She has watched her own work on Mara's table and was proud of being in the file (Ch 8). She is interested in Brandt in a way she was not interested in Diane, and has not examined it. She has rewound his stillness three times (Ch 8 Snapper).
- **Appetite at exit:** Still unnamed, but it has turned towards a person. Being found does not frighten her. It feeds her, and the page shows it through the body rather than a stated pleasure: she eats more than she meant to, finds interest where fear should be, and grades the man who broke into her flat *Excellent*. She sleeps nine hours. The reader sees the kinship and the hunger as one thing. Iris files them separately. Body temperature: Act I cold all chapter, with one small warmth at the S2 Turn that she puts down to the tea. Do not let it become Act II warmth yet (that arrives from Ch 10).
- **Knowledge constraints (from the ledger and later outlines):**
  - Iris believes she is the only woman the campaign silenced. She learns of the eleven names only in Ch 16, and that knowledge is the Ch 16 Turn ("I had believed myself alone"). So in this chapter she must **not** see the list, count names, or think "signatories" in the plural. She deduces that he found *her* NDA, her signature on the campaign's paper. "We are the same kind of alone" has to be sincere on the page and ironic in hindsight.
  - Iris does **not** know Brandt chose the template or that he recognised its typeface (interlude-2-cam is the reader's alone). The page says "the campaign's paper", never "his template".
  - Iris does not yet know about the shell company or the off-book surveillance (Ch 11). Her S2 reasoning for the police wall has to rest on what she does know: the NDA itself, and how he would explain getting from shredded paper to a name three days out.
  - Her access is the War Room feed from Ch 8 only, with picture and sound. She cannot see his phone screen or follow him outside the room. Brandt does not detect the feed (interlude-2-cam constraint).
  - Iris has not met Brandt at close quarters. The vetiver is not identified here (see S1 sensory lead).

## Scene 1: The Entry

1. Wednesday, early evening, the third day after Diane. Iris comes home from a day shift with a paper grocery bag on her hip and the morgue still on her skin. Her key goes in, the deadbolt turns the same weight it always turns, and the flat is as she left it. By the end of the first paragraph she knows it is not. (Unmarked rhyme, never pointed at: the woman at her own front door with groceries is the photograph Ch 11 will find.)
2. Smell first. The flat smells of itself: chlorine from the sink, the lemon in the fruit bowl, a radiator not yet on. Under that, at the level of a standing man's chest, something dry and green and expensive, like a cellar somebody keeps well. It is not hers. She owns unscented soap and nothing else. She does not name it. (Ch 17 names it.)
3. She does not go to the desk. She goes to the kitchen and puts the bag down. Knife block: full, every handle at its angle. Fridge: she checks the seal on the butter, the cling film over the half lemon, the milk a finger below the shoulder of the bottle with that morning's dry drip line on the neck. The kettle with its inch of water, not a millimetre more. Nothing has been opened, added or swapped.
4. Procedure, briefly, before the desk. The tablet has been in her work bag all day (it goes where she goes since Mon 14 Sep). The clean laptop is shut behind a password that exists only in her head, and its log shows no attempts. She sweeps the flat for devices (smoke alarm, sockets, lamp base, desk underside) and finds nothing. He came to read, not to listen.
5. Then the desk and the locked drawer. The smell is stronger here, at standing-chest height, strongest on the brass pull and the chair back. The key has been on her ring, in her coat pocket, in a building across the city all day, and the lock face shows no marks, no scratches, no bright crescents. She checks the front door the same way: clean. She grades the entry competent and leaves the method open: a key, or someone better with a pick than she would have guessed. Inside: three dossiers, the photographs, her signed NDA with Diane's cover email clipped behind it, and the clear sleeve with all three syllabus pages, the working masters. (Lesson One's master never left the drawer; what went to the reservoir on Diane's chest was a fresh clean print.) The back compartment's panel is flush. What it holds lies as she left it, and she cannot tell whether he found it. A man who had found it would have put it back exactly; so would a man who hadn't. It is the one thing in the flat she cannot grade.
6. The evidence. She squares the dossier on top to a knot in the grain of the drawer's floor, left edge on the knot, every time. Tonight its edge sits a quarter-inch to the left of the knot. She measures bodies in centimetres and furniture in inches and does not know why, and says so. Her hand goes out to square it, stops, hovers, fingers slightly flexed. She notes that, and takes the hand back.
7. She tells the reader which of the two checks was the tell. The drawer is the evidence. The kitchen is the tell, and the tell is hers: she checked the food before the files, before she had a reason to. Her own order told her what her body was afraid of, and the fridge told it back that he had not come to kill her. Not today. She is hungry under all of it, notes it once, and leaves it on the counter with the eggs.
8. Whose file was moved, and how. The dossier on top is Brandt's, left there since Monday night (Ch 8): she read the Lesson Two page for an hour with his file open beside it and put his file back last, on top, because it was the one she was working. The sleeve lies at the bottom, so a man who wants the pages has to lift the dossiers off as a block, slide the sleeve out from under the photographs and the NDA, and put it all back afterwards. The block came back a quarter-inch left. A man who opens a drawer holding three files reads his own first. His file holds her note from two years ago in the margin beside the Tabard photograph, *the only one who'll understand it*. He has read that, in her handwriting, about himself.
9. She reconstructs his visit like an examination after the fact, from what was done to the room. He came in daylight while she was at the table with someone else's lungs. He read everything. He took nothing, because taking is evidence and he removes evidence for a living. He put it all back almost exactly, and the quarter-inch is the size of a man whose hands were not quite as steady as he thought. Grade, dry and fair: entry competent, restoration very nearly excellent, one error. She found it because she has spent fourteen hundred cases finding the one thing somebody else put back wrong.
10. **[TURN]** He has read the syllabus. He knows Lesson One is done and two lessons are still to be set, and the dossiers lying over the pages tell him whose names go on them: his and Holt's. And under the dossiers in the same drawer, on the campaign's paper, is her signature, in the blue ink they gave her, rounder than it is now. Mine was a name to him now.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell (her flat's own baseline, then the unplaced dry green note at chest height, strongest on the brass pull and chair back). Then sound: the deadbolt's familiar weight, the fridge seal unpeeling, the drawer runners. Texture: the drawer floor's grain under her fingertip, the knot. Temperature: the flat at the temperature she left it, the radiator cold, her own hands cold from the street. Light: she does not switch on the overhead. The last of the day through the window, colour of weak tea, then the desk lamp. (Not sodium: it is a mid-September evening, and the street lamps are not yet on.)
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "I measure bodies in centimetres and furniture in inches. Nobody has ever asked me why, and the furniture has never complained."
  - On the kitchen: "Most women who come home to a burglary check the jewellery. I checked the butter. I'm a pathologist; I know where the jewellery is."
  - On the lock: "The key was on my ring all day, in a building where I am surrounded by people who cannot be burgled. He had not needed it, which I took personally."
  - On the restoration: he had put everything back "the way the bereaved tidy a room before the police come: lovingly, and a quarter-inch wrong."
- **Body:** Cold hands from the street. She keeps the grocery bag on her hip through the first beats and puts it down only in the kitchen. Her hand hovers over the moved dossier and **does not** square it. She notes the hover as a finding. She has not eaten since a mid-morning coffee, and the hunger is there under the inspection, reported once and left alone. Hands steady throughout (no tremor anywhere; ledger).
- **Motifs:** Hands (the hover, the fingertip on the knot). The grade (his entry graded like a student's paper). Tape (the black vinyl in the back compartment, lying as left). Cold (Act I, the unchosen kind). The meal (hunger noted, deferred).

## Scene 2: The Three Walls

1. Same night. She cooks standing up, because she is planning: four eggs, soft-scrambled in too much butter, on toast spread with anchovy butter, the lemon zested over it. The tablet leans against the fruit bowl with the War Room feed on it, as it did for the debate in Ch 1. She scrubs back through the recording of the day and finds the gap. Brandt left the room at 12:58 p.m. and came back at 3:06 p.m., in the same charcoal suit, and went to the urn and did not drink the coffee.
2. Live feed, after nine. The room is quieter than on Sunday. Holt has given his statement on Diane. Staff are drafting a vigil. Brandt sits at the end of the conference table, not working, with his phone face-down by his hand. She eats and watches him decide, three times, not to do something. She narrates his reasoning for him, in her register and as a second person half-addressed to him.
3. **Wall one, the police.** The campaign security lead brings him a slip: the county sheriff's investigator wants a liaison. Brandt holds the slip a long time, folds it in half and puts it in his inside pocket without dialling. Iris gives the reasoning. To give them her name he would have to explain how a man gets from shredded paper to a pathologist in three days. That means explaining the paper, and the paper is the thing the campaign cannot be seen to have made, seven weeks out. (She does not know about the shell company and must not cite it.)
4. **Wall two, Holt.** Holt comes in at about half past ten, tie loosened, and is kinder in that small room than any of his staff. He has a hand on a weeping junior staffer's shoulder, and he knows her parents' names. He stops beside Brandt and asks if there is anything he needs to know. "No, sir. Statement landed." Brandt watches Holt go. Iris has watched Holt stop knowing men; there is a section of his dossier for it. The moment Holt hears that someone has been reading the old files, the problem is the man who kept them. (She knows "the old files" only as her own phrase for the campaign's closed complaints; she has not heard Holt's "I thought Diane closed those".)
5. Her body at Holt on the feed: Act I cold in the forearms, volar surface, moving into the backs of her hands, the same report as Ch 1, brief. She keeps eating through it, on purpose, and tells the reader that is a choice.
6. **Wall three, the clean brief.** Near midnight Brandt picks up the phone, scrolls a long way down past everyone he likes, and his thumb stops. She can't read the screen at that resolution and doesn't need to. She reads the thumb. He puts the phone face-down again, takes his hand off it, and lays the hand flat on the inside pocket holding the folded slip (about 11:50 p.m.), and leaves it there. Her reasoning: he doesn't know what she has or who she's told. A dead pathologist with a dossier somewhere he can't reach is worse than a live one. She knows she has told no one and owns no safe-deposit box, and she wants to keep that from him the way you keep the end of a book from someone who is enjoying it. She fills the kettle while she thinks.
7. She makes toast for the last of the eggs, gone cool and firm in the pan, and eats that too. It is more than she meant to eat. She reports it once and moves on; the reader keeps it. (No explicit comparison to the meal after a kill: the page leaves that to the reader.)
8. She has read him correctly from across a city, and he has handled her things; she read him back, got it right, and watched him get her right in real time. Nobody has done that to her since she signed. Nobody has looked at her long enough. Shown through the body and the eating, not announced as pleasure: no "I enjoyed it". He will go home and put on a record (it is in his file); she has nothing to put on.
9. **[TURN]** On the tablet his hand is still flat on that pocket. He is going to come for her himself, with no one's permission: not the police's, not Holt's, not a stranger's on a clean brief. He has walled himself in with her. We are the same kind of alone. The small warmth that comes with that she puts down to the tea she has just made, strong with milk, the mug held in both hands in the blue of the tablet and not yet drunk.

- **Sensory lead:** Taste (butter browning at the pan's edge, hazelnut; the salt-rot of anchovy against lemon zest, eggs barely set). Sound: the feed's compressed audio, the HVAC hum, the *tock* of his phone face-down on the table (the same small separate sound as in Ch 8 and interlude-2-cam), the kettle clicking off. Light: the tablet's blue in a dark kitchen. Texture: toast crust, the warm plate she holds rather than sets down, the mug at the Turn.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "I watched him not make three phone calls, which is the most intimate thing I had seen a man do in years."
  - On Holt and the intern: "He has a good memory for the first names of people he is about to make into paperwork." (Keep it to the intern and the general habit. Do not claim Holt ever knew Iris's name or her parents'; his non-recognition of her in Ch 22-23 depends on it.)
  - "I have told no one. I don't own a safe-deposit box. I own a fruit bowl."
- **Body:** Eating standing up (planning). Four eggs and the toast, more than she meant. Cold forearms and backs of hands at Holt's entrance. The hand holding the plate is steady. Tea made at the Turn, strong with milk, and held. The one warmth at the Turn is misfiled.
- **Motifs:** The grade (she gives his reasoning full marks: "Correct", "Full marks"). Hands (his thumb on the phone, his flat hand on the pocket, read like findings). Cold (Act I, briefly, at Holt). Vinyl, offstage: she knows from his dossier that he will go home tonight and put on a record. She has nothing to put on.

## Scene 3: The Inversion

1. After one. Desk lamp only. She unlocks the drawer again, lifts the dossiers as a block, as he did, fingers spread under the bottom file and thumb flat on the top, and sets it on the desk without letting the top one slide. She slides the sleeve out from under the photographs and the NDA and takes the Lesson Two master from it.
2. She reads the page through as he would have read it this afternoon: the redactions, the second person, *You see every room exactly as it is.* She hears it in his voice, because now she knows he has heard it in his. A brief callback only. Do not reproduce the insert (it heads Ch 17). One phrase at most, and not the Evaluation, which Ch 8 already used.
3. She asks herself whether today changes the Evaluation. She considers it properly, as she would revisit a finding when new evidence arrives. It holds. It reads better for his having read it. (This seeds the Ch 17 line "It still read well. It had held." Do not quote the Evaluation again here.)
4. The update. She says it out loud to the empty room in the syllabus voice: "Addendum. The Architect has sat the paper early." The Architect is hunting the student. The curriculum assumed she would set every lesson. It did not allow for the subject turning up in her flat, uninvited, and reading the marking scheme.
5. She notices she is not afraid, and treats the absence the way she would treat a missing organ: what should be here, and what is here instead. What is here is interest. She reports it, does not name it, and does not use any banned word (no "empty", "hollow", "numb").
6. The Apex page lies under Lesson Two in the sleeve. She does not take it out or look at it, and the narration does not describe it. She only notes that he has read it, and that he is now the second person alive who has.
7. The pen tray holds both a red pen and a desk red marker (author ruling). She takes the pen, the ordinary one she uses on the masters, not the marker. Ink smell, faint and a little bitter, a school desk rather than a whiteboard, nothing like the solvent in Ch 1. The marker is for circling people. Tonight she uses the pen the way red was meant to be used, by a teacher.
8. **[TURN]** In the margin of Lesson Two, beside the subject line, in red, one word: *Excellent.*
9. She caps the pen and lays the page back in the sleeve, squared to its own edge, not to his. She thinks about changing the lock and about moving the sleeve somewhere he has not been, and does neither. She slides the sleeve home under the photographs and the NDA and lowers the dossiers on as a block. She puts the edge of his file to the knot out of habit, moves it off again, checks the offset against a fingernail, and lays her hand flat on Brandt's dossier without moving it. Tiredness arrives all at once. She takes the hand away.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell (the red pen's faint ink, the paper soft at the corners from two years of handling). Sound: the nib's dry scratch on the paper, small in a quiet flat. (Ch 17's marker squeaks "like a mouse"; keep this one plainer, a different instrument, so the echo is in the word and not the noise.) Texture: the paper's softened edges, the drawer floor, the knot under her palm. Temperature: the flat cold now, radiator off, her feet cold through her socks. Light: one lamp, the desk lamp, a pool on the walnut the size of a dinner plate. (This rhymes with "the one lamp in Brandt's suite". Do not point it out.)
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "He had broken into the staff room to read the marking scheme, and then put it back nearly where he found it, which in my experience is the difference between a cheat and a scholar."
  - "The last thing I had circled in red was pinned to a woman's chest. It felt almost wholesome to use red on paper nobody was wearing."
- **Body:** Hands: lifting the stack as a block, the pen, then the flat palm on his dossier, touching and not correcting. Cold feet. Tiredness arriving all at once, the first time in the chapter her body asks for something other than food.
- **Motifs:** The grade (the word is a grade she writes down, in her own hand, addressed to him; Ch 17's "Preparation: excellent" is its echo). Hands. Tape: none needed. The quarter-inch as an object now, a mark she chooses to keep.
