# Beat sheet: ch04, The Stalking

- **Unit:** ch04 (`manuscript/ch04.md`, not yet drafted)
- **POV:** Iris Vale, first person, past tense
- **Target:** ~3,150 words, three scenes of ~1,050 each.
- **Dates:** S1 Sun 30 Aug and Sun 6 Sep. S2 Tue 8 Sep, about 9 p.m. S3 Sat 12 Sep, late afternoon into evening.
- **Act pressure:** Act I, The Prerequisite. The pressure is the campaign's certainty that nothing is coming. It has to be felt in every scene. Scene 1: the Chief of Staff walks alone with no detail and her phone in her pocket. Scene 2: the War Room laughing. Scene 3: a padlock nobody has checked in years, and a navy-and-gold MARCHETTI sign on the county road. Brandt is not yet a pressure (Ch 8). In Scene 2 he is noted only as absent. Under all of it runs appetite, present and unnamed.
- **Appetite at entry:** appetite as competence. In Ch 3 she ate standing up while planning, felt "nothing I can name" and wrote down what she ate, and was "breathing hard and happy" after the rug. She files all of it under the work.
- **Appetite at exit:** competence starting to turn into something else. The dark inside the pump station is the first pleasure in the book she names as liking ("I find I like it"), but she names it about a room and not about what the room is for. Then she eats a whole dinner and licks the container, seated, as she sits after a kill, with no kill to blame it on. The reader notices that the physiology excuse has nothing to explain yet. She doesn't notice.
- **Shape:** Scene 1 is affection and appraisal. Scene 2 is the system talking about her without knowing she's in the room. Scene 3 is the place, and the pleasure of the place. Each scene ends with her body doing something. She caps the lens, sets the laptop down, then stands still in the dark.

## Scene 1: The Reservoir

1. Open on the bench through binoculars, first Sunday (30 August), 8:09 a.m. Diane sits down within ninety seconds of what three years of the fitness app predicted, and Iris gives the app a grade before she gives Diane one. Iris is across the water on the west shore, about 180 metres away, dressed as a birder: field guide, thermos, a heron logbook she actually keeps.
2. Diane takes a paperback out of her jacket and reads. Iris times it on her watch, not her phone (her phone is at home in the foil sleeve; that's tradecraft, stated in one line). Eleven minutes. Diane marks the page with a folded receipt, puts the book away, and is up at 8:20.
3. Diane's body, in one long hungry paragraph of affection and appraisal. About 165 cm, about 70 kg. Compact, with a swimmer's shoulders gone soft, in the olive waxed-cotton jacket over a slate shirt. The fat has started to migrate to the waist the way it does after menopause, when the oestrogen stops telling it where to sit. A short brisk stride with very little arm swing, "the gait of a woman carrying a folder that isn't there." Iris guesses the liver at fifteen hundred grams, a good colour, a glass of white most nights and never two. Then the line where affection and appraisal touch: she likes her, and she has already weighed her.
4. The route as Iris has it from the app, now confirmed by eye. The south car park. A clockwise loop, 6.4 km. The bench at 3.1 km, the halfway point, eleven minutes. Then on round the second half, below the dam, past the trail marker post where the sumac spur climbs 40 metres to the pump station (the brick roof just visible through the scrub), and back to the south car park. Diane reaches the post about 8:33 (the app's window, plus or minus nine minutes), once every Sunday, after the bench, and has never looked up the spur. Iris notes that nobody walks with her, nobody waits in the car park, and there is no detail, and that Diane never takes her phone out of her jacket. The Chief of Staff of a campaign nine weeks out is as easy to find as a mailbox. That is the campaign's certainty, felt.
5. Cool and damp, the Act I cold carried by the body and not the weather. Early-morning damp coming off the water into her hands on the binoculars, knuckles first. The thermos coffee is burnt and black and she drinks it anyway. She's hungry and doesn't eat. Report it as a fact about the morning.
6. Second Sunday (6 September), the same post. Iris had planned a close pass this time: walk the loop the other way and time the stretch from the bench to the spur marker post at Diane's pace (about thirteen minutes, 8:20 to 8:33), at the place it will happen.
7. Diane comes out of the trees at 8:04 and she isn't alone. A young woman walks beside her, taller, with an iced coffee, talking with her hands. Iris knows the face from the dossier photographs before the binoculars have finished focusing. It is Olivia.
8. Iris grades herself instead of Diane. It's Labor Day weekend, and she didn't check the Wellesley academic calendar. A two-year plan, failed on a public holiday. The mark is docked and logged.
9. They share the bench. Diane doesn't take out the book. Through the glass Iris watches the daughter make the mother laugh, and notes that Olivia walks with Diane's short stride and none of her stillness. Heritable gait, she observes, and nothing more than that.
10. [TURN] Iris aborts. She caps the lens, closes the logbook, and walks back to the car the long way. The target has a daughter in frame now, not in a file.

- **Sensory lead:** smell. Reservoir water in late summer: wet stone, algae at the margin, pine resin, goose droppings on the path, all sharpened by morning damp. Sound: the lap at the riprap, a cormorant drying its wings, and across the water the small flat sound of a paperback being opened, which she can't possibly hear and imagines anyway (she says so). Temperature: damp in the knuckles. Taste: burnt thermos coffee.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Birdwatchers are the only people allowed to stare at a stranger through ten-times magnification for forty minutes and be thought gentle."
  - On the liver: "I would have put money on fifteen hundred grams and a good clean colour. I have been wrong about livers before, but never by much, and never in their favour."
- **Body:** hands, cold on the binoculars, then capping the lens (the abort is done with the hands). Hunger noticed and not fed. Diane's body is the long sentence. Iris's is the short one.
- **Motifs:** the grade (the app, Diane, herself for the calendar). Cold. Hands. Voicemail, by absence: the phone in foil at home, so nobody can reach her either, which rhymes with Diane's hour without saying so.

## Scene 2: The Campaign's Arrogance

1. Tuesday evening, 8 September, the flat, about 9 p.m. Iris is standing at the kitchen counter with the laptop balanced on her left forearm and a peach in her right hand, eating over the sink. The War Room feed is live, picture and sound. She eats standing up when she's planning. Taste: the peach late-season, the skin furred, juice running to her wrist.
2. The room through the feed: the camera in the wall-screen bezel looks down the table, so she sees faces, glass walls and the city lit up behind them, but not the polls on the screen; she hears them read out. A dozen staff. Brandt's chair is empty because it's Tuesday and the Tabard set runs nine to eleven. Iris notes his absence the way you note a missing instrument on a tray. One line, and she doesn't dwell on it.
3. Holt is briefed on the numbers, read aloud. He's up six in the tracking average. An aide says "his to lose" (an echo of the Ch 1 anchor), and Holt corrects the aide's grammar, charmingly, and is right. Iris grades him on syntax again, fairly, and he gets full marks again.
4. A donor has asked, by email, for Holt to attend his daughter's wedding, and for a photograph with the donor's dog. Holt does the donor's voice. The room laughs. It is very funny and Iris laughs too, alone, in her kitchen, and records that she did.
5. Diane manages him. She doesn't laugh. She takes the wedding off the table in one sentence ("A card, a staffer, and no dog"), moves him to the next item, and on the way pauses once before a word, the pause the staff take for thoughtfulness. Iris, who has the dossier, knows what it is and says so in one dry line. She doesn't name the stutter. She calls it "a door she taught herself to hold shut."
6. Holt, who memorises children's names, asks after Olivia: "She get back all right?" Diane: "Monday night. The train." Holt says something warm and specific about Wellesley. Iris was on the far shore of the same daughter two days earlier. One line on that, then on.
7. The system's self-regard, in summary. Nobody in the room looks at the glass, the door, the bezel, or the credenza by the urn. They're the safest people in the city because they've decided they are. Iris lets this be both funny and the injury, in one sentence.
8. [TURN] Holt, gathering his jacket, says lightly over his shoulder, "And the old files. Those are closed?" Diane says "Yes", with no pause at all. It takes five seconds. No name is spoken, and Iris cannot know whether he means her. She has just watched two people discuss her in five seconds, and she has to put the laptop down.
9. Close on the body, inside the Turn: she sets the laptop on the counter. The peach, bitten down to the last third of its flesh, is still in her right hand, sticky to the wrist. There's a metal taste in her mouth (the original injury's taste, first given in Ch 2), and the cold has come into her forearms. She doesn't finish the peach. It is the first food she puts down unfinished on the page (Ch 1's third sandwich was declined, not abandoned), so make it exact. Hands steady throughout; no tremor.

- **Sensory lead:** taste, then sound. The peach: furred skin, the give of the flesh at the stone, sweet and slightly fermented at the bruise. Then the feed's audio: compressed laughter, the HVAC under everything, a chair on carpet, Holt's voice clearer than anyone's because he is the best-miked man in America even when nobody's miking him. Temperature: the cold arriving at the Turn.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - On the room: "Twelve people in a glass box on the thirty-first floor, entirely confident that glass only works in one direction."
  - On Holt's donor impression: "It was a good impression. He has always been very good at doing other people."
- **Body:** eating standing up (planning mode). Hands: peach juice to the wrist, the laptop on the forearm, then the laptop set down, squared to the counter edge. Metal taste. Cold. The unfinished peach.
- **Motifs:** the grade (Holt full marks for syntax, Diane's management graded and passed, the system failed). Cold. Hands. Squaring (the laptop set parallel to the counter edge). The Marine's photograph is **not** shown here; save it.

## Scene 3: The Place

1. Saturday, 12 September, late afternoon. Iris drives in on the old water-authority service track off the county road. She passes a navy-and-gold MARCHETTI lawn sign at the turn, and the grass around it has been mown more recently than anything at the pump station. She parks in the gravel lay-by out of sight of the trail and walks the 900 metres out along the service track.
2. The pump station, below the dam: a single-storey brick building, decommissioned, with iron pipework, a dry grated wet-well, and a steel door with a hasp. A water-authority notice on the door, sun-bleached to the colour of weak tea. On an earlier night (reported in one line, not staged) she cut the authority's padlock and replaced it with an identical one of her own. Nobody has noticed. Nobody checks. That's the pressure again, from the county's side.
3. Preparations, done like an instrument tray. She sweeps the concrete floor and bags the sweepings. She lays heavy plastic sheeting and tapes the edges. She stages the kit along the far wall in a row: zip-ties, industrial tape, black vinyl tape, the red marker, the foil sleeve. She notes the iron bracket at the base of the pump housing and tests it with her weight. She names what an investigator would look for in the room and takes each thing away before he can find it. The competence is the pleasure. Don't name it as pleasure yet.
4. The recorder. A cheap digital voice recorder, not her phone, is taped to the back of the trail marker post where the spur meets the path, 40 metres downslope. That's where the trail is. She starts it recording and walks back up.
5. Clap one, inside, door shut. Then out to the recorder to play it back. Under the birds there's a faint flat knock that came through the door seam. It fails, and she marks it as a fail.
6. Clap two, outside the door, for reference. On playback it's loud and clean and the birds stop for it. Now she knows what a sound from here weighs at the trail.
7. She fixes the seam. Adhesive foam weatherstrip along the frame, a moving blanket hung on the inside of the door, the hasp side packed. This is tape, done properly.
8. [TURN] Clap three, inside, door shut. She walks down and plays it back. Birds, the lap of the water, a cyclist's freewheel ticking past, and nothing from the building. Nothing reaches the trail. She goes back up and inside, shuts the door, and stands in the dark until her eyes adjust. (Here is the forensic sentence: rhodopsin regenerating, the rods taking over from the cones, the room coming back to her in no colour at all, twenty minutes by her count.) She finds she doesn't mind it. She finds she likes it.
9. Hold on the Turn and don't explain it. The liking is about the room, the dark and the quiet. The reader knows what the room is for.

- **Sensory lead:** smell, then sound, then texture. Inside: rust, cold wet concrete, old machine oil gone to varnish, mouse, the mineral breath of the dry wet-well. Sound: the clap in a brick room (sharp, with a short slap-back off the pipes) set against the clap outside (dry, swallowed by the scrub). Texture: the sheeting's static, the weatherstrip's peel-back paper, her palms stinging from the claps. Temperature: the station is about 11 °C, colder than the evening, and stays cold. Light: dusk through the door seam before the fix; after it, none; then the rods.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "There is no dignified way for a woman in her thirties to applaud an empty building three times and then go and listen to a tape of it."
  - On the room going quiet: "My patients are very quiet. It had never occurred to me to want that from a room."
- **Body:** hands, literally the test. Her palms sting from three claps, and she notes the stinging comes from the capillaries in the thenar eminence. Hands steady through all three claps (no tremor; the ledger's one tremor is Ch 2). Cold. Standing still in the dark, her pulse slowing and audible. Hunger is held back for the Snapper.
- **Motifs:** hands (the claps). Tape (the weatherstrip, the sheeting edges, the recorder taped to the post, the kit's two tapes in the row). Cold. The grade (clap one fails, clap three passes). Squaring (the kit in a row, parallel to the wall). The meal comes in the Snapper, as a pre-echo with no kill behind it.
