## Scene 1: Recon

1. Open in the car, Thursday morning, parked across the service road from a single-storey brick-and-steel unit in the warehouse district. There's no sign over the door, but there is a leasing agent's board on the fence. She's eating a char siu pork bun from the bakery at the corner, in her car, at 7:40 a.m. First page: she is already on the job. (She has told the office she has a morning appointment and will be in by ten.)
2. How she got here, in one compressed paragraph. Since Ch 9 she has been doing to him what he did to her. Ten days ago (about Mon 21 Sep) she followed his car from the War Room's hotel garage to this street at 11 p.m. and watched him badge in at a side door and stay forty minutes. She came back once in daylight, Thu 24 Sep, to watch the building from outside. It's paperwork from there, which is her medium. The leasing board names only the managing agent, but the public permit for the fenced diesel generator names the occupier: Lantern Data Services LLC.
3. The building, by smell before sight. Through the cracked window: the roof condensers breathing out hot dust and the faint ozone of electronics, a smell like the inside of a hairdryer. Then diesel from the generator cage, and wet cardboard from the bins of the unit next door. The bun is char siu, the glaze lacquered and sticky, the dough steamed soft and slightly sweet. She wipes her fingers on a napkin before she touches the laptop.
4. The register chain on her clean laptop, tethered to a prepaid hotspot (her own phone is at home in the foil sleeve). Lantern Data Services is wholly owned by Ostler Holdings LLC. Ostler is owned by Brightwater Row Partners LLC. Brightwater Row is owned by Fenwick Lane Holdings LLC. Three holding companies deep. She grades the structure with an anatomical joke: three layers, like the meninges, dura, arachnoid and pia, and no harder to get through if you know where the seams are.
5. The technician. At 8:05 a white van from a climate-control contractor pulls up for his weekly Thursday visit. A man in a hi-vis vest with a lanyard badge goes to the side door, badges in and keys a code on a panel inside the glass vestibule. Through the binoculars (Ch 4) she reads the shape of the code, not the digits. It's a straight line down the middle of the keypad: the midline. She notes that people choose incisions the way she would.
6. The clone, reported in one line as already done. At 7:20, in the queue at the arterial-corner bakery, she stood behind the same man (his Thursday coffee run, which is why she was there) with a card reader in her tote, and his badge's number went into it while he paid for two coffees. His first call is elsewhere in the district; he reaches the unit at 8:05. Her competence is stated, not dwelt on. He bought the second coffee for someone, and she notes it and lets it go.
7. The operator's pattern, in a sentence. One man, mid-thirties, badges in at about 7:55 this morning, as he did on the 24th. No guard, no second car. Brandt has built a room that one camera and one sensor watch, and no person, and she admires it. (His leaving at six belongs to S2.)
8. The top of the chain. Fenwick Lane Holdings' most recent annual report on the state register lists one officer, signed eleven months ago. The signature has no hesitation in it. She sees the signature before she reads the name, and she knows the hand from the dossier in her drawer.
9. **[TURN]** Officer of record: Diane Pell. The Gatekeeper kept the Architect's door. Iris laughs out loud in a parked car with her mouth full of pork bun, and laughs until she has to put the laptop on the passenger seat.
10. The tail of the Turn, one line, not a new beat of plot. The register hasn't been told. Diane has been dead nearly three weeks and is still the officer of four companies, and Iris knows which of those facts is on file where. Leave it there.

- **Sensory lead:** smell (condenser exhaust: hot dust, ozone, the hairdryer note; generator diesel; wet cardboard). Taste: the char siu bun, glaze and steamed dough. Sound: the roof condensers' steady roar, which never stops, because the room inside never sleeps. Temperature: a mild first-of-October morning, with the building's warm breath coming across the road through the cracked window. Say nothing about cold.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - The meninges joke: "dura, arachnoid, pia: three layers, and the last one sticks to the brain." Diane is the pia.
  - "Nearly three weeks after her autopsy, Diane Pell was the officer of record of four companies, which is more than most people manage alive."
  - Optional: "It is very hard to laugh with a mouthful of pork bun. I recommend it."
- **Body:** eating seated, in the car, working: a stakeout meal, not a kill meal and not planning-standing (do not call it either). Greasy fingers wiped before the keys. The laugh is physical: diaphragm, crumbs, eyes watering. Hands steady on the binoculars.
- **Motifs:** the grade (the shell structure, the technician's code, Diane's signature). Paperwork (Iris finding them the way Reyes will find her, by filing; let the reader notice the rhyme and don't point it out). Voicemail, by absence: the phone in its foil sleeve at home. Tape: none yet.

## Scene 2: The Infiltration

1. The same Thursday night. She watched the operator leave at about 6:03 p.m. and the unit go dark, and waited. At 9:05 p.m., parked two streets away, she checks the War Room feed on the tablet: Brandt at the glass corner table, jacket on, phone out, going nowhere yet. He is across the city on the 31st floor and she can't watch him once she's inside. She plans to use forty minutes. The pressure is the feed she is about to leave behind in the car.
2. Dress and kit, done like an instrument tray: workwear from a trade-supply shop matching the climate contractor's (vest, cap, no logo she couldn't explain), nitrile gloves, the cloned card, a foil survival blanket folded flat, a head torch with a red filter, tension wrench and pick, a blade and a roll of black vinyl, glassine envelopes, and the passive tap in a padded pouch. Her phone stays at home in its sleeve; the tablet stays in the car. About 9:40 she walks in with her head down, cap brim to the door camera, the only camera that can see her face.
3. The side door. The cloned card turns the reader green on the first try. In the vestibule she keys the midline into the alarm panel and the panel accepts it. She says to the reader that breaking and entering is mostly entering, and the breaking is for amateurs.
4. The server hall, smell first. Hot, dry, close air from the racks' exhaust, warm metal, a burnt-dust note, the plastic of new cable. Sound: fans in hundreds, a wall of white noise that would swallow a scream, which she notes professionally and moves on from. Temperature: the hot aisle is near 30 degrees and she's sweating under the vest within a minute. The ceiling LEDs wake on motion and stay dark for her: she reads the pale sensor lenses in the red torchlight like a floor plan and walks between their cones along the route she will reuse (ch12). Rows of status LEDs, green and amber. It's the warmest room she has ever broken into, and she likes it.
5. She says it plainly: she is enjoying herself. "The last time I enjoyed a room this much there was a liver in my hands." She doesn't file it under adrenaline. This is the chapter's declaration of appetite as identity.
6. At the end of the hall a glass-fronted office looks back over the racks. That's the room the operator runs. Above its doorway is a separate ceiling-mounted motion sensor (passive infrared) with its own small LED, which the panel in the vestibule did not cover. She reads it as a second lock on a second door: the room inside the room.
7. The near-trip. She steps into the doorway and the sensor's LED flickers amber once, the pre-alarm. She freezes mid-step with her weight on the front foot and holds it. She counts. The flicker stops. Nothing sounds.
8. The recovery, by her own competence. A PIR reads change: a warm body moving fast across a cooler background. She shakes out the foil blanket and holds it in front of her like a sheet held up at a viewing, and crosses the office at the speed of the slowest thing she knows. That's a body cooling. "I have timed a great many people losing a degree an hour. I could go slower than that." It takes her about four minutes to cross three metres. The LED stays dark.
9. The office, sight last. One desk, one chair, two dark monitors (they don't reflect her), a keyboard, a mug with a ring of cold coffee, and a four-drawer steel filing cabinet with a cheap wafer lock she opens with a tension wrench and pick in under a minute. She opens every drawer. The top three hold the shell company's life: incorporation papers, annual reports with Diane's signature, equipment warranties and service contracts. The bottom drawer holds the building's own paper (lease, insurer's schedule, fire certificate) and, hung between the lease and the fire certificate, one folder whose tab carries a name.
10. **[TURN]** A printed file, three years old, with her name on the tab: the campaign's dossier on Iris Vale. Clipped inside the front cover is a colour photograph taken with a long lens. It's her face, outside her own building, younger.

- **Sensory lead:** smell (rack exhaust, warm metal, burnt dust, new cable), then sound (the fans' white noise, the foil blanket's crackle, which is the loudest thing she does all night), then temperature (the hot aisle, sweat at her collar, warm in every sense the Act II register wants). Texture: the foil's crinkle, the wafer lock's pins under the pick. Sight last: the LEDs, the amber flicker.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Breaking and entering is mostly entering. The breaking is for amateurs."
  - The body-cooling crossing: "a degree an hour. I could go slower than that."
  - On the fan noise: "a room you could scream in all night and be filed as a cooling fault."
- **Body:** gloved hands, steady on the card, the keypad and the pick. Her weight frozen on one foot, the calf burning, reported anatomically (gastrocnemius and soleus holding the ankle in plantar flexion, which they are not built to do for long). Sweat. Pulse up and reported as a finding. She's hungry and enjoying it, and she holds it the way she held the step.
- **Motifs:** hands; the grade (Brandt's security graded, high marks for the room inside the room, the operator docked for the mug); the foil (the phone sleeve's big sister, an echo worth one clause); the room that keeps its sounds (pre-echo of the editing suite in Ch 16-17; one line at most and don't explain it). Mirrors withheld: the office glass and monitors give back LEDs only.

## Scene 3: The Insertion

1. She closes the file without reading further. The job comes first. She tells the reader so and is a little proud of the discipline. This is the one beat of restraint in a chapter of indulgence.
2. The tap. Back in the hall, at the rack where the building's outside line comes in, she fits the passive tap inline on the uplink behind the cable management and plugs it into a USB port on the rack's power strip. It has no radio. It stores the full capture on its card, which has to be collected by hand, and sends only a thin encrypted summary and a heartbeat out over the building's own uplink, hidden in the building's own traffic. She bought it, read its manual the way she reads a tox report, and fitted it three times on her kitchen table, and she says so in one line.
3. The reasoning she narrates for Brandt, correctly, the way she did in Ch 9. He will know someone was here, because she is about to tell him. He will sweep for a transmitter and find no radio. He can't call the police about a break-in at a building owned by a dead woman's shell company and used to watch the people who signed his paperwork. The three walls still stand, and she is standing behind them.
4. The calling card that isn't a calling card. Back through the doorway at body-cooling speed under the foil, then at the desk she cuts a single rectangle of black vinyl tape, the length of a pair of eyes, and lays it on the desk in front of the keyboard, squared to the edge. There's no page and no marker. It's a redaction bar with nothing under it. She means it to say only that she was here (ch12 prose: "I had meant it to tell him only that I had been"). The syllabus is teaching back: this is what a student leaves when she has read the teacher's file.
5. She grades the dossier before she leaves it, because she can't not. Her address (the same flat). Her salary band. Her height, two centimetres out. A pencilled column of her movements over two weeks. A printout of the blind item that ended her credibility, with handwritten edits she recognises as Brandt's from his dossier. Then a typed page headed with a summary of her complaint, which she turns over without reading: "I know how it ends." On the routing slip, in Diane's hand, "Reviewed. Contained. D.P." She notes that she was filed between the lease and the fire certificate, as a fire risk.
6. The photograph, looked at properly, sight last because she looks after she knows. A six-by-four print, long lens, slightly soft. She is coming out of her building's front door with a paper bag of groceries in one arm, hair longer than she has worn it since, in a coat she gave away. On the back, in pencil, a date three years ago and a reference number. She notes that the paperwork has a number for her face. (She does not recognise the number's format; see Conflicts, control numbers.)
7. One inference, reported and then refused, in the same move as ch10. The woman in the photograph looks more like Tessa Quinn than Iris looks now. Brandt didn't match Tessa to Iris. He matched Tessa to this. She tells the reader she is not going to think about that, and she doesn't, for the length of the chapter.
8. **[TURN, part 1]** She takes her photograph out of the dossier, slides it into a glassine evidence envelope from her pocket, and leaves the rest. Paperclip and all. The file goes back in the bottom drawer between the lease and the fire certificate, the drawer shut, the wafer lock re-pinned. Out at body-cooling speed, the alarm re-armed on the midline, the side door closed behind her at 10:21 p.m. Forty minutes and change.
9. **[TURN, part 2, the Turn proper]** The car, two streets away, engine off. On the tablet, Brandt is still at the War Room table, knowing nothing yet; she watches him for a minute and turns the screen face-down. She strips her gloves and is hungry in the old, frank way, and reports it as a finding. She doesn't start the engine. She takes the photograph out of the envelope bare-handed, holding it by the edges as if it were evidence, which it is, and looks at it for a long time. She knows the hour passing and lets it pass. She doesn't recognise her either. *("Either": Diane's eyes passed over her in Ch 2 and registered nothing; the campaign filed her and didn't know her; now Iris joins them. Don't explain the antecedent on the page; the reader has Ch 2.)* The scene ends here, on the Turn.

- **Sensory lead:** texture and touch. The vinyl tape's dull sheen and the snick of the blade through it. The glassine envelope. The photograph's satin finish under bare fingertips once the gloves are off: the first thing she has handled bare-handed since the bun. Temperature: out of the hot aisle into the car, with sweat drying on her back, a mild October night, reported as evaporation and nothing else (no cold: Act II is warm, and ch18's cold is not to be pre-empted). Sound: the condensers fading two streets away, then the car's silence, then the engine not starting.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Filed between the lease and the fire certificate. I had been assessed as a fire risk. I had been assessed correctly."
  - "They had my height wrong by two centimetres and everything else right, which is a better mark than most of my residents got."
  - On turning over the complaint summary: "I know how it ends. I was there for the ending."
- **Body:** hands gloved, then bare. The bare hands on the photograph are a quiet seed for the bare right hand in Ch 13 and Ch 17; don't underline it. Sweat drying. Hunger reported straight, before the long look. She sits still for a long time, as she did for an hour in Ch 1, and the reader may hear the echo.
- **Motifs:** tape (the black vinyl rectangle, the redaction bar). The grade (the dossier graded; Diane's routing slip graded posthumously). Paperwork (the reference number on the back of the photograph). Hands (gloved, then bare). Tessa, by face only, not by voicemail: the voicemail motif stays with ch10's timestamp and is not reopened here.
