## Scene 1 — Isolation

1. Sunday 25 October, early afternoon, in the flat. The heating is off, and has been since the small hours of 14 Oct. The phone is face-up on the kitchen counter beside a mug of tea going cold. She opens with the bill: the syllabus had cost her everything outside it, and she could itemise it. Hannah had called three times that week, Monday, Wednesday and Friday, and left no message.
2. The itemising, in a short, flat inventory. Yesterday was June's sixth birthday party at Hannah's house, and Iris did not go and sent no word. On Saturday evening Hannah texted a photograph, no caption: the table after the opening, June in the foreground holding a child's microscope, torn paper in a heap, and on top of the heap the gift tag in Hannah's handwriting, *from Iris*. Hannah had bought her sister's present for her and signed it for her.
3. Iris zooms in on the photograph, as she would on a wound. On the torn paper Hannah's tape is still clinging in strips, every seam taped twice over, the way she has wrapped presents since she was nine. Iris notes that she has seen better occlusion, and she has done better occlusion. It is the scene's one hard joke, and it lands on herself (motif: tape).
4. Mara. Mara has stopped texting. The last text was the one in Ch 18 that Iris didn't answer, and nothing has come since. At work this week Mara has said "morning" and meant only morning, and has taken her coffee to her own desk. Iris reports this exactly, the way she would report a finding. She does not grade it, and she doesn't say she isn't grading it. The gap is left for the reader to notice.
5. Reyes, in the feed. On her tablet against the fruit bowl, where there is no fruit, the War Room is quiet on a Sunday. Campaign counsel (she) is on a call about "the Bureau's records request" for the second time that week. Someone says "Reyes" with the tone people use for weather. Iris notes that the request is still stalled, and that stalls end. (She does not yet know the list goes out Saturday. That is Ch 20.)
6. The work. The fourth page, the one with her name on it, is still face-down on the desk where she left it in Ch 18. She unlocks the drawer (the brass key on the ring with her car key). The third page is there, still unpinned, and she does not take it out. She slides the fourth page in under it, face-down, without turning it over. In Ch 18 she had refused to file it with the syllabus because it was not part of the syllabus, and she notes only that her hand has now filed it there. The work is the only thing she still wants, and she wants it the way you want to finish a book you have stopped enjoying. She locks the drawer.
7. Food, briefly. She made a piece of toast at noon, and the half she didn't eat is still on the plate, its butter set opaque in the cold. She eats standing when she plans, and she has been standing all week. That doesn't make it eating. She doesn't finish the toast.
8. 4:00 p.m. The phone rings on the counter and says HANNAH. Iris registers the hour before the name: Sunday at four is when Diane Pell called her mother, and on 13 September she hadn't. She gives that one line and leaves it. (Hannah's "call me on Sunday" from Ch 1 is not quoted; the reader holds it.) She watches the phone ring out. She does not touch it. Her hands are flat on the counter on either side of it, and she reports how cold the counter is under her palms.
9. She stands and watches the dark screen for a minute and a quarter without touching it, because she knows what a voicemail of that length is: somebody talking into a quiet room. Then it lights with a notification, a voicemail from Hannah, 1:14. It shows a name and a duration and nothing else, because she turned off transcription years ago. She picks the phone up, and her thumb stops over the play arrow. She hasn't decided anything. The thumb has.
10. [TURN] She finds she cannot listen to voicemails any more. She knows what the other two in the folder cost (Ch 18), and her body has filed this one with them before she could. She leaves the message on the phone, unheard, and tells the reader plainly, from where she is narrating, that it is still there and she has never played it.

- **Sensory lead:** Temperature first: the flat unheated in late October, the counter cold under her flat palms, the tea gone tepid with a skin of tannin on it. Sound: the phone's ringtone in a quiet flat, four rings and then the voicemail click, and the War Room murmur at low volume. Texture: the clear tape's glossy ridges in the zoomed photograph. Taste: the tea, stewed and bitter, the only thing she swallows in the scene. Light: the flat in grey Sunday daylight, and the phone screen's blue on her face.
- **Candidate jokes or dry observations:**
  - On Hannah's taping: "She had sealed every seam twice. I have closed airways with less conviction."
  - On the bill: "The syllabus had cost me everything outside it. I had, at least, kept the receipts."
  - On standing: "I eat standing up when I'm planning. I had been standing up all week. This is not the same as eating."
- **Body:** Cold hands on a cold counter. A thumb that won't press, which is the scene's Turn and has to happen in the body. She doesn't decide it. Half a slice of toast, uneaten. She touches the left-flank wound once, through her jumper, while the phone rings. The cold has been back since Ch 18.
- **Motifs:** Voicemail (the third unreturned, and the first she can't play). Tape (Hannah's on the paper). Hands (thumb over the play arrow, palms on the counter). The grade, by its absence: Mara isn't graded.

## Scene 2 — The Apex

1. Same evening, standing at the writing desk (she plans standing), the clean laptop open on it, the desk lamp on and the rest of the flat dark. The Election Eve schedule has reached her through the tap's mirror, the route she has used since the letter in Ch 14. It is the advance team's run-of-show for Monday 2 November: the Calloway's grand ballroom, doors at 6:00 p.m., the Senator's remarks at 9:00 p.m., the green room in a converted meeting suite off the ballroom corridor. She reads it top to bottom, the way she reads a referral.
2. The fortress, layer by layer. A Secret Service perimeter, magnetometers at the ballroom doors, hotel staff vetted and badged, and a managed press pool escorted between a riser and a holding room. She lays it out the way she'd describe the coverings of an organ, plane under plane, each one there to stop the next one being reached. This is one dry anatomical image and no more.
3. The distribution list at the foot of the security annex includes *SA E. Reyes, FBI (campaign protection liaison)*. Reyes will be in the building. Iris reads the name twice, as she did in Ch 14. There is still no page for Reyes, and now there won't be one.
4. She maps it for the third time. The first map was two years ago, in the abstract, before there was a venue. The second was in the week after Ch 17, from public floor plans. This is the third, with the real run-of-show, in pencil on squared paper set parallel to the desk edge. Her desk red stays capped in the pen tray, as in Ch 18. Each layer holds. She tests every approach she has, and each one ends at a man with an earpiece.
5. She checks herself the way she checks everything. She takes her own radial pulse out of habit, and it's 58, resting. Her mouth is dry. She notes this against Ch 17, without saying "Ch 17": the last time she planned a body her mouth watered for hours. She waits for the metal taste that comes whenever the original injury surfaces, and it doesn't come.
6. She names what she feels, once, as the outline does: for the first time in two years of planning, nothing. Not dread and not hunger. Paperwork. Then move straight on and don't elaborate.
7. The footage. There are two previous rallies. The first is Thursday 8 October, at a convention centre: the campaign's own behind-the-scenes clip, posted by the digital team, with a corridor shot that catches the detail closing a door and taking up position outside it. The second is Thursday 22 October, at a university field house: a local station's raw livestream that started early and left the camera on a holding-area door for anyone patient enough to watch. (She has never stood in a rally; she has only ever seen him on footage.)
8. She scrubs both frame by frame. The door shuts, a hand flat on it from outside, the agent's back to it. Then the timecode runs. In both, the door opens again at sixty seconds, give or take a frame, and Holt comes out already smiling. He has never gone over. Detail out, door shut, sixty seconds. Brandt's gift, confirmed.
9. [TURN] She thinks it in a whole sentence: he told her the truth while she was inside him. She gets up, goes to the bathroom, turns on the light over the mirror (the first time she has looked into it since the morning of 14 October, when she did not look up), and says the sentence aloud to her reflection to find out whether it sounds like a joke. It doesn't. End the scene on "It doesn't" or its past-tense equivalent, with no gloss.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first, for once. The laptop fan, the timecode's silence, the click of the trackpad as she scrubs, the local station's ambient crowd noise rising behind a closed door. Then texture: pencil on squared paper, and the graphite shine on the side of her hand. Temperature: the cold flat, with the laptop's warmth on the heels of her hands the only heat in the room. Taste: dry mouth, and the metal that doesn't come. Light: one desk lamp, the screen's blue, then the bathroom's light coming on hard over the mirror at the Turn.
- **Candidate jokes or dry observations:**
  - On the security layers: "Every layer existed to stop the next layer being reached. Whoever designed it had either studied the peritoneum or been very frightened of something."
  - On the press pool: "The press were escorted from riser to holding room like a patient between departments, and with about as much say."
  - On the footage: "He had never gone over. In my experience that is rarer in men than a missing palmaris longus." (Calls back Ch 17 without naming him; fourteen per cent lack one. Use it only if the drafter can make it land dry rather than fond.)
- **Body:** Hands on the trackpad and the pencil, steady, and she reports the steadiness without pleasure. Pulse 58. Dry mouth. No metal. She hasn't eaten since the half-slice at noon, and she doesn't mention food in this scene at all. Its absence is the point. She touches the left-flank wound when she reaches the minute, through her jumper.
- **Motifs:** Mirrors (the first glance, at the Turn, which sets up S3). The Marine's photograph, by implication only, since she can't see inside the room. The Turn depends on the door, not the photograph. Hands. The grade: she grades the security "sound" and does not enjoy grading it. Tape, only if it comes free: the detail's hand flat on the door.

## Scene 3 — The Mirror

1. She stays at the bathroom mirror. The fluorescent over the basin is a hard white strip that makes everyone look like a decedent on intake.
2. Smell first. Unscented soap, and the faint morgue under it that no shower removes, and then something new on her own breath, caught in a cupped hand: pear drops, nail varnish, the sweetish solvent note of acetone. She knows it from a hundred diabetic and starved decedents. Ketosis. Her body has started eating itself for fuel and she can smell it doing it.
3. A long look. Whatever was there in Ch 1 is gone: Hannah's sister, who ate two sandwiches in a park in August, and Mara's mentor, whom Mara watched like a chef. She states it plainly. The person in the mirror is a replacement.
4. She takes her jumper off, then the rest, and does an external examination in the dictaphone voice, echoing Ch 17's opening of Brandt: "The body is that of an adult female, consistent with the stated age..." (no number: Iris's age is never stated in the book). She reaches the word "well-nourished" and can't say it. She settles on "adequately nourished" and lets the reader see the downgrade. She finishes the sentence; she does not stop mid-way (that is Ch 25's).
5. Findings, in exact anatomical language. Her clavicles stand proud and the suprasternal notch is deeper. The anterior superior iliac spines show. Her belt has a new hole, which she punched on Thursday 22 October with the point of a scalpel blade, because it was the tool she trusted and she wasn't going to buy an awl for it. She is about four kilos down since the start of October (never a weight in kilos or a scale reading; she has not weighed herself). Her skin is dry over the knuckles and cracked from nitrile.
6. The wound. The Ch 12 scar on the left flank, below the tip of the twelfth rib, is three and a half weeks old: pink and still raised, a 3.2 cm line with seven small paired track marks where the nylon went in, not yet silvered. With the fat gone from around it it sits closer to the rib than it used to. She touches it, which is how the reader knows she's thinking. It's the only part of her that hasn't changed its opinion of her.
7. Tired, rendered as findings. Periorbital darkening. She pulls down her lower lid and the conjunctiva is pale, so her iron is down. Her tongue has a white coat. She would write "very tired" on nobody's form, so she writes it on hers. She also notes the competence: hands steady held out flat, no tremor. The replacement is competent, very tired, and has lost weight. Grade: competent (in Ch 18 she graded the exit and "did not grade myself"; this is the first time she does). This is the syllabus voice, briefly, turned on herself.
8. Reyes as the borrowed eye. She looks at herself the way a witness would have to describe her: height, build, the jaw, the widow's peak and the parting that Tessa Quinn was picked for, and the hands. She has no idea yet that a hand will be the thing noticed (Ch 26 and Interlude 5). This is dramatic irony only, and it stays off the page as a statement. Iris thinks of descriptions on paper because paper is what Reyes has. She states no height figure.
9. [TURN] She checks the replacement for flaws the way she'd check a body on the table: methodically, region by region, nothing skipped. She finds them. She doesn't look away. End the scene on the not-looking-away, held. No reflection on what it means.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell (ketones, the acetone sweetness on her own breath, soap over morgue). Then temperature: the bathroom tiles cold under her bare feet, gooseflesh on her upper arms when the jumper comes off, and she names the arrector pili. Texture: the cracked skin over the knuckles, the ridge of the scar under her fingertip. Light: the white strip over the mirror, flat and intake-pale.
- **Candidate jokes or dry observations:**
  - On the downgrade: "I got as far as well-nourished and had to amend the report."
  - On the ketones: "I smelled of pear drops. In my professional experience, that is how you know a body has started on the furniture."
  - On the belt: "I punched the new hole with a scalpel blade. I'd like to say it was the only tool to hand. It was the only tool I trusted."
  - Optional, on the mirror: "I had brushed my teeth for eleven days looking at the tap."
- **Body:** The whole body, examined: clavicles, iliac spines, belt, scar, conjunctiva, tongue, hands. Cold feet on tile, gooseflesh. Her hand on the wound. She hasn't eaten since noon. The ketone breath is how starvation gets onto the page without the word.
- **Motifs:** Mirrors (19, the body; the bathroom mirror). Hands (held out flat to check for tremor, and the cracked knuckles). The grade (she grades herself "competent", the only grade she gives a person in the chapter). Voicemail, which carries into the Snapper.
