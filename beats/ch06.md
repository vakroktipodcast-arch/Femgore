## Scene 1 — The Sentencing

1. Open inside the trouble. Sunday mid-morning in the pump station, after Ch 5 closes. The foil-wrapped phone is still on the floor between them, the lantern is on the concrete, and Diane is on the concrete floor against the pump housing, one zip-tie per wrist joined to the iron bracket at its base. Iris is in a white coverall with the hood up. "I looked like a crime scene that had arrived early."
2. Iris explains the curriculum plainly, like a course convenor on the first day: three pages, three subjects, three evaluations. Diane is first. The grade is not negotiable and has already been given; today is only when it is handed back.
3. Diane listens the way a compliance director listens, already drafting the minutes. She tries process one last time ("If this is about a complaint, there are channels."). Iris: "I know. I used them." That's the joke and the indictment in five words, and Iris is pleased with it.
4. Diane asks once, with care, whether anyone is near Olivia. Iris: "No one is near Olivia." It's true. Iris notes that she gave the answer quickly, and grades the speed as a kindness, and marks herself down for it.
5. Iris recites a number from memory: the matter number from the footer of every page of the campaign NDA, LC-0311. It's the kind of thing Diane would know by heart, because she assigned it. Diane's eyes go to the number first and then, in the lantern, to Iris's face.
6. Diane realises who she is: a small sound at the back of the throat. Report it anatomically: glottal, involuntary, the larynx closing on something it had not been told to swallow. Iris has heard that sound on the phone from families. She has never been the news before.
7. Diane looks at her for a long time. Iris lets her. It's the first time in three years anyone from the campaign has looked at her and registered something (it pays off Ch 2's "her eyes pass over me").
8. Diane apologises. It's sincere, fluent and quiet: "I'm sorry. I'm so sorry. I should have protected you from that." It is not for the NDA, the file or the cabinet. It is for not keeping Iris safe from what the file was about. She means it.
9. [TURN] It was the worst thing she could have said. Diane still believes the file was protection and that only the protecting failed, which is exactly the Evaluation. And she said it kindly, and Iris has no column for Diane's kindness. Iris watches her not know why. For one line Iris is not amused by anything. Allowed here: the metal taste from Ch 2 surfaces, one clause only. Then the line ends.

- **Sensory lead:** smell first. Damp concrete, old machine oil gone to varnish in the pump's seals, rust (Iris notes it is iron oxide, a cousin of what she smells at work), and Diane: a floral drugstore shampoo, the walk's clean sweat turning to the other sweat, the waxed cotton of her jacket. Sound: one slow drip somewhere in the dark. From the trail outside, nothing (the Ch 4 clap test held). Temperature: about 11 °C inside, a cellar cold that comes up through the floor. Light: one battery LED lantern on the floor, blue-white, casting both their shadows up the curved wall.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Zip-ties are designed by people who never have to take one off, which is how you know they work."
  - She listened "the way compliance directors listen: as if she would be asked to initial it later."
  - "I know. I used them."
- **Body:** Iris's hands are double-gloved in nitrile and cold, and steady. She notes they are steady and doesn't make anything of it (ch01's baseline, ch02's single tremor). Diane's wrists are already pink at the zip-ties. Iris's pulse is still up from Ch 5. She is hungry and doesn't mention it yet.
- **Motifs:** the grade (the curriculum explained, Iris marking her own answer about Olivia); tape (the kit bag open, the black roll visible, not used yet); hands; cold.

## Scene 2 — The Method

1. Iris tells the reader she is going to describe it properly, because she is the only person qualified to, and because "you came for this." It is spoken to the reader as an accessory (style 01), and it implicates.
2. The black vinyl tape goes over the eyes first: two strips, temple to temple. The roll gives a dull, almost polite unpeeling sound. Diane's eyelashes move against the adhesive once and then stop. Diane is now in the dark. Iris notes that sensory deprivation lowers the startle threshold and that everything after this will arrive as an ambush. (Something happens between this beat and the next. It is withheld here and returned in beat 10.)
3. The paper comes from a clear zip bag: photocopies of pages 1–8 of Iris's own campaign NDA, no signature page, cross-cut at home into confetti the size of fingernail parings. Her signed original stays in the drawer. She shows the bag to the blind woman, for form's sake. "It was the first time anyone at the campaign had been asked to swallow one."
4. Iris steadies Diane's head with the fingertips of one hand either side of the larynx and packs the mouth with two gloved fingers of the other, past the incisors and over the tongue, seating the bolus back towards the oropharynx with the pressure she would use for a laryngoscope blade, so the gag reflex fires once, early, and is spent. This is the precision Mara will call "practised" in Ch 8 (and the fingertips are the thyroid-border bruises she will find). Then the texture: dry paper drinking saliva and softening, weight for weight, the way she'd tested it in a glass at her kitchen counter "like a woman checking whether a cake was done."
5. Then the industrial tape, and its noise: the shriek of it coming off the roll, which no one can unroll discreetly. "The only tool in the kit that announces itself." Three turns around the lower face and the back of the head, over the mouth and both nostrils. It catches the hair at Diane's nape. Iris notes it and doesn't solve it. That's the adhesive Ch 8 will hold against her.
6. What a body does in the dark when it cannot breathe, in order. First, nothing: twenty or thirty seconds of Diane holding still. It's discipline, the same muscle that beat the stutter, and Iris admires it out loud. Then the first involuntary inspiration against the closed airway. The diaphragm pulls, the tape dimples inward over the nares, and the suprasternal notch and intercostal spaces draw in. It's carbon dioxide that drives the panic, not lack of oxygen. "The body is not afraid of having no air. It is afraid of its own exhaust."
7. Then the struggle. The zip-ties bite against the bracket. Her heels go against the concrete, scraping and then drumming. A hum through the tape with nowhere to go. Her fingers behind her open and close on nothing. Iris narrates each phase with its textbook timing and checks them against her watch. The joke rate drops through this beat and is gone by the end of it.
8. Convulsion, then loss of consciousness, then the bladder: warm ammonia in the cold air, expected, logged. Then the agonal gasps, spaced out further each time like someone remembering something halfway through a sentence. Under the black tape the petechiae are blooming in the conjunctivae, and the face is going dusky above the pewter band. Iris has opened this from the inside dozens of times and is now watching the outside of it. She doesn't need to see the eyes. She knows what they look like.
9. The quiet. Two fingers on the carotid, counting it down until there is nothing to count. Then a long, greedy sentence about the quiet itself: the drip, the lantern's faint electrical whine, her own pulse loud in the hood, her face hot. She reports the pleasure as a finding and gives it the same weight as the time (10:47 a.m.). She has stopped being funny and doesn't notice. Don't flag it on the page. No joke appears from beat 8 to the end of the scene.
10. [TURN] Then she gives the reader the thing she skipped. After the black went over Diane's eyes and before the paper and the tape, Diane began a word, and the old hesitation caught it on its first syllable: once, trochaic, the pause her staff took for thoughtfulness. Iris put the paper in before the word arrived. She never heard what it was. She has heard the hesitation ever since.

- **Sensory lead:** sound. Vinyl tape's dull unpeel, then industrial tape's shriek and tear, the hum through tape, heels on concrete, the gasps, then the quiet. Texture: the paper softening under her fingertip, the tongue's resistance, the larynx under her other fingertips. Smell: paper and toner as the bolus wets, then urine, then nothing new. Temperature: the cold of the station against the heat in her own face. Sight last and minimal: the dimple of tape over the nostrils, the dusky colour above the band (name it precisely: plum going to slate).
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (beats 1–6 only):**
  - "It was the first time anyone at the campaign had been asked to swallow one."
  - "Industrial tape cannot be unrolled discreetly. It is the only tool in the kit that announces itself."
  - "The body is not afraid of having no air. It is afraid of its own exhaust", ideally followed by a beat about which institution that also describes, left unsaid.
- **Body:** Iris's hands work at the throat and in the mouth, gloved and cold, steady, exact. Two fingers on Diane's carotid. Her own pulse is loud inside the hood and her face is hot (the warmth is reported, not explained; it's an Act I exception that she files as exertion). Her left hand does nothing it shouldn't. Ch 7's "perfectly still" needs this baseline.
- **Motifs:** tape (black vinyl and industrial in one scene, the motif's first use at full strength); hands; the grade (her admiration of Diane's discipline is a mark awarded); cold. The crack is the seed for Ch 18, where Iris becomes the Gatekeeper herself. The word she never heard lines up with the voicemails she never listened to. Don't point at it.

## Scene 3 — The Mark

1. Afterwards. Iris sits back on her heels on the concrete for a while. Report the duration in minutes. She notes that Diane's warmth is leaving her at the rate she would expect, and that this is the first time she has been present for the start of a cooling curve she usually only reads at the end.
2. She peels off the industrial tape, slowly, the reverse shriek. It takes fine hair from the nape and leaves a grey adhesive line across the cheeks and the back of the head. She bags the tape. The jaw stays open around the bolus. The paper shows at the lips, softened and grey-white, with a fragment on the lower lip that still carries part of a footer: LC-03… She leaves it.
3. She cuts the zip-ties at the bracket, one per wrist, and bags them (they carry lot numbers). The wrists come round into Diane's lap with the ligature furrows already printed in them, and Iris lets the furrows stay; they are evidence of nothing she owns.
4. Diane's olive waxed-cotton jacket comes open to the sternum. The page is a fresh print of Lesson One, so the drawer's master stays in the drawer. She pins it with a steel pin through the page, the slate shirt and the skin over the manubrium, in the midline. Nothing comes. The dead don't bleed. Note it plainly. It is the line Ch 17 will overturn.
5. The red marker. Its solvent smell in the cold, the cap held in her teeth, the squeak of the nib, and the circle round THE GATEKEEPER drawn the way she'd circle a finding for a registrar who missed it. The Evaluation was typed two years ago: CONFUSED PROCESS FOR PROTECTION. She reads it against the woman who apologised in S1. It held.
6. The shredded NDAs are left where they are: in the mouth, and the few that fell onto the jacket and the floor. She explains why to the reader as policy. The matter number is the campaign counsel's, and it points at the campaign. She does not follow the number any further. (Ch 7 makes that admission. Here it is simply not thought.)
7. She files the feeling. The first lesson went well, and the pleasure was in the grade. She says this with the confidence of someone who has decided to, and the reader has just watched S2.
8. [TURN] She wants the first investigator to read them, whoever it is, standing where she is standing. And she notices, as a finding, that she wants a lot of things right now: to take the black off and look at the eyes, to open her and check the liver against her Ch 4 guess, to sit here longer, to eat. None of them is to leave.

- **Sensory lead:** temperature. Diane cooling, Iris's own fingers stiff inside the nitrile in the cellar cold. Then smell: the marker's solvent, which is the only new smell in the room and almost festive. Sound: the reverse shriek of the tape, the snip of the ties, the nib's squeak. Taste: her own mouth dry under the hood, then watering, which she reports without comment.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (humour returns here, thinner and drier, and she doesn't notice that either):**
  - On the pin: "Through skin that has stopped bleeding it goes in like a pin through a hem. The fabric doesn't argue."
  - "The campaign never used red. Red was for corrections."
  - "I wanted to open her. Professional curiosity. I had a bet with myself about her liver."
- **Body:** cold hands working the cutters, the pin and the marker, steady. She sits on her heels and her knees ache on the concrete, and her stomach starts to ask. Her mouth waters near the end. She doesn't eat. There is nothing to eat, and she reports that as a planning flaw, and grades herself down for it.
- **Motifs:** the grade (THE GATEKEEPER circled, the Evaluation read, the feeling filed under the grade, her own planning marked down); tape (the industrial tape removed and bagged, the black left on); hands; the meal after (anticipated: "ravenous" sets up Ch 7); cold.
