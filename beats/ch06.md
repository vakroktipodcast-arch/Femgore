## Scene 1 — The Sentencing

1. Open inside the trouble, about 8:40 a.m., right after Ch 5. The foil-wrapped phone is on the floor beside the lantern, which stands on the concrete between them. Diane is on the floor against the pump housing, knees drawn up, one zip-tie per wrist joined to the iron bracket at its base, ankles bound. Iris is in a white coverall with the hood up, on the pump's plinth, out of her reach, face turned a little off hers ("I know what breath carries"). "I looked like a crime scene that had arrived early."
2. Iris explains the curriculum plainly, like a course convenor on the first morning: three pages, three subjects, three evaluations. Diane is first. The grade is not negotiable and is not pending. It was given two years ago; today is only when it is handed back.
3. Diane listens the way a compliance director listens, already drafting the minutes. She tries process one last time ("If this is about a complaint, there are channels."). Iris: "I know. I used them." That's the joke and the indictment in five words. It was not planned, and Iris is pleased with it.
4. Diane asks once, with care, whether anyone is near Olivia ("Is anyone near Olivia?"). Iris: "No one is near Olivia." It's true. Iris notes that she gave the answer quickly, and grades the speed as a kindness, and marks herself down for it. Nobody asked her to be kind. It wasn't on the paper.
5. Iris recites a number from memory: the matter number from the footer of every page of the campaign NDA, "LC-0311", under a thin rule. Diane knows it as she knows her own postcode, because she walked that matter through the building herself. Her eyes go to the number first (up and to the left, a woman reading a footer off the inside of her own skull) and then, in the lantern, to Iris's face.
6. Diane realises who she is: a small sound at the back of the throat. Report it anatomically: glottal, involuntary, the larynx closing on something it had not been told to swallow. Iris has heard that sound on the phone from mothers and husbands at the moment her sentence reaches its noun. She has never been the noun before.
7. Diane looks at her for a long time. Iris lets her. It's the first time in three years anyone from the campaign has looked at her and had something happen behind the face (it pays off Ch 2's "her eyes pass over me"). Something is lit behind Diane's face floor by floor, and Iris has waited long enough to wait through it.
8. Diane apologises. It's sincere, fluent and quiet: "I'm sorry. I'm so sorry. I should have protected you from that." It is not for the NDA, the file, the folder, the cabinet or the room no one enters. It is for not keeping Iris safe from what the file was about. She means it.
9. [TURN] It was the worst thing she could have said. Diane still believes the file was protection and that only the protecting failed, as though the cabinet were a good cabinet someone forgot to lock. That is exactly the Evaluation, read back in her own words, and said kindly, and Iris has no column for Diane's kindness. Iris watches her not know why. For one line Iris is not amused by anything. The metal taste from Ch 2 surfaces, one clause only (a coin held at the back of the tongue). Then the line ends.

- **Sensory lead:** smell first. Damp concrete, old machine oil gone to varnish in the pump's seals, rust (iron oxide, a cousin of what she smells at work, and she likes having it in the room), and Diane: a floral drugstore shampoo (freesia, or something allowed to use its name), the walk's clean sweat being replaced from the axillae outward by "the other sweat", the waxed cotton of her jacket. Sound: one slow drip somewhere in the dark. From the trail outside, nothing (the Ch 4 clap test held). Temperature: about 11 °C, a cellar cold that comes up through the floor. Light: one battery LED lantern on the floor, blue-white ("the light of a fridge opened at night"), casting both their shadows up the curved brick, four of them in the room and two very tall.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Zip-ties are designed by people who never have to take one off, which is how you know they work."
  - The phone in its foil sleeve, "wrapped like a leftover."
  - She listened "the way compliance directors listen: as if she would be asked to initial it later."
  - Waxed cotton "smells like a wet dog that went to a good school."
  - "I know. I used them."
- **Body:** Iris's hands are double-gloved in nitrile and cold, and steady. She checks them and does nothing with the information (ch01's baseline, ch02's single tremor). Her pulse is still up from the door, the knee and the tape, felt in the hinge of the jaw. Diane's wrists are already pink at the zip-ties. Iris is hungry and doesn't mention it yet.
- **Motifs:** the grade (the curriculum explained; Iris marking her own answer about Olivia); tape (the kit bag open nearby, black roll not yet used); hands; cold; the paperwork thread (the matter number, strategy 09).

## Scene 2 — The Method

1. Iris tells the reader she is going to describe it properly, because she is the only person qualified to, and because "you came for this." It is spoken to the reader as an accessory (style 01), and it implicates. Then the unremarked time: she lets Diane sit with the grade for about two hours, Diane against the housing, Iris on the plinth, neither speaking. "The part of the course where the student reflects." Then she stands.
2. The black vinyl tape goes over the eyes first (about 10:36): two strips, the first over the brows and upper lids, the second a little lower and overlapping, temple to temple, pressed down along the zygomatic arches with her thumbs. The roll gives a dull, almost polite unpeeling sound. Diane's eyelashes move against the adhesive once and then stop. Diane is now in the dark. Iris notes that sensory deprivation lowers the startle threshold and that everything after this will arrive as an ambush. (Something happens between this beat and the next. It is withheld here and returned in beat 10.)
3. The paper comes from a clear zip bag. The sound makes Diane's shoulders come up, a noise she has heard over a thousand packed lunches and never as a threat. It is photocopies of pages 1–8 of Iris's own campaign NDA, no signature page, cross-cut at her kitchen table into confetti the size of fingernail parings. Her signed original stays in the drawer. She shows the bag to the blind woman, for form's sake. "It was the first time anyone at the campaign had been asked to swallow one."
4. Iris steadies Diane's head with the fingertips of her left hand either side of the larynx, at the thyroid borders, so she can't swallow or retch the paper away. Her face is turned up and away over Diane's head. She has tipped Diane's chin down, because breakfast at about seven can come back at half past ten. With two gloved fingers of the right she packs the mouth past the incisors and over the tongue and seats the bolus towards the oropharynx in one push, with the pressure she'd use for a laryngoscope blade. One push gags once, hard, and is spent (a pinch at a time gags in a series). This is the precision Mara will call "practised" in Ch 8 (and the fingertips are the thyroid-border bruises she will find). Then the texture: dry paper is card, then drinks saliva weight for weight, card to felt to the skin on a pan of porridge, as she'd tested it in a glass at her kitchen counter the week before, "like a woman checking whether a cake was done". It smells of toner as it wets.
5. Then the industrial tape, and its noise: the shriek of it coming off the roll, which no one can unroll discreetly. "The only tool in the kit that announces itself." She shuts the jaw over the bolus with the heel of her left hand and anchors the free end on the cheek. Then, with her palm on the occiput tipping the head forward, three turns over the mouth and both nostrils and round the back of the skull, pewter, snug. 10:40 by her watch. On the second turn it catches the fine hair at Diane's nape. Iris notes it and doesn't solve it ("I had a lot on"). That's the adhesive Ch 8 will hold against her.
6. What a body does in the dark when it cannot breathe, in order. First, nothing: twenty to thirty seconds of Diane holding still. It's discipline, the same muscle that beat the stutter, and Iris admires it out loud ("That's very good"), as if holding still were a channel. Then the first involuntary inspiration against the closed airway. The diaphragm pulls, the tape dimples inward over the nares, and the suprasternal notch and intercostal spaces draw in. It's carbon dioxide that drives the panic, not lack of oxygen. "The body is not afraid of having no air. It is afraid of its own exhaust." She has some sympathy with the brainstem. (The ready joke is "I have been somebody's exhaust"; the institutional reading is left unsaid.)
7. Then the struggle. The zip-ties bite and knock against the bracket ("a sound like someone trying a locked gate"). Her bound heels go against the concrete, scraping and then drumming. A hum through the tape with nowhere to go. Her fingers behind her open and close on nothing. Iris's pulse comes up into her jaw and keeps time with the heels, and she lets it. She narrates each phase with its textbook timing and checks them against her watch. The joke rate drops through this beat and is gone by the end of it.
8. Loss of consciousness (you can see it in the hands: they stop meaning anything), then convulsion, then the bladder: warm ammonia in the cold air, expected, logged. Then a stillness long enough to be mistaken for the end, and the agonal gasps, spaced out further each time like someone remembering something halfway through a sentence. Above the pewter band the face is going dusky, plum going to slate. She can't see the eyes. She would put money, unseen, on petechiae in the conjunctivae (smothering doesn't promise them, but Diane fought hard enough to earn them). She has opened this from the inside dozens of times, and is now watching the outside of it, from the beginning, in order. No joke from here to the end of the scene.
9. The quiet. Two fingers on the carotid, counting it down: slowing, thinning, a few beats and a gap, until there is nothing to count. She keeps her fingers there anyway until she is sure: 10:47. Then a long, greedy sentence about the quiet itself: the drip, the lantern's faint electrical whine she couldn't hear over Diane, her own pulse loud inside the hood ("a hundred and something", in the jaw and throat), her face hot in a room at eleven degrees. She reports the pleasure as a finding and gives it the same weight as the time ("It goes on the sheet next to 10:47"). She has stopped being funny and doesn't notice. Don't flag it on the page.
10. [TURN] Then she gives the reader the thing she skipped. After the black went over Diane's eyes and before the paper went in, Diane began a word, and the old hesitation caught it on its first syllable: once, trochaic (a stress and a fall), not the pause her staff took for thoughtfulness. This one stuck, and that is why her mouth was open. Iris put the paper in before the word arrived. She never heard what it was. "I have heard the hesitation since."

- **Sensory lead:** sound. Vinyl tape's dull unpeel, the zip bag's rustle, then industrial tape's shriek, the hum through tape, plastic on the iron bracket, heels on concrete, the gasps, then the quiet. Texture: the paper softening under her fingertip, the tongue's resistance, the larynx under her other fingertips. Smell: paper and toner as the bolus wets, the other sweat sharpening through the freesia, then urine. Temperature: the cold of the station against the heat in her own face. Sight last and minimal: the dimple of tape over the nostrils, the dusky colour above the band (plum going to slate).
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (beats 1–6 only):**
  - "It was the first time anyone at the campaign had been asked to swallow one."
  - "Industrial tape cannot be unrolled discreetly. It is the only tool in the kit that announces itself."
  - "The body is not afraid of having no air. It is afraid of its own exhaust."
  - "That's very good," said of Diane holding still, "as if holding still were a channel."
- **Body:** Iris's hands work at the throat and in the mouth, gloved and cold, steady, exact. Two fingers on Diane's carotid. Her own pulse is loud inside the hood and her face is hot (the warmth is reported, not explained; an Act I exception she files as exertion). Her left hand does nothing it shouldn't. Ch 7's "perfectly still" needs this baseline.
- **Motifs:** tape (black vinyl and industrial in one scene, the motif's first use at full strength); hands; the grade (her admiration of Diane's discipline is a mark awarded); cold. The crack is the seed for Ch 18, where Iris becomes the Gatekeeper herself. The word she never heard lines up with the voicemails she never listened to. Don't point at it.

## Scene 3 — The Mark

1. Afterwards. Iris sits back on her heels on the concrete for nine minutes by her watch (to about 10:56). She notes that Diane is cooling at the rate she would expect, which in the first hour is almost no rate at all (the core holds on a plateau, and only the periphery goes early: fingertips, nose tip, backs of the hands). This is the first time she has been present for the start of a cooling curve she usually only reads from the far end. She watches it, "the way you watch the first minutes of a bath running". Her knees ache. Her fingers are stiff in the nitrile. The heat has gone out of her face.
2. She peels off the industrial tape, slowly, the reverse shriek. It takes fine hair from the nape and leaves a line of adhesive, the colour of dishwater, across both cheeks and round the back of the head. She bags the tape. The jaw stays open around the bolus. The paper shows at the lips, softened, bone-pale going to ash at the edges, with a fragment on the lower lip that still carries part of a footer: LC-03 and then the cut. She leaves it.
3. She cuts the zip-ties at the bracket, one per wrist, and bags them (they carry lot numbers). The ankle ties and the tape band there go into the bag with them. The wrists come round into Diane's lap with the ligature furrows already printed in them (pale troughs, pink margin), and the ankles keep theirs. Iris lets them stay. They are evidence of nothing she owns.
4. Diane's olive waxed-cotton jacket comes open to the sternum. The slate shirt is buttoned to the second button, warm still at the centre. The page is a fresh print of Lesson One, so the drawer's red-annotated master stays in the drawer. She sets it flat over the manubrium, in the midline, and pins it with one steel pin through page, shirt and skin. "Through skin that has stopped bleeding it goes in like a pin through a hem. The fabric doesn't argue." Nothing came. Say it plainly and without the aphorism (Ch 17 owns "the dead don't bleed"). It is still the plainest thing in the room.
5. The red marker: its solvent smell in the cold, the only new smell in the room and almost festive after the ammonia. She holds the cap in her teeth, lips shut, face up off the page, because a cap in a glove touches everything the glove touches after. The nib squeaks. She circles THE GATEKEEPER the way she'd circle a finding for a registrar who missed it: once, closed, no going round twice. The cap drops from her teeth into a glassine envelope and into her pocket. The Evaluation was typed two years ago and isn't written here: CONFUSED PROCESS FOR PROTECTION. She reads it against the woman who asked about channels, then Olivia, then apologised. It held.
6. The shredded NDAs are left where they are: in the mouth, and the few pieces that fell onto the jacket and the floor. She explains why to the reader as policy. The matter number is counsel's, and it points at the people who keep the file. She is satisfied with where it points and stops thinking there. (Ch 7 makes the admission that it points further. Here it is simply not thought.)
7. She files the feeling. The first lesson went very well, and the pleasure was the pleasure of the grade being handed back to the right person, in the right room, in full. She says it in the dictation voice with the confidence of someone who has decided to be confident, and the reader has just watched S2.
8. [TURN] Her mouth had gone dry under the hood, and then it was watering. She wants the first investigator to read the fragments, whoever it is, crouching where she is crouching with a torch and tweezers, picking a wet scrap off a dead woman's lip and reading counsel's number off it and understanding, slowly. And she notices, as a finding, that she wants a lot of things right now: to take the black off and look at the eyes, to collect on the petechiae, to open her and check the liver against her guess (about 1,500 g), to sit here longer, to eat. None of them is to leave.

- **Sensory lead:** temperature. Diane cooling at the edges, Iris's own fingers stiff inside the nitrile in the cellar cold, the heat gone from her face. Then smell: the marker's solvent, the only new smell in the room. Sound: the reverse shriek of the tape, the snip of the ties, the nib's squeak. Taste: the marker cap in her teeth ("solvent and somebody else's stationery cupboard"), then her own mouth dry under the hood, then watering, which she reports without comment.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (humour returns here, thinner and drier, and she doesn't notice that either):**
  - On the pin: "Through skin that has stopped bleeding it goes in like a pin through a hem. The fabric doesn't argue."
  - "The campaign never used red. Red was for corrections."
  - "I wanted to open her. Professional curiosity. I had a bet with myself about her liver."
- **Body:** cold hands working the cutters, the pin and the marker, steady. She sits on her heels and her knees ache on the concrete, and her stomach starts to ask. Her mouth waters near the end. She doesn't eat. There is nothing to eat (she brought nothing), and she reports that as a flaw in the planning and takes a mark off for it.
- **Motifs:** the grade (THE GATEKEEPER circled, the Evaluation read, the feeling filed under the grade, her own planning marked down); tape (the industrial tape removed and bagged, the black left on); hands; the meal after (anticipated: "ravenous" sets up Ch 7); cold.
