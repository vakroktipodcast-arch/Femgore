# Beat sheet — ch22: The Point of No Return

- **Unit:** ch22 (`manuscript/ch22.md`; not yet drafted)
- **POV:** Iris, first person, past tense. The outline's Turns are in present tense, so render them in past tense on the page ("The sticker was torn. Someone would see it. My clock had become a shorter clock.").
- **Target words:** ~3,150. Suggested split: S1 ~1,100 / S2 ~1,000 / S3 ~1,050. The whole chapter covers only about fifteen seconds of real time, and the page slows them right down. Keep the count in whole seconds and mention it rarely (see the timing note under Conflicts).
- **Chapter head:** no syllabus insert. Inserts appear only at the heads of 1, 6 and 17 (style 08).
- **Act pressure:** Act III, The Final Exam. Reyes is closing on paper, and the knowledge Iris has carried since 16–18 is in every room. In 22 Reyes is in the building: Iris knows from the run of show (p. 4, "FBI LIAISON: SA E. REYES") and the security annex distribution ("SA E. Reyes, FBI (campaign protection liaison)") that she is here tonight, and from the tap that the list was produced on Saturday. Iris does not know the list's order or her own booking, and she never thinks of herself by a number. The knowledge is the voicemail ("I think we may have... known the same person"), the eleven names, and the page with Iris's own name on it, face-down in the locked drawer at home on top of the working masters and the dossiers now that the third page is in her blazer.
- **Appetite at entry:** Starved, but pointed at one thing. She has eaten nothing since black coffee and a heel of bread on Sunday morning ("You don't eat before", ch20). The only pleasure left in ch21 was the habit of reading the crowd as bodies. She comes through the door hungry for the one meal the whole curriculum was written for, which is his recognition. It is the first real hunger since 18, and it is aimed at him.
- **Appetite at exit:** The meal doesn't come. He smiles, and there is nothing in her mouth: no watering, no heat, no penny taste from the original injury (ledger: metal taste absent in 22; ch23 S2 checks for it again, so give it one clause here at most). She hasn't checked for the appetite yet. That checking is ch23's Snapper ("I check for it the way you check a pocket"), so don't pre-empt it. In 22 the absence is shown through the mouth and the hands only, never named. No "empty", "hollow" or "numb" (style 11).
- **Voice rules in force:**
  - The syllabus voice and grading are **gone** in 22–25 (style 02). Nobody is graded, including the Service, Holt and herself. Make the absence visible once (S2, beat 8), the way Mara's ungrading is made visible elsewhere.
  - Jokes are at their thinnest so far: one or two dry lines in S1, at most one in S2, and in S3 one joke she declines to make.
  - Latin and anatomy are still present in S1 and S2 (she reads his face like a chart), and they thin out over S3.
  - The sentences in S3 get shorter as the scene goes on. That is how "I hear how small my voice has got" happens on the page.
  - No "beautiful".
  - No em-dash cut-offs. That licence belongs to Holt in 24 only (style 07).
- **Dialogue design:** Holt's line in S3 is the only spoken dialogue in the chapter, and it is his. Iris says nothing aloud anywhere in 22. Her name is spoken first in ch23, Scene 1, so she must not give it here, not even to the reader as something she nearly says.

## Scene 1 — Trapped

1. First sentence: my gloved hand flat on the sticker, the count at five. This picks up straight from 21's Snapper. The seal is a white tamper-evident label laid across the gap between door and jamb at shoulder height, corridor side. It bears the Service's printed legend, and a VOID pattern is hidden in the adhesive. The pantry service door opens inward into the suite. The Service sealed it instead of locking it because the hotel can't lock it from the corridor side.
2. Her plan, given in one clause (ch20 supplied only the seal's make; the plan is fixed here): lift the label whole along the jamb with a warmed scalpel blade, the way you lift tape off skin without taking the epidermis, so that it can be laid back down afterwards and still read as intact. The plan allowed fifteen seconds for it, and it was built for the sixty-second minute on the old footage.
3. She doesn't have fifteen seconds. She runs the blade down the gap instead, a cut where there should have been a lift. As the door moves, the label's face film catches on the door edge and parts with a small, dry zip. It sounds like a plaster coming off. She hears it and keeps going. The blade goes back into its sheath and into her right inside pocket.
4. Through, and the door closer brings the door to on her heel. On the suite side there is a thumb-turn. She turns it. The click commits her, and she tells the reader so in one line: nobody comes in behind me, and I have chosen that. The ballroom brass through the wall covers the click, and the man in the chair does not turn.
5. She's behind a folding banquet screen, the kind hotels stand in front of service doors so the guests don't have to see who brings the food. Through the hinge gap she can see a slice of the room: the upholstered chair, the navy jacket already off and hung on its back, his shoulder in shirtsleeves and a knotted tie, the dressing table. Across the room the main door is shut on its closer, and its privacy latch is thrown. He threw it himself when he came in, as part of the minute. Both doors are locked, and he locked one of them.
6. The egress arithmetic, done the way she'd do a differential. There are two ways out. The main door goes through the detail. The service door goes back into the passage and the stair, and the stair landing is unwatched only while the cordon is out at the green-room door. When the minute ends the cordon relaxes and the stair post steps back to the landing (ch21's choreography), which is at the end of the forty seconds. No way out doesn't go through the detail or back the way she came, and the way back closes when the minute does.
7. Smell, which arrives once she has stopped moving. The carpet had been shampooed that afternoon. Name it exactly (style 05: "the hotel's carpet cleaner in 22"): the synthetic floral of the solution over the wet-dog note of nylon pile that hasn't quite dried. Under it, in this room, his cedar aftershave and hot lamp dust. Keep the smell to one sentence and don't let it run on. In 17 smell was a paragraph, and the shortfall should be noticeable.
8. Reyes in one line: somewhere in this building a woman has a list with my name on it, and she's the kind of person who reads labels.
9. **[TURN]** She looks down at her right glove. Caught on the index fingertip is a tongue of the label's face film, the size of a fingernail, with the VOID letters standing out pale where the adhesive let go. The rest of it is on the corridor side of the door, split and lifting. She peels the scrap off the glove and puts it in her right outer pocket, beside the tape roll, which is evidence control done out of reflex. The sticker was torn. Someone would see it. My clock had become a shorter clock.

## Scene 2 — Recognition

1. She steps out from behind the screen. Four paces of shampooed carpet take her to the near side of the dressing table, across it from his chair. She is careful in one specific way: she doesn't come round to his side. She puts the table between them, which leaves the photograph between them too.
2. Describe the table. It's a banquet table with a skirt, standing out from the wall and dressed as a dressing table: a lamp, a bottle of water with the cap still sealed, a printed copy of tonight's remarks clipped at the corner, and the photograph of Mark in dress blues, in a plain black frame on a cardboard easel, turned to face his chair. She sees the back of it: felt backing and the easel leg. She can't see Mark. He can. Note for the drafter: there is no mirror on this table and none anywhere in the room. Mirrors are reserved for 15, 19, 20 and the reader's 27.
3. He is sitting forward in the upholstered chair, in shirtsleeves, the jacket on the chair back behind him, with his forearms on his knees and his hands loosely together, looking at his brother. It's what the footage couldn't show (ch19): what he does inside the minute. He looks at a photograph, and that's all. She reports it exactly and without commentary.
4. He looks up from the photograph.
5. She reads his face the way she reads a face on intake, since it's the only way she has ever been able to look at one. There is a startle: a brief brow lift, the frontalis going up. His pupils don't change. There is no flinch at the eyes, no blanching, and his hands don't move apart. The startle is the ordinary kind, the one a man gives when a door opens that he thought was shut. She has been waiting two years for a second expression to follow it, and it doesn't come.
6. He sees me. He does not recognise me. Say it in those words, or as close as past tense allows, with nothing before it and nothing after.
7. Why he isn't frightened, in one cold line. A woman in a lanyard has appeared in a room he locked, and his face files her the way rooms like this have always filed women with lanyards: staff, here for something, about to tell him the time. His eyes go to the lanyard and the badge on it (VOLUNTEER / PRESS ASSISTANT, RUTH CALDER, no photograph). They don't go to her hands. (This is a filing glance. In ch23 S1 his eyes go to the badge again as part of a search. Don't pre-empt that.)
8. Make the missing grade visible once. Two years of rehearsal would have given her an Evaluation for this face by now, and she reaches for one, and nothing comes to hand. One sentence. Don't comment on it.
9. **[TURN]** He smiles. It's the smile from the debate: the whole face he turned on the other man in ch01, listening, just after the cufflink. It's a full smile: zygomaticus major drawing the corners of the mouth up and back, orbicularis oculi engaged and creasing the skin at the outer canthi. It's a real smile, the kind you can't do on purpose. It is for me, and it means nothing. I had been hungry for this for two years, and I found there was nothing in my mouth. The mouth is checked here and found dry: no watering, no heat, and the penny taste not there (one clause). Don't extend the check into the pocket-check gesture, which belongs to ch23.

- **Sensory lead:** sight, against her rule, because this is the face she came for and she looks at it first. Name that inversion in one clause, for example: "I looked first. I never look first." Then sound: the ballroom through the wall has gone up a notch, and a man on a microphone is telling the crowd it won't be long now. Then taste, at the Turn: none.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations (one at most):**
  - "I had imagined this face for two years, in every state but the one it was in, which was pleased to see me."
  - "He smiled at me the way you smile at someone who has brought the right thing."
- **The body:** her mouth, dry. Her hands at her sides, gloved; the left wants the blazer's left inside breast pocket, where the third page lies with the syringe case, the marker and the pin. She doesn't let it go there yet. Her pulse goes unreported: she doesn't take it, and the reader should notice she always took it before (17, 19, and after screening in 21). Cold steady. The scar is quiet.
- **Motifs:** the Marine's photograph, face-up, its back to her and its face to him; hands (his loosely together, hers kept still); the grade (missing); the meal after, inverted (the meal she came for isn't served).

## Scene 3 — The Pause

1. A long moment. She stands across the table from him and waits. Every rehearsal she ever ran started here, with him knowing her, and she keeps standing in the place where the rehearsals started as if the next line will arrive if she holds still long enough.
2. The room fills the silence for her: the ballroom's music, the earpiece radio through the main door, the lamp's faint tick as it heats, his breathing. She has the drafter's licence to notice a detail of his body and not use it. A fleck of make-up powder sits in the crease beside his nose, and she notes it the way she'd note it on a table and then lets it go.
3. Reyes and the clock together, in one short line: the scrap of label in her pocket, the split one on the door, the corridor about to be watched. The count is at about thirteen.
4. He stands. It's courtesy, his reflex, because a woman is standing. In standing he does the cuff gesture from ch01: right thumb and forefinger give the left cufflink (plain silver) a quarter-turn, his eyes down for two seconds. With the jacket off, the cuff is bare shirt and the gesture is fully visible. Name it exactly. Don't say what it is to her, and don't say whether she has seen it in person before (the ledger fixes only that she has seen it on footage). Keep the narration off it.
5. He says, helpfully: "I think there may have been a mix-up. Are you with the network?" This is Holt's best syntax: warm, unhurried, offering her a way out of her own embarrassment. It is the kindness in small rooms (cast). Use the line exactly as the outline gives it. Iris can see where "network" came from: the badge says PRESS ASSISTANT.
6. The joke she doesn't make. Narration only, one or two lines: there's a joke in "the network", about eleven names on a list, mine among them, and a woman who left a message saying *I think we may have... known the same person*. She doesn't make it. Quote the voicemail line once, plainly, with its one stop before "known". It is the knowledge she has carried since 16–18, standing in the room, and it is the only time the chapter goes near the dead woman. The narration may say Nora signed (ledger: Iris's belief is correct); it must not put her among the eleven, and must not hint at a struck name (see Conflicts 3).
7. Her left hand goes to the blazer and finds the third page through the cloth (carried from ch20's Snapper). The paper is folded in thirds and warm from her body. She doesn't take it out, and she doesn't quote or paraphrase it (style 08). She only says what it assumes: that the man reading it knows who wrote it.
8. **[TURN]** I had no line for this. The syllabus began with his recognition. I am telling you that, and I can hear how small my voice has got. Render the smallness in the sentences, which shorten here to their shortest in the book so far. Don't explain it. No word for her state.
9. Run straight into the Snapper with no scene break. He is still standing and still waiting, politely, for an answer about the network.

- **Sensory lead:** sound. The silence she can't fill and the room filling it, with his voice landing in it at full warmth: a baritone, unamplified, at small-room volume. Texture second: the folded page through the blazer lining under her fingertips.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - The declined joke in beat 6 is the scene's only humour, and it lands as an absence.
  - Reserve (use only if beat 6 is cut): "He asked if I was with the network. He meant television."
- **The body:** her left hand on the pocket; her voice, which is the narration's voice, getting smaller; her feet planted on the damp-smelling carpet; cold in the fingers. His body: standing in shirtsleeves, tie knotted, the cufflink, the powder fleck, his hands open, palms half-turned to her in the politician's "can I help" posture.
- **Motifs:** hands (the cufflink; her hand on the page); the syllabus page (unread, unreproduced, in the pocket); voicemail (the line quoted once); the Marine's photograph (Snapper); the grade (still missing).
