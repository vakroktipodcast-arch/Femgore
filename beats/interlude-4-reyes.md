# Beat sheet: interlude-4-reyes

- **Unit:** Interlude 4, Reyes (after Chapter 20, before Chapter 21). Manuscript file `manuscript/interlude-4-reyes.md` (not yet drafted). Heading in manuscript: `# Interlude — Reyes`.
- **POV:** Special Agent Eleanor Reyes, close third, past tense. She is never in Iris's head, and Iris is never in hers.
- **Story time:** Sat 31 Oct, about 5:40 p.m., the field office. This steps back from the end of Ch 20 (Mon 2 Nov). Nothing on the page may assume what Iris has done since Saturday.
- **Target words:** 600–900 (aim ~850). One scene, one Turn. Suggested weight: the profile ~250, the envelope ~250, the list and the schedule ~350.
- **Act pressure:** Act III. "Reyes, closing on paper; the knowledge from 18 in every room." This is the only place in the book where the reader stands inside the pressure rather than under it. Reyes is not a threat in a room. She is a threat on a desk: a matter number, a protective order, a calendar. The menace is that she is patient and correct, and the dread is that the procedure she follows is the one thing slowing her down.
- **Iris's appetite at entry (reader's time, end of Ch 20, Mon 2 Nov):** Act III, appetite starved. She plans Holt without pleasure and is barely eating; her last food was black coffee and a heel of bread on Sun 1 Nov morning. In the hall mirror she looks like someone going to work. The letter to Hannah is squared on the desk, the drawer locked. The third page is in her navy blazer (Mon 2 Nov, per ledger); the fourth page lies under where it goes in the drawer. In Reyes's own story time (Sat 31 Oct) the third page is still in the drawer, and nothing about this is mentioned on the page.
- **Iris's appetite at exit:** Unchanged. Iris is off the page and the interlude cannot touch her appetite. What changes is the reader, who now knows there is a date after Election Eve with her name on it, and carries that date into Ch 21–26. It turns her "after" (Ch 26's two-hour walk, Ch 27's plate) into a countdown she does not know about. Nothing of Iris's appetite leaks into Reyes.
- **Register (matches `anchors/interlude-2-cam.md`; Reyes's own colouring):** plainer and cooler than Iris. Layman's words for bodies: Diane's mouth is "full of paper", and Brandt was "opened" and "closed again". No Latin, no direct address, no appetite, no jokes in Iris's style. As in the anchor, one italicised flicker of thought is allowed, and the ending can be a held plain sentence. Reyes's dryness is a case agent's: procedural and tired, and aimed at institutions rather than bodies, the way Cam's is aimed at the room ("In that order"). She thinks in complete sentences, the way her reports are written. That is the contrast with Cam's fragments. In her head the killer is "she" from the moment the profile settles. No nickname, no "Butcher", no press name. Ambient objects do the pacing (the anchor's urn, the hum, the cold coffee).

## Scene 1: The list, Saturday

1. **Saturday 31 October, late afternoon, the field office.** Reyes is at her borrowed desk on a floor that is almost empty for the weekend. She has two photocopied pages side by side under the desk lamp. They are not the originals, which are in evidence, but copies she has read until the toner rubbed off at the folds. *Confused process for protection. Mistook clarity for cruelty.* Two job titles circled in the same red, with the same pressure, each closed in a single stroke.
2. **The pattern, in her complete sentences.** Two bodies, weeks apart. Each has a page, a circled role, a one-line Evaluation and black tape. In Diane Pell's mouth were shreds of the campaign's own agreements, each scrap of footer carrying the same matter number under a thin rule. Brandt was opened on the edit desk in his suite and closed again, roughly; the Chief signed that file, with Lin assisting. The word "practised" from the Pell file is the word she has underlined twice in her own copy. Whoever did this knew the inside of a body the way Reyes knows a form.
3. **The profile, and where each term came from.** Medically trained: the two autopsy files. A signatory: the agreements in the mouth (her inference: a signatory's copy is the likeliest source, and she has not ruled out counsel's). Female: campaign counsel's own brief, which argued for the signatories' privacy as "former female staff and volunteers". Counsel told her who they were protecting in the course of refusing to say. She has had all three terms for a week. What she has not had is names.
4. **The fight ends.** The order came down on Friday. Counsel complied at 5:40 p.m. Saturday, the last hour that still counts as "promptly", two days before Election Eve on Halloween weekend, and she understands the timing exactly. The courier envelope is on the firm's letterhead and sealed with red tamper tape. She slits the tape with a letter opener, and it gives with a small dry tearing sound.
5. **Inside: one page.** Weeks of motions for one sheet of paper. Matter LC-0311 in the header; eleven lines, each with a surname, an initial, the date of signing and a control number. All the dates fall in the same eight months, three years ago. The page is silent on anything else: no count of how many signed, no note of a name removed. Clipped behind it is the protective order: the signatories are to be approached "in a fixed and non-prejudicial sequence", which counsel has helpfully specified as alphabetical. Any departure goes back to the judge. She reads that paragraph twice.
6. **Paper against paper.** She lays the lab's reconstructions of the fragments from Pell's mouth against the header. They are wet-damaged footers, pieced back together. Three of them match matter LC-0311. The killer put the right paper in the right mouth. Reyes feels something that in another job would be pleasure and in hers is only a match. (The footers carry the series number only. The individual control numbers are on the list, not on the paper in the mouth.)
7. **The two holes, from counsel's covering note.** The list itself carries nothing but the four fields. Counsel's covering note, a short paragraph on the firm's letterhead, says what the list does not: the ninth signatory has only a forwarding address in another country; the eleventh has a disconnected phone number and an address years out of date. She notes both against counsel's name; they will know where their own people went. She puts in for background checks on all eleven, and the system tells her the queue is three to four days, because it is election week and every agent in the building is on a rope line.
8. **The tenth line.** *VALE, I.* It gets what every line gets: surname, initial, date, control number, read once and in order. Reyes makes no mark against it and shows no recognition of the name (author ruling). She keeps the sequence. Nine names come first, and the sequence is the sequence.
9. **The schedule.** Sunday, Monday morning before she reports to the venue for the Election Eve detail, and Tuesday between the polls opening and the returns: eight interviews, with the woman abroad noted and skipped. Skipping her is not a departure, because no approach is made; she logs it anyway. She fills the slots in her own hand on her paper desk calendar, names in block capitals, because the paper is what will be read later.
10. **[TURN]** Vale is tenth. Reyes books her for the day after the election, Wednesday at ten, and writes the name in the square in block capitals, the same as all the others.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first, because the floor is empty. The ventilation, a copier cycling somewhere on standby, the dry tearing of the tamper tape, the scratch of pen in a paper calendar's square. Texture: toner worn smooth at the folds of the copied Evaluations, and the heavier, cleaner stock of the firm's single page. Temperature: the building's weekend heating set low, dusk coming at the window, her hands cold enough that she holds the coffee for the heat and not to drink it. Taste: the coffee, long stewed, and one fun-size chocolate bar from the Halloween bowl at the lobby security desk, eaten without noticing. The reader notices, against Iris's fasting in Ch 20. Do not point that out. Smell: barely. Toner and the warm dust of the lamp. Reyes does not smell things the way Iris does.
- **Dry observations (Reyes's register, not Iris's; no jokes in her style):**
  - On the brief: counsel argued for the women's privacy so well that she described them for her.
  - On the timing: "promptly", in a law firm's mouth, means the last hour before it becomes contempt.
  - (Optional) On the clocks: they go back tonight. The government is giving her an hour, and she already knows she will spend it on the schedule.
  - (Optional) On her posting: she asked for anything but protection, and protection is where they put people they want somewhere they can be seen. Keep it to one clause if used (interlude-5 uses it too).
- **The body:** Hands, plain and cold: short unpainted nails, the callus in the web of the right thumb and forefinger, the biro mark on the right middle finger. Her pen, a letter opener, a coffee held for warmth. One chocolate bar from a bowl meant for children, eaten standing at the window while the copier cycles. She is tired in the shoulders and does not rest them. Sidearm on the right hip, not mentioned unless it catches on the chair. She studies the photocopied red circles as evidence of someone's hand: steady, a single stroke, no hesitation. That seeds "something about the hands" in interlude-5 without naming it. No ring on the page (see New facts).
- **Motifs:**
  - **Tape:** the red tamper tape on the courier envelope, which she cuts. It is the system's seal, broken by procedure, and it rhymes with the sticker seal on the green room's service door (Ch 20–21). The black tape appears only as one word in her account of the pattern.
  - **The grade:** she reads the two Evaluations the way she would read an evaluation of herself, as fair, specific and from someone who has been graded. One line. She does not give anyone a mark. (Cam in the anchor turns "Fair mark" face-down; Reyes does the same in kind, not in words.)
  - **Paperwork thread (strategy 09):** the matter number against the list's header, the protective order and the calendar square. The system's own filing finds Iris, and the same filing holds her at number ten.
  - **Hands:** see the body, above.
  - **Voicemail:** by absence. Reyes calls no one on the page.
  - **No mirrors, no vinyl, no meal after.** Brandt's records are not mentioned.
