# Beat sheet: interlude-4-reyes

- **Unit:** Interlude 4, Reyes (after Chapter 20, before Chapter 21). Manuscript file `manuscript/interlude-4-reyes.md`. Heading in manuscript: `# Interlude — Reyes`.
- **POV:** Special Agent Eleanor Reyes, close third, past tense. She is never in Iris's head, and Iris is never in hers.
- **Target words:** 600–900 (aim ~850). One scene, one Turn. Suggested weight: the profile ~250, the envelope ~250, the list and the schedule ~350.
- **Act pressure:** Act III. "Reyes, closing on paper; the knowledge from 18 in every room." This is the only place in the book where the reader stands inside the pressure rather than under it. Reyes is not a threat in a room. She is a threat on a desk: control numbers, a protective order, a calendar. The menace is that she is patient and correct, and the dread is that the procedure she follows is the one thing slowing her down.
- **Iris's appetite at entry:** Act III, appetite starved. In Ch 20 she plans Holt without pleasure, eats nothing before ("You don't eat before", hollowed), and in the mirror she looks like someone going to work. The letter to Hannah is squared to the desk. The third page is in her jacket.
- **Iris's appetite at exit:** Unchanged. Iris is off the page and the interlude cannot touch her appetite. What changes is the reader, who now knows there is a date after Election Eve with her name on it, and carries that date into Ch 21–26. It turns her "after" (Ch 26's two-hour walk, Ch 27's plate) into a countdown she does not know about. Nothing of Iris's appetite leaks into Reyes.
- **Register reminder (interlude rules; matches interlude-2-cam and interlude-3-cam):** plainer and cooler than Iris. Layman's words for bodies: Diane's mouth is "full of paper", and Brandt was "opened" and "closed again". No Latin, no direct address, no appetite, no jokes in Iris's style. Reyes's dryness is a case agent's: procedural and tired, and aimed at institutions rather than bodies. She thinks in complete sentences, the way her reports are written. That is the contrast with Cam's fragments. In her head the killer is "she" from the moment the profile settles. There is no nickname, no "Butcher", and no press name.

## Conflicts

None. The outline agrees with the ledger: the list arrives 48 hours before Election Eve, it has eleven names, the interviews are alphabetical, and Vale is tenth. The ledger's Ch 27 entry ("Iris's interview is Thursday, three days away") agrees with booking her for the day after the election, because the ch27 outline says the interview was "postponed by Election Eve and rescheduled for Thursday". The ledger's Ch 26 entry (Reyes sees Iris's face, not her name) is about the corridor. It does not stop Reyes reading the name "Vale" here, and these beats keep the two apart. Reyes never sees a photograph of Iris in this interlude (ch10 beats: the ME's office releases no photographs of its pathologists).

Constraints the beats keep to:
- **Reyes's competence has to survive the Turn.** By Ch 10 Iris's name has been in the wire story as "the senior pathologist who reviewed the autopsy", so Reyes will know the name. A medically trained woman on the list is the obvious fit for the profile. If Reyes simply fails to notice, she is a fool and the Turn goes soft. The beats therefore have her notice, and the *procedure* keeps Vale in tenth place. Counsel won the protective order as the price of losing the subpoena. It requires the signatories to be approached in a fixed, non-prejudicial sequence, alphabetical, and any deviation has to go back to the judge. The campaign fought for that order to protect itself, and it now protects Iris. Do not point out the irony. Let the reader find it.
- **She does not decide it is Vale.** She writes one line in the margin and does not let herself have a favourite. The employment and licensing checks on all eleven are in a queue that election week has filled. The reader has the suspicion. Reyes has a fact she has filed.
- **The interlude does not contact Iris.** No phone call and no voicemail. The motif fixes three unreturned voicemails, and a fourth would break it. How and when Iris learns of the interview stays open until Ch 27, where she hears it from Mara.
- **The "two signatories she can't find" in interlude-5 are seeded here.** On the list, one name has a forwarding address abroad and one has no current address. Ch 27 says the two left uninterviewed a week later are "a woman who has left the country, and Dr. Vale", so the name with no address is found and interviewed between Election Eve and Ch 27. The beats make that name the eleventh, after Vale, so that it does not touch the count of nine ahead of her.
- **No Holt.** Reyes does not think about the man the list is about. The list never says why the agreements were signed, and she does not let herself write it down yet.
- **No interval in weeks on the page** unless the calendar is settled. The beat sheets disagree on Diane's date: ch05/ch06 have 13 September and ch10 has 30 August. Write "weeks apart".

## Scene 1: The list, Saturday

1. **Saturday 31 October, late afternoon, the field office.** Reyes is at a borrowed desk on a floor that is almost empty for the weekend. She has two photocopied pages side by side under the desk lamp. They are not the originals, which are in evidence, but the copies she has read until the toner rubbed off at the folds. *Confused process for protection. Mistook clarity for cruelty.* Two job titles circled in the same red, with the same pressure, each closed in a single stroke.
2. **The pattern, in her complete sentences.** Two bodies, weeks apart. Each has a page, a circled role, a one-line Evaluation and black tape. In Diane Pell's mouth were shreds of agreements carrying the campaign's legal control numbers. Brandt was opened on his own desk and closed again, roughly. The pathologist's word "practised" from the Pell file is the word she has underlined twice in her own copy. Whoever did this knew the inside of a body the way Reyes knows a form.
3. **The profile, and where each term came from.** Medically trained: the two autopsy files. A signatory: the control numbers. Female: campaign counsel's own brief, which argued for the signatories' privacy as "former female staff and volunteers". Counsel told her who they were protecting in the course of refusing to say. She has had all three terms for a week. What she has not had is names.
4. **The fight ends.** The order came down on Friday. Counsel complied at 5:40 p.m. Saturday, the last hour that still counts as "promptly", two days before Election Eve on a holiday weekend, and she understands the timing exactly. The courier envelope is on the firm's letterhead and sealed with red tamper tape. She slits the tape with a letter opener, and it gives with a small dry tearing sound.
5. **Inside: one page.** Weeks of motions for one sheet of paper. There are eleven lines, each with a surname, an initial, the date of signing and a control number. All the dates fall in the same eight months, three years ago. Clipped behind it is the protective order: the signatories are to be approached "in a fixed and non-prejudicial sequence", which counsel has helpfully specified as alphabetical. Any departure goes back to the judge. She reads that paragraph twice.
6. **Paper against paper.** She lays the fragment numbers recovered from Pell's mouth against the control numbers on the list. They are half-numbers, wet-damaged and reconstructed by the lab. Three of them fall inside the series. The killer put the right paper in the right mouth. Reyes feels something that in another job would be pleasure and in hers is only a match.
7. **The list's two holes.** One line has a forwarding address in another country. The eleventh has no current address at all. She marks both for counsel; they will know where their own people went. She puts in for employment and licensing checks on all eleven, and the system tells her the queue is three to four days, because it is election week and every agent in the building is on a rope line.
8. **The tenth line.** *VALE, I.* She knows the name the way she knows a signature block: the senior reviewing pathologist on the Pell autopsy, and the one-line statement in the wire story. She writes one thing in the margin, the word *reviewer* and a question mark, and stops her pen there. A name that fits is not evidence. It is a temptation, and counsel's order exists to punish temptation. Nine women come first, and the sequence is the sequence.
9. **The schedule.** Sunday, Monday morning before she reports to the venue for the Election Eve detail, and Tuesday between the polls opening and the returns: eight interviews, with the woman abroad noted and skipped. She fills the slots in her own hand on a paper calendar, because the paper is what will be read later.
10. **[TURN]** Vale is tenth. Reyes books her for the day after the election, Wednesday at ten, and writes the name in the square in block capitals, the same as all the others.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first, because the floor is empty. The ventilation, a copier cycling somewhere on standby, the dry tearing of the tamper tape, the scratch of pen in a paper calendar's square. Texture: toner worn smooth at the folds of the copied Evaluations, and the heavier, cleaner stock of the firm's single page. Temperature: the building's weekend heating set low, early dark at the window, her hands cold enough that she holds the coffee for the heat and not to drink it. Taste: the coffee, long stewed, and one fun-size chocolate bar from the Halloween bowl at the lobby security desk, eaten without noticing. The reader notices, against Iris's fasting in Ch 20. Do not point that out. Smell: barely. Toner and the warm dust of the lamp. Reyes does not smell things the way Iris does.
- **Dry observations (Reyes's register, not Iris's; no jokes in her style):**
  - On the brief: counsel argued for the women's privacy so well that he described them for her.
  - On the timing: "promptly", in a law firm's mouth, means the last hour before it becomes contempt.
  - (Optional) On the clocks: they go back tonight. The government is giving her an hour, and she already knows she will spend it on the schedule.
- **The body:** Hands, plain and cold. Her pen, a letter opener, a coffee held for warmth. One chocolate bar from a bowl meant for children, eaten standing at the window while the copier cycles. She is tired in the shoulders and does not rest them. She studies the photocopied red circles as evidence of someone's hand: steady, a single stroke, no hesitation. That seeds "something about the hands" in interlude-5 without naming it.
- **Motifs:**
  - **Tape:** the red tamper tape on the courier envelope, which she cuts. It is the system's seal, broken by procedure, and it rhymes with the sticker on the service door in Ch 20. The black tape appears only as one word in her account of the pattern.
  - **The grade:** she reads the two Evaluations the way she would read an evaluation of herself, as fair, specific and from someone who has been graded. One line. She does not give anyone a mark.
  - **Paperwork thread (strategy 09):** control numbers against the list, the protective order and the calendar square. The system's own filing finds Iris, and the same filing holds her at number ten.
  - **Hands:** see the body, above.
  - **Voicemail:** by absence. Reyes calls no one on the page.
  - **No mirrors, no vinyl, no meal after.** Brandt's records are not mentioned.
