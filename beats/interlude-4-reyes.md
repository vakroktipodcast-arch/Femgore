# Beat sheet: interlude-4-reyes

- **Unit:** Interlude 4, Reyes (after Chapter 20, before Chapter 21). Manuscript file `manuscript/interlude-4-reyes.md`. Heading in manuscript: `# Interlude — Reyes`.
- **POV:** Special Agent Eleanor Reyes, close third, past tense. She is never in Iris's head, and Iris is never in hers.
- **Target words:** 600–900 (aim ~850). One scene, one Turn. Suggested weight: the profile ~250, the envelope ~250, the list and the schedule ~350.
- **Act pressure:** Act III. "Reyes, closing on paper; the knowledge from 18 in every room." This is the only place in the book where the reader stands inside the pressure rather than under it. Reyes is not a threat in a room. She is a threat on a desk: a matter number, a protective order, a calendar. The menace is that she is patient and correct, and the dread is that the procedure she follows is the one thing slowing her down.
- **Iris's appetite at entry:** Act III, appetite starved. In Ch 20 she plans Holt without pleasure and is barely eating; her last food will be black coffee and a heel of bread on Sunday morning. In the hall mirror she looks like someone going to work. The letter to Hannah is squared on the desk. The third page is still in the drawer (it goes into the navy blazer on Mon 2 Nov).
- **Iris's appetite at exit:** Unchanged. Iris is off the page and the interlude cannot touch her appetite. What changes is the reader, who now knows there is a date after Election Eve with her name on it, and carries that date into Ch 21–26. It turns her "after" (Ch 26's two-hour walk, Ch 27's plate) into a countdown she does not know about. Nothing of Iris's appetite leaks into Reyes.
- **Register reminder (interlude rules; matches interlude-2-cam and interlude-3-cam):** plainer and cooler than Iris. Layman's words for bodies: Diane's mouth is "full of paper", and Brandt was "opened" and "closed again". No Latin, no direct address, no appetite, no jokes in Iris's style. Reyes's dryness is a case agent's: procedural and tired, and aimed at institutions rather than bodies. She thinks in complete sentences, the way her reports are written. That is the contrast with Cam's fragments. In her head the killer is "she" from the moment the profile settles. There is no nickname, no "Butcher", and no press name.

Resolved by the merged ledger:

- Nora Wade is not on the list and never signed an NDA. The eleven are all living; the eleventh merely lacks a current address. Ch 27's count of nine interviewed stands.
- The NDA fragments carry only the series matter number LC-0311; individual control numbers exist only in counsel's register and on the list.
- Brandt's autopsy was performed by the Chief, Mara assisting.

Constraints the beats keep to:
- **Reyes's competence has to survive the Turn.** Since 29 Sep Iris's name has been public as the senior pathologist who reviewed the autopsy, so Reyes will know the name. A medically trained woman on the list is the obvious fit for the profile. If Reyes simply fails to notice, she is a fool and the Turn goes soft. The beats therefore have her notice, and the *procedure* keeps Vale in tenth place. Counsel won the protective order as the price of losing the subpoena. It requires the signatories to be approached in a fixed, non-prejudicial sequence, alphabetical, and any deviation has to go back to the judge. The campaign fought for that order to protect itself, and it now protects Iris. Do not point out the irony. Let the reader find it.
- **She does not decide it is Vale.** She writes one line in the margin and does not let herself have a favourite. The background checks on all eleven are in a queue that election week has filled (three to four days). The reader has the suspicion. Reyes has a fact she has filed.
- **The interlude does not contact Iris.** No phone call and no voicemail. The motif fixes three unreturned voicemails, and a fourth would break it. Iris hears of the Wednesday booking only from Mara on 9 Nov.
- **The "two signatories she can't find" in interlude-5 are seeded here.** On the list, one name has a forwarding address abroad and one has no current address. Ch 27 says the two left uninterviewed a week later are "a woman who has left the country, and Dr. Vale", so the name with no address is found and interviewed between Election Eve and Ch 27. The beats make that name the eleventh, after Vale, so that it does not touch the count of eight ahead of her.
- **No Holt.** Reyes does not think about the man the list is about. The list never says why the agreements were signed, and she does not let herself write it down yet.

## Scene 1: The list, Saturday

1. **Saturday 31 October, late afternoon, the field office.** Reyes is at her borrowed desk on a floor that is almost empty for the weekend. She has two photocopied pages side by side under the desk lamp. They are not the originals, which are in evidence, but the copies she has read until the toner rubbed off at the folds. *Confused process for protection. Mistook clarity for cruelty.* Two job titles circled in the same red, with the same pressure, each closed in a single stroke.
2. **The pattern, in her complete sentences.** Two bodies, weeks apart. Each has a page, a circled role, a one-line Evaluation and black tape. In Diane Pell's mouth were shreds of the campaign's own agreements, each scrap of footer carrying the same matter number under a thin rule. Brandt was opened on his own desk and closed again, roughly; the Chief signed that file, with Lin assisting. The word "practised" from the Pell file is the word she has underlined twice in her own copy. Whoever did this knew the inside of a body the way Reyes knows a form.
3. **The profile, and where each term came from.** Medically trained: the two autopsy files. A signatory: the agreements in the mouth, which only a signatory's copy would supply. Female: campaign counsel's own brief, which argued for the signatories' privacy as "former female staff and volunteers". Counsel told her who they were protecting in the course of refusing to say. She has had all three terms for a week. What she has not had is names.
4. **The fight ends.** The order came down on Friday. Counsel complied at 5:40 p.m. Saturday, the last hour that still counts as "promptly", two days before Election Eve on Halloween weekend, and she understands the timing exactly. The courier envelope is on the firm's letterhead and sealed with red tamper tape. She slits the tape with a letter opener, and it gives with a small dry tearing sound.
5. **Inside: one page.** Weeks of motions for one sheet of paper. Matter LC-0311 in the header; eleven lines, each with a surname, an initial, the date of signing and a control number. All the dates fall in the same eight months, three years ago. Clipped behind it is the protective order: the signatories are to be approached "in a fixed and non-prejudicial sequence", which counsel has helpfully specified as alphabetical. Any departure goes back to the judge. She reads that paragraph twice.
6. **Paper against paper.** She lays the lab's reconstructions of the fragments from Pell's mouth against the header. They are wet-damaged footers, pieced back together. Three of them match matter LC-0311. The killer put the right paper in the right mouth. Reyes feels something that in another job would be pleasure and in hers is only a match.
7. **The list's two holes.** One line has a forwarding address in another country. The eleventh has no current address at all. She marks both for counsel; they will know where their own people went. She puts in for background checks on all eleven, and the system tells her the queue is three to four days, because it is election week and every agent in the building is on a rope line.
8. **The tenth line.** *VALE, I.* She knows the name the way she knows a signature block: the senior reviewing pathologist on the Pell autopsy, the name above the office's one-line "does not comment on active investigations". She writes one thing in the margin, the word *reviewer* and a question mark, and stops her pen there. A name that fits is not evidence. It is a temptation, and counsel's order exists to punish temptation. Nine women come first, and the sequence is the sequence.
9. **The schedule.** Sunday, Monday morning before she reports to the venue for the Election Eve detail, and Tuesday between the polls opening and the returns: eight interviews, with the woman abroad noted and skipped. She fills the slots in her own hand on her paper desk calendar, names in block capitals, because the paper is what will be read later.
10. **[TURN]** Vale is tenth. Reyes books her for the day after the election, Wednesday at ten, and writes the name in the square in block capitals, the same as all the others.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first, because the floor is empty. The ventilation, a copier cycling somewhere on standby, the dry tearing of the tamper tape, the scratch of pen in a paper calendar's square. Texture: toner worn smooth at the folds of the copied Evaluations, and the heavier, cleaner stock of the firm's single page. Temperature: the building's weekend heating set low, early dark at the window, her hands cold enough that she holds the coffee for the heat and not to drink it. Taste: the coffee, long stewed, and one fun-size chocolate bar from the Halloween bowl at the lobby security desk, eaten without noticing. The reader notices, against Iris's fasting in Ch 20. Do not point that out. Smell: barely. Toner and the warm dust of the lamp. Reyes does not smell things the way Iris does.
- **Dry observations (Reyes's register, not Iris's; no jokes in her style):**
  - On the brief: counsel argued for the women's privacy so well that she described them for her.
  - On the timing: "promptly", in a law firm's mouth, means the last hour before it becomes contempt.
  - (Optional) On the clocks: they go back tonight. The government is giving her an hour, and she already knows she will spend it on the schedule.
- **The body:** Hands, plain and cold: short unpainted nails, the biro mark on the right middle finger. Her pen, a letter opener, a coffee held for warmth. One chocolate bar from a bowl meant for children, eaten standing at the window while the copier cycles. She is tired in the shoulders and does not rest them. She studies the photocopied red circles as evidence of someone's hand: steady, a single stroke, no hesitation. That seeds "something about the hands" in interlude-5 without naming it.
- **Motifs:**
  - **Tape:** the red tamper tape on the courier envelope, which she cuts. It is the system's seal, broken by procedure, and it rhymes with the sticker seal on the green room's service door (Ch 21–22). The black tape appears only as one word in her account of the pattern.
  - **The grade:** she reads the two Evaluations the way she would read an evaluation of herself, as fair, specific and from someone who has been graded. One line. She does not give anyone a mark.
  - **Paperwork thread (strategy 09):** the matter number against the list's header, the protective order and the calendar square. The system's own filing finds Iris, and the same filing holds her at number ten.
  - **Hands:** see the body, above.
  - **Voicemail:** by absence. Reyes calls no one on the page.
  - **No mirrors, no vinyl, no meal after.** Brandt's records are not mentioned.
