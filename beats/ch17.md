## Scene 1 — The Method

1. Open in motion with her sleeves already at her elbows, carried over from the Ch 16 Snapper. Gloves from the box two at a time, left then right, each cuff snapped over the wrist. In the morgue the extraction fans swallow that snap; here there are none. The record ended a minute ago and the tone arm has gone home by itself, and the deaf room takes the sound of the nitrile and gives none of it back. His breathing sounds as if it is inside her own ear, and "had been invited".
2. Smell first: vetiver soap (the "good cellar" note, named here for the first time; Ch 16 left it unnamed), rye on his breath and the glass rim, warm dust off equipment like an ironing board left standing, and the first sharp apocrine sweat rising from his axillae into the charcoal shirt. On a decedent she would write it down: fear, recent onset. On him she writes nothing. She breathes it in the way you smell a glass before you may drink, and it is the first time a body has been afraid of her. Sight last: he lies on the long edit desk, wrists taped palm-up to its edge and zip-tied down through a grommet and round the cable-tray rail beneath, ankles taped and tied to the desk legs at the far end (the anchor's "done the same"), a band of industrial tape across the thighs. The one lamp is behind his head, the amber standby LEDs run along the wall at knee height like the eyes of animals at the edge of a road. A towel under each arm and one along each side; she opens his shirt collar to hem with a blade, like a letter she wants the envelope of.
3. "Should I watch?" / "Most of my patients don't get a vote." / "That isn't an answer." / "Watch, then." She examines him first ("you don't open a book at the index"), dictaphone voice: well-nourished adult male, about 183 cm, about 84 kg. "Eighty-two." "Clothed." He laughs, rectus abdominis tightening segment by segment, and she lays her palm below the costal margin to feel the last of it: he is warm, which she has known for weeks "the way you know a fact about a country. It is a different thing to put your hand on the country." Appendicectomy scar, about 6 cm, RLQ: "I was nine." "History is not usually available." The funniest thing she has said at work.
4. The rest of the external: nails short and filed square, cuticles pushed back; flattened distal pads and a fine right-hand callus (forty years of keyboard); chest hair sparse and going ash at the sternum; no tattoos, no injection marks; showered and shaved before she came, with a small sealed nick at the angle of the jaw, the first wound of the night and his own. She grades him in her head: preparation excellent, "he had dressed for me too". Then she tells the reader why the forearm goes first: his hands, and the one piano key she touched in his house (Ch 13), "the machinery" she has thought about ever since.
5. First incision: the volar surface of the left forearm, a hand's width above the wrist crease toward the elbow, 10 cm, through epidermis, dermis and superficial fascia. The skin opens "like a mouth that has decided to". He bleeds: the dead seep, like a cheap biro on its side; he bled "like a fountain pen, which is to say with intent". A bead stands along the whole line at once; it is thirty-seven degrees and she feels it through the glove. "Everything I touch arrives at four." The smell is iron, but the coin being struck, not the coin that has been in a mouth. She looks last: the colour of the inside of a cherry, and moving.
6. Small retractor. Palmaris longus lies over everything like a guitar string (fourteen per cent of people lack one; of course he has one, "a spare in a drawer"); she eases it and flexor carpi radialis aside and exposes the four tendons of flexor digitorum superficialis, pale, glossy, the inside of a shell. "Play something." / "I can't see the keys." / "You've never needed to." He plays a chord against the wood of the desk; under her forceps the tendons slide, four cables in four lift shafts, the actual cords of music. "That is beautiful," and it was. [The word's one use in the book; the anchor has her say she will not use it again: "You will have to take that one and keep it somewhere."] "Again." He plays it again; she could watch all night, "and that was the problem with the night". She picks up the larger blade.
7. The Y-incision, and why a Y: it keeps the neck clean, the family will want to view the neck, and nobody will, but she is a creature of habit. Right acromion to xiphoid, then left acromion to meet it, then down the midline curving left around the umbilicus to the pubis. He makes sounds, and she reports them exactly: not screams (a scream is a decision), below decision, glottal, involuntary, "a heavy door being tried from the other side". The room keeps every one; in another building they would be in a neighbour's phone. She can almost hear them settle on the carpet.
8. She reflects the upper flap with the blade nearly flat, like filleting a fish. Butter-yellow fat beaded with blood "like condensation, except the reverse"; pectoralis major in its fan, plum-coloured, and it moves when he breathes, which on her table it never does. She lifts the flap to feel its weight, a living weight, about a good rib-eye before salting, and his heat comes up through the nitrile "like something being handed to me across a table". Then her own findings: carotid pulse his 96, hers 110, "one of us was having a harder night than the other, and it was not, physiologically speaking, him". Hot face, a "frank, wet wanting", filed in the same column as his sweat. A drop of sweat runs from his hairline toward his eye and she wipes it with the back of her wrist, the one clean inch, and he closes his eyes as a man does when a woman touches his face. "Of everything I did that night, that was the most obscene. I would like that noted."
9. She knows what comes next, because she knows what bodies do: shock, pallor, a thready pulse, and the voice goes first, "like a house shutting its rooms for the winter". Fourteen hundred cases taught her that, every one after the fact. She planned the rest of the night around his silence, and looked forward to it, "the way you look forward to the point in a dinner when the other person stops talking and you can eat".
10. [TURN] He speaks, level, in one full sentence with two subordinate clauses: "You're very good at this... it's like watching someone who really knows her instrument..." She had not expected him to be able to. She slowed down so he could.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell first (vetiver, rye, warm electronics, fear-sweat, then the iron of fresh blood, which is not the morgue's smell). ## Scene 2 — The Conversation, Continued

1. He talks about the system and does not plead: "People think it's lying. It isn't... The job is making sure that nobody ever has to decide anything... Nobody decides. That's the product. I was very good at it, and I want you to know I knew what it was the whole time." / "I know." / "Of course you do. You wrote it down." Complete sentences, and the grammar is the unbearable part. She works at the speed of his sentences.
2. She opens the anterior rectus sheath along its length: glossy white aponeurosis "tough as the skin of a drum", rectus abdominis in segments, tendinous intersections like the scoring on a bar of chocolate ("every man who has ever paid a trainer has paid for those lines"). Down the linea alba, "the body's seam... where the garment gives most willingly", to the transversalis fascia and the soft fat. She stops there with the peritoneum shining like skin on hot milk, unbroken, on purpose: opening it ends the conversation inside ten minutes and she is not ready. "If you are keeping a list, and I think by now you are, put that near the top."
3. He talks about himself: the house, too quiet for two years, filled with records "the way people leave a radio on for a dog"; his wife, without unkindness and without her name (she notes it and would do the same). The Tabard Club. "It's Tuesday. I'm missing my set." / "They'll get the house pianist." / "The house pianist plays like he's being paid by the note." She laughs out loud, and in that room it sounds like someone else's, played back too close to the microphone. She drinks from her glass, in the dirty glove, which she should not do: good rye, dry and spiced like the crust of a dark loaf, the iron of the air going into it. Her mouth is watering and has been for some time; she reports it and will not explain it.
4. "You broke the Dolphy." / "*Out to Lunch.* It was a first pressing." / "I chose it for the sleeve. There's a clock on it with too many hands." He is pleased. He asks if she knows how Dolphy died. Berlin, 1964, thirty-six, collapsed on tour; the doctors looked at what he was and what he did and decided he had taken something, let him sleep it off; he was diabetic and nobody tested him: "He died of sugar." She has signed that death in other names, a test that costs less than a sandwich, not run. Neither says what it rhymes with. He chose the anecdote; she gives him full marks in her head "before I could stop myself, and then I let myself".
5. "I told him. After you left me the page. I went to Holt and I told him there was a problem from the old files." Holt: "I thought Diane closed those," then asked about the numbers in the suburbs. Next morning half of what Brandt built was closed to him and nobody told him. "You don't get fired. Your furniture gets moved, a little at a time." Brandt has watched Holt do it to other men for twenty years. She learns this from his mouth, not from the interlude; until now she has seen only the lockout. She thinks "I had closed Diane" and does not say it. He is the first man in twenty years to tell her something true without counsel present, with his abdominal wall open to the lamp, and she will not interrupt with a joke, even a good one.
6. The gift. "He takes one minute alone before every speech. His brother's photograph. Mark. He sits with it, or he stands with it, I've never been in the room, nobody has. The detail waits outside the door. Standing instruction. Nobody goes in, and nobody knocks." He stops for air; she waits for him. "Watch the old footage. Sixty seconds. He's never gone over." Then, as if handing her a key across a counter: "That's your door. It's the only one."
7. "Thank you." She means it, and is exact about how: the way you mean it when the man behind the counter goes into the back without being asked and comes out with the thing in his hands.
8. She touches the knife wound on her left flank (quiet all night; she touches it when thinking) with the inside of her forearm, the bare skin above the glove against the shirt above the scar, because her hands are not fit. That is how the reader knows she is thinking.
9. Her technique changes without her deciding it. At some point she put the blade down on the towel by his hip, and she is using her fingers: blunt dissection, easing planes apart along their own grain "the way you separate two pages that have been stuck together when you want to read both sides". Slower, less damage, "what you do when the tissue is something you mean to keep". Brandt, oral component: distinction.
10. [TURN] She has her way into the room. It came from the man she is taking apart, and she is taking him apart more gently now. She noticed, and she did not stop.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound: his voice with no reverb, close enough to be her own thought; her own laugh played back too close. Texture: the rectus sheath glossy and tough; fascia like wet pearl-onion skin; blunt dissection by feel. Taste: rye in a dirty glove against the iron in the air. Smell: the blood is now thick enough to be a room smell. Light: lamp behind his head, amber LEDs.
- **Candidate jokes:** "The house pianist plays like he's being paid by the note." / "He was the first man in twenty years to tell me something true without counsel present, and he was doing it with his abdominal wall open." / "Every man who has ever paid a trainer has paid for those lines." / "If you are keeping a list, put that near the top."
- **Body:** Hands gentler and ungrudging, fingers where there was a blade. Forearm on the wound. She drinks once, gloved, and the glass goes with her in S3. Mouth watering, reported without comment. Warm, pulse still up.
- **Motifs:** The Marine's photograph (described by Brandt, not seen). Vinyl (the Dolphy, the Tabard). The grade (full marks for the anecdote; "distinction" echoes the Lesson One insert's "with distinction"). Hands (blade down, fingers on). The meal after (watering mouth).

## Scene 3 — The Truth

1. He begins to go a little after one, and she reads it like a chart: colour first, forehead sheened like tallow, lips putty, pulse under two fingers up to 130 and thinned to a cord, breathing fast and shallow "as if he were trying to get it done before something closed". Seven towels used, counted by wrung weight against dry; a litre and a half, "give or take what the carpet kept for itself". Class II going on class III. His sentences are getting shorter and are still complete.
2. "There's one more thing. Then you can finish." She has moved to the chest (abdomen done, "the night was not") and set the rib shears to the left costal cartilages, second and third, a centimetre in from the bone, as every working day. "Three years ago a woman tried to get in touch with you. She left you a message. At your office. You never called her back." / "How do you know." / "I was watching her too."
3. He says her name. The shears go through the fourth cartilage at that moment, with the sound of a stick of celery, and the name goes under it. She heard that a name was said; the narration does not give it ("You will have it when I had it, and not before, which seems fair"). Brandt does not say the woman is on the list or connect her to the eleven, and Iris draws no inference here. Air goes into his left chest with a small, polite hiss; the left lung lets go of the wall; he takes half a breath and spends it.
4. [Beat of speech] "She died eight months later." He swallows. "I'm sorry. I thought you should know." No cause of death is given. These are his last complete sentences: subject, verb, object, even the contraction. Three hours earlier he told her there were eleven names and was kind about it; this is the same kindness, recognised "the way you recognise a voice in the next room without going in".
5. She finishes: right cartilages, second to seventh; the sternoclavicular joints opened with the blade "the way you joint a chicken at the thigh: find the place, and it gives". She lifts the sternal plate away onto the towel by his shoulder; the right lung goes down like the left and the talking stops for good, "because the bellows were open to the room". She opens the pericardium with one stroke and then takes off her right glove, the one thing that night not in the plan. Bare right hand, she takes his heart: hot, slick, much harder than you would think, a fist working against her palm; the beat breaks into fibrillation, "a bag of worms", and she holds it through all of it until it slows, stills, and his heat leaves it under her skin a degree at a time. Everything she has touched had already done that before it reached her. She had never felt it go. Three hundred and eighty grams, give or take. Her watch says ten to two. It is Wednesday. He missed his set altogether.
6. She closes him as they do at work when the family is not coming: quickly, no sutures, sternal plate set back, flaps folded home, "a kind of love that nobody is there to see". Two strips of black vinyl across his eyes, temple to temple. He watched the whole time and she let him; they would not close by themselves and it seemed only polite.
7. The page was on the table beside the photograph, folded in quarters, with a ring on it where he used it as a coaster (she takes that as a compliment); his own copy, from the pillow, read until the folds went soft. She opens it on his chest, smooths it with her bare hand, pins it through the skin above the sternum with the single steel pin. The marker squeaks "like a mouse" in that room. She circles THE ARCHITECT. The Evaluation, typed two years ago under her own redactions: MISTOOK CLARITY FOR CRUELTY. "It still read well. It had held."
8. Exit, "the part nobody admires", complete: instruments into the roll, blades into their sleeve, shears last; towels double-bagged; her glass with the print of her mouth; the right glove from the towel, then the left peeled off into the bag with its brother. The Diane print stays where he kept it, the Mingus sleeve beside it. Brandt's phone stays where it was, in its foil sleeve in his jacket on the chair back; she does not go near it. The record is on the platter, side one finished, the label turning under no needle. She does not turn it over: "I have never owned a record. I wouldn't have known how to hold it." (Ledger: true through Ch 27's eve; do not explain.) She opens the suite door with her elbow; the corridor's ventilation comes in "like weather". Stairwell door with her hip, car-park door with her back, as in a hospital when scrubbed.
9. She is elated and says so, plainly, before anything else: down the stairs two at a time, bags knocking against her knee. The suite is in her hair (vetiver, rye, warm dust, him) and she turns her head more than she needs to. The chicken: out of the brine, patted dry, oven as hot as it goes, the skin; she will sit at her own table "for once" and eat half with her fingers. "I want you to know I was thinking about the chicken." It was the best night of her life; she was hungry; she told herself it was physiology "and I believed me".
10. [TURN] She was in the car before the elation reached the part of her that had heard what he said.

- **Sensory lead:** Temperature: his heat leaving the heart under her bare palm, the inverse of everything she knows, since bodies reach her cold. Touch: fibrillation, a bag of worms against her skin. Sound: the celery crunch of the shears, the polite hiss of air, no breath at all, then the corridor's ventilation hum when the soundproof door unseals.
