## Scene 1 — The Method

1. Open in motion with her sleeves already rolled, carried over from the Ch 16 Snapper. Nitrile gloves go on and the silence of the suite comes in: the record has ended and the auto-return arm has lifted, the room has no reverb, so his breathing sounds as if it is inside her own ear.
2. Smell first. Vetiver soap, rye on his breath, warm dust from the equipment, and the first sharp apocrine sweat starting at his axillae. She logs it the way she would log it on a decedent: fear, recent onset.
3. She does an external examination aloud, as if dictating. Well-nourished male, 48, about 1.83 m and 84 kg, a well-healed appendicectomy scar in the right lower quadrant, the pads of the fingers flattened and a right-hand callus from piano. She remarks that in fourteen hundred cases she has never once been able to take a history from the patient.
4. First incision, on the volar left forearm: through the epidermis, the dermis and the superficial fascia. He bleeds. The dead don't bleed, they seep, and the difference delights her more than anything has in three years.
5. She retracts the skin and exposes the tendons of flexor digitorum superficialis. She asks him to play a chord on the desk and he does, and she watches the tendons slide under her forceps. She calls it beautiful and means it. This is the word's one use in the book.
6. The Y-incision runs from each acromion to the xiphoid process, then down the midline to the pubis, curving around the umbilicus. He makes sounds and she reports them precisely: involuntary, glottal, not a scream. The room keeps every one of them.
7. She reflects the chest flap. The subcutaneous fat is butter-yellow and beaded with blood, and under it the fibres of pectoralis major lie in their fan. She lifts the flap to feel its weight, a living weight, and his heat comes up through the nitrile. She has never touched anything this warm inside a body.
8. Her own body, reported like a finding: pulse 110, counted against his 96, and hers is the faster. There is heat in her face and arousal, named plainly and not apologised for. She wipes his forehead with the back of her wrist, and that is the tender, obscene beat.
9. She expects shock to close him down: pallor, a thready pulse, silence. That is what fourteen hundred cases have taught her a body does. She has planned around his silence.
10. [TURN] He begins to talk, in a full and level sentence. She had not expected him to be able to. She slows down so he can.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell first (vetiver, rye, warm electronics, fear-sweat, then the iron of fresh blood, which is not the morgue's smell). Then sound: a dead acoustic room, the faint zip of the blade through dermis, his breathing. Texture: the give of living dermis compared with refrigerated dermis. Temperature: 37 degrees, which is the shock of the scene. Light: the one lamp, plus amber standby LEDs.
- **Candidate jokes:** "Fourteen hundred patients and not one of them had ever told me where it hurt." / "The dead seep, like a cheap biro. He bled like a fountain pen, which is to say with intent." / He asks whether he should watch. "Most of my patients don't get a vote."
- **Body:** Gloved hands, steady, and the steadiness itself is a pleasure (no tremor; the ledger allows none after Ch 2). Pulse raised, face hot, aroused. She hasn't eaten since the light meal in Ch 15, which sharpens her senses. The left flank wound, eleven days old and stitches out, is present but quiet, and she doesn't touch it in this scene because her gloves are dirty.
- **Motifs:** Hands (both hers and his: the piano tendons). Vinyl (the silence after the record). The grade (her exam is a grading).

## Scene 2 — The Conversation, Continued

1. He talks about the system and doesn't plead. Twenty years of making sure that no one ever has to decide anything, which is the job, and he knows it is the job. He says it in complete sentences, and the grammar is the unbearable part.
2. She works the anterior abdominal wall slowly: the anterior rectus sheath, the rectus abdominis, the linea alba. She keeps the peritoneum closed on purpose, because opening it would end the conversation.
3. He talks about himself: the divorce, the house that is too quiet, the Tabard Club. It is Tuesday and he is missing his set. He thinks the house pianist will be playing it badly right now.
4. He brings up the broken record from Ch 13, Dolphy's *Out to Lunch!*. He tells her Dolphy died at thirty-six because the doctors assumed a Black jazz musician was on drugs and never tested him for diabetes. She knows that kind of death. Neither of them says what it rhymes with.
5. He talks about Holt and the "old files". Holt said "I thought Diane closed those," and quietly reassigned Brandt's access, and Brandt has watched Holt do it to other men for twenty years. She learns this from his mouth, not from the interlude; until now she has seen only the lockout.
6. The gift: one minute alone before every speech, with Mark's photograph, the detail outside the door by standing instruction. "Watch the old footage. Sixty seconds. He's never gone over." Then: "That's your door. It's the only one."
7. She thanks him and means it. She touches the knife wound on her left flank with the inside of her forearm, because her gloves are dirty, and that is how the reader knows she is thinking.
8. Her technique changes without her deciding it. She moves from sharp dissection to blunt, using fingers instead of the blade and separating planes she would normally cut. In her head she grades him: distinction.
9. [TURN] She has her way into the room. It came from the man she is taking apart, and she is taking him apart more gently now. She notices this and doesn't stop.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound: his voice with no reverb, close enough to be her own thought. Texture: fascia like wet pearl-onion skin, the rectus sheath glossy and tough. Taste: rye from her glass (from Ch 16) against the iron in the air. Smell: the blood is now thick enough to be a room smell.
- **Candidate jokes:** "He was the first man in twenty years to tell me something true without counsel present, and he was doing it with his rectus sheath open." / "It's Tuesday. I'm missing my set." "They'll get the house pianist." "The house pianist plays like he's being paid by the note."
- **Body:** Hands gentler and ungrudging. She uses her forearm on the wound. She drinks once, gloved, and the glass goes with her in S3. Her mouth waters at the smell and she reports that without comment. She is warm.
- **Motifs:** The Marine's photograph (described, not seen). Vinyl (the Dolphy, the Tabard). The grade ("distinction" echoes the Lesson One insert). Hands.

## Scene 3 — The Truth

1. He is fading, and she reads it as a finding: class II haemorrhage heading for class III, pallor, tachycardia, his sentences getting shorter but still complete. She estimates the blood loss at a litre and a half; seven of her ten towels are used.
2. He says there is one more thing. Three years ago a woman tried to reach her and left a voicemail that Iris never returned. Brandt knew because he had the woman under his own off-book surveillance too.
3. He says the woman's name. Iris is at the costal cartilages with the shears and the name goes past her. The narration does not give it to the reader (it is recovered in Ch 18). Brandt does not say the woman is on the list; he does not connect her to the eleven, and Iris draws no inference here.
4. "She died eight months later." Then: "I'm sorry. I thought you should know." These are his last complete sentences. No cause of death is given.
5. She finishes. She cuts the costal cartilages and lifts the sternal plate, his lungs collapse, and the talking stops for good. She strips off her right glove because she wants to feel the heart without nitrile. She holds it while it fibrillates and stops, and estimates its weight at about 380 g. She notes the time: about 1:50.
6. She closes him roughly, the way the dieners do at the morgue: sternal plate back in place, flaps folded over, no sutures. Two strips of black vinyl tape go over his eyes, temple to temple, as they went over Diane's, because he saw her.
7. She pins the page with a single steel pin over the manubrium. It is his own copy from the Ch 13 pillow, folded in quarters and ringed by a glass from this suite. She circles THE ARCHITECT in red marker, and the Evaluation reads MISTOOK CLARITY FOR CRUELTY.
8. Exit protocol, competent and complete: her glass, the remaining glove, the blades, the instruments and the used towels, all bagged. Her close-up print of Diane's mouth stays on the table where he has kept it nine days. The Mingus sleeve stays beside it. She does not turn the record over. She leaves the suite the way she came in.
9. In the corridor, the stairwell and the rear car park she is elated and says so to the reader, twice. She is already planning the chicken.
10. [TURN] She is in the car before the elation reaches the part of her that heard what he said.

- **Sensory lead:** Temperature: his heat leaving him under her bare palm, which is the inverse of everything she knows, since bodies come to her already cold. Touch: the heart's fibrillation as a "bag of worms" against her skin. Sound: no breath at all, then the corridor's ventilation hum when the soundproof door opens. Smell: the suite in her hair (vetiver, rye, warm electronics, blood), which carries into Ch 18.
- **Candidate jokes:** "I had a chicken brining at home. I want you to know I was thinking about the chicken." / "I closed him the way the dieners close them at work: badly, lovingly, for nobody." (No named morgue staff.)
- **Body:** Her right hand is bare from the heart onwards, and that one glove left off is the single protocol lapse, and it comes from appetite. She doesn't wash before she leaves, and the thin black leather driving gloves stay in her pocket, because her gloves were already off and so she didn't think of her hands. She is hungry and tells herself it is physiology. Pulse still high, still warm.
- **Motifs:** Tape (black vinyl over the eyes). Voicemail (the first of the three, spoken). Vinyl (sleeve on the table, record unturned). Hands. The meal after (planned, not taken).
