# Beat sheet: interlude-5-reyes

- **Unit:** Interlude 5, Reyes (after Chapter 26, before Chapter 27). Manuscript file `manuscript/interlude-5-reyes.md`, heading `# Interlude — Reyes`.
- **POV:** Special Agent Eleanor Reyes, close third, past tense. Never Iris's head, never Holt's. In her own head she is "Reyes".
- **Target words:** 600–900 (aim ~800). One scene, one Turn. Suggested weight: counsel and the paper ~300, the radios ~150, the half-second ~250, the Turn ~100.
- **Act pressure:** Act III, The Final Exam. "Reyes, closing on paper." This is the one place in the book where the paper thread and the woman it is looking for are in the same corridor. Reyes is still working on paper when it happens: she has come down to ask counsel about two names. The pressure, for the reader, is the distance between what she has in her pocket (a schedule with Vale on it) and what she has in front of her (a face with no name on it). The ledger keeps those two apart, and this interlude must too.
- **Iris's appetite at entry:** Gone. Ch 25: no taste. Ch 26: "I am not hungry." She would once have called that a finding.
- **Iris's appetite at exit:** Unchanged. She is off the page, and an interlude cannot touch her appetite. What Reyes sees is hands that are calm when nothing else is. The reader knows what those hands have just done and that nothing was felt while they did it. Reyes doesn't know either thing. No appetite, hunger or food from Iris leaks into Reyes's head.
- **Register (interlude-drafter rules):** plainer and cooler than Iris. Layman's words. No jokes in Iris's style, no direct address, no grading, no anatomy. Reyes thinks the way she files: nouns first, in order, what she saw before what she thinks about it. Her sentences are complete and short. She is not a fragment-thinker like Cam. When she gets something wrong, it is a gap in the order.

## Scene 1: The Service Corridor

1. **Open on paper.** Election Eve, back of house, the hotel. Reyes is in the building as campaign-protection liaison (her name is on the security annex, beats/ch19.md). She is a guest at someone else's job and knows it. Her notebook is in her jacket. It holds the eleven names in alphabetical order, nine with interview dates against them (Vale is down for Wednesday, 10 a.m.) and two with a question mark.
2. **The two question marks.** One woman has left the country. Counsel has a forwarding address and is calling it privileged. The other woman's agreement gives a phone number that is disconnected and an address she left four years ago. Reyes has left counsel two voicemails about them today, and neither has been returned. Counsel is here tonight, so Reyes has come to ask in person. (One line for the voicemail rhyme. Do not lean on it.)
3. **Down.** A staffer upstairs tells her counsel is "downstairs, by the dock, taking a call where there's signal". Reyes takes the service stair and comes out into the service corridor: painted cinderblock, a laundry cart, a catering trolley parked against the wall, the ballroom's bass coming through the ceiling as a pressure more than a sound.
4. **Counsel.** By the loading-dock bay doors, in the cold coming off the dock, counsel has her phone to her ear and a coat over her shoulders. She sees Reyes, lowers the phone and says, "After the election, Agent Reyes." She says it pleasantly, the way she has said it in writing since Ch 14. Reyes opens her mouth to answer.
5. **The radios go.** Every radio in the corridor at once: the hotel security man's on his hip, a kitchen porter's, the earpiece in Reyes's own ear. A lockdown order with all exits held, and under it the word for the principal. Keep the wording generic. No Secret Service jargon, and no statement of what has happened. Counsel's phone is still at her ear and she is still smiling, and the smile hasn't caught up with the news.
6. **Body first.** Reyes's right hand is on the holster before she has decided anything. Twenty years of that. She turns away from counsel, back the way she came, towards the stair and the green-room floor, and runs. The flat shoes are why she can.
7. **The corridor reacts.** People flatten against the walls: a porter, two servers holding trays at chest height, a security man shouting into his radio. Everyone in the corridor is either stopping or turning towards the noise. One person is walking the other way, towards the dock, in a catering jacket. She is not hurrying and she is not stopping.
8. **Half a second.** They pass close in the narrow corridor. Order of observation, Reyes's way: the jacket, then the hands, then the face. The woman looks at Reyes's hands first, and only then at her face, the way police do. Reyes looks at hers. One hand is flat on the jacket over the left side of the chest, holding something through the cloth. The other hangs at her side, open and still. She is carrying nothing, which is wrong for catering staff. Hers are the only steady hands in the corridor. The face is a woman in her thirties, unmade-up and pale under the strip light, with no expression. It isn't blank. It is the face of someone at work. Reyes gets no words for it tonight, and the lack of words is what she will keep coming back to.
9. **The choice, unmade.** Something in the back of her head starts writing it down: wrong hands, wrong direction. Then the earpiece says the green-room floor again, and she has sworn herself to the principal, and twenty years have taught her which way to run. The hand on the chest stays with her up the first flight: flat, like a woman saying the pledge.
10. **[TURN]** *She will spend the rest of her career remembering that face. Tonight she doesn't stop.* Render it in past tense. It is the last sentence of the interlude, complete and unsoftened. Nothing comes after it, not the stair door, not the radio.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound. The ballroom bass through the ceiling, felt in the chest. Counsel's pleasant voice. Then every radio in the corridor at once, the same order coming from five places half a beat apart. Then her own breathing and her shoes on the concrete. Temperature: the cold off the open dock against the warm, wet air from the kitchen. Smell (Reyes's, plain and brief): fryer oil, bleach, cigarette smoke from the bay. Texture: the notebook's spiral through her jacket, the holster under her palm. Sight comes last and is the shortest: the half-second.
- **Dry observations (Reyes's register, not Iris's, and no jokes in her style):**
  - On counsel: "After the election" is the only answer counsel has given her in five weeks. Reyes has it in writing three times.
  - On the corridor: everyone in it was doing one of two things, stopping or turning. Reyes had stood in enough corridors to know there was a third thing, and that it was rare.
  - (Optional) On her posting: she had asked for anything but protection. Protection is what you get when they want you somewhere you can be seen.
- **The body (Reyes's):** Her hand on the holster before the thought. Flat shoes and a run. Her breath short on the stair. She has not eaten since a sandwich at four; one clause, no more, and no meal motif drawn from it. Her hip is sore from the holster, which she has stopped noticing. **The body (Iris's, as seen):** hands only, one flat on the chest over the folded page and one open and still, then the face. Do not say whether the hands are gloved (beats/ch25.md leaves gloves to ch24 and ch26). Reyes sees posture, not skin.
- **Motifs:**
  - **Hands:** the outline's "something about the hands". Each woman looks at the other's hands first (this mirrors ch26 S2 exactly). Reyes's hand on her holster, Iris's on the folded page. This is the one motif the interlude owns.
  - **Paperwork thread:** the notebook, the question marks, privilege, "after the election", and Vale on Wednesday at 10 a.m., in her pocket as she passes the face. The system's filing is in the corridor with her. Don't point at it.
  - **Voicemail:** Reyes's two unreturned messages to counsel. One line.
  - **The catering jacket:** staged in Ch 20 and worn here. Reyes reads it as "catering" and nothing more.
  - **Not here:** tape, vinyl, mirrors, the Marine's photograph, the grade. Do not add them.

## Snapper

The outline's Turn is the last line, and in an interlude it doubles as the Snapper. The closing image goes in the sentence just before it: **the woman's hand flat on her chest as she walks away towards the dock, like someone saying the pledge**, on Election Eve, in a building full of flags. Reyes thinks the comparison in plain words and doesn't comment on it. Then: *She would spend the rest of her career remembering that face. That night she didn't stop.* (The drafter picks the exact past-tense wording. "Tonight" can stay as free indirect style if it reads better.)

The reader knows what is under the hand: the third page, folded once and unread (ch25). Reyes never does. Do not say so.
