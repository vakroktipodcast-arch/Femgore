# Beat sheet — ch03: The Re-engineering

## Conflicts

None found. The outline and the ledger agree. There are two soft dependencies on the ch02 beat sheet, which didn't exist when this was written, so the dates and the fundraiser recall below are proposals. See New facts.

- **Unit:** ch03 (`manuscript/ch03.md`)
- **POV:** Iris Vale, first person, past tense
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Three scenes of ~1,050 each.
- **Act pressure:** Act I, The Prerequisite. The pressure is the campaign's certainty that nothing is coming, and it has to show in every scene. In 1, the dossier shows a woman so well guarded that no one guards the guard. In 2, the Compliance Director publishes her own Sunday to the world and nobody on a ten-million-dollar campaign has noticed. In 3, a late call-in show on the kitchen radio says the race is over. Brandt doesn't come in until Chapter 8. Running under everything is the fifth pressure, appetite. It's present and unnamed, and it should frighten the reader more than it frightens her.
- **Single evening:** the whole chapter takes place on one night in the flat, from early evening to about 1.30 a.m. There are no exteriors. The only places in the chapter are the counter, the desk and the floor, and the chapter should feel that small.
- **Appetite at entry:** appetite as competence, newly unsettled. Chapter 2 left her smiling in the lift without knowing when the smile started, and holding her left hand with her right. She comes home and does what she does with an unexplained finding: she works it up. Planning is the pleasure she's allowed, and she doesn't call it one.
- **Appetite at exit:** it is now in the body and she has a number for it. Rehearsing the capture on a rug leaves her "breathing hard and happy". She reports this as respiratory rate and flush and files it under the stopwatch, as a personal best. There are three feedings along the way. She eats the kidneys standing up, writes the meal down next to the line saying she feels nothing nameable, and is happy on the floor. Each time she reports straight, and each time she misfiles it. The reader should finish the chapter knowing that the lesson is a meal.
- **Grading rule for this chapter:** Iris grades her own dossier, Diane's privacy settings, her three attempts, and how she looks doing it. Mara and Hannah are absent. No syllabus insert at the chapter head. Lesson One is on the desk as a working document, not reproduced.

## Scene 1 — The Margins

1. First lines. She is home from the fundraiser with the lift still on her. The locked drawer is open, and on the desk are the Pell dossier and her own working copy of the Lesson One page, with the red marker uncapped beside them. The task is set in the first paragraph: she needs an hour of Diane's life that belongs to no one, and she is going to find it tonight.
2. The plan revises itself in red. Last night's opportunity at the hotel goes first. She strikes the hotel through with one line and the margin note reads: *Too many doors. Too much carpet. Everyone she has is in the room with her.* The reader watches her think in her own handwriting, quoted in short margin fragments set against the long narrating sentences.
3. She cooks while she thinks. Six lamb's kidneys from the butcher, out of the paper. She halves each one along its convex border and snips out the white core of the pelvis and the calyces with kitchen scissors. Cortex, medulla, hilum: she names it as she trims, because she can't not. This is the kidney joke (see below). The raw faint ammoniac note under the sweetness of lamb.
4. Back to the desk with her hands rinsed and still damp. The dossier is a week grid of a hundred and sixty-eight hours, and she goes down it in red. Weekdays: the driver at 6.50, HQ, the War Room, donor dinners, home after eleven. Her house: an alarmed townhouse with a neighbour who walks a dog at all hours. Her phone is never off, and she answers every call. Iris remembers that last fact from the fundraiser and underlines it twice: *She answers. She always answers.* This seeds Chapter 5's planted phone.
5. The pan. Butter until it smells of hazelnuts, the kidneys cut side down for ninety seconds, then English mustard, cayenne, Worcestershire, a splash of the water she has been keeping off the dossier, and two slices of toast. She eats from the pan, standing at the counter, with the papers on the far side out of the splash line. One sentence on the rule: when she is planning she eats on her feet.
6. Saturday and Sunday on the grid. Saturdays are HQ from ten. On Sundays she calls her mother at four, and the dossier sources that to a trade-magazine profile: "I call my mother every Sunday at four, no matter what." Sunday afternoon is HQ, and Sunday evening is the weekly call sheet. Sunday morning says *home* in Iris's own hand, with a question mark she put there months ago and never followed up. She circles the question mark in red.
7. She grades the dossier, which is her own work. It's thorough, sourced and handsomely kept, and it fails. "B-minus. For diligence, which is the grade you give a student who has done everything except the thing."
8. She does the sum again, with a fingertip on each hour. Every hour of Diane Pell's week is either guarded or attended: by a driver, by staff, by an alarm, by a phone she always answers, by her mother at four. The campaign has made her the one person on it who is never alone.
9. [TURN] The dossier has a gap, and she sees what the gap is. In a hundred and sixty-eight hours there is not one hour in which Diane is unsecured, and nobody lives like that, not even Diane. The hour exists and Iris doesn't have it. She writes in the margin, in red: *Where does she go to not be reached?*

- **Sensory lead:** smell, first and throughout. The marker's solvent when it's uncapped. Raw kidney, faintly ammoniac, which she identifies before she likes it. Butter going to noisette. Cayenne at the back of the throat. Then taste: mustard heat, the iron-rich give of the kidney cooked pink at the centre, toast gone soft under the sauce. Texture: the kidney's capsule, which peels like the skin on a grape, and the dossier's paper gone soft at the corners.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - On the kidney: "The kidney is the body's compliance department. It sees everything that passes, keeps what's useful, and sends the rest down a tube to be forgotten about. I had six of them in a pan, and I'd never enjoyed a department more."
  - On the grid: Diane's week "was so well defended it had no room left in it to be a life, which is the kind of thing you only ever say about other people's diaries."
- **Body:** eating, the chapter's first meal, standing up, all six kidneys and both slices. Hands: damp from the tap, then steady on the marker. She checks the left hand once, the one from the lift. It's still, and she says so and moves on. Heat from the pan on her face against the cool of the flat.
- **Motifs:** the grade (her own dossier, B-minus); hands (the left hand checked); the meal, pre-echoed (standing to plan, as the bible requires); tape is not used here.

## Scene 2 — The Pattern

1. Later. She has moved to the writing desk with the laptop, and the screen is the only light on. The pan is soaking. The question in the margin is the one thing on the page that isn't crossed out. She reasons from the body, because she always does: if you want to know where a woman goes, look at what the going has done to her.
2. She remembers the fundraiser. Under Diane's dress watch, on the left wrist, was a band of paler skin, wider than the watch and the shape of a sports strap. It's the same pale band as the Jane Doe's cut-off hospital bracelet in Chapter 1, and she makes the connection to the reader in one line, no more. Diane wears something on her wrist that counts steps, on days when she isn't being looked at.
3. The paperwork. Iris's own NDA file, three years old, has a cover email from Diane with one stray CC to a personal address, a slip Diane will have made once in her career. Iris kept it because she keeps everything. She types the address into the "find people you know" field of a walking and fitness app, Trailmark. Iris notes that the most dangerous document in the campaign is her own NDA, and nobody at the campaign thinks of it as dangerous.
4. The profile comes up. It was never set to private, because the default is public and Diane has never read the settings. Her display name is *D. Pell*. The avatar is the reservoir at dawn, with no face in it. There are a hundred and forty-one recorded walks, all on Sundays.
5. She reads the walks the way she reads a body, from the outside in. Every walk starts at the south car park and goes clockwise round the same loop, 6.4 km. Start times run from 7.31 to 7.48 a.m. The first walk is logged eleven days after the date on Iris's NDA. She reports that date and does not interpret it.
6. The pause. At the same coordinates every week, 3.1 km in, the GPS track stops moving for eleven minutes, give or take one. She knows it's a bench before the satellite view shows her one. Diane sits somewhere for eleven minutes every Sunday, and the campaign that pays for her driver and her alarm could find out where by typing her name.
7. The anomaly she misreads. On a handful of Sundays the pace drops by a third: the Sunday after Thanksgiving, two at Christmas, one in late May. She logs them as weather or a bad knee and moves on. The reader may notice those are college holidays, and Iris doesn't, which plants Chapter 4's Turn without spoiling it.
8. The intersection. She lays the track over the satellite map, and four hundred metres past the bench the trail passes within eighty metres of the abandoned pump station below the dam. A spur path runs to it, screened by trees. Allowing for pace and the eleven minutes, she calculates when Diane passes the spur: 8.33 a.m., give or take nine minutes. She marks the X in red on a printout and writes the time beside it. The intersection point is set.
9. She waits for the feeling, as she would for a lab result. She takes her own radial pulse (sixty-four), checks her hands (still) and looks for the thing she is supposed to feel at having found the hour. She can't find it.
10. [TURN] In the dossier margin she writes: *Affect: nothing identifiable.* Then, immediately below and in the same hand, the meal: *Lamb's kidneys ×6, devilled. Toast ×2. Standing.* She doesn't ask why it belongs on the same page. Neither line gets a comment from her.

- **Sensory lead:** light and sound. The laptop's blue-white glow at the edge of the page. The fan spinning up. The soft dry tick of the trackpad. The flat's fridge through the wall. Then texture: the printout warm from the printer, and the marker's slight drag on cheap paper. Taste lingers: cayenne still on her lips, which she notices when she licks them before writing.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "The Compliance Director of a presidential-tier campaign had accepted the terms and conditions without reading them, which I found so moving I had to look away from the screen for a moment."
  - On the GPS pause: "Eleven minutes of nothing, every week, logged to the second. The only person I had ever met who kept better records of her own absence was me."
- **Body:** hands (pulse at the wrist, the marker, the left hand checked a second time); the meal written down as a finding; eyes dry from the screen. She is cold. It's the flat at night in Act I, and she doesn't fetch a jumper.
- **Motifs:** the grade (Diane's privacy settings fail, as does the campaign that never thought to check); hands; the pale band of skin (internal callback to Ch 1, not a bible motif); the meal (written into the record, pre-echoing the meal-after motif).

## Scene 3 — The Rehearsal

1. Past midnight. The pan is washed, the counter wiped, and the dossier squared on the desk. She takes the rug from where it lives under the writing desk. It's a flatweave, oxblood and bone, about 170 cm long. She rolls it tight and belts it at two points with her own belts. Iris knows exactly how this looks and says so before the reader can. She grades the picture: a woman in a T-shirt and socks, at one in the morning, squaring up to a carpet.
2. She estimates Diane from the fundraiser at 168 cm out of heels and about seventy kilos. The rug is perhaps eight, and she notes the difference and the advantage. Then she gives it a face. Near the top end, a strip of surgical tape, and on the tape in red marker two dots for the nostrils and a line for the mouth. For the first time the marker touches something standing in for Diane.
3. She props the rug upright against the counter edge, with the face at Diane's height. Industrial tape, pre-torn into strips, goes along the counter's edge in a row, as on an instrument tray. She folds the end of each strip back on itself to make a tab, the way dressings are made, so that a gloved finger can find it. Her phone is propped against the fruit bowl (as the tablet was in Ch 1) and set to record video. On the kitchen radio, a late call-in show: a man from the suburbs says it's done and he'd put his house on it.
4. The shape of the plan in one paragraph, with nothing explained. Diane will have a phone at her ear. A woman on a phone has one hand and half her hearing, and you come from the phone side. Hand over the nose and mouth, weight back and down, a tape strip across the mouth, then the wrists. Iris has watched this happen from the wrong end fourteen hundred times. On the table she reads what a struggle leaves, and tonight she's working out how to leave none of it.
5. Attempt one. The rug folds at the wrong place, the strip sticks to itself, and she ends up on one knee holding a carpet in an embrace. On the video at half speed, watched like footage of a stranger, it's 7.2 seconds. She grades it, without mercy, and the grade is funny.
6. Attempt two. She corrects for the fold, takes the weight down across her own thigh, and finds the tab first time: 5.1 seconds. The weave gives her a friction abrasion about 2 cm long on the inside of her right wrist, superficial and weeping slightly, and she documents it as she'd document it on someone else. The tiles under her knees are cold. The flat below has a retired couple who have complained once in four years. She lowers the rug the last few inches by hand, out of courtesy.
7. A short competent aside to the reader. Closed is not the same as kept closed. The airway on Sunday will be closed only long enough to take the fight out, then let go, because Diane will need her voice for what comes after. Iris knows to the second what a body does without air and in what order. Give one line of it and keep the rest for Chapter 6.
8. Attempt three. Phone side, hand, weight, tab, mouth. The rug goes down without a sound she didn't choose.
9. [TURN] On the video: 3.8 seconds. Under four. She is sitting back on her heels on the kitchen floor with a carpet in her arms, and she is breathing hard and she is happy. Report it as a finding: respiratory rate up, a flush across the chest and throat, and the pleasure of the number. Don't name anything past that.
10. Out. She deletes the video and then deletes it again from the deleted folder, because she knows where people look. She peels the surgical-tape face off the rug and folds it in on itself, sticky side in. She unbelts the rug and unrolls it, and carries the kit and the marker back to the desk. (Carry into the Snapper.)

- **Sensory lead:** sound and texture. The rip of industrial tape, which is louder than she'd like in a sleeping building. The dull pat of wool on tile. The radio voice certain about November. The weave's rasp against the inside of her wrist. Then temperature: cold tile, and her own heat coming up through it. Then smell: wool and old dust from the rug, adhesive, her own sweat.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - On the rug's weight: it "was a lighter woman than Diane by some sixty kilograms, and a great deal more cooperative, and if I'm honest it had better taste in Sundays."
  - On reviewing attempt one: "I've sat through a great many teaching videos. None of them showed a consultant pathologist losing a wrestling match to soft furnishings, and the profession is poorer for it."
- **Body:** the whole body, for the first time in the book. Knees on cold tile, a thigh taking the weight, both hands working and the right wrist abraded. Her breathing at the Turn. The cold of Act I is still the baseline, and the heat at the Turn is the first flicker of Act II's warmth. Report it, don't flag it.
- **Motifs:** tape (industrial tape in the hand for the first time; surgical tape made into a face); hands (the abrasion; the tab found blind); the grade (three attempts graded, and how she looks); the red marker (it touches a stand-in for Diane).
