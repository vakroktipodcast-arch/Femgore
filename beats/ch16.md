## Scene 1: Arrival

1. The corridor of the media consultancy after hours, lit by its one light: the hum of the ventilation, carpet tiles, and the Mingus leaking round a door that is latched but not pressed into its seals, turned down so he would hear her knock. She doesn't knock. She keys in the code, and the door gives with a small sucking sound.
2. Inside, the room swallows the corridor. Smell first: rye; warm dust off equipment that has been on all day; the new-foam smell of the acoustic panels; and, at the height of a seated man, the dry green "cellar" note she last met in her own flat in Ch 9, now with a man attached. She doesn't name it, and she doesn't re-describe it. Sight last: the one lamp, the row of amber standby lights along the wall at knee height, the long edit desk, the dark monitors, which show nothing, and a low table beside a leather chair.
3. He looks up from the chair. Charcoal shirt, no tie, sleeves down, the jacket over the chair back. "I wondered if it would be you." He isn't afraid, he's curious. She reads that as a finding: pupils normal for the light, hands loose on the chair arms, breathing slow, no sweat. She grades his composure in her head: excellent.
4. She reads "if it would be you" for what it implies. Someone else might have come first: Holt's people, now that Holt has locked him out of his own campaign (the card that failed at 7:51, Ch 14). He has been waiting for whichever of them arrived first, and he is glad it is her. She notices that she is pleased he is glad.
5. On the low table beside his drink is the photograph. It is her own print, the 8x10 close-up of Diane's mouth with the shredded NDA at the lips and half the matter number LC-0311 legible, the one she left squared on his kitchen counter nine days ago. She reads it like a body. The corners have gone soft. There is a crease across the lower third where it has been folded and unfolded (he carried it in his breast pocket), and a thumbprint, his, ghosted into the gloss at the left edge. Beside it stand the Mingus sleeve and her Lesson Two page, folded in quarters, under his glass (no joke about the ring yet; Ch 17 has the coaster line). He kept it, and he has been looking at it every day since.
6. Pleasure, reported without apology: she has been read before (a War Room reading the page, Ch 8), but she has never been kept. She allows herself to say it plainly: nobody has ever looked at her work this long, including her.
7. Brief, in fragments: "Phone?" "Jacket. Inside left." She takes it from the breast pocket with a gloved hand (thin black leather gloves, worn from the car) and puts it in a second foil sleeve (her own phone is already in one, in her jacket). The copy of the scene photograph he saved from the channel goes dark in her hand. He watches her do it and approves the method.
8. The door has closed behind her and seated into its seal by itself. Her body checks the room before she does: one other person breathing, no second door, nothing behind the acoustic baffles. She notes that her own pulse is up (no number; Ch 17 counts) and that it isn't fear.
9. [TURN] On the low table, beside his glass and the photograph, there's a second glass: heavy, clean, empty, set out before she arrived. He has set out two glasses.

## Scene 2: The Conversation

1. He pours for her without asking how she takes it, two fingers, neat, which is how she takes it. She takes off her right glove to hold the glass and tells the reader the inventory: this hand will touch the glass and nothing else, and the glass leaves with her. (This seeds Ch 17's bare right hand without naming it.) She sits on the arm of the second chair, not in it.
2. Taste: the rye is dry and peppery, with no sweetness anywhere and the heat arriving late at the back of the tongue. It is the first thing she has put in her mouth since the light meal, and she describes it as exactly as tissue. She drinks the first mouthful slowly. (Avoid Ch 17's "crust of a dark loaf".)
3. He knows the syllabus is for him, and he says so in fragments that are starting to join up. "Mine. Read it a lot." And then: "I read the other one too. In your drawer." (He means the Apex page, from Ch 9. Don't quote it or describe it.) She says nothing. She grades his restraint for not saying the name.
4. He asks how she came to it. He means the syllabus, not the injury. She answers out loud in short sentences: she reported, she kept records, she signed in Diane's office, and then the blind items came, his. In narration she gives the reader more: the night two years ago at the writing desk when the first sentence arrived already in the second person (Ch 1: "written two years ago at this desk"). She doesn't describe what Holt did. Keep the original injury out of the room.
5. Partway through her answer the side that was playing runs out. She doesn't hear the arm go home (talk covers it) and only notices the room has got bigger. Neither of them changes it. (The audible arm is saved for the Snapper.) He doesn't beg, apologise or explain the blind items. His grammar comes together for the first time aloud: "I would have done the same thing." He doesn't mean the NDA. He means the syllabus.
6. She doesn't know what to do with that, so she drinks. She tells the reader so: she has a procedure for every kind of statement a man can make in a room, and this one is not on it.
7. Now he is honest, and his sentences are complete. After the photograph he went down the NDA signatory list privately, looking for the one he wanted, and that is how he found her: not her own NDA alone, as she has believed since Ch 9, but the list. The template is his (he chose the face, Century Schoolbook, with the footer rule). "You think you're the only one." A pause, which is kind. "There are eleven names on that list." He says eleven flatly, with no twelfth and no struck name. He doesn't read the names. He doesn't say which one has medical training, because he doesn't need to.
8. Her body before she does. Her left hand goes to the flank wound and presses it through her shirt. She knows this is her thinking gesture and it is happening without her. Ch 9's "We are the same kind of alone" comes back in one clause and fails. He seems about to say something more, decides not yet, and lets it go. (This seeds Ch 17's "There's one more thing." No hint of the woman or of death.)
9. [TURN] She had believed herself alone. She had built two years on being the only one. He watches it land and is kind about it. He gets up and turns his back so she can have her face to herself. He sets the needle back at the start of side one and turns it up a notch, so that neither of them has to speak for a while. That kindness, which he gives without being asked and which costs him nothing he will ever need again, is unbearable.

When the needle drops again there is the soft crackle of the lead-in groove and the music starting over. ## Scene 3: The Preparation

1. The arm comes home again, unremarked, at about 11:10, and the quiet is her cue. She puts down her glass beside the photograph and puts her right glove back on. She takes off her jacket (her phone in its foil in the pocket) and hangs it over the second chair. She stands. She clears the long edit desk (about ninety-six inches, and she does the conversion because she always does): the keyboards, the jog wheel and the coiled cables go to the floor along the wall, the monitors are pushed back on their arms, dark. The cable grommets along the desk edge are where she noted them from the plans in Ch 15.
2. She unrolls the instrument roll on the low table, beside the photograph and the two glasses, and sets the box of nitrile and the towels in their bags beside the second chair. Ch 15's tasting menu, served. He comes to look at it with his hands in his pockets, the way a man reads a wine list he can't afford. He asks no questions. She notes that he doesn't, and grades it.
3. "On the desk. On your back." He lets her. He asks for one thing first: the record. He crosses to the turntable and drops the needle at the start of side one, again, and she lets him, because she wouldn't have known how to. Then he sits on the edge of the desk, swings his legs up and lies down without being helped, head toward the lamp, and lays his arms out along the edges, palms up, waiting.
4. The binding, done with procedure and pleasure. She unbuttons each of his cuffs and rolls the sleeve to the elbow (she tells the reader she likes the forearms bare, which is as far as she'll say). She tapes each wrist palm-up to the desk edge, then zip-ties it through a cable grommet, and the ratchet clicks under the Mingus. Ankles the same at the far end. A band of industrial tape goes across his thighs, because she is thorough and not because he is going anywhere. She keeps the leather gloves on throughout, because tape adhesive takes prints, and she tells the reader that without making a lesson of it.
5. He tests each tie for her when she finishes it, a small courteous flex against the plastic, the way a man tries a door for a woman. His hands lie open on the wood. She looks at them longer than she needs to: the pianist's fingers and the flattened pads (save the detail of the pads for Ch 17's examination and note only that she looks). She does not report that he is warm.
6. Brandt, in full and level grammar: "Take your time. I would rather it was you than anyone else."
7. She tells the reader plainly that no one has ever said anything like that to her. Not a lover, not Hannah, not a single colleague in fourteen hundred cases, and not in any form. She is going to take him at his word.
8. She takes off the leather gloves one finger at a time and puts them in her jacket pocket. She tells the reader this is the last of what she came in wearing to come off, and that she won't need them again tonight. (The nitrile is in its box. Ch 17 opens it.)
9. [TURN] She tells the reader, plainly and in hindsight, that it was going to be the best night of her life. She knew that much while she stood there. What she did not know was that the woman who had keyed in the code would not be the one who walked out. The reader understands both halves. She, in the room, has only the first.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound: the ratchet of the zip-ties, the tear of the industrial tape (loud in a deaf room), and the Mingus louder now, filling what the conversation left. Texture: tape adhesive dragging on leather, the hard plastic of the grommets, the smooth laminate of the desk. Smell: rye on both their breaths, and the cellar note rising off him now that he is lying down (still unnamed; no fear note). Temperature: she has known he would be warm and doesn't touch him bare-handed or learn it through leather (Ch 17's palm on him is the first). The suite is warm, and so is she.
- **Candidate jokes:**
  - The instrument roll: "He read it like a wine list he couldn't afford, which, in fairness, he couldn't."
  - The ties: "I have tied a lot of tags to a lot of toes. None of them had ever helped."
  - "Take your time": "It is what you say to a woman you are not in a hurry to be finished with. I had heard it said in films. I had assumed it was a special effect."
  - The desk: "A hundred and eighty-three centimetres of man on ninety-six inches of desk. I have never in my life been so well supplied with margin."
- **Body:** Gloved hands, then bare. Pulse high and unhurried, which she notes as the best combination. Arousal is present and named as a finding, briefly (Ch 17 reports it fully). Hunger is there, and she tells herself it's because she hasn't eaten. Warm. She doesn't touch the wound in this scene: her hands are busy, and she is not thinking, she is doing.
- **Motifs:** Tape (industrial and black vinyl in the roll, the tape on his wrists). Hands (his palms up and waiting, hers ungloving). Vinyl (side one playing out). The grade (his not asking questions, graded). The meal after, by implication: the tasting menu is laid, and the meal itself is Ch 17.
