## Scene 1: Arrival

1. The corridor of the media consultancy, after hours: the hum of the ventilation, carpet tiles, the Mingus faint under the suite door, turned down so he would hear her knock. She doesn't knock. She keys in the code she took from him in Ch 15, and the acoustic seal lets go with a soft exhalation, like a fridge door.
2. Inside, the room swallows the corridor. Smell first: rye; warm dust off equipment that has been on all day; the new-foam smell of the acoustic panels; and at the height of a seated man, the dry green note she last smelled at her desk drawer in Ch 9, "like a cellar somebody keeps well", now with a man attached. She doesn't name it. Sight last: the one lamp, the row of amber standby lights, the long edit desk, the dark monitors, which show nothing, and a low table beside a leather chair.
3. He looks up from the chair. Charcoal shirt, no tie, sleeves down, the jacket over the chair back. "I wondered if it would be you." He isn't afraid, he's curious. She reads that as a finding: pupils normal for the light, hands loose on the chair arms, breathing slow. She grades his composure in her head: excellent.
4. She reads "if it would be you" for what it implies. Someone else might have come first: Holt's people, now that Holt has moved his furniture (Ch 14). He has been waiting for whichever of them arrived first, and he is glad it is her. She notices that she is pleased he is glad.
5. On the low table beside his drink is the photograph: Diane on the pump-station concrete, black tape over her eyes, the shredded NDA at her lips. It is a print, and she reads it like a body. The corners have gone soft. There is a crease across the lower third where it has been folded and unfolded, and a thumbprint, his, ghosted into the gloss at the left edge. He kept it, and he has been looking at it for weeks. Her page lies folded beside it, under his glass (no joke about it yet; Ch 17 has the coaster line).
6. Pleasure, reported without apology: she has been published before (Ch 8), but she has never been kept. She allows herself to say it plainly: nobody has ever looked at her work this long, including her.
7. Brief, in fragments: "Phone?" "Jacket. Inside left." She takes it from the breast pocket with a gloved hand (thin black leather gloves, worn from the car) and puts it in the foil sleeve. The only campaign copy of the photograph goes dark in her hand. He watches her do it and approves the method.
8. The door has closed behind her and sealed by itself. Her body checks the room before she does: one other person breathing, no second door, nothing behind the acoustic baffles. She notes that her own pulse is up and that it isn't fear.
9. [TURN] On the low table, beside his glass and the photograph, there's a second glass: heavy, clean, empty, set out before she arrived. He has set out two glasses.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell (rye, warm electronics, acoustic foam, the unnamed cellar-green). Sound: the door's seal letting go, then the dead room, where the Mingus is low but very close because nothing bounces, like music in a padded case. Texture: the leather of her gloves, the photograph's softened gloss. Temperature: the suite is warm, a room full of equipment, and she is Act II warm. Light: the one lamp, the amber standby LEDs, the dark monitors (no reflection).
- **Candidate jokes:**
  - He turned the music down so he would hear her knock. "It was the most considerate thing a man had done for me in years, and I ignored it, which is also traditional."
  - On the photograph's wear: "I have seen less handling on a family Bible. I have seen less handling on some of my patients."
  - On "I wondered if it would be you": "Most men say that at a door with flowers in their hand. He had a rye in his."
- **Body:** Gloved hands, leather. She stands by the door for longer than she needs to. Pulse up and warm, and she reports it as not fear. She hasn't eaten since the light meal (Ch 15), and the hunger is sharp and pleasant, mentioned once. The left flank wound is quiet.
- **Motifs:** Vinyl (the Mingus through the dead air). Tape (black, over Diane's eyes, in the photograph). The grade (his composure graded). Hands (his on the chair arms, her gloves). The foil sleeve and the phone as a small piece of procedure.

## Scene 2: The Conversation

1. He pours for her without asking how she takes it, two fingers, neat, which is how she takes it. She takes off her right glove to hold the glass and tells the reader the inventory: this hand will touch the glass and nothing else, and the glass leaves with her. (This seeds Ch 17's bare right hand without naming it.) She sits on the arm of the second chair, not in it.
2. Taste: the rye is dry and spiced, the heat arriving late at the back of the tongue. It is the first thing she has put in her mouth since the light meal, and she describes it as exactly as tissue. She drinks the first mouthful slowly, the way you taste a glass you have been allowed.
3. He knows the syllabus is for him, and he says so in fragments that are starting to join up. "Mine. Read it a lot." And then: "I read the other one too. In your drawer." (He means the Apex page, from Ch 9. Don't quote it or describe it.) She says nothing. She grades his restraint for not saying the name.
4. He asks how she came to it. He means the syllabus, not the injury. She answers out loud in short sentences: she reported, she kept records, she signed in Diane's office, and then the blind items came, his. In narration she gives the reader more: the night two years ago at the writing desk when the first sentence arrived already in the second person. She doesn't describe what Holt did. Keep the original injury out of the room.
5. He doesn't beg, apologise or explain the blind items. His grammar comes together for the first time: "I would have done the same thing." He doesn't mean the NDA. He means the syllabus.
6. She doesn't know what to do with that, so she drinks. She tells the reader so: she has a procedure for every kind of statement a man can make in a room, and this one is not on it.
7. Now he is honest, and his sentences are complete. After the photograph he pulled the signatory list privately. It is his template: he drafted it, Century Schoolbook with the footer rule. "You think you're the only one." A pause, which is kind. "There are eleven names on that list." He doesn't read the names. He doesn't say which one has medical training, because he doesn't need to.
8. Her body before she does. Her left hand goes to the flank wound and presses it through her shirt. She knows this is her thinking gesture and it is happening without her. Ch 9's "We are the same kind of alone" comes back in one clause and fails. He seems about to say something more, decides not yet, and lets it go. (This seeds Ch 17's "There's one more thing." No hint of the woman or of death.)
9. [TURN] She had believed herself alone. She had built two years on being the only one. He watches it land and is kind about it. He gets up and turns his back so she can have her face to herself. He lifts the tone arm, sets the needle back at the start of side one, and turns it up a notch, so that neither of them has to speak for a while. That kindness, which he gives without being asked and which costs him nothing he will ever need again, is unbearable.

- **Sensory lead:** Taste (the rye: dry, rye-bread spice, late heat). Sound: two voices with no reverb, his close enough to be in her own head, and the record low under them. When the needle drops again there is the soft crackle of the lead-in groove and the music starting over. Texture: the heavy glass, cool and wet against her bare palm, the first bare skin of the night. Temperature: warm. The names bring heat to her face, not cold. (Cold belongs to Ch 18. Don't preempt it.)
- **Candidate jokes:**
  - "Two fingers, neat. He had either guessed or read it somewhere, and I had never written it down, so he had guessed, which was worse."
  - On "I would have done the same thing": "In fourteen hundred cases nobody has ever agreed with me. It isn't a thing the dead go in for."
  - Before the names: she tells the reader she had prepared for begging, bargaining, threats and a lawyer, "and I had a joke ready for each. I had not prepared a joke for being understood."
- **Body:** Right hand bare on the glass, left still gloved, an asymmetry the reader should register. The left hand on the wound. She is sitting on the chair arm and doesn't settle. Her mouth stays dry after the rye. Her face is hot.
- **Motifs:** Vinyl (the needle back to the start, by his hand). Hands (the bare right hand, the gloved left on the wound, his back turned while his hands work the turntable). The grade (his restraint graded, and her own procedure failing a test). The original NDA as paper: his template, the footer rule.

## Scene 3: The Preparation

1. She puts down her glass beside the photograph and puts her right glove back on. She stands. She clears the long edit desk: the keyboards, the jog wheel and the coiled cables go to the floor along the wall, the monitors are pushed back on their arms, dark. The cable grommets along the desk edge are where she noted them from the plans in Ch 15.
2. She unrolls the instrument roll on the low table, beside the photograph and the two glasses. Ch 15's tasting menu, served. He comes to look at it with his hands in his pockets, the way a man reads a wine list he can't afford. He asks no questions. She notes that he doesn't, and grades it.
3. "On the desk. On your back." He lets her. He sits on the edge of the desk, swings his legs up and lies down without being helped, and lays his arms out along the edges, palms up, waiting.
4. The binding, done with procedure and pleasure. She tapes each wrist palm-up to the desk edge, then zip-ties it through a cable grommet, and the ratchet clicks under the Mingus. Ankles the same at the far end. A band of industrial tape goes across his thighs, because she is thorough and not because he is going anywhere. She keeps the leather gloves on throughout, because tape adhesive takes prints, and she tells the reader that without making a lesson of it.
5. He tests each tie for her when she finishes it, a small courteous flex against the plastic, the way a man tries a door for a woman. His hands lie open on the wood. She looks at them longer than she needs to: the pianist's fingers and the flattened pads (save the detail of the pads for Ch 17's examination and note only that she looks).
6. Brandt, in full and level grammar: "Take your time. I would rather it was you than anyone else."
7. She tells the reader plainly that no one has ever said anything like that to her. Not a lover, not Hannah, not a single colleague in fourteen hundred cases, and not in any form. She is going to take him at his word.
8. She takes off the leather gloves one finger at a time and puts them in her bag. She tells the reader this is the last of what she came in wearing to come off, and that she won't need them again tonight. (There are nitrile gloves in the roll. Ch 17 puts them on.)
9. [TURN] She tells the reader, plainly and in hindsight, that it was going to be the best night of her life. She knew that much while she stood there. What she did not know was that the woman who had keyed in the code would not be the one who walked out. The reader understands both halves. She, in the room, has only the first.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound: the ratchet of the zip-ties, the tear of the industrial tape (loud in a deaf room), and the Mingus louder now, filling what the conversation left. Texture: tape adhesive dragging on leather, the hard plastic of the grommets, the smooth laminate of the desk. Smell: rye on both their breaths, and the cellar-green rising off him now that he is lying down and warm. Temperature: he is warm through the shirt where her knuckles brush his wrist. She has known he would be warm and doesn't touch him bare-handed yet (Ch 17's palm on him must stay the first).
- **Candidate jokes:**
  - The instrument roll: "He read it like a wine list he couldn't afford, which, in fairness, he couldn't."
  - The ties: "I have tied a lot of tags to a lot of toes. None of them had ever helped."
  - "Take your time": "It is what you say to a woman you are not in a hurry to be finished with. I had heard it said in films. I had assumed it was a special effect."
- **Body:** Gloved hands, then bare. Pulse high and unhurried, which she notes as the best combination. Arousal is present and named as a finding, briefly (Ch 17 reports it fully). Hunger is there, and she tells herself it's because she hasn't eaten. Warm. She doesn't touch the wound in this scene: her hands are busy, and she is not thinking, she is doing.
- **Motifs:** Tape (industrial and black vinyl in the roll, the tape on his wrists). Hands (his palms up and waiting, hers ungloving). Vinyl (side one playing out). The grade (his not asking questions, graded). The meal after, by implication: the tasting menu is laid, and the meal itself is Ch 17.
