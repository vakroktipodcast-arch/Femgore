## Scene 1: The Ambush

1. First line, inside the task. Friday night, a little after ten. She is on her way out of Brandt's server farm, the egress half of a job. She keyed 2-5-8-0 into the panel on the way in, as before; she has swapped the tap's card (first full card out, fresh one in, tap stays seated on the uplink), and the first full card is in her inner pocket. The side door to the loading bay is twelve metres ahead. She chose Friday because Brandt is at a public donor dinner, being seen by a room of witnesses. She says so, pleased with herself, in one sentence.
2. The hall as she leaves it. Racks in rows, the roar of the cooling, cold-aisle air pouring up through the floor grilles. The motion-sensor lights are off because she has kept to the route she mapped in Ch 11. She grades her own exit as she walks it: route clean, time under budget.
3. The loading bay. A van is backed up to the roller door with its side door open, which wasn't there on the way in. She clocks it before she clocks the men, the way you smell a room before you look at it. Cigarette smoke, recent and stubbed out. A menthol lozenge. Somebody's cheap cologne over a long wait.
4. Two men come out of the dark at either side of the door. They are not police, because police announce themselves, and these two are trying not to make a sound. The heavier, older one has a beard and stands as if he has done this before (Contractor A). The younger one is quick, with a lock-knife clipped to his belt and his hand nowhere near it (Contractor B).
5. She reads their kit like a tray laid out for a procedure. Zip-ties looped at each man's belt. A black cloth hood in B's left hand. No firearm drawn or printing through a jacket. The van door open. Nobody brings a hood to a killing. The brief is on the tray: take her, don't damage her. Brandt needs to know what she has and whom she's told. Wall three (Ch 9), worn by two strangers.
6. The small hurt, from Ch 9's carry: she had believed he would come himself. He has sent hands and stayed at his dinner table. She reports it in one line, dry, the way you'd report being stood up. Then the inference, marked as hers and unconfirmed: the rectangle she left squared on the operator's desk told him she would come back, when she meant it to tell him only that she had been; so two men have been parked nearby since morning, and when her cloned card turned the reader green it rang their phone. Her not rising to Tessa (Ch 10) is why he escalated: he has stopped fishing and sent men to wade in.
7. She does the arithmetic of the room. They can't afford to hurt her badly, and she can afford anything. This is the first sentence of the chapter's pleasure: an asymmetry she didn't plan for and likes very much. She doesn't call it pleasure yet. She calls it an advantage.
8. A puts his hands up, palms out, the gesture men use on horses. He takes a step towards her. "Easy," he says.
9. [TURN] Then he says her name. Not "Dr. Vale", which is how the paperwork, the wire stories and every stranger who has ever been briefed about her would say it. "Iris." It is the name a man uses when someone has briefed him with it on purpose. Brandt's choice is in a stranger's mouth, in the dark, across town from his donor dinner.

- **Sensory lead:** sound first, then temperature. The server hall's roar is a dense wall of fans and air handling, louder than the morgue's extraction and with the same function: keeping something cold that would otherwise spoil. Cold-aisle air comes up through the floor grilles into her trouser legs, against the early-October warmth she walked in through. Smell: ozone and warm dust from the hot aisles (hairdryer), then in the bay cigarette, menthol and cologne-over-waiting. Light: the standby LEDs on the racks, jade and amber, and in the bay the sodium of the yard lamp through the open roller door, weak-tea coloured on the concrete.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**

  - "He was at a donor dinner, being photographed with a fork in his hand. I had chosen Friday for exactly that reason. It is very annoying to find you have the same diary as the man you are planning to kill."
  - "I felt it the way you feel being stood up, briefly and low in the stomach, and then as information."
- **Body:** Hands gloved in nitrile and steady. The kit scalpel (No. 4 handle, No. 22 blade) sits sheathed in the inner sleeve pocket of her jacket, against her left forearm. She does not touch it yet. She notes that she is not touching it. Pulse up, warm in the face, and she reports it as finding rather than fear. No hunger yet.

## Scene 2: The Violence

1. Fast. B comes in from her left with the hood. A moves round behind her, quick for his weight, and takes her in a bear hug that pins her upper arms. He lifts her off her heels; his breath is menthol. Short declaratives from here. The long sentences are kept for her body.
2. Anatomy as tactics. She lets her whole weight go dead, and when her heels come back down she drives her right heel into the dorsum of his foot, onto the first metatarsal. Then she slams the back of her skull into his face and feels the nasal bones go. A's arms open. She is not a fighter. She has never been in a fight. She is someone who knows where everything is.
3. The scalpel is in her right hand before she decides it should be. B is inside her reach with the hood still in his left fist, and he has done the thing his brief told him not to: the lock-knife is open in his right.
4. He puts it into her left flank as she turns, and he pulls the thrust, because he has been told not to kill her, and she knew he would. She narrates the wound in the moment as she will later write it up. Skin. Subcutaneous fat. External oblique, its fibres running down and forward like hands into pockets, cut across the grain. Internal oblique under it, fibres the other way. Then the bite of transversus abdominis, and the blade stops. She guesses which layer it stopped in (at home she finds she was right: below the tip of the twelfth rib, forward of the kidney, a layer short of the colon). *I'll live,* she thinks, in the dictation voice, and is delighted.
5. She cuts across the volar surface of B's right wrist: superficial and deep flexor tendons and the median nerve. His hand forgets how to hold anything. The knife drops onto the concrete with a ring. She stops the blade short of both the radial and ulnar arteries, deliberately, and tells the reader so. That is the last planned cut of the night.
6. The lights. The fight has moved onto the warehouse floor between the first racks, and the motion sensors find them. The hall floods with cold white LED light, one row after another, stuttering on with a clack. Now it is a room she knows: tiled-white light, fans, cold air, a body on the floor. She says it outright. The whole time, it felt like the morgue with the lights on. It felt like work, and she has always loved work, and it was the most fun she has had in three years. She says that too, and doesn't apologise.
7. A comes back with his face streaming and the brief gone out of him, angry now rather than careful. They go down together on the painted concrete. Her flank tears as she lands (filed under *later*). He is heavier and she is better informed. She lets his weight carry them over and comes up on top, with her knee in the angle under his ribs and the scalpel in her right hand.
8. The moment she could stop. B is against a rack upright holding his wrist and is out of it. A is pinned and gasping through a broken nose. She has won, and she reports that she knows it: the fight is over. One clear beat of stillness on the page ("I held it for one breath").
9. She opens the side of A's neck anyway. Anterior triangle, at the front edge of sternocleidomastoid: the common carotid and the jugular in one deep stroke. It is fast, ugly and loud with the fans. The spray is warm across her face and down her front [prose lingers here; see Conflicts]. He is the man who said her name. She steps back off him and takes both hands away. She counts: unconscious inside fifteen seconds, gone in about three minutes. She enjoys the counting. She does not touch him, does not kneel, does not examine him, and writes no time of death.
10. [TURN] One contractor is dead on the warehouse floor, on his back between the first racks, blood fanned across the white concrete to the nearest grille. It wasn't planned. There is no page for him and no circled role. He is the first thing she has done that isn't on the syllabus, and the first thing she has ever done for no reason except that she could.

- **Sensory lead:** smell first, as always with her. Her own sweat, sharp at the collar under the cold air. A's nosebleed, iron, and then the arterial iron arriving all at once and heavier. Burnt dust from the hot aisle. Sound: the fans under everything, louder than any of them; the lock-knife ringing on concrete; the lights clacking on row after row; A's breathing wet through the broken nose and then gone. Temperature: cold-aisle air on her wet face against the heat of his blood on her skin, which she registers as heat and does not dwell on (Ch 17 owns the thirty-seven degrees). Texture: the concrete's grit under her knee, and the scalpel's handle slick inside the nitrile.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "A knife in the left flank below the twelfth rib has four things to worry about: the kidney, the colon, the spleen, and your opinion of yourself. He got none of them."
  - "I have read fourteen hundred wounds after the fact. I had never had one read to me as it happened, by the nerve endings, in the first person."
  - On B's wrist: "I took the tendons and left him the arteries. It is the most considerate thing I have ever done for a man who had just stabbed me, and I would like it noted."
- **Body:** The wound is the scene's body. It is precise in the narration and almost pleasurable as information. Her pulse is high, she is hot, her mouth is dry. Hands: the scalpel steady; the knee and (per prose) the left hand flat on A's chest over the shirt for leverage, never inside him; both hands off him when the stroke is made. She bleeds into her waistband. No eating, but she notes as a finding that she is going to be very hungry.
- **Motifs:** Hands (the scalpel; B's hand that can no longer close; both arteries spared). The grade, by absence: she grades every move of the fight, and then at the Turn there is no grade. Tape and kit: none of hers is used; their zip-ties go on B in S3's opening beat. Cold and warm inverted: the room is cold and she is hot in it.

## Scene 3: Self-Repair

1. The exit, in short beats before she reaches home. The card is still in her inside pocket; she checks it before she checks herself. She zip-ties B to a rack upright with his own ties, one round his good wrist and one round the post. He is alive, bleeding brisk, venous and survivable, and he will be there for Brandt to collect. She takes A's wallet from his back pocket with two gloved fingers, the only time she touches him after, and takes out the driving licence without reading it. She folds the hood they brought for her into a pad and presses it under her jacket against her flank. Her blood is on the floor. Her DNA is on the office's elimination register (Ch 8), and she reasons it through: this floor belongs to a man who cannot let anything have happened on it, in a building three companies deep with Diane Pell as officer of record. He will clean it better than she could. She gives the job to the Architect, keys 2-5-8-0 out, and walks the two streets to her car. Before she touches the wheel she does face, neck, hairline and wrists by feel with the car's isopropyl wipes and bags the wipes with her gloves. It is not enough and she knows it. She drives home at the limit with her jacket zipped to the chin.
2. Home around eleven. She doesn't turn on the overhead light. In the bathroom she brings in the desk lamp on its long flex and sits on the edge of the bath. She cuts off the shirt with kitchen scissors. No mirror (see Handling notes). She looks down and twists her torso into the lamp.
3. She examines her own wound as a case, in the dictation voice. A single-edged blade, the posterior end squared where the spine of the blade sat and the anterior end acute. 3.2 cm at the skin, longer than the blade is wide, because the skin was pulling the other way. A track of about four centimetres running upward and medially. She reads the absences one by one: no guarding, no rebound, no shoulder-tip pain, urine clear. The peritoneum is intact and the kidney untouched. Blood loss is perhaps three hundred millilitres, "a generous donation", most of it in the hood meant for her head.
4. Mara's voice in her head, from the lecture they gave together last spring to detective trainees at the police academy on wound geometry. Mara at the projector says: *The skin tells you about the blade. The muscle tells you about the hand.* And later, about closure: *Close it along the lines it wants to keep.* Iris taught her that and is now hearing it back. She does not grade Mara or the line. Keep the gap visible.
5. Irrigation. Normal saline made in the kitchen: nine grams of salt to a litre of boiled water, cooled. She irrigates the track until it runs clear into the bath. The suture kit she has kept since residency is in the drawer under the sink, not the mirrored cabinet. In it: a needle driver, Adson toothed forceps, suture scissors, 3-0 braided absorbable and 4-0 nylon on a reverse-cutting needle. There is no local anaesthetic.
6. The stitching, anatomically exact and in a long, greedy sentence. Three buried interrupted absorbable sutures through the external oblique and its fascia, knots turned down so the deep layer holds when she bends. Then seven simple interrupted nylon sutures in the skin, edges everted, instrument ties, square knots, tails left long enough to find again on the tenth day. The pain is reported precisely as a finding, and the pleasure of doing the thing properly sits right beside it in the same sentence. Her hands are steady. This is the needle in 12 (style 05). She puts the needle down on the sink edge (the Snapper places it; here just "I put the needle down"). A dressing goes on, taped with the same micropore she uses on specimen labels at work.
7. The meal after, about 12:15 a.m., seated at the kitchen counter, left arm held against her side. She makes steak tartare from a fillet tail bought for something else, chopping it by hand with a single-edged knife, and notes, once and without comment, what she has just been stabbed with. Shallot, capers, cornichons, Dijon, Worcestershire, a yolk broken over the top, on toasted sourdough. She eats all of it raw on the night of the first death she never planned. The physiology excuse runs one clause long: haemorrhage, iron, replacement. The reader believes it less than they did in Ch 7.
8. She is not tired. She dresses carefully over the dressing in dark, high-waisted clothes and goes out at about 12:40. A late bar in a business hotel by the river. A man, name not asked, and then his flat, which smells of someone else's laundry powder. She keeps her shirt on. Two or three sentences at most, physical and unsentimental (prose: sitting up, fast, and she feels every one of the seven stitches), and the scene's point is elsewhere. She checks his hands. The fingertips are soft, and nobody ever made him practise anything. She notes it and does not say what she was checking for. He has a drawer of spare toothbrushes still in their cellophane. She uses one and takes it home in a tissue, because it has her DNA on it. Home at four.
9. Sleep from about ten past four until twenty to seven: two and a half hours, deep and dreamless. She wakes rested and reports it as a finding. She dresses for the Saturday rota, and the morning is the point: badge, keys, a wound under her shirt, a day of cases ahead.
10. [TURN] At the bathroom sink, brushing her teeth, eyes down on the foam, she reaches for the grade for last night's man and finds she hasn't given one. She doesn't know how. He had no page, no subject and no function, and she can't think what he was being examined on. Her fingertips have gone to the dressing and she didn't send them. And she slept better than she has in years. The facts sit side by side, and she connects none of them.

- **Sensory lead:** texture and taste. The pull of nylon through her own dermis, the drag of the needle and the bite of the forceps' teeth in her own skin edge. The tartare: cold, mineral and soft, the yolk coating it, sharp with capers and cornichon, with the crunch of the toast against it. Smell: dish soap and blood in the bathroom, the saline steam, and later the stranger's flat (someone else's laundry powder). Temperature: the flat warm, her skin hot around the wound, the tile of the bath edge cool under her thighs. Light: the desk lamp brought into the bathroom, then the dawn.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Lidocaine lives in a cabinet at work. The dead never ask for it, and it had never occurred to anyone to issue me any for the living."
  - "Nine grams of salt to a litre is normal saline. It is about a third the strength of what I brine a chicken in, which tells you what I think of chickens." (Ledger brine is 3%, so the ratio is about a third. This is her habitual brine, not the Ch 15 chicken, which is not bought until Mon 12 Oct; the line must not imply a chicken is in brine now.)
  - "He had a drawer of spare toothbrushes still in their cellophane, which told me more about him than anything he said, and he said a great deal."
  - At the Turn: "I have graded everyone I have met since August, including a donor's dog. I stood at the sink with a mouthful of foam and couldn't think what he'd been examined on."
- **Body:** All of it, in sequence. The wound: examined, irrigated and closed with her own hands, then dressed. Eating: seated, raw, all of it. Sex: brief and with her shirt on. Sleep: short and the best in years. Hands: the needle driver; the knife at the chopping board; the stranger's hands, checked; and from now on, her fingertips going to the dressing when she thinks. The first unthinking touch of the wound is at the Turn, while she is trying to grade him. (Ch 17 later has her press it with the inside of her forearm.)
- **Motifs:** The meal after (Ch 12's instance: raw, seated, after a death with no page). Hands (the needle in 12, per style 05). Tape (micropore on the dressing, the morgue's tape on her own body). The grade, which fails for the first time in the book. Squaring (the needle, parallel to the tap, left out overnight: a tell in a woman who cleans up). Voicemail: none. Do not add one.
