- **Date:** Fri 2 Oct, ambush about 10:05 p.m.; S3 runs into the small hours of Sat 3 Oct, when she works the Saturday rota.
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Three scenes of ~1,050 each. S2 can run to ~1,150 at S3's expense. No syllabus insert at the chapter head.
- **Act pressure:** Act II, The Practicum. The pressure is Brandt, until Ch 17. Ch 9 had him in her flat and Ch 10 had him fishing with Tessa. Ch 11 had her inside his machinery and leaving him a black rectangle. Here he answers with hands. He has to be felt in every scene. S1: his men, his brief, and his Friday (he is at a public donor dinner, being seen). S2: his brief is the rule she breaks, on his floor. S3: the cleanup is his problem now, and in the stranger's soft fingertips she checks for something she doesn't name. The fifth pressure, appetite, is the chapter's engine. The reader should be frightened by how much fun she is having before she says she is having any.
- **Appetite at entry:** Act II, appetite as identity, named and indulged. In Ch 10 she said she enjoys being hunted by someone good and ate a whole lunch after deleting Tessa's voicemail. In Ch 11 she enjoyed the break-in and said so, and laughed out loud in a parked car. She is warm (Act II). She has never fought anyone. Her pleasure so far has been in planning, reading and being read.
- **Appetite at exit:** Indulged in the body three ways in one night: violence, food, sex. For the first time it acts outside the curriculum: a death with no page, no subject and no grade. She tries to grade the dead man and can't. The syllabus has failed to contain the appetite, and she slept better than she has in years. She reports both facts side by side and connects neither. It is not yet *seen* (that is Ch 18). The wound is now permanent. From here she touches it when she is thinking.
- **Body temperature:** Act II warm all chapter. The server hall's cold aisle is the coldest room in the chapter, and she is hot inside it. That inversion is the S2 sensory spine. No Act I cold anywhere, not even after the kill.

## Scene 1: The Ambush

1. First line, inside the task. Friday night, a little after ten. She is on her way out of Brandt's server farm, the egress half of a job she has done once before. She has swapped the tap's card, and the first full card is in her inner pocket; the side door to the loading bay is twelve metres ahead. She chose Friday because Brandt is at a public donor dinner, being seen by a room of witnesses. She says so, pleased with herself, in one sentence.
2. The hall as she leaves it. Racks in rows, the roar of the cooling, cold-aisle air pouring up through the floor grilles. The motion-sensor lights are off because she has kept to the route she mapped in Ch 11. She grades her own exit as she walks it: route clean, time under budget.
3. The loading bay. A van is backed up to the roller door with its side door open, which wasn't there on the way in. She clocks it before she clocks the men, the way you smell a room before you look at it. Cigarette smoke, recent and stubbed out. A menthol lozenge. Somebody's cheap cologne over a long wait.
4. Two men come out of the dark at either side of the door. They are not police, because police announce themselves, and these two are trying not to make a sound. The heavier, older one has a beard and stands as if he has done this before (Contractor A). The younger one is quick, with a lock-knife clipped to his belt and his hand nowhere near it (Contractor B).
5. She reads their kit like a tray laid out for a procedure. Zip-ties looped at A's belt. A black cloth hood in B's hand. No firearm drawn or printing through a jacket. The van door open. Nobody brings a hood to a killing. The brief is on the tray: take her, don't damage her. Brandt needs to know what she has and whom she's told. Wall three (Ch 9), worn by two strangers.
6. The small hurt, from Ch 9's carry: she had believed he would come himself. He has sent hands and stayed at his dinner table. She reports it in one line, dry, the way you'd report being stood up. Then the second line: her not rising to the Tessa bait (Ch 10) is why he escalated. He has stopped fishing and sent men to wade in.
7. She does the arithmetic of the room. They can't afford to hurt her badly, and she can afford anything. This is the first sentence of the chapter's pleasure: an asymmetry she didn't plan for and likes very much. She doesn't call it pleasure yet. She calls it an advantage.
8. A puts his hands up, palms out, the gesture men use on horses. He takes a step towards her. "Easy," he says.
9. [TURN] Then he says her name. Not "Dr. Vale", which is how the paperwork, the wire stories and every stranger who has ever been briefed about her would say it. "Iris." It is the name a man uses when someone has briefed him with it on purpose. Brandt's choice is in a stranger's mouth, in the dark, across town from his donor dinner.

- **Sensory lead:** sound first, then temperature. The server hall's roar is a dense wall of fans and air handling, louder than the morgue's extraction and with the same function: keeping something cold that would otherwise spoil. Cold-aisle air comes up through the floor grilles into her trouser legs, against the early-October warmth she walked in through. Smell: ozone and warm dust from the hot aisles, then in the bay cigarette, menthol and cologne-over-waiting. Light: the standby LEDs on the racks, green and amber, and in the bay the sodium of the yard lamp through the open roller door.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Nobody brings a hood to a killing. The dead are never in any danger of seeing where they're going."
  - "I could tell from the way they stood that someone had told them I was small. I am. It is the single most useful fact about me, and I have never once corrected anyone."
  - "He was at a donor dinner, being photographed with a fork in his hand. I had chosen Friday for exactly that reason. It is very annoying to find you have the same diary as the man you are planning to kill."
- **Body:** Hands gloved in nitrile and steady. The kit scalpel (No. 4 handle, No. 22 blade) sits sheathed in the inner sleeve pocket of her jacket. She does not touch it yet. She notes that she is not touching it. Pulse up, warm in the face, and she reports it as finding rather than fear. No hunger yet.
- **Motifs:** The grade (her own egress, and Brandt's choice of a public Friday, graded and passed). Tape and kit, inverted: zip-ties on *their* belts, the kit turned around and pointed at her. Her black-vinyl rectangle from Ch 11 as the tell that brought them, the syllabus teaching back and the student learning. The drafter can give it one clause.

## Scene 2: The Violence

1. Fast. B comes in from her left with the hood. A moves round behind her, quick for his weight, and takes her in a bear hug that pins her upper arms. He lifts her off her heels. Short declaratives from here. The long sentences are kept for her body.
2. Anatomy as tactics. She drops her full weight and drives her heel down into the dorsum of his foot, onto the first metatarsal. Then she slams the back of her skull into his face and feels the nasal bones go. A's arms open. She is not a fighter. She is someone who knows where everything is.
3. The scalpel is in her hand before she decides it should be. B is inside her reach with the hood still in one hand, and he has done the thing his brief told him not to: the lock-knife is open in the other.
4. He puts it into her left flank as she turns, and he pulls the thrust, because he has been told not to kill her. She narrates the wound in the moment as she will later write it up. Skin. Subcutaneous fat. External oblique, its fibres running down and forward like hands into pockets, cut across the grain. Internal oblique under it, fibres the other way. Then the bite of transversus abdominis, and the blade stops. She can feel which layer it stopped in. Below the twelfth rib, lateral to the kidney, above the colon. *I'll live,* she thinks, in the dictation voice, and is delighted.
5. She cuts across the volar surface of B's right wrist: the superficial and deep flexor tendons and the median nerve. His hand forgets how to hold anything. The knife drops onto the concrete with a ring. She misses the radial artery deliberately and tells the reader so. That is the last planned cut of the night.
6. The lights. The fight has moved onto the warehouse floor between the first racks, and the motion sensors find them. The hall floods with cold white LED light, one row after another, stuttering on. Now it is a room she knows: tiled-white light, fans, cold air, a body on the floor. She says it outright. The whole time, it felt like the morgue with the lights on. It felt like work, and she has always loved work, and it was the best fun she has had in three years. She says that too, and doesn't apologise.
7. A comes back with his face streaming and the brief gone out of him, angry now rather than careful. They go down together on the concrete. Her flank tears as she lands. He is heavier and she is better informed. She gets his weight working for her and comes up on top, with her knee in the angle under his ribs and the scalpel in her right hand.
8. The moment she could stop. B is against a rack upright holding his wrist and is out of it. A is pinned and gasping through a broken nose. She has won, and she reports that she knows it: the fight is over. Count one clear beat of stillness on the page.
9. She opens the side of A's neck anyway. Anterior triangle, medial to sternocleidomastoid. The internal jugular and the common carotid in one deep stroke. It is fast, ugly and loud with the fans. The spray is warm across her face and down her front. He is the man who said her name. She watches from a step back and does not touch him. She counts. He takes under a minute.
10. [TURN] One contractor is dead on the warehouse floor. It wasn't planned. There is no page for him and no circled role. He is the first thing she has done that isn't on the syllabus, and the first thing she has ever done for no reason except that she could.

- **Sensory lead:** smell first, as always with her. Her own sweat. A's nosebleed, iron, and then the arterial iron arriving all at once and heavier. Burnt dust from the hot aisle. Sound: the fans under everything, louder than any of them; the lock-knife ringing on concrete; the lights clacking on row after row; A's breathing wet through the broken nose and then gone. Temperature: cold-aisle air on her wet face against the heat of his blood on her skin, which she registers as heat and does not dwell on (ch17 owns the thirty-seven degrees). Texture: the concrete's grit under her knee, and the scalpel's knurled handle slick inside the nitrile.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "A knife in the left flank below the twelfth rib has four things to worry about: the kidney, the colon, the spleen, and your opinion of yourself. He got none of them."
  - "I have read fourteen hundred wounds after the fact. I had never had one read to me as it happened, by the nerve endings, in the first person."
  - On B's wrist: "I took the tendons and left him the artery. It is the most considerate thing I have ever done for a man who had just stabbed me, and I would like it noted."
- **Body:** The wound is the scene's body. It is precise in the narration and almost pleasurable as information. Her pulse is high, she is hot, her mouth is dry. Hands: the scalpel steady, the left hand flat on A's chest for leverage (over the shirt, never inside him), then both hands off him when he dies. She bleeds into her waistband. No eating, but she notes as a finding that she is going to be very hungry.
- **Motifs:** Hands (the scalpel; B's hand that can no longer close; the radial artery spared). The grade, by absence: she grades every move of the fight, and then at the Turn there is no grade. Tape and kit: none of hers is used. Their zip-ties go on B in S3's opening beats, see below. Cold and warm inverted: the room is cold and she is hot in it.

## Scene 3: Self-Repair

1. The exit, in three short beats before she reaches home. She zip-ties B to a rack upright with his own ties. He is alive, bleeding modestly, and he will be there for Brandt to collect. She takes A's wallet and, in it, his driving licence. She takes the hood they brought for her, folds it, and presses it against her flank as a dressing. Her blood is on the floor. Her DNA is on the office's elimination register (Ch 8), and she reasons it through out loud. This floor belongs to a man who cannot let anything have happened on it, with Diane Pell as officer of record. He will clean it better than she could. She gives the job to the Architect.
2. Home around eleven. She doesn't turn on the overhead light. In the bathroom she brings in the desk lamp and sits on the edge of the bath. She cuts off the shirt with kitchen scissors. No mirror (see Handling notes). She looks down and twists her torso into the lamp.
3. She examines her own wound as a case, in the dictation voice. A single-edged blade, the posterior end squared where the spine of the blade sat and the anterior end acute. 3.2 cm at the skin, longer than the blade is wide, because the skin was pulling the other way. A track of about four centimetres running upward and medially. She reads the absence of signs one by one: no guarding, no rebound, no shoulder-tip pain, urine clear. The peritoneum is intact and the kidney untouched. Blood loss is perhaps three hundred millilitres, "a generous donation".
4. Mara's voice in her head, from the lecture they gave together last spring to detective trainees at the police academy on wound geometry. Mara at the projector says: *The skin tells you about the blade. The muscle tells you about the hand.* And later, about closure: *Close it along the lines it wants to keep.* Iris taught her that and is now hearing it back. She does not grade Mara or the line. Keep the gap visible.
5. Irrigation. Normal saline made in the kitchen: nine grams of salt to a litre of boiled water, cooled. The suture kit she has kept since residency is in the drawer under the sink, not the mirrored cabinet. In it: a needle driver, Adson toothed forceps, suture scissors, 3-0 braided absorbable and 4-0 nylon on a reverse-cutting needle. There is no local anaesthetic.
6. The stitching, anatomically exact and in a long, greedy sentence. Three buried interrupted absorbable sutures close the external oblique aponeurosis. Then seven simple interrupted nylon sutures in the skin, edges everted, instrument ties, square knots, each cut to three millimetres. The pain is reported precisely as a finding, and the pleasure of doing the thing properly sits right beside it in the same sentence. Her hands are steady. This is the needle in 12 (style 05). She puts the needle down on the sink edge (the Snapper places it; here just "I put the needle down"). A dressing goes on, taped with the same micropore she uses on specimen labels at work.
7. The meal after, about 12:15 a.m., seated at the kitchen counter, left arm held against her side. She makes steak tartare from a fillet tail bought for something else, chopping it by hand with a knife. She notes, once and without comment, what she has just been stabbed with. Egg yolk, shallot, capers, cornichons, Dijon and Worcestershire, on toasted sourdough. She eats all of it raw on the night of the first death she never planned. The physiology excuse runs one clause long: haemorrhage, iron, replacement. The reader believes it less than they did in Ch 7.
8. She is not tired. She dresses carefully over the dressing in a dark, high-waisted outfit and goes out about 12:40. A late bar in a business hotel by the river. A man, name not asked, and then his flat. She keeps her shirt on. Two or three sentences at most, physical and unsentimental, and the scene's point is elsewhere. She checks his hands. The fingertips are soft, and nobody ever made him practise anything. She notes it and does not say what she was checking for. He has a drawer of spare toothbrushes still in their cellophane. She uses one and takes it home in a tissue, because it has her DNA on it. Home at four.
9. Sleep from about ten past four until twenty to seven: two and a half hours, deep and dreamless. She wakes rested and reports it as a finding. She dresses for the Saturday rota, and the morning is the point: badge, keys, a wound under her shirt, a day of cases ahead.
10. [TURN] At the bathroom sink, brushing her teeth, she reaches for the grade for last night's man and finds she hasn't given one. She doesn't know how. He had no page, no subject and no function, and she can't think what he was being examined on. And she slept better than she has in years. The two facts sit side by side, and she connects neither.

- **Sensory lead:** texture and taste. The pull of nylon through her own dermis, the drag of the needle and the bite of the forceps' teeth in her own skin edge. The tartare: cold, mineral and soft, the yolk coating it, sharp with capers and cornichon, with the crunch of the toast against it. Smell: dish soap and blood in the bathroom, the saline steam, and later the stranger's flat (someone else's laundry powder). Temperature: the flat warm, her skin hot around the wound, the tile of the bath edge cool under her thighs. Light: the desk lamp brought into the bathroom, then the dawn.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Lidocaine lives in a cabinet at work. The dead never ask for it, and it had never occurred to anyone to issue me any for the living."
  - "Nine grams of salt to a litre is normal saline. It is about a third the strength of what I brine a chicken in, which tells you what I think of chickens." (Ledger brine is 3%, so the ratio is about a third. This is her habitual brine, not the Ch 15 chicken, which is not bought until Mon 12 Oct; the line must not imply a chicken is in brine now.)
  - "He had a drawer of spare toothbrushes still in their cellophane, which told me more about him than anything he said, and he said a great deal."
  - At the Turn: "I had graded everyone I had met since August, including a donor's dog. I stood at the sink with a mouthful of foam and couldn't think what he'd been examined on."
- **Body:** All of it, in sequence. The wound: examined, irrigated and closed with her own hands, then dressed. Eating: seated, raw, all of it. Sex: brief and with her shirt on. Sleep: short and the best in years. Hands: the needle driver; the knife at the chopping board; the stranger's hands, checked; and from now on, her fingertips going to the dressing when she thinks. The drafter should place her first unthinking touch of the wound in beat 10, at the moment she is trying to grade him.
- **Motifs:** The meal after (Ch 12's instance: raw, seated, after a death with no page). Hands (the needle in 12, per style 05). Tape (micropore on the dressing, the morgue's tape on her own body). The grade, which fails for the first time in the book. Voicemail: none. Do not add one.
