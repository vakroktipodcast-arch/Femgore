# Beat sheet: interlude-1-diane

- **Unit:** Interlude 1, Diane (after Chapter 3, before Chapter 4). Manuscript file `manuscript/interlude-1-diane.md`. Heading in manuscript: `# Interlude — Diane`.
- **POV:** Diane Pell, close third, past tense.
- **Target words:** 600–900 (aim ~800). One scene, one Turn.
- **Act pressure:** Act I, The Prerequisite. The pressure is the campaign's certainty that nothing is coming, and Diane is that certainty in a dressing gown. She is tired because she is managing everything, and she is calm because everything is managed. Nothing in the house is locked against anything. The fifth pressure, appetite, belongs to Iris and is off the page. The reader brings it in with them from ch03's rug.
- **Iris's appetite at entry:** Act I, appetite as competence, present and unnamed. At the end of ch03 she closed the rug's "airway" in under four seconds and found herself "breathing hard and happy". She has the fitness-app routes and the intersection point (ch03 S2) and wrote down what she ate in the same hand.
- **Iris's appetite at exit:** Unchanged, because Iris is not here and the interlude must not leak her. What changes is the reader. They have now met the person Iris's appetite is aimed at, in her kitchen, being a mother, and from here on they cannot watch the reservoir in ch04 without this phone call in their ear. "The reader is given exactly what Iris will end."
- **Register reminder (interlude rules):** plainer and cooler than Iris. Layman's words for bodies. No Latin, no direct address, no appetite, no jokes in Iris's style. Diane's own dryness is managerial and tired. She is funny the way a chief of staff is funny at 10 p.m., briefly and to end a subject. Her stutter is a single trochaic hesitation, never phonetic (style/07), used once only.

## Conflicts

None. The outline agrees with the ledger: Diane is 52, Olivia is at Wellesley, there is a Sunday reservoir walk to be unreachable, and the mother call is Sundays at 4 p.m. Constraints these beats keep to:
- The walk's details as the ledger fixes them (the bench, eleven minutes of reading) are Iris's measurements, confirmed in ch04. Diane may describe her walk only loosely and slightly wrong (she says "ten minutes, maybe"), so the reader feels how much more exactly Iris knows it than she does.
- Diane must not say or think anything about "the old files". Holt asks her that in ch04 S2, and her "yes" has to land there first.
- Diane must not sense Iris. No noise outside, no sense of being watched, no premonition. The house is safe and the dramatic irony does all the work.
- Olivia is "home from college" on the second Sunday in ch04 S1. The beats seed that visit and do not fix its date.
- In ch05 S1 a planted phone rings on the path and Diane answers it "Olivia?". In ch05 S2 Diane's own phone is in her coat and gets a text from Olivia. So Diane carries her phone on the walk. The beats make Olivia the one exception to "unreachable", which explains both ch05 moments without contradicting the ledger.

## Scene 1: The kitchen, Wednesday night

1. 10:05 p.m. Diane comes into her own kitchen and doesn't turn on the overhead light. She uses the light over the range. Shoes off in the hall, one, then the other, with the toe of the opposite foot. The tile is cool through her tights. Her phone has 61 unread messages, and she turns it face-down on the counter. She has been "on" since 6 a.m. Eight weeks and six days to go, and she knows the number without counting.
2. Dinner, standing at the counter. A heel of cheddar, the last of a sleeve of water crackers, half a glass of white wine from a bottle in the fridge door. Everything in her fridge is labelled with a strip of masking tape and a date in black marker, in her hand. The cheddar's says Thursday last week. She cuts round the dry edge and eats what's under it. *(The reader may notice she eats standing, as Iris does when planning in ch03 S1. Do not point at it.)*
3. She calls Olivia. Not a text: a call, because a text can be managed and a voice can't, and she would like for once not to be managing. Olivia answers on the fourth ring, whispering, then not whispering. She has gone out into the corridor of her residence hall because the roommate is asleep. "With the window open," Olivia says. "In September. Like a Viking."
4. The roommate dispute, quick and real. The roommate, Kenzie, sleeps with the window wide open and a fan on, Olivia lies awake cold under one blanket, and two nights ago Kenzie "borrowed" Olivia's good sweater to sleep in. Diane listens the way she listens to staff, with one ear on content and one on what the speaker wants. She sits down for the first time, on the step stool by the pantry, and rubs the ball of one foot with her thumb.
5. Diane gives advice, and the reader should see Lesson One in miniature without anyone naming it. Buy a second blanket and she'll pay. Don't make it a fight. Write it down: what you want, what she wants, a schedule for the window. Take it to the RA if you have to, and get it agreed. Olivia: "Mom. I don't want a process. I want her to close the window." Diane: "The process is how she closes the window." She believes it completely. It is kind, it is good advice, and it is the same sentence she would say at work.
6. The stutter, once. Olivia asks, more gently, how *she* is, and whether she's sleeping. Diane starts to say "Fine" and doesn't. There is one hesitation before the word, the old catch, a beat where the word won't come. Then it comes: "Tired." Olivia waits through the pause without filling it, the way she has since she was small. Staff mistake that pause for thoughtfulness. Olivia never has. *(Iris will hear this hesitation in ch06 S2 and never hear the word after it. The reader hears it here first, with a word on the other side.)*
7. Olivia, teasing, asks whether she's still doing "the walk", and whether she still wears the watch. Olivia gave Diane the watch three Christmases ago and set it up on her phone herself. Diane looks at her wrist, where it is, and says every Sunday. Seven o'clock, the reservoir loop, a bench halfway round, "ten minutes, maybe," with whatever she is reading, then home. Mother at four.
8. **[TURN]** Olivia asks why she doesn't just sleep in. Diane says, plainly and for the first time in the scene without managing anything: "Because it's the one hour of my life I'm not reachable." Then she tightens it, because she edits everything: "To not be reachable for one hour of my life. That's all I want. One." The reader holds the gap she is describing and knows it is no longer a gap. Three years of it sit on a public profile Olivia set up and Diane never set private, and a woman with a rolled rug has already found it (ch03 S2).
9. The exception, so ch05 can work. Olivia says, "You answer when I call, though." Diane says, "You're the list." Olivia says, "There's a list?" Diane says, "There's you." The phone is on Do Not Disturb on the walk, and Olivia's number is the only one that rings through. Then Olivia threatens to come home some weekend soon and walk it with her. Diane says it's not a walk if you talk. Olivia says she'll be quiet. Diane says she has never once been quiet, and they both laugh, and Diane says go to bed, and close the window, and Olivia says *you* close the window, and they hang up.
10. Diane rinses the glass and leaves it upside-down on the drainer. She takes the watch off and puts it on its charger at the end of the counter for Sunday. Then the Snapper.

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first: the call. The small acoustic of a dorm corridor coming through the phone (a fire door's hiss, someone's music two rooms away), Olivia's whisper rising to ordinary voice. Under it the house: the fridge, the dishwasher's last cycle ticking, nothing else. Temperature: cool tile through tights, the cold of Olivia's room reported second-hand, the fridge door's breath on Diane's shins. Taste: the waxy rind of old cheddar, a cheap Sauvignon gone flat from sitting in the door. Texture: the ball of her foot under her thumb, the crumbs of a cracker sleeve. No smell to speak of. Diane doesn't smell things the way Iris does, and the absence is part of the register.
- **Dry observations (Diane's register, not Iris's):**
  - "Like a Viking," which is Olivia's line, and Diane's reply: "Vikings didn't borrow sweaters. They took them. Write that down too."
  - In narration, on the masking-tape labels: everything in the fridge had a date on it, so at least something in her life could be thrown away on schedule.
- **The body:** Tired, ordinary, middle-aged, and plainly rendered in layman's terms. Stockinged feet on cold tile. A thumb pressing the ball of a sore foot. Eating cheese standing up, then sitting down on a step stool for the call. Reading glasses pushed up into her hair and forgotten there. The watch on her wrist, which is the one object in the scene that is also evidence. She is in a body Iris has already described from a dossier and will describe again in ch04 (build, gait, "what the liver probably looks like"). Here nobody is appraising it, and that is the point.
- **Motifs:**
  - **Tape:** masking tape on the leftovers, dated in black marker in her own hand. It is a domestic, filing version of the tape that will be on her eyes.
  - **The grade:** reversed. Diane is the one marking, gently, the roommate and the window, and Olivia's plan. Her advice ("write it down", "get it agreed") is the Gatekeeper function in miniature. Nobody says so.
  - **Hands:** her thumb on her foot. Her wrist with the watch on it. The watch taken off and put to charge.
  - **The meal, inverted:** Diane's supper is scraps eaten for fuel, standing. It is the opposite of every meal Iris narrates, and it is the last dinner the reader sees her eat.
  - **Voicemail (adjacent):** none left. The point of the call is that it is answered. Olivia picks up on the fourth ring.

## Snapper

The kitchen dark except for the watch on its charger at the end of the counter, its small green light going round as it filled, getting ready for Sunday.

*(The outline gives no Snapper for interludes. This is proposed. It is an image, not a cliffhanger. The watch is the gift from Olivia, the device that recorded three years of routes, and the thing Diane will wear to the reservoir. Keep "keeping count" or any watching-verb off it. The light just goes round.)*

## Carries in / carries out

**Carries in (from ch01–ch03):**
- ch02 S2: Diane at the fundraiser, ending a young staffer's career in thirty seconds of hallway, competent and decent in nearly every register. The interlude is the register that doesn't touch the campaign, and it has to feel like the same woman: the same efficiency, aimed at a window.
- ch03 S1 Turn: "Diane has no unsecured hour." ch03 S2: the fitness profile never set private, three years of Sunday reservoir routes, the intersection point set. The Turn (beat 8) depends on the reader holding this.
- ch03 S3: the rug, the airway closed in under four seconds, "breathing hard and happy". The reader enters Diane's kitchen carrying that.
- Ledger: Diane is 52, has a childhood stutter, calls her mother Sundays at 4 p.m., walks the reservoir on Sundays and sits on the bench to read.

**Carries out (to ch04 and later):**
- ch04 S1: Iris watches two Sundays at distance and measures the bench reading at eleven minutes. The reader now knows Diane thinks it is "ten minutes, maybe". On the second Sunday Olivia walks beside her, which pays off Olivia's threat in beat 9. The reader will know before Iris that Olivia came because she asked to.
- ch04 S2: Holt asks whether the old files are closed, and Diane says yes. The interlude has kept her silent on the subject so the yes is fresh.
- ch05 S1: Diane answers a ringing phone on the path with "Olivia?". Beat 9 has told the reader that Olivia is the only one who rings through on the walk, so the misrecognition is explained before it happens. Iris does not know this. The reader does.
- ch05 S2: Olivia's text in Diane's coat, and the phone sealed in foil. The reader knows what that number means to her.
- ch06 S2: the stutter's hesitation before a word Iris never hears. The reader has heard it once, here, with "Tired" on the other side.
- ch06 S1: Diane's apology to Iris "for not having protected me from it". Her process-as-protection advice to Olivia is the domestic rehearsal of that worst sentence.
- ch24 S2: Holt's "Her daughter — Olivia — I was at the —". The fridge photograph (see New facts, optional) can give that unfinished clause a referent the reader recognises.
