## Scene 1 — Entry

1. Open in the trouble. At 4:40 p.m. on Monday, Election Eve, Iris is in the staff-and-vendor line at the loading-dock entrance of the Calloway. She wears a navy blazer, a lanyard and a badge she printed herself on her own secondhand card printer: VOLUNTEER / PRESS ASSISTANT, RUTH CALDER, with a gold back-of-house band. No photograph (ch20). Ruth Calder is a real volunteer on the roster who made eleven calls in July and has not been back since (ch20: still marked active). Iris has borrowed the record, not written one.
2. The crowd as bodies, out of habit. This is the one long sentence of the scene. A sound tech with a nicotine patch outlined under his sleeve. A field organiser whose hands are too still for the hour, so propranolol. A woman of about twenty-five with a staffer's lanyard, nauseated, sipping ginger ale, who hasn't told anyone yet. A Service post stander whose ring finger still has the dent of a ring he has stopped wearing. The habit is the only pleasure left, and she reports it as a habit.
3. The knowledge from 18, one line: she looks at the young women in the line and finds herself checking them for something there is no sign for on a body, which is whether anyone has already handed them a page to sign.
4. Her own body: she has eaten nothing since Sunday morning, and she can smell the sweetish ketone note on her own breath. She files it as ketosis (the Act III body, ledger). She does not make the Dolphy connection out loud (Dolphy died in a diabetic coma), and the reader who can may.
5. The Secret Service magnetometer and bag check, the first screen. Her bag holds a press-kit folder, a phone charger with no phone and a tin of unscented lip balm. Her own phone is at home (see New facts). The kit is not on her: it was staged in the closet on Sunday precisely because it could not pass this arch, and the page is the only thing she carries that she cannot leave. The wand chirps at her belt buckle, so: a pat-down by a female officer.
6. Near-failure. The officer's hand runs down Iris's left side and presses flat on the knife wound from 12, now a month-old scar. It pulls hard, and the flinch starts in the external oblique before she has decided anything.
7. Recovery through a pathologist's calm. Fifteen years of standing still while things are done to bodies. She lets the breath out the way she tells families to, lets the muscle go and keeps her face pleasant. The hand moves on across the inside breast pocket, where the paper crackles: the run of show she printed from the Saturday card's files, folded in front of the third page. "Run of show," she says, before she is asked. The officer says, "Everybody's got one," and waves her through. Iris counts her own radial pulse on the far side of the arch: sixty-four.
8. Past the Service line is the campaign's own credential desk at the head of the back-of-house corridor. This is the check that didn't exist at the fundraiser. A volunteer of nineteen (Iris dates her by her skin and by her saying "first time voting tomorrow"), with a laminated list sorted by surname and a dry-wipe marker.
9. The girl runs her finger down the C's, finds CALDER, RUTH, ticks it in marker and says "You're all set," with total sincerity. Grade, the last full one in the chapter: diligence, full marks; authority, none. The list checks names, and the name is real; nobody checks a face against a badge with no photograph on it. Matching badges to people who had actually been issued them was the credential audit's job, and the audit was Compliance's, last modified by the woman with paper in her mouth.
10. **[TURN]** I passed because the checker was a campaign volunteer, nineteen, with a laminated list. The hole was exactly where I'd said it would be.

- **Sensory lead:** smell. Cold November diesel off the loading dock, then hairspray, coffee breath, a cheap blazer's sizing, and her own ketone note last. (Keep the hotel's carpet cleaner out; it is 22's.)
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - On the volunteer's age: "Nineteen. I'd have put money on it. I've put it on certificates."
  - On the pat-down: "She was thorough. She would have made a good mortuary technician, if she could have learned to be thorough about the people who didn't mind."
- **The body:** fasting (ketone breath, gastric contractions logged and not wanted); the wound, pressed and pulling, the near-flinch; cold hands (the cold is her continuous report from 18); the blazer loose at the new belt hole (ch20); her own pulse counted at sixty-four.
- **Motifs:** the grade (the volunteer, the officer, thinning as it goes); hands (the officer's hand on her side; her own hands kept loose); the meal after (absent, and the fasting stands in for it); paper (the run of show, the laminated list, the page behind them).

## Scene 2 — The Map

1. Back corridors she has memorised from the hotel floor plan (ch20; the plans came off the Sat 24 Oct card). She walks them as if she has done it a hundred times, carrying a box of press folders from the press table, because nobody stops a woman with a box. She has lifted it by its outer flaps with her sleeves pulled down over her hands, so that nothing of hers is on the cardboard; she carries it that way the whole walk. Service level, ballroom level, the green-room corridor, the service stair (Stair C) beside it.
2. Reyes as paper. In the stairwell she unfolds the run of show to check her timings. Page four, security contacts: USSS shift lead, venue security, hotel engineering, and FBI LIAISON: SA E. REYES. It is the name that has been in her feed since 14, and she reads it as "E. Reyes" and nothing more. One line: somebody else in this building reads paper for a living.
3. At about 6:15 p.m., a quarter of an hour after doors open to the public, she takes Stair C down one floor to the housekeeping closet (thirty-one seconds from the stair door on three to the closet door on two, as timed on Sunday). The catering jacket is where she left it: folded at the bottom of the stack of round ivory cloths not due until a Wednesday wedding, over a plain black tote. Its inside pocket holds the kit. The wound pulls again on the stairs because of the box (style 05: "It pulls in 21").
4. First thing in the closet, before she sets anything down: the pale nitrile from her left outer pocket, snapped on inside the closet with the box still held by its flaps, so that setting the box down, and her bag beside it, leaves no bare print. Box and bag go down squared beside the cloth stack (the box is the campaign's own stock and stays in the closet). She strips the pale pair, folds them small into the left outer pocket (they go back on in the service passage), and spreads the rest of the kit through the blazer pockets, each item where her hand already knows it: the syringe of the agent in its hard case, the red marker and the steel pin in the left inside breast pocket, beside the page; the roll of black vinyl tape in the right outer pocket; the long zip-tie flat along the lining; the scalpel (No. 4 handle, guard on) in the right inside pocket, for the seal and nothing else; and a black pair of nitrile gloves in the right outer pocket (the second pair is ch24's). The catering jacket goes back over the tote at the bottom of the cloth stack for later (26).
5. Up again. The Service stair post, at the landing, takes her gold band at a glance and lets her go by. The sealed service door, seen in the flesh for the first time: a plain hotel door at the end of a short service passage behind the green room, its gap sealed at shoulder height with a white tamper-evident sticker bearing the Service legend that prints VOID when lifted. Tape motif: she has spent her career peeling tape off people, and she knows exactly what this one will do.
6. Counting the Service. They are competent, not omniscient. Each time she passes she counts the post at the stair landing, the two at the green-room door and the handoff when the shift lead walks through. When the detail arrives with the principal, the stair post steps forward to the corridor to widen the door cordon. The stair landing is unwatched while that happens.
7. The test run. Holt's first event is the volunteer thank-you in the mezzanine ballroom, the room where Iris first watched Diane in 2. His minute before it is in the green room. At about 7:19 she is in the service passage behind the sealed door as the stair post steps forward. At 7:21 she stands with her ear near the gap (not touching the label), a press assistant pretending to look for a signal. She hears the main door close on its closer, and she counts.
8. The latch goes and he comes out. She counts it again in her head and gets the same number. She does not know why he has cut it, whether the schedule, the night or nothing. She doesn't get to ask.
9. **[TURN]** The rhythm was tighter than the footage. The minute alone had become forty seconds. My plan was built for sixty.

- **Sensory lead:** sound. Radios with their squelch, the hum of the service corridors, a hotel trolley wheel with a flat spot, the hiss of the green-room door closer and the click of the latch. Counting is the scene's rhythm.
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Nobody stops a woman carrying a box. I could have carried a body through that hotel if I'd thought to put it in a box."
  - On the sticker: "I have taken tape off more faces than anyone in that building. I knew what it would do. It would say so."
- **The body:** the wound pulling on the stair under the box; sleeves pulled down over her hands and the cardboard edges through the cuff; her hands placing the kit by feel; the snap of the pale nitrile in the closet; her ear to the door gap; her heartbeat kept under the count; the stomach quiet now, which she notes and doesn't like; cold hands in the closet's laundry warmth.
- **Motifs:** tape (the VOID sticker; the vinyl roll in her pocket); hands (sleeved hands on the box, kit by feel, gloves); paper (the run of show with Reyes's name, the page in the breast pocket); the minute (sixty becomes forty).

## Scene 3 — The Threshold

1. The rally speech is at 9:00 p.m. in the grand ballroom. At 8:50 she is in the green-room corridor with her badge out, a clipboard and the face of a woman waiting to be told where to stand, near the service-stair door. The stair door is ten paces from the green-room door. Behind it is the passage to the sealed door, eight paces more.
2. Re-plan in her head, fast. Forty seconds, not sixty. Twelve seconds from the green-room door to the sticker at a working walk, so she must already be at the stair door when he goes in, with only the eight paces of passage left when the latch goes. She decides the arithmetic still works, and notices that she decided it without feeling anything about it.
3. The detail comes down the corridor at 8:52: the shift lead, two agents, an aide with a tablet, Sloane at Holt's elbow. Sloane's hand leaves his sleeve at the door, and she goes on towards the ballroom with a staffer without looking back. Iris reads her: a carotid going fast under a calm jaw. A woman who knew enough not to know more.
4. Holt passes her, the first time she has been within a few metres of him (before this he was footage; she was never at a rally). He doesn't look at her. He is listening to the aide, and the magnetism works even sideways. Cedar aftershave in his wake. She keeps her eyes on the clipboard (22 must be the first time he sees her).
5. The cordon tightens at the door exactly as she counted it, and the stair post steps forward into the corridor. Iris's hand is on the stair-door push bar.
6. The detail stops at the door. He goes in alone. The door begins to close on its closer.
7. **[TURN]** In the gap before it shut: a framed photograph of a Marine on the dressing table, already turned to face the chair. From ten paces and side-on she gets the dress-blue shoulder, the white cover and the edge of a plain black frame on its easel, angled to the chair, and no more. Someone set it out for him. The dressing table is a skirted banquet table with a lamp; there is no mirror in the room, and none for her to catch herself in (the mirror motif belongs to 15, 19, 20 and 27).
8. The latch. She is already counting, and already through the stair door while the post's back is turned; it closes behind her on its own weight. The wound pulls as she turns into the passage. Eight paces, and the pale nitrile goes on by feel as she walks.
9. At the sealed door with the clock running. The count reaches five as she arrives, with his attention on the photograph and the detail's on the corridor, which is the plan.

- **Sensory lead:** texture and temperature. The carpet underfoot giving back nothing, the corridor's air-conditioning cold on the back of her neck, the warm air that comes out of the green room in the gap, the slick PVC of her own badge under her thumb, the cedar as he goes by, and the snap of nitrile at the wrist.
- **Jokes / dry observations (thin):**
  - On Holt passing without looking: "He was the only man in the building who didn't read my badge. He'd have known my name if I'd been his staff. That was the whole problem, in a sentence."
  - Reserve: "Everybody in that corridor was keeping someone alive. I was the only one with a qualification."
- **The body:** her hands (clipboard, badge, the stair-door push bar, the gloves, then the sticker); the wound pulling on the turn; cold neck; heart rate low and steady, which is the problem.
- **Motifs:** the Marine's photograph (face-up and facing the chair, the first sight of it, per the ledger); tape (the sticker under her hand); hands (Snapper); the minute (forty seconds, counting).
