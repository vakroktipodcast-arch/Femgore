Her hand on the sticker is steady, and her wish that it weren't is the first admission that steadiness is all she has left. Never name the absence (style 11: no "empty", "hollow" or "numb").

## Scene 1 — Entry

1. Open in the trouble. At 4:40 p.m. on Monday, Election Eve, Iris is in the staff-and-vendor line at the loading-dock entrance of the Calloway. A navy blazer, a lanyard and a badge she printed herself: VOLUNTEER / PRESS ASSISTANT, ANNE CALDER, with a gold back-of-house band. No photograph, because the template has none (ch20).
2. The crowd as bodies, out of habit. This is the one long sentence of the scene. A sound tech with a nicotine patch outlined under his sleeve. A field organiser whose hands are too still for the hour, so propranolol. A woman of about twenty-five with a staffer's lanyard, nauseated, sipping ginger ale, who hasn't told anyone yet. A Service post stander whose ring finger still has the dent of a ring he has stopped wearing. The habit is the only pleasure left, and she reports it as a habit.
3. The knowledge from 18, one line: she looks at the young women in the line and finds herself checking them for something there is no sign for on a body.
4. Her own body: she has fasted for more than twenty-four hours, and she can smell faint acetone on her own breath. She files it as ketosis. She does not make the Dolphy connection out loud, and the reader can.
5. The Secret Service magnetometer and bag check. Her bag holds a press-kit folder, a phone charger with no phone and a lip balm. Her own phone is at home (see New facts). The wand chirps at her belt buckle, so: a pat-down by a female officer.
6. Near-failure. The officer's hand runs down Iris's left side and presses flat on the healed knife wound from 12. It pulls hard, and the flinch starts in the external oblique before she has decided anything.
7. Recovery through a pathologist's calm. Fourteen years of standing still while things are done to bodies. She lets the breath out the way she tells families to, lets the muscle go and keeps her face pleasant. The hand moves on across the inside breast pocket, where the paper crackles: the run of show she printed, folded in front of the third page. "Run of show," she says, before she is asked. The officer says, "Everybody's got one," and waves her through. Iris counts her own radial pulse on the far side of the arch: sixty-four.
8. Past the Service line is the campaign's own credential desk at the back-of-house corridor. This is the check that didn't exist at the fundraiser. A volunteer of nineteen (Iris dates her by her skin and by her saying "first time voting tomorrow"), with a laminated list and a dry-wipe marker.
9. The girl runs her finger down the C's, finds CALDER, ANNE, ticks it in marker and says "You're all set," with total sincerity. Grade, the last full one in the chapter: diligence, full marks; authority, none. The list was printed from the same database Brandt's access let Iris write into. The audit that would have caught the extra name was Compliance's job, and Compliance was the woman with paper in her mouth.
10. **[TURN]** I passed because the checker was a campaign volunteer, nineteen, with a laminated list. The hole was exactly where I'd said it would be.

- **Sensory lead:** smell. Cold November diesel off the loading dock, then hairspray, coffee breath, a cheap blazer's sizing, and her own acetone last.
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - On the volunteer's age: "Nineteen. I'd have put money on it. I've put it on certificates."
  - On the pat-down: "She was thorough. She would have made a good mortuary technician, if she could have learned to be thorough about the people who didn't mind."
- **The body:** fasting (acetone breath, gastric contractions logged and not wanted); the wound, pressed and pulling, the near-flinch; cold hands; her own pulse counted at sixty-four.
- **Motifs:** the grade (the volunteer, the officer, thinning as it goes); hands (the officer's hand on her side; her own hands kept loose); the meal after (absent, and the fasting stands in for it); paper (the run of show, the laminated list, the page behind them).

## Scene 2 — The Map

1. Back corridors she has memorised from the hotel floor plan (ch20). She walks them as if she has done it a hundred times, carrying a box of press folders, because nobody stops a woman with a box. Service level, ballroom level, the green-room corridor, the service stair beside it.
2. Reyes as paper. In the stairwell she unfolds the run of show to check her timings. Page four, security contacts: USSS shift lead, venue security, hotel engineering, and FBI LIAISON: SA E. REYES. It is the name that has been in her feed since 14. One line: somebody else in this building reads paper for a living.
3. At about 6:15 p.m., while doors open to the public, she takes the service stair down one floor to the housekeeping closet. The catering jacket is where she hung it on Sunday, behind the housekeeping tunics. Its inside pocket holds the kit. The wound pulls again on the stairs because of the box (style 05: "It pulls in 21").
4. She takes the kit and spreads it through the blazer pockets, each item where her hand already knows it: the syringe of the agent in its hard case and the red marker inside the left breast pocket, beside the page; the roll of black vinyl tape in the right outer pocket; the zip-ties flat along the lining. The catering jacket stays on its hanger for later (26). She leaves the box of folders in the closet.
5. Up again. The sealed service door, seen in the flesh for the first time: a plain hotel door at the end of a short service passage behind the green room, its gap sealed with a white tamper-evident sticker that prints VOID when lifted. Tape motif: she has spent her career peeling tape off people, and she knows exactly what this one will do.
6. Counting the Service. They are competent, not omniscient. Each time she passes she counts the post at the stair landing, the two at the green-room door and the handoff when the shift lead walks through. When the detail arrives with the principal, the stair post steps forward to the corridor to widen the door cordon. The stair landing is unwatched while that happens.
7. The test run. Holt's first event is the volunteer thank-you in the mezzanine ballroom, the room where Iris first watched Diane in 2. His minute before it is in the green room. At 7:21 p.m. Iris is in the service passage behind the sealed door, ear to the gap, a press assistant pretending to look for a signal. She hears the main door close on its closer, and she counts.
8. The latch goes and he comes out. She counts it again in her head and gets the same number. She does not know why he has cut it, whether the schedule, the night or nothing. She doesn't get to ask.
9. **[TURN]** The rhythm was tighter than the footage. The minute alone had become forty seconds. My plan was built for sixty.

- **Sensory lead:** sound. Radios with their squelch, the hum of the service corridors, a hotel trolley wheel with a flat spot, the hiss of the green-room door closer and the click of the latch. Counting is the scene's rhythm.
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - "Nobody stops a woman carrying a box. I could have carried a body through that hotel if I'd thought to put it in a box."
  - On the sticker: "I have taken tape off more faces than anyone in that building. I knew what it would do. It would say so."
- **The body:** the wound pulling on the stair under the box; her hands placing the kit by feel; her ear to the door gap; her heartbeat kept under the count; the stomach quiet now, which she notes and doesn't like.
- **Motifs:** tape (the VOID sticker; the vinyl roll in her pocket); hands (kit by feel); paper (the run of show with Reyes's name, the page in the breast pocket); the minute (sixty becomes forty).

## Scene 3 — The Threshold

1. The rally is at 9:00 p.m. in the grand ballroom. At 8:50 she is in the green-room corridor with her badge out, a clipboard and the face of a woman waiting to be told where to stand. Ten paces from the green-room door is the service-stair door. Behind it is the passage to the sealed door, eight paces more.
2. Re-plan in her head, fast. Forty seconds, not sixty. Twelve seconds from the stair door to the sticker. She decides the arithmetic still works, and notices that she decided it without feeling anything about it.
3. The detail comes down the corridor at 8:52: the shift lead, two agents, an aide with a tablet, Sloane at Holt's elbow. Sloane's hand leaves his sleeve before the door, and she goes on towards the ballroom with a staffer without looking back. Iris reads her: a carotid going fast under a calm jaw. A woman who knew enough not to know more.
4. Holt passes her. He doesn't look at her. He is listening to the aide, and the magnetism works even sideways. She keeps her eyes on the clipboard (22 must be the first time he sees her).
5. The cordon tightens at the door exactly as she counted it, and the stair post steps forward into the corridor.
6. The detail stops at the door. He goes in alone. The door begins to close on its closer.
7. **[TURN]** In the gap before it shut: a framed photograph of a Marine on the dressing table, already turned to face the chair. Someone set it out for him. Do not let her see her own reflection in the dressing-table mirror (the mirror motif belongs to 15, 19, 20 and 27).
8. The latch. She is already counting. She is through the stair door while the post's back is turned, and it closes behind her on its own weight. The wound pulls as she turns into the passage. Eight paces.
9. At the sealed door with the clock running. She stops herself and counts to five, because she needs his attention on the photograph and the detail's on the corridor, and because that is the plan.

- **Sensory lead:** texture and temperature. The carpet underfoot giving back nothing, the corridor's air-conditioning cold on the back of her neck, the warm air that comes out of the green room in the gap, and the slick laminate of her own badge under her thumb.
- **Jokes / dry observations (thin):**
  - On Holt passing without looking: "He was the only man in the building who didn't read my badge. He'd have known my name if I'd been his staff. That was the whole problem, in a sentence."
  - Reserve: "Everybody in that corridor was keeping someone alive. I was the only one with a qualification."
- **The body:** her hands (clipboard, badge, the stair-door push bar, then the sticker); the wound pulling on the turn; cold neck; heart rate low and steady, which is the problem.
- **Motifs:** the Marine's photograph (face-up and facing the chair, the first sight of it, per the ledger); tape (the sticker under her hand); hands (Snapper); the minute (forty seconds, counting).
