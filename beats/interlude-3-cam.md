- **POV:** Carmichael "Cam" Brandt, close third, past tense. Never Holt's head, never Iris's. Narration calls him **Cam**, as the approved anchor does (`anchors/interlude-2-cam.md`); "Brandt" is what other people's chapters call him. This sheet says Brandt in its notes only for clarity.
- **Target words:** 600–900 (aim ~850). One scene, one Turn. Suggested weight: night one ~350, the days ~300, the suite ~200.
- **Act pressure:** Act II, The Practicum. "Pressure: Brandt, until Chapter 17." Here the pressure is turned inside out: we see the man who is Iris's pressure come under pressure himself, from her gifts and from Holt, and walk towards her. For the reader, the menace in this interlude is intimacy. He is going to let her.
- **Iris's appetite at entry:** Act II, appetite as identity. In Ch 13 she has entered his home, chosen his things "like I'm choosing a gift", eaten from his fridge in the dark, touched one key of his piano, and listened to a whole side of Mingus with him from her car. The midpoint gifts are flirtation, and the reader understood that before she did.
- **Iris's appetite at exit:** Unchanged. She is off the page and the interlude cannot touch her appetite. What changes is the reader. The flirtation has been received, read correctly and returned. When Ch 14–16 show her herding him, the reader already knows he is coming willingly, which makes his "I wondered if it would be you" (Ch 16) a line he has had days to choose. No appetite from Iris leaks into Cam's head.
- **Register reminder (matches the approved `anchors/interlude-2-cam.md`):** plainer and cooler than Iris. Layman's words for everything (a lung is something Diane is no longer using). No jokes in Iris's style, no direct address, no appetite, no anatomy words. Short fragments for perception ("Tepid." "Fair mark."), then plain complete sentences. The anchor does smell and taste ordinary things (the urn, the wax lining, the hotel carpet "of everybody"), so Cam may notice them, but only in layman's terms and never as appetite. In his head she is "Vale" (ledger), until the Turn.

## Scene 1: The Pillow, the Days, the Suite

1. **Night one, his house, 12:26 a.m. Monday.** Cam comes in by the front door, then goes through to the kitchen. On the counter, squared to its edge beside the kettle, is an 8x10 glossy of Diane's mouth full of paper, half the matter number LC-0311 legible. He has a copy on his phone in his left inside breast pocket, and this one is sharper. He stands with his keys still in his hand. Fragments: Not the copy off the channel. Hers. He does not open the fridge and does not look into the front room.
2. **Bedroom.** He turns on the bedside lamp. On his pillow, where his head goes (not the far pillow, which has been unslept for two years), lie the halves of a record from his own shelf, Dolphy, *Out to Lunch!*, first pressing, with the sleeve beside them.
3. He sits on his side of the bed, takes a half in each hand and fits them together. The break is one clean line and there is a score along it, so someone cut it first and then snapped it; it was done with care and not in temper. He sits like that for a long time. Twenty years of arranging what happens to other men, and he is doing it now for himself: who has seen what, what it costs, what comes next. The arithmetic comes out the same every time. Then he picks up the driving licence lying beside the halves and reads the name on it: Dale Garrity, 43, bearded, a small red heart in the corner. The name means nothing to him. He hired through a cut-out and paid not to be told, so this is the first time he has seen it; he knows only that the heavy one did not come back from the warehouse. The licence sits a quarter-inch to the left of true. He knows that measure. It is his own.
4. Then the page, already folded in quarters. He puts on the reading glasses with the taped arm, opens it, and reads it through once. Lesson Two. The Architect. A clean print, no marks. *Mistook clarity for cruelty.* **He laughs, a real one, surprised out of him, his head going back, and the sound is too loud in the bedroom. He doesn't know why he is laughing.** (This syncs to the Ch 13 S3 Turn, which Iris watches on the feed.)
5. He folds the page back along her creases and puts it in his inside breast pocket, left side, with the phone. The halves and the licence go back into the Dolphy sleeve, and he takes the sleeve through to the front room and reshelves it in its place. He pours a rye from the tray by the turntable, puts on Mingus, *The Black Saint and the Sinner Lady*, and plays side one through from the chair by the piano without touching the piano. The tone arm lifts and goes home. He does not turn it over. It is twenty to one and the drink does not count; a drink after midnight belongs to the day before. He sleeps on top of the covers in his clothes.
6. **The days, in fragments.** Monday morning in the War Room he accuses Theo Akers, in front of the room, of leaking "the photograph". Theo goes white and says he deleted it like he was told. Cam knows the print on his counter did not come from the boy, and he accuses him anyway. It is the first thing in twenty years he has done without managing it. He goes home before eleven. At 11:40 a.m. he pours a rye at the kitchen counter, where the print is. First drink before noon in nine years. He notes the time the way he would note another man's. Monday to Wednesday he stands rather than sits at the War Room table, carries his coffee about and does not drink it, and every night plays side one and only side one.
7. **Wednesday 7 Oct, evening.** He asks Holt for a minute. Holt puts a hand on his shoulder and walks him out of the glass room to a room out of the lens's reach. Cam says there is "a problem from the old files". Holt says, "I thought Diane closed those," then asks what he makes of the numbers in the suburbs, and listens to the answer with every sign of interest. Cam watches Holt's attention leave him and go somewhere else. He has written that exit for other men and knows the next line, and it comes. Holt goes back first, after four minutes. Cam stands there two more, putting his face in order, and goes back and does not sit.
8. **Thursday 8 Oct.** The huddle starts at 7:42 and he is not in it. At 7:51 his card fails at the War Room door, twice; an IT man lets him in with a smile that is sorry about something. His login is refused on the campaign drive, three times, the third slowly, a key at a time. Holt does not look up through the glass, and takes real skill not to. The staff channel, which he made and has administered and deleted a photograph from the night Diane died, is gone from his phone. Nobody tells him; Holt never says a word. Moved, a little at a time. (This syncs to the Ch 14 S1 Turn, which Iris watches in real time.)
9. **Tuesday 13 Oct.** One line for the week's closing doors, in the order they shut: Monday the farm goes dark (Mon 12 Oct), then that night a reporter's voicemail, a good voice, who will "try him at the house", then Tuesday the car service freezes his card. Each shut door points out of the house, the same way, and he recognises the hand: the same care as the break in the record. He does not go to the Tabard, though it is Tuesday and she will know it is Tuesday. In the afternoon he showers (vetiver soap), shaves, and nicks himself at the angle of the jaw, feeling the nick with a fingertip rather than looking. Charcoal cotton shirt, jacket over it. The folded page is in the left inside pocket with the phone, the photograph folded once in the right. He leaves by taxi at about 5:55 with a paper bag with the neck of a bottle in it and the Black Saint in a card mailer, and gets out at the printworks at about 6:20. In the suite he lets the door latch and lock but does not press it into its seals (like a door you notice you have already locked); the deaf room takes the sound of his own footsteps. He pours one rye and sets the glass down on the folded page. He puts the record on and turns it down so he will hear a knock. He does not know what time.
10. **[TURN]** In the quiet he finally does the arithmetic he has been avoiding since the pillow, and his grammar comes together for the first time. She stood where his head goes, and what she had done was score a record and snap it so it would fit back together. She could have killed him in his sleep, and she chose to warn him. She wants him to understand first, before anything else happens. He finds, to his surprise, that he wants to. (Last sentence of the interlude. It is the outline's Turn, complete and unsoftened. Like the anchor, it closes on one long plain paragraph; "it did not feel like a decision at all" rhymes with the anchor but should not be copied.)

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first, because the scene is his ear. The two halves of vinyl ticking together as he fits them. His laugh, too loud in the bedroom. The tone arm lifting and going home. Then the suite's deadness: footsteps that go nowhere, Mingus turned down to almost nothing. Texture: the clean edge of the break and the score under his thumb (a rhyme with the thumb on the glass in interlude-2-cam), the laminated licence, the softening folds of the page. Taste: rye, and rye at 11:40 a.m., which tastes different and which he notices. Temperature: the lamp-warm bedroom, the pillow cool where nobody has slept, the suite's still climate-held air. Smell: plain and brief. The rye, the vetiver from the shower; the house he lives in, which he does not smell.
- **Dry observations (Cam's register, not Iris's; no jokes in her style):**
  - On Holt and the suburbs: he has written that exit for other men. He knows the next line. He waits for it, and it comes.
  - On the record: anyone can break a record. It takes a steady hand to break one so it fits back together.
  - On the night-one drink: it was twenty to one and it did not count. A drink after midnight belongs to the day before. A fixer's accounting.
  - On the nine years: he notes the time of the first drink "the way he would note another man's".
  - On the quarter-inch: she had put it back where he had left hers. Even.
- **The body:** Hands carry it, as in interlude-2-cam: two halves of a record held together, a thumb on the break, a licence, the reading glasses with the taped arm, a glass, a page opened and folded back along her creases. He drinks, before noon for the first time in nine years, and nothing is eaten on the page. The fridge stays shut, which is the irony against Ch 13 S2. He sleeps in his clothes, on top of the covers. A razor nick at the jaw, found by touch (it is the "first wound of the night, and his own work" in the Ch 17 anchor). Charcoal throughout.
- **Motifs:**
  - **Vinyl:** the broken Dolphy, the Black Saint played through and not turned over: night one, every night that week, and the suite. This is the rule Ch 16 and Ch 17 honour, "side two never played". The tone arm "goes home" (the Ch 13 and Ch 17 phrase).
  - **Hands:** his on the break, the page and the glass. Hers are present only as evidence of care: the clean break, the squared photograph, the page folded in quarters, the quarter-inch.
  - **The grade:** he reads the Evaluation and, as with Diane's in interlude-2-cam, finds it fair, this time about himself. He does not turn the thought face-down. This is the step on from interlude 2.
  - **Paperwork thread:** "the old files", Holt's "I thought Diane closed those" (which echoes Holt's "Those are closed?" in Ch 4), the access that goes quietly dead. The system filing him away.
  - **The meal after (by absence):** he does not open the fridge. Do not point it out.
  - **Tape:** none on the page, apart from the mended glasses (clear tape). Do not add any.
  - **The locked door:** the anchor's "the way you notice you have already locked a door" returns, lightly, at the suite door. Do not point at it.
