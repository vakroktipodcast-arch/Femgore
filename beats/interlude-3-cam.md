# Beat sheet: interlude-3-cam

- **Unit:** Interlude 3, Cam (after Chapter 13, before Chapter 14). Manuscript file `manuscript/interlude-3-cam.md`, heading `# Interlude — Cam`.
- **POV:** Carmichael "Cam" Brandt, close third, past tense. Never Holt's head, never Iris's.
- **Target words:** 600–900 (aim ~850). One scene, one Turn. Suggested weight: night one ~350, the days ~300, the suite ~200.
- **Act pressure:** Act II, The Practicum. "Pressure: Brandt, until Chapter 17." Here the pressure is turned inside out: we see the man who is Iris's pressure come under pressure himself, from her gifts and from Holt, and walk towards her. For the reader, the menace in this interlude is intimacy. He is going to let her.
- **Iris's appetite at entry:** Act II, appetite as identity. In Ch 13 she has entered his home, chosen his things "like I'm choosing a gift", eaten from his fridge in the dark, touched one key of his piano, and listened to a whole side of Mingus with him from her car. The midpoint gifts are flirtation, and the reader understood that before she did.
- **Iris's appetite at exit:** Unchanged. She is off the page and the interlude cannot touch her appetite. What changes is the reader. The flirtation has been received, read correctly and returned. When Ch 14–16 show her herding him, the reader already knows he is coming willingly, which makes his "I wondered if it would be you" (Ch 16) a line he has had days to choose. No appetite from Iris leaks into Cam's head.
- **Register reminder (interlude-drafter rules; matches interlude-2-cam):** plainer and cooler than Iris. Layman's words for everything. No jokes in Iris's style, no direct address, no appetite. Cam thinks in fragments. His grammar becomes complete only when he is honest with himself, which happens once, at the Turn. In his head she is "Vale" (see New facts), until the Turn sentence.

## Scene 1: The Pillow, the Days, the Suite

1. **Night one, his house, late.** Brandt comes in through the kitchen. On the counter, squared to its edge, is the photograph of Diane's mouth full of paper, printed, glossy. He has the same picture on his phone in his breast pocket, and this one is sharper. He stands with his keys still in his hand. Fragments: Not the sheriff's copy. Hers.
2. Bedroom. On his pillow, where his head goes, a record from his own shelf, broken cleanly in half: Dolphy, *Out to Lunch!*, the first pressing. Beside it, a laminated ID card. He reads the name and it means nothing to him. It is the contractor who did not come back from the warehouse. He had never known the man's name. He had paid not to.
3. He sits on the edge of the bed with a half of the record in each hand and fits them together. The break is clean, a single line through the grooves, so it was done with care and not in temper. He sits like that for a long time. Twenty years of arranging what happens to other men, and he finds he is doing it now for himself: who has seen what, what it costs, what comes next. The arithmetic comes out the same every time he does it.
4. Then he sees the page, which was under the record. Lesson Two. The Architect. A clean print with no marks on it. He reads it through once. Mistook clarity for cruelty. **He laughs, a real one, surprised out of him, and the sound is too loud in the bedroom. He doesn't know why he is laughing.** (This syncs to the Ch 13 S3 Turn, which Iris watches on the feed.)
5. He folds the page into quarters and puts it in his inside breast pocket, left side, with the phone. He puts the two halves and the ID back into the Dolphy sleeve and reshelves it in its place. He pours a rye in the front room, puts on Mingus, *The Black Saint and the Sinner Lady*, and plays side one through without moving. The tone arm lifts and goes home. He does not turn it over. (This is the Ch 13 Snapper, from his side. He never checks the fridge. He sleeps on top of the covers in his clothes.)
6. **The days, in fragments.** Next morning in the War Room he accuses Theo Akers, in front of the room, of leaking "the photograph". Theo, white, says he deleted it like he was told. Brandt knows exactly where the photograph came from, and he goes on accusing the boy anyway. It is the first thing in twenty years he has done without managing it. He goes home before eleven. At 11:40 a.m. he pours a rye at his kitchen counter, where the photograph had been. It is the first drink before noon in nine years. He notes the time the way he would note another man's.
7. That evening, in a small room off the War Room, he tells Holt there is "a problem from the old files". Holt says, "I thought Diane closed those," and then asks what Brandt makes of the numbers in the suburbs, and listens to the answer with every sign of interest. Brandt watches Holt's attention leave him and go somewhere else. He has seen that look given to other men for twenty years. He has written their exits.
8. Next morning his login is refused on the campaign drive. He is no longer admin of the staff channel. That is the channel he deleted her work from the night Diane died, and nobody tells him. Holt never says a word. Your furniture gets moved. (This syncs to the Ch 14 S1 Turn, which Iris watches in real time.) Then, in one line: over the next days the rest of what he built goes dark too, faster than it should, and each closed door points the same way. He recognises the hand. It is the same care as the break in the record.
9. **Tuesday evening.** He does not go to the Tabard. He showers. He shaves and nicks himself at the angle of the jaw, and feels the nick with a fingertip rather than looking. Vetiver soap, a charcoal shirt, the folded page and the photograph in his breast pocket, the Mingus under his arm. The editing suite: the door seals, and the deaf room takes the sound of his own footsteps. He pours one rye and sets the glass down on the folded page. He puts the record on and turns the volume down so he will hear her knock.
10. **[TURN]** In the quiet he finally does the arithmetic he has been avoiding since the pillow, and his grammar comes together for the first time. She stood where his head goes, with a blade in her kit, and broke a record instead. She could have killed him in his sleep, and she chose to warn him. She wants him to understand first, before anything else happens. He finds, to his surprise, that he wants to. (Last sentence of the interlude. It is the outline's Turn, complete and unsoftened.)

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first, because the scene is his ear. The two halves of vinyl ticking together as he fits them. His laugh, too loud in the bedroom. The tone arm lifting and going home. Then the suite's deadness: footsteps that go nowhere, Mingus turned down to almost nothing. Texture: the clean edge of the break under his thumb (a rhyme with the thumb on the glass in interlude-2-cam), the laminated ID, the softening folds of the page. Taste: rye, and rye at 11:40 a.m., which tastes different and which he notices. Temperature: the pillow cool where nobody has slept, and the suite's still, climate-held air. Smell: barely. Vetiver from the shower, his own house. Cam does not smell things the way Iris does.
- **Dry observations (Cam's register, not Iris's; no jokes in her style):**
  - On Holt and the suburbs: he has written that exit for other men. He knows the next line. He waits for it, and it comes.
  - On the record: she broke it cleanly. Anyone can break a record. It takes a steady hand to break one so it fits back together.
  - (Optional) On the nine years: he notes the time of the first drink "the way he would note another man's", which is a fixer's joke on himself and not Iris's.
- **The body:** Hands carry it, as in interlude-2-cam: two halves of a record held together, a thumb on the break, a glass, a page folded smaller and smaller. He drinks, before noon for the first time in nine years, and he doesn't eat. The fridge stays shut on the page, which is the irony against Ch 13 S2. He sleeps in his clothes, on top of the covers. A razor nick at the jaw, found by touch (it is the "first wound of the night, and his own work" in the Ch 17 anchor). He wears charcoal throughout.
- **Motifs:**
  - **Vinyl:** the broken Dolphy, the Black Saint played through and not turned over, twice: night one, and the suite. This is the rule Ch 16 and Ch 17 honour, "side two never played".
  - **Hands:** his on the break, the page and the glass. Hers are present only as evidence of care: the clean break, the squared photograph.
  - **The grade:** he reads the Evaluation and, as with Diane's in interlude-2-cam, finds it fair, this time about himself. He does not turn the thought face-down. This is the step on from interlude 2.
  - **Paperwork thread:** "the old files", Holt's "I thought Diane closed those" (which echoes Holt's "Those are closed?" in ch04), the access that goes quietly dead. The system filing him away.
  - **The meal after (by absence):** he does not open the fridge. Do not point it out.
  - **Tape:** none on the page. Do not add any.
