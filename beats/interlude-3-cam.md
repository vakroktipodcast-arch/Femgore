- **Act pressure:** Act II, The Practicum. "Pressure: Brandt, until Chapter 17." Here the pressure is turned inside out: we see the man who is Iris's pressure receive her gifts, read them correctly, and decide. For the reader, the menace in this interlude is intimacy. He is going to let her. The pressure Holt puts on him is no longer in this unit; Ch 14 shows it from Iris's side.
- **Iris's appetite at entry:** Act II, appetite as identity. In Ch 13 she has entered his home, chosen his things "like I'm choosing a gift", eaten from his fridge in the dark, touched one key of his piano, and listened to a whole side of Mingus with him from her car. The midpoint gifts are flirtation, and the reader understood that before she did.
- **Iris's appetite at exit:** Unchanged. She is off the page and the interlude cannot touch her appetite. What changes is the reader. The flirtation has been received, read correctly and returned, eleven hours after she left it. When Ch 14–16 show her watching him fracture and then herding him, the reader already knows he is coming willingly, which makes his "I wondered if it would be you" (Ch 16) a line he has had nine days to choose. No appetite from Iris leaks into Cam's head.
- **Register reminder (matches the approved `anchors/interlude-2-cam.md`):** plainer and cooler than Iris. Layman's words for everything. No jokes in Iris's style, no direct address, no appetite, no anatomy words. Short fragments for perception ("Vale's." "Not temper. Care." "Fair mark." "Even."), then plain complete sentences. He may notice ordinary smells and tastes, in layman's terms and never as appetite (the bed smells of him; the rye tastes thinner in daylight). The anchor closes on one long, complete, honest paragraph, and the Turn here does the same. His grammar becomes fully complete only when he is honest with himself, which happens once, at the Turn. In his head she is "Vale" (ledger), and "she" only in the Turn.

## Scene 1: The Pillow and the Morning

1. **Night one, his kitchen, 12:26 a.m. Monday.** The print is on the counter beside the kettle, squared to the edge. His keys are still in his hand. Diane's mouth, half the matter number in the footer. Sharper than the copy on his phone. "Vale's."
2. He stands and looks at it. Not off the channel. Off the file, and her name is on the file. The house is warm, the heating still on its timer, and nothing in the kitchen is out of place but the one thing, which is squared better than he would have squared it. (Irony against the pork in Ch 13 S2; not pointed out.)
3. He does not open the fridge. He has crossed the front room without turning his head to the records. He goes to the bedroom and turns on the lamp.
4. **Bedroom.** The bed smells of him, as it always does, and of nothing else. The record is on his own pillow, where his head goes: two halves a hairline apart, the sleeve beside them. Dolphy, *Out to Lunch!* His own.
5. He sits on his side of the bed and fits the halves together. They meet with a small tick. Under his thumb there is a groove along the break: cut first, then snapped. "Not temper. Care."
6. He sits like that a long time (the one "long time" in the unit, matching Ch 13 S3), doing for himself what he has done for other men for twenty years: who knew, what it cost, what came next. The sum comes out the same every time.
7. He puts on the reading glasses with the taped arm and picks up the licence. Dale Garrity, forty-three, a beard, a donor heart. The name means nothing; he had paid not to be told it. "The heavy one, then." It had lain a quarter-inch to the left of true, and he had seen that from the door, before he touched anything. His own measure, given back. "Even."
8. Then the page, folded in quarters. Lesson Two. The Architect. He reads it once, to the foot. *Mistook clarity for cruelty.* "Fair mark." He does not turn it over.
9. The page goes back along her creases into his inside pocket, against the phone. He sleeves the licence with the halves. **Then he laughs, his head going back, too loud for the room. He does not know what at. It stops the way it started, without asking him.** (This syncs to the Ch 13 S3 Turn, which Iris watches on the feed: page pocketed and halves sleeved first, then the laugh.)
10. **Front room.** He takes the sleeve through and puts it back in its place on the wall, among the D's, spine out like the rest. Twenty to one, a rye from the tray. It does not count; a drink after midnight belongs to the day before. He puts on side one of the Black Saint and sits in the chair by the piano with the glass on his knee, not touching the piano. He knows every bar. He listens as if he did not, for anything that is not where he left it, and finds nothing. The tone arm lifts and goes home, and he does not get up to turn it over.
11. **Sleep.** He sleeps on top of the covers in his clothes, the jacket buttoned over the page, his head where the halves had been. He leaves the lamp on.
12. **Six o'clock.** The lamp is still on, yellow against the black of the window. The page is where he put it, warm from him. He changes his shirt and not his suit. In the kitchen the print is where Vale squared it; he makes coffee beside it, drinks it standing, and does not move it.
13. **The War Room, one sentence.** He accuses the Akers boy, in front of everyone, of something the boy had not done, and is home before eleven. Not staged: no dialogue, no reaction.
14. **11:40 a.m., the counter.** He pours a rye beside the print. First drink before noon in nine years. He notes the time the way he would note another man's. It tastes thinner in daylight. He takes out the page, opens it on the counter and reads it again, to the foot, more slowly, with the glass beside it (not on it; no ring). Then he folds it along her creases and puts it back in his inside pocket.
15. **[TURN]** The house is quiet the way it is only quiet on a weekday, and in the quiet he does the other sum, the one he had left on the pillow, and it comes out whole; his grammar comes together for the first time. She had been in this house with a blade, and had stood in the dark at the place where his head goes, and what she had cut was a record. She could have killed him in his sleep, and she chose to warn him instead. She wanted him to understand first, and he found, to his surprise, that he wanted to. (Last paragraph and last sentence of the interlude. It is the outline's Turn, complete and unsoftened, moved to the Monday morning by author ruling 3.)

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first, because the scene is his ear. The two halves of vinyl ticking together as he fits them. His laugh, too loud for the room. Side one listened to for anything out of place, and the tone arm lifting and going home. Then the weekday quiet of the house, in which the Turn happens. Texture: the groove along the break under his thumb (a rhyme with the thumb on the glass in interlude-2-cam), the page along her creases, warm from him in the morning. Light: the lamp left on all night, yellow against a black window at six; daylight only at 11:40. Taste: rye, and rye at 11:40 a.m., which tastes thinner and which he notices; coffee, drunk standing. Temperature: the house warm on its timer. Smell: plain and brief. The bed smells of him and of nothing else.
- **Dry observations (Cam's register, not Iris's; no jokes in her style):**
  - On the print: squared better than he would have squared it.
  - On the night-one drink: it was twenty to one and it did not count. A drink after midnight belonged to the day before. A fixer's accounting.
  - On the nine years: he notes the time of the first drink "the way he would note another man's".
  - On the quarter-inch: his own measure, given back. Even.
  - On the laugh: it stopped the way it had started, without asking him.
- **The body:** Hands carry it, as in interlude-2-cam: keys still in the hand, two halves of a record fitted together, a thumb on the break, a licence, the reading glasses with the taped arm, a glass on his knee, a page opened and folded back along her creases, twice. He drinks rye twice and coffee once, and nothing is eaten on the page. The fridge stays shut, which is the irony against Ch 13 S2. He sleeps in his clothes on top of the covers, the jacket buttoned over the page, and changes only his shirt. No shower, no shave, no nick; those belong to 13 Oct and Ch 17.
- **Motifs:**
  - **Vinyl:** the broken Dolphy, sleeved and reshelved among the D's; side one of the Black Saint played through once and not turned over. The tone arm "goes home" (the Ch 13 and Ch 17 phrase). "Every night since the page" is Ch 14 S3's to say, not this unit's.
  - **Hands:** his on the break, the page and the glass. Hers are present only as evidence of care: the cut-then-snapped break, the squared print he will not move, the page folded in quarters, the quarter-inch.
  - **The grade:** he reads the Evaluation and, as with Diane's in interlude-2-cam, finds it fair, this time about himself. He does not turn the page over, and he reads it again in the morning. This is the step on from interlude 2.
  - **Paperwork thread:** "Off the file, and her name was on the file." He places the print as coming from the case file and from her. The Holt strand of this thread ("the old files") has left the unit.
  - **The meal after (by absence):** he does not open the fridge. It is stated once and not pointed at.
  - **Tape:** none on the page, apart from the mended glasses. Do not add any.
  - **Sums:** two. The fixer's sum on the bed, which comes out the same every time, and "the other sum, the one he had left on the pillow", which comes out whole at the Turn.
