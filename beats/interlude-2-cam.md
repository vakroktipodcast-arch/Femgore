## Scene 1: The War Room, Sunday night

1. Brandt is the last one in, eight minutes to midnight (11:52 p.m.), summoned from home. Charcoal suit, no tie: the only sign. The War Room is loud: a dozen people standing, nobody sitting, every face lit from below by a phone.
2. He takes a coffee from the urn by the door without asking how long it has been there. Tepid; it tastes of the urn and the wax lining of the cup. He drinks it anyway because the cup gives one hand something to hold while the room tells him things.
3. Comms wants a statement by six. Counsel wants nobody to say anything. Cam thinks that both will get what they want, in that order. Someone says *activist*. Someone says *lunatic*.
4. The photograph is up on the wall screen and everyone is reading the page in it. Brandt says, "Phone. Not the wall." Someone kills the screen; he opens the channel on his own phone.
5. Diane on a concrete floor (he knows the pump-station walk). Black tape across her eyes, the dull black of a record sleeve. A printed page pinned to her chest, one word circled in marker: GATEKEEPER, and under it the typed line *Confused process for protection.* He reads it once. *Fair mark.* The thought comes and he turns it face-down.
6. Then he looks at what nobody else is looking at, her mouth: packed with paper, the paper gone soft where it was wet. Something she was no longer using, stuffed with something nobody had ever been meant to read twice. He puts two fingers to the glass and spreads them: a serif face, a thin rule along the foot of a page, half a number below the rule (the LC-03 of LC-0311). He knows the typeface because he chose it, four years ago, for the template he had Diane use: Century Schoolbook, because it looked like a judge had written it. His thumb stops on the glass and stays. **This is the stillness Iris watches in ch08 S3: 2 min 11 s, coffee cooling in his other hand, the air system humming, a phone set face-down on the conference table behind him, room-service fries going cold on a tray by the window, the carpet smelling of everybody. He does not move.**
7. "Cam?" The deputy comms director (unnamed) wants to know if it's political. "Crank. Probably." Then: "Who's in the channel." Fourteen people. He asks who posted it. A boy near the window puts a hand half up: Theo Akers, junior digital. A contact at the county sheriff's office sent it to campaign security, security sent it to Theo, Theo thought everyone should see it.
8. Brandt's thumb saves the image to his own phone, then, as channel admin, deletes the post. The order is not quite a decision; he notices afterwards, the way you notice you have already locked a door. "Off your phones. All of it. Nobody has seen this." Theo starts to say something about the record, about keeping a record; Cam looks at him until he stops.
9. Campaign counsel stands at the glass with the city behind her, one hand flat on the window, and asks whether the paper in the mouth is "anything", without looking at the photograph. Cam watches her not look. "Paper." She drafted those agreements and has never seen what they look like torn up and wet. He thinks how many things have Diane's name on them, some of them his, and does not say that either. He finishes the coffee, cold now, drops the cup in the bin by the urn, puts the phone in the inside breast pocket of his jacket, left side, where the page was pinned on her, and buttons the charcoal jacket over it.
10. **[TURN]** He does not tell anyone what he has recognised. Not counsel, who wrote the agreements and would not know them in pieces; not comms, who want a sentence by six; not the Senator, who will be told in the morning that this was a tragedy and handed a statement that says so. In a room of fourteen people who all think they are working on the same problem, his grammar completes for the only time in the scene: he is already working alone, he began the moment his thumb stopped on the glass, and it does not feel like a decision at all. It feels like twenty years of habit, turned for the first time on the room he is standing in.

- **Sensory lead:** Taste first: tepid urn coffee and the wax lining of the cup. Then sound: a dozen people talking over the air system's hum, the small separate sound of a phone set face-down on the conference table. Texture: the phone glass under his thumb, the two-finger spread. Temperature: the coffee falling to room temperature across the scene. Smell is the hotel's, plain and unremarked: carpet "of everybody" at midnight, someone's room-service fries. Cam does not smell things the way Iris does.

- **The body:** His hands carry the scene. One holds a cup going cold. The other holds a thumb that stops on the glass and then acts before he does (save, then delete). He does not sit. Diane's body is seen only in the photograph and only in layman's terms: black tape across the eyes, the mouth packed full, "something she was no longer using."
