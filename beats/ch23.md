## Scene 1 — The Naming

1. First sentence: "I told him my name." Give the full name, in dialogue, as one line: "Iris Vale." It is the first thing she says to him on Election Eve (22 ended with her having no line). No preamble, no setting. 22's Snapper (the Marine between them on the table, facing him) is already in place, and the reader needs nothing more.
2. He sits again. He had come up out of the chair in 22; at her name he lowers himself back into the one upholstered chair by the dressing table, his jacket on the chair back behind him, the way a man sits down for a constituent. Positions in plain words: she is standing on the other side of the photograph, with her back to the service door she came through. Her hands are in pale nitrile. Her right hand is in the right outer pocket of the navy blazer, empty-handed, beside the roll of tape. Her left is flat against the outside of the blazer over the left inside breast pocket. She touches nothing in the room in this chapter.
3. He searches. It is visible and sincere, a man going through a drawer. His eyes go to her face, then down to the badge on her chest, which carries someone else's name, then back to her face. He doesn't mention the mismatch, or the gloves. Report it and do not interpret it.
4. Nothing. The pause is long enough for her to hear the ballroom through the wall: warm-up music, bass coming through the floor, a crowd being kept warm. Give it one sentence of sound and no more.
5. He apologises for his memory, in his best syntax: "I'm so sorry. I meet a great many people, and I'm afraid it shows." Then, ruefully: "It's the one thing I'm supposed to be good at." Let it stand. The reader knows he memorises staffers' children's names. Iris does not say so.
6. He offers his hand, the right one, leaning forward from the chair across the dressing table and past the photograph. It is the hand of a man who has shaken a million hands, palm slightly up, patient.
7. She doesn't take it. Her hands stay where they are: the right in the outer pocket, the left flat over the inside pocket where the third page and the hard case are. Report it flatly: "I didn't take it." He lowers his hand without offence, the way you put down a glass someone has declined. He makes no comment, and that is his grace.
8. The single flat statement the outline asks for, with no commentary: she has rearranged two years of her life around this man, and he has looked at her face and found nothing in it. Make it one short line of fact and do not elaborate it, so the flatness does the work. Something close to: "I had built two years around him. He had looked. There was nothing there." The drafter may find a better version, but it must stay this plain.
9. Pressure, one line, also flat: she has said her own name out loud in this building. Somewhere in it that name is on a list, in the hands of the FBI liaison she knows only as a line on page 4 of the run-of-show. She does not say Reyes, and she does not give the name a number. The fact sits in the paragraph like a dropped key.
10. **[TURN]** He used my first name back to me, warmly, as if he had just learned it. He had. Give his line as one word: "Iris." Then her single sentence, "He had.", and end the scene.

- **Sensory lead:** sound. The ballroom through the wall and the bass through the carpet. His voice in a small room, which is the instrument he is best at. The shampooed carpet from 22 is still there underneath, given at most one word. Temperature and taste are withheld, because the flat voice reports almost nothing from her own body.
- **Jokes / dry observations:** none. The outline makes this the one flat place in the book, so there is no joke. The reader should feel the floor go before they can say why. (If the drafter must have something dry, keep it in his mouth: "It's the one thing I'm supposed to be good at." He doesn't know it's funny. She doesn't laugh.)
- **The body:** gloved hands kept where they are, the right in the pocket and the left over the page and the case. She doesn't take his hand. She stands still. No report of pulse, heat or mouth, and their absence is the content.
- **Motifs:** hands (his offered, hers withheld and gloved); the Marine's photograph (face-up, facing him, between them, and his hand passes over it); the minute (her count running, not stated); the grade, by its total absence.

## Scene 2 — The Reminder

1. Voice comes back a little, but only on her side of the page. She decides to tell him, and narrates the decision in one sentence: she had always assumed the syllabus would begin with his face, and since it hadn't, she would hand him the evidence.
2. She tells him exactly what he did. Out loud it is clipped: four sentences in the order it happened. The date. The place. The door. What he did. No adjectives and no euphemism. She speaks the way she dictates a finding.
3. How to render the telling (fixed by the ledger): the reader hears the first three sentences verbatim (the June three years ago; a hotel room after an evening function, not this hotel; "You closed the door") and then the narration says only "Then I told him the fourth." The act stays unscripted on the page, consistent with every chapter before. The weight lands on the fact that she said it, to him, at last, in the voice she uses for strangers on a table.
4. A dry line on herself, the first allowed in the chapter: two years of drafts, and out loud it took four sentences. "I have dictated longer findings on a hand."
5. He listens. He is a superb listener and kind in small rooms (cast). He leans forward slightly. He doesn't interrupt. At some point during the fourth sentence, his right thumb and forefinger give his left cufflink, plain silver, a quarter-turn and his eyes go down to it for two seconds (the gesture fixed in Ch 1 and seen again in 22). Name the gesture. Do not say what it is to her. It is either a habit or a tell, and the book never says which.
6. He frowns. It is a small, troubled, careful frown, the frown of a man being asked about a constituent's case he hasn't been briefed on.
7. With what he believes is gentle correction: "I think you may be thinking of someone else." Give it exactly as the outline has it, said kindly.
8. The knowledge from 18, one line and no gloss: she hears the shape of that sentence before she hears its meaning, the same shape as the dead woman's, with its one stop before "known" ("I think we may have... known the same person."). Two women, three years apart, beginning the same way. Iris notices the rhyme and does not explain it. Then she waits for the metal taste that has always come when the original injury surfaced, and it doesn't come. Her mouth is dry. Keep it to a clause.
9. Whether he is lying the reader is never permitted to know, and neither is she. Stay in Iris: she looks at his face for the finding and the face gives her nothing she could testify to. Give one exact physical observation of him, such as the fleck of make-up powder beside his nose in the table lamp's light, or the frown already easing, and let it settle nothing. Her eyes also go, once and without purpose, to where the chair's arm meets its front leg, where there is a gap under the arm. She notes it as she would note a feature of a room (24's prose recalls "the gap I had looked at while he was telling me I was thinking of someone else"). No gloss, no plan.
10. **[TURN]** He glanced at the door. Not for rescue. For his minute. He was worried about the time. (In real time this falls at forty seconds. He has never gone over. Let the reader feel that his body knows the slot even though his memory doesn't know her.)

- **Sensory lead:** taste, by its absence: dry mouth, and the metal not coming. Then sound: her own voice in the small room, flatter than his, and the ballroom's bass under it. The single lamp on the dressing table lights his face from the side. There is no mirror in the room (the mirror motif belongs to 15, 19, 20 and 27).
- **Jokes / dry observations (Act III ratio, so use one, two at most):**
  - "Two years of drafts. Out loud it took four sentences. I have dictated longer findings on a hand."
  - Reserve, on his frown: "It was the frown of a man being told about a pothole in a district he didn't represent."
- **The body:** gloved hands where they were, the right closing once, on nothing, inside the outer pocket while she says the fourth sentence. Dry mouth. Her left hand moves from the inside pocket to the left-flank scar through the blazer when he says "someone else" (style 05: she touches it when she is thinking), then back. His hands: the cuff.
- **Motifs:** voicemail (the dead woman's sentence, echoed, and the knowledge from 18); hands (his cuff, hers gloved); the minute (his glance); the Marine's photograph (in his eyeline the whole time she talks: he listens to her across his brother); tape, by absence (the roll she doesn't touch).

## Scene 3 — The Refusal

1. The overrun begins, and only she is counting it. He brings his eyes back from the door to her with a visible, courteous effort: he is giving her time he doesn't have, and he wants her to know it.
2. He declines to be the antagonist. He does it as he does everything, generously and in whole sentences. "Whoever did that to you, I'm sorry. I mean that." He gives her sympathy as a bystander. He offers it to her as he would to a woman at a rope line with a story about her son.
3. He goes further, and it is the most self-aware he gets. He tells her he has done a great many things in his life he would do differently, and he can name them. "Most of them were votes." Render him as a man who has done many things and remembers almost none of them as wrong. He is not defensive. He is at ease. That is what is unbearable.
4. The offer: "Let me get someone for you. There are people here who are very good at this." He means staff. He means a quiet room, a person with a clipboard, a follow-up, which is to say a file. Iris hears Diane's whole function in one courtesy. One line of the knowledge from 18, unglossed: it is also exactly what she did with two voicemails. She does not draw the line. The reader does.
5. She says "No." Out loud, one word. He accepts it at once and gracefully, the way he lowered his hand in S1.
6. The syllabus does not anticipate this. State it as structure, not emotion. Lesson Three as written starts from his recognition and moves to his exposure. There is no clause for a man who will not stand where the page puts him. She knows every line of the page in her inside pocket, and none of them is addressed to someone who isn't there. Do not reproduce or quote the page (style 08).
7. The one dry line, turned on herself: she had prepared for denial, rage, bargaining, even tears. She had a contingency for a panic button. She had nothing for kindness. "I had planned for every response but the one he was best at."
8. Her hands, named plainly. The right in the outer pocket, beside the roll of tape. The left flat over the inside breast pocket, and under it, through the lining, the hard case: the syringe, beside the marker, the pin and the page. The reader can see her hands. She can't yet see what they are going to do. (Say nothing about what is in the syringe.)
9. **[TURN]** I didn't yet know what I was going to do, and for the first time in the book you were ahead of me, and I knew it. Use the direct address to implicate, as style 01 allows. This is the line, and it ends the scene.

- **Sensory lead:** temperature, hers alone. She is cold (cold since 18): the nitrile against her palms, the back of her neck. The page asserts nothing about the temperature of the room; no room-wide warmth or chill on the page. The bass through the floor goes on. Texture: the hard case under her left palm through the blazer's lining, its edge and its hinge, felt through nitrile.
- **Jokes / dry observations:**
  - "I had planned for every response but the one he was best at."
  - Reserve: "He offered to find me someone. The campaign had always been very good at finding someone."
- **The body:** cold, hers alone; right hand in the pocket beside the tape, left over the case and the page; standing very still. No hunger, no heat, no salivation. Don't name the absence. He is seated and at ease, hands loose on the arms of the chair, and he checks the door once more, briefly.
- **Motifs:** hands (hers, gloved, and the reader watching them); the syllabus page (in her inside pocket, unquoted, unpinned); the minute (the overrun); the Marine's photograph (still facing him, and he glances at it once as if for permission to be late); the meal after (its absence carries into the Snapper); voicemail (the "someone" she once got for two callers).

The sharpened version, per the ledger's Snapper ruling (drafters take it). The left hand goes in under the lapel to the inside breast pocket, where the case is, so the literal hand in the literal pocket makes the outline's simile physical and the reader feels that the only thing she can find in herself is the means. It is the last line. Do not add "appetite": the reader supplies it. Do not say what the case holds. (Outline minimum, superseded: *Thirty seconds on a clock only I was counting. No appetite.
