# Beat sheet — ch02: The Breach

- **Unit:** ch02 (`manuscript/ch02.md`)
- **POV:** Iris Vale, first person, past tense. The outline's Turns and Snapper are in present tense, so render them in past tense on the page.
- **Target:** ~3,150 words. Three scenes of ~1,050 each.
- **Act pressure:** Act I, The Prerequisite. The pressure is the campaign's certainty that nothing is coming, and in this chapter Iris is standing inside it. Nobody checks her badge, the staffer waves her past, Diane looks straight through her, and the campaign leaves its own connecting door unbolted. Every scene should make the reader feel that the system's arrogance is the hole she is walking through. Brandt doesn't come in until Chapter 8. Running under everything is appetite, present and unnamed: she eats off a returning tray, the metal taste comes, the hand trembles, and she smiles. The reader should feel it as a danger before she does.
- **Appetite at entry:** appetite as competence. She left the flat at the end of Ch 1 hungry and didn't eat. The decision has been made and the kit is out of the drawer. She tells herself tonight is recon, and therefore work, and therefore hers.
- **Appetite at exit:** it has come up four times, and each time it was misfiled. She eats a returning tray of lamb in a service stair and blames the skipped dinner. The metal taste comes with being invisible. Her hand trembles once, the first time since her second year of residency, and she describes it rather than reading it. In the lift there is a smile she didn't decide on. She hasn't connected any of it. The reader has.
- **Grading rule for this chapter:** Iris grades the staff (Scene 1), Diane at length in the syllabus voice (Scene 2), her own cover story, and the janitor (Scene 3). This is the chapter where the reader is meant to recognise the syllabus voice as Iris's own (style 08). Let it leak into her narration of Diane on purpose.
- **No mirrors:** the mirror motif belongs to 15, 19, 20 and 27. The lift in the Snapper has brushed-steel walls, not mirrored ones, and she knows she is smiling from the muscles of her face, not from seeing it. The War Room's glass shows the city, not her.

## Conflicts

None. The outline and the ledger agree. Constraints the beats keep to:
- Diane must not recognise Iris. Ch 6 S1 needs Diane to work out who she is only at the pump station.
- The planted access to the War Room has to exist by Ch 4 S2 ("HQ, seen through my planted access") and Ch 8 S3. Ch 3 is at home, so this chapter is where the access is planted. Interlude 2 requires that Brandt never detects it, which is why it's small, physical, and placed where nobody looks.
- Ch 21's Turn ("a credential check that didn't exist at the fundraiser … The hole is exactly where I said") needs this fundraiser to have no real credential check, and needs Iris to note the hole in so many words. Scene 1, beat 3 does both.
- Ch 1 fixed the baseline that her hands haven't shaken since her second year of residency, and held back the metal taste for this chapter's Scene 2 Turn. Both are used here for the first time.

## Scene 1 — The Approach

1. First page: she is already inside. The service entrance on the side street, an evening in late August, a cloned press credential on a lanyard against her sternum. It reads CLARE WHITLOW / PRESS, a real freelance byline she has borrowed. One clause covers the two nights it took to make, and one covers why the name: it belongs to a woman who exists, files rarely, and was, as far as Iris could establish, out of the country.
2. The fundraiser is a donor reception for the host committee in the hotel's mezzanine ballroom. It is the same downtown hotel whose high floor holds the War Room. Holt is on the road after Tuesday's debate and is due to join by video link. Diane's schedule, in the dossier, puts her on the road with him.
3. The press table: a volunteer with a box of lanyards who glances at the one Iris is already wearing and points. No list. Nobody checks. Iris states the hole outright, as she would dictate a finding, and it is the line Ch 21 calls back to: the door everyone walks through, because everyone already has a lanyard.
4. She leaves the press pen within four minutes and takes the staff corridor behind the ballroom, at the pace of someone slightly late, with her phone held face-up as if it had instructions on it. She has walked fifteen years of hospital corridors, and the corridor behind a ballroom is the corridor behind a ward: steel doors, fluorescent tubes, carts, and everyone with a lanyard presumed to belong.
5. A server comes back from the floor with a tray of lamb cutlets, frenched and barely touched, the salsa verde already skinning over. Iris takes four, with a napkin, without breaking stride. She eats them in the service stair and gnaws the bones clean. She reports this as physiology, because she skipped dinner, which she did.
6. From the mouth of the corridor she reads the staff the way she reads anything that comes in through the doors at work. The deputy finance director has the yellowed sclera and spider naevi of a man whose liver is filing a complaint. The advance man has bitten his nails to the quick and has a smoker's cough he doesn't smoke for. A comms woman stands with a scoliotic tilt at the shoulder that nobody has told her about.
7. Who she'd open first, and why. She ranks them by what she'd find inside, purely as professional curiosity. The finance man wins, for the liver. It is funny, and she knows it's funny. Then she admits, in one line, that this is not the ranking she came here to do.
8. A staffer in the corridor, twenty-something, with an earpiece and a clipboard, looks at the badge and not at her, and waves her through to the back of the ballroom. "Go on, Diane's coming down, she'll want the pen tidy."
9. [TURN] Diane's name in his mouth. She's here tonight, unscheduled. Iris lets it land as a fact about the schedule and then as what it is: the recon has become an opportunity. One short sentence. No plan yet.

- **Sensory lead:** smell. In the service corridor: chafing-dish fuel, beef jus reducing somewhere, floor cleaner with a pine note, the warm-laundry breath of the linen carts. Temperature: the ballroom's air-conditioning, set for men in wool, cold on her forearms. That is Act I cold, but chosen, so she likes it. Taste: lamb fat, the char at the bone, salsa verde gone a little warm.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - The deputy finance director's liver "was filing a complaint I could hear from the doorway, and like every complaint in that building, it was being managed."
  - "Nobody checks a lanyard. A lanyard is a promise that somebody else has already checked."
  - On the lamb: "Donors don't eat at fundraisers. Eating is what you do when you haven't already paid for the room."
- **Body:** eating, standing up in a stairwell (planning posture, style 05). Four cutlets, the bones gnawed. Cold on her forearms. Her hands are steady, holding her phone like a clipboard. The badge lies flat on her sternum.
- **Motifs:** the grade (the staff, ranked); tape (none yet); cold (the ballroom's, chosen); the meal, pre-echoed. She eats seriously and blames physiology before there is anything to blame it on, the second time after Ch 1's sandwiches.

## Scene 2 — The Observation

1. Diane comes down the main stair at 8:20. Visual contact for the first time in three years. Iris describes her briefly, saving the full appraisal for Ch 4: fifty-two, a slate suit, low heels, reading glasses pushed up into an ash-blonde bob, and the carriage of a woman who stands up straight because she was once made to.
2. Donors. She works the room in loops of ninety seconds each. She remembers which man gave the maximum and which only said he would, and she touches no one longer than a handshake. Iris grades it as technique. It is very good.
3. Holt comes up on the screen at the end of the ballroom by video link, for four minutes. Iris doesn't watch him. "I had seen the show." She watches Diane watch him, and Diane mouths a single word of his speech a beat before he says it.
4. A journalist (unnamed, from a real outlet) gets her at the rope line with a question about staff turnover in the Senator's office. Diane pauses once before her answer, the single trochaic hesitation (style 07; describe it, never spell it phonetically). The reporter reads it as thoughtfulness. Iris knows from the file what it is: a stutter beaten into a pause. The answer gives him nothing and makes him feel thanked.
5. The hallway, seen through the porthole window of the service door. Evan, a young staffer, is the one who told that reporter where Diane would be standing. Diane ends his career in thirty seconds without raising her voice. She uses his first name once. She tells him to leave his badge at the desk, and then she tells him to go home and eat something. She means the second thing, and that is the decent part.
6. She is never alone. Iris clocks it the whole time: in ninety minutes Diane is never more than an arm's length from a donor, a staffer or her phone. She reads one text and her face does something private for one second. Then it's put away. (Seed for Ch 3 S1's gap: no unsecured hour.)
7. The grade, in the syllabus voice, leaking into the narration on purpose: donors, distinction; press, distinction; personnel, distinction; decent in nearly every register. Then: fails. The reader should hear Lesson One in it without being shown the page.
8. Diane leaves the ballroom by the side aisle, past the press pen, and looks along the row of faces as she goes. She looks at Iris for exactly as long as she looks at the man beside her. Three years ago she sat across a conference table from Iris for forty minutes while Iris signed.
9. [TURN] Her eyes pass over Iris and register nothing. Invisible to these people. It is her advantage and the original injury, and she feels both. The second one tastes like metal. This is the first time the taste surfaces in the book. Name it exactly, as taste (style 06), and don't explain it.

- **Sensory lead:** sound. The ballroom roar at donor pitch, the string trio playing under it, the hiss of the video link's audio a half-second behind Holt's mouth, ice in glasses. Then the hallway, which is quiet behind the porthole glass: she sees Diane's mouth move and hears none of it, and reads it like a body. Taste comes last, at the Turn. Metal: the battery-on-the-tongue tang of a childhood dare, or foil on a filling. Pick one, and don't use the coin, which belongs to 17's blood.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - On Diane with donors: "She handled money the way I handle organs: by weight, with both hands, and with no illusions about where it had been."
  - On the firing: "Thirty seconds. I have seen slower deaths certified as sudden."
  - On the video link: "Holt was four minutes late to his own fundraiser, and the screen made him look taller, and I watched the woman who had arranged both."
- **Body:** cold (the AC again, now on her neck as well). Her hands are still at her sides; the lamb is in her stomach. The taste in her mouth at the Turn is the body reporting before she does. She does not swallow it away.
- **Motifs:** the grade (Diane, at length, the syllabus voice audible for the first time); the metal taste (first instance); cold. Hands go unremarked, held still and kept for Scene 3.

## Scene 3 — The Janitor

1. The service lift to the 31st floor. Behind a card-reader door, the corridor carpet changes from banquet to executive. The corner suite with glass walls is the War Room, dark tonight because everyone is downstairs. Next to it is the empty suite: campaign-booked overflow, used as a supply room. Its door is held ajar on the swing bar, as Iris watched a staffer leave it on her first pass up the service stair (Scene 1 recon, shown in one clause here). That makes it the campaign's carelessness, not luck, and she had banked on it.
2. The empty suite: cases of water, a rack of rope-line stanchions, lawn signs in shrink-wrap, a bed nobody has slept in under a drift of tote bags. She stages her tools on the desk the way she would lay out instruments: nitrile gloves, two button-sized camera-and-microphone units bought for cash, a roll of the black vinyl tape, and a small tablet for pairing.
3. The connecting door to the War Room. Her side has a deadbolt and so does theirs. She turns hers and tries the handle, betting that nobody bolts a door against themselves. They haven't. She names it as a grade on the campaign: the door everyone walks through and no one checks.
4. The War Room in the dark: glass on two sides, the city below in sodium, the conference table, the wall screen, the coffee urn by the door. Gloved, she fixes one unit behind the bezel of the wall screen, facing the table, and one under the lip of the credenza by the urn. Each gets one short strip of black vinyl. The tape's ratchet sound as it comes off the roll, which she heard at her desk in Ch 1. Two minutes. She bolts nothing behind her and leaves the door as she found it.
5. Back in the overflow suite, she is pairing the units on the tablet, with the War Room's dark table already on the screen, when the suite door swings in. A night houseman with a cart, about sixty, name tag DESMOND, here because the door was ajar.
6. She improvises. She is press and the campaign let her use the room to file. She has her shoe off, because she does in fact have a blister from the stair, and she says so. She turns the tablet face-down on the desk as if it were simply where she'd put it down. She is clipped out loud and greedy in the narration (style 07).
7. He looks at her properly, the only person tonight who has. He offers a plaster from the first-aid box on his cart. She reaches for it with her left hand, and the hand trembles once, a single fine oscillation at the fingertips, and then stills. He sees nothing. She sees all of it.
8. She grades him, out of habit and out of gratitude. He gets full marks for looking and full marks for the plaster. He props the door wider on his way out ("for the air") and goes.
9. [TURN] She has noticed the tremor. She doesn't know what to do with it, so she describes it to the reader instead, clinically: amplitude, frequency, duration, the left hand, the first since her second year of residency. She doesn't diagnose it. The address to "you" implicates the reader (style 01), because now the reader is holding it too.

- **Sensory lead:** texture and sound. Nitrile going on. The vinyl tape's ratchet and its dull sheen. The War Room's hush, with the HVAC and the urn's element ticking as it cools. The plaster's paper backing. Temperature: the suite's AC colder than the ballroom, and the glass cold where her gloved knuckle grazes it. Smell: the War Room's stale coffee, carpet shampoo, and the citrus of a whiteboard marker left uncapped. Iris registers that it isn't her red one.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - On the connecting door: "Nobody bolts a door against themselves. It is the founding principle of every institution I have ever worked for."
  - On her cover story: "It was a good lie, and it had the great advantage of a blister in it. The best lies are mostly true and partly bleeding."
  - On Desmond: "He looked at me as though I was there. I gave him an A. It was the only one I gave all night."
- **Body:** hands, which are the scene. Gloved and steady on the tape, then bare. The tremor, left hand, once. The blister on her heel is real, and the plaster goes on it after he's gone. Cold. She has eaten, so no hunger is reported, which leaves room for the smile in the Snapper.
- **Motifs:** hands (the lift in 2 is set up here); tape (black vinyl fixing the units, and the plaster from his cart, an adhesive given kindly); the grade (the campaign's door, her own lie, Desmond); cold.

## Snapper

In the lift down I held my left hand with my right, two fingers across the wrist the way you hold a patient's to count, until the doors opened. I was smiling. I didn't know when that had started.
