## Scene 1: The Autopsy

1. The suite, Monday morning, first case. Iris stands on the far side of the table, which is the observer's side, where Mara stood last week. She is gowned and ungloved, with her hands clasped behind her back like a visitor in a gallery. Mara has the near side and the knife. Diane lies on the steel under the overhead lights, and the first sentence lets the reader know that Iris has seen this body before from closer.
2. The campaign is already in the building. On the break-room television, glimpsed through the office door with the sound down, the 5:58 statement runs as captions: "A senseless act. Our hearts are with Olivia and the whole Pell family. We ask for privacy at this time." Iris reads it the way she read the ticker in ch01 and grades it as Brandt's work, from the syntax: three sentences, nothing a lawyer could object to, the daughter's name set where the eye lands, and privacy asked for as if it were being offered. Pass with distinction. It is the first time his name comes into the chapter, and it comes as a style.
3. External examination, read as a critic reads. Iris narrates her own work from the other side of the table: what was elegant and what was rushed. Elegant: no wasted bruise anywhere, and the paper packed exactly as she meant. Rushed: the left zip-tie went one notch tighter than the right, and the ligature furrow is deeper on that wrist. The ties themselves are in her possession, not the suite's; only the furrows are here to judge. She notes it the way she'd note a junior's error. Mara finds the ligature marks at both wrists and both ankles, measures and photographs each, and notes that the restraints were removed after death and taken: none were found at the scene or came in with the body. Iris listens to this the way she'd listen to a correct answer she'd set herself.
4. Mara lifts the black vinyl from the eyes. The adhesive comes away with lashes and leaves a residue on the lids and in the brows, darkened with grit from the pump-station floor. Mara uses solvent and swabs, and the swabs go into bags. Iris anticipated the lashes but not the residue. She runs her own list in her head, gloves and sleeves and hair, and finds nothing. She runs it again anyway.
5. Mara notes the adhesive line across the lower face and the torn hairs at the nape, where a tape was and is no longer, and a band of residue at each ankle. She does not say "removed after death" about the face; she photographs it. Then she takes the paper from the mouth in forceps-fuls, grey and soft and swollen with saliva. Each fragment goes into its own evidence bag. Iris watches the footers, LC-0311 under a thin rule, go off to the people who read paper. She is pleased, and she reports it as approval of the chain of custody.
6. Internal examination, briefly and exactly. Petechiae under the lids where the vinyl was. The congestion of a face that ran out of air. A small puncture over the manubrium where the pin went through, with no vital reaction round it: it went in after. (The page itself was taken at the scene and is not in the suite.) Mara opens the stomach: a thin residue, mostly emptied, but the blueberry skins are unmistakable, with porridge and coffee. Iris had assumed breakfast at about seven and finds she assumed right. As the smell comes up she finds she is hungry, and she reports it as a fact about the hours since she last ate and does not connect it.
7. The neck. Mara finds two small bruises, one on each side, at the upper border of the thyroid cartilage, symmetrical and fingertip-sized, with no fracture of the hyoid and no more force than was needed. She is quiet for a while. Then she says, carefully, that whoever did this has an unusual level of anatomical knowledge, because they knew exactly where to put their hands.
8. Iris agrees, in the tone she'd use for weather: "Yes. It looks that way." Underneath, for the reader, she says what this is. Someone has noticed her work, and it is the only review she has ever had from someone qualified to give it.
9. **[TURN]** Mara picks up the pen to write the finding on the worksheet and stops before one word. The pause is a single beat, and Iris hears Diane's hesitation inside it (ch06), the word she never got to hear. This time the word comes: *practised*. Mara writes it. It is in the file now. Iris is proud of her, without giving her a mark, and Iris is in the file. The reviewer's line at the foot of the worksheet is where Iris's own signature will go.

- **Sensory lead:** Smell first: chlorhexidine and cold iron under the extraction fan, then the gastric contents, sweet and sour with blueberry, which is what wakes her hunger. Sound: the fan, the forceps ticking on the steel tray, the small wet sound of paper coming out of a mouth, Mara's pen. Texture: the adhesive residue, tacky and gritted, and the paper swollen and soft like wet bread. Temperature: the suite's chosen cold on her forearms. Taste: nothing in her mouth yet, only the sudden saliva at the stomach contents.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - Most people have to die before they get a retrospective. "I got mine on a Monday, from the other side of the table, and the subject was the one who'd had to die."
  - On contamination: every pathologist in the office has an elimination profile on file, so that if a trace of one of us turns up on a body it can be written off as us. "It's the only filing system in the building designed to make you disappear, and I'd never once been grateful for it."
  - Agreeing with Mara: "I said it the way you'd say it looked like rain, to someone holding an umbrella."
- **The body:** Her hands are clasped behind her back the whole scene and never touch Diane. She notes she has never stood at an autopsy with her hands doing nothing, and that they are perfectly still, carried over from the ch07 Turn. There is the hunger at the stomach contents. She is alert, not tired, after twelve hours' sleep.
- **Motifs:** Tape (the vinyl lifted from the eyes, the adhesive line and torn hair at the nape where the industrial tape was, and surgical tape on the evidence labels). Hands (hers idle, Mara's working, and the bruises that are the print of Iris's own fingers). The grade (her own work, the statement, and Mara withheld). The meal after, in echo: Diane's last breakfast and Iris's hunger at it. Diane's stutter, heard again in Mara's pause.

## Scene 2: The Second Page

1. Home, Monday evening, with the light going sodium outside. She makes tea (strong, with milk), which is the small daily gesture and not the meal, and eats standing at the counter: rye toast, salted butter, two anchovies from a tin. She's planning, so she eats standing. She notes she is hungry again and sets it down to the long sleep and a day with nothing in it but one meal at three in the morning.
2. The drawer and the key. The Lesson Two page goes on the desk. She reads it for an hour. It's a callback, not a re-quote. One phrase at most, and the Evaluation can be named: *Mistook clarity for cruelty.* The insert itself belongs at the head of ch17. No red mark on it tonight (*Excellent.* is ch09).
3. Brandt's dossier, taken from the top of the stack where she keeps the file she is working and squared to the knot in the drawer floor, open beside the page. The original layer is two years old: typed sheets, long-lens photographs and her pencil annotations. Over it lie later additions in ballpoint: the divorce, the house sale that didn't happen, the records. A clipped set of the blind items he planted about her is held with a bulldog clip at the back. She doesn't reread them. She knows them by heart, and she says so flatly.
4. The facts, given with the pleasure of a good case history. Mingus, Dolphy, Coltrane, on vinyl only. Tuesday nights at the Tabard Club, a private club where he plays the piano for nobody in particular (a set from 9 to 11). Charcoal suits. The divorce he does not talk about, which she knows about because he doesn't. A long-lens photograph shows him leaving the Tabard on a Tuesday, just after eleven, collar up. She looks at his hands in it longer than she needs to.
5. She plays *The Black Saint and the Sinner Lady* through the laptop speakers, because it's on his list and she has never owned a record. It sounds thin and wrong. She turns it off after a few minutes. It's like being read a menu when you're hungry.
6. She tells the reader directly that she is interested in him in a way she was never interested in Diane. Diane was a file. Brandt is a reader: he is the one who will read the page and see what it is doing. She says this and moves on. She does not examine it, and the prose must not examine it for her.
7. She compares the two dossiers by weight in her hands. Diane's is thicker, and Brandt's is more handled: the corners are soft and the pencil is shiny where her thumb has been. She grades her own research and gives Brandt's file the higher mark for care, without asking why she took more care.
8. **[TURN]** In the margin beside the photograph of him at the Tabard there is a note in pencil, in her handwriting from two years ago: *the only one who'll understand it.* She doesn't remember writing it. She ages it the way she'd age a bruise: the slope of the hand is more upright than hers is now, and the graphite is soft and smudged. It is hers, and it is two years old, and she cannot find the night she wrote it. (End the scene here. Do not narrate putting the file back; ch09 S1 has it on top of the drawer, squared to the knot, "since Ch 8".)

- **Sensory lead:** Taste first: salt, the oily fish, butter on hot rye, tea too strong because she forgot it. Then sound: the Mingus, small and tinny through the laptop, and the silence after she shuts it off, which is better. Texture: the dossier's soft corners and pencil gone glossy under a thumb. Smell: paper and graphite, the tin of anchovies on the counter. Temperature: Act I cold in the flat after dark, her feet on the floorboards.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - "I can age a bruise by its colour to within a day. My own handwriting I could only get to within a year, which tells you which of the two I had spent more time studying."
  - On the divorce: "He didn't talk about it, which is how I knew it was the most important thing in the file. People talk about their kidneys right up until the kidneys are the problem."
  - On the laptop Mingus: "like having a menu read to you when you're hungry. Accurate, and beside the point."
- **The body:** Eating standing up (planning). Hands holding the two dossiers to weigh them, and her thumb on the soft corner of his. Cold feet. Hunger again, misfiled as the long sleep and the empty day.
- **Motifs:** Vinyl (his list, the Mingus she can only hear through a speaker; she has never owned a record). Hands (his, in the long-lens photograph, and her thumb on his file). The grade (her own research, Brandt's file marked higher). The meal (a small one, standing; the post-kill meal is over). Voicemail and mirrors: none.

## Scene 3: The Feed

1. Later on Monday night, in the dark, the clean laptop open on her knees. She tells the reader she has a recording of the War Room from Sunday night, and that she hasn't watched it yet. At 11:52 on Sunday night she was asleep, about ten hours into twelve; when she woke she cooked, and then she dressed for work, with the laptop closed on the desk. She saved it the way you save the best thing on the plate. She says that, and moves on.
2. The feed, from the unit in the wall-screen bezel, wide on the glass-walled suite on the 31st floor, facing the table. When anyone looks at the screen, they look into her lens, and the screen itself is behind her: she can never see what is on it, only faces turning to it. The timestamp in the corner opens at about 11:40 on Sunday night, twelve minutes before he comes in. A dozen staff, nobody sitting, faces lit blue from below by phones.
3. Panic. Comms wants a statement by six. Counsel says some version of "nobody say anything" four times. Iris grades the room as a body, by what it does in the order it does it: first denial, then noise, then the search for someone senior.
4. The photograph comes up on the wall screen; she knows it from the faces, every one lifted toward her. Staff photograph the screen with their phones, at an angle, before anyone knows what it is. Her page is being copied all over the room by people who think they are recording evidence. She reports her pleasure at being published as a professional observation about how fast the campaign leaks. (She does not see Theo's post or the channel; none of that is on the feed.)
5. Brandt is summoned. Someone says his name into a phone, and the room holds itself the way a room does when the person who decides is on his way. Someone says *activist* and someone else says *lunatic*, while they wait.
6. He comes in at 11:52 by the timestamp. He wears the charcoal suit with no tie, which tells her he came from home. She switches to the credenza unit by the urn to see him take a coffee without looking at it, then back to the bezel. She narrates him the way she narrated him in the file, and checks the file against the man: posture, hands, the way the room rearranges itself around him.
7. Containment. He says, "Phone. Not the wall." The wall screen goes dark, the glare goes out of the room, and faces are lit by phones alone. He opens the image on his own phone. She can see his face from slightly above. She cannot see what is on his screen.
8. He goes still. Iris times it the way she'd time anything: the coffee in his other hand, no blink she can find, his thumb not moving, two minutes and eleven seconds by the timestamp. She knows exactly how long the page takes to read, because she wrote it: under a minute, read slowly. Nobody reads it for two minutes. The only sound is a phone set down on the table, a small separate tock. Then someone off-camera says, "Cam?" and she pauses the feed before he answers.
9. **[TURN]** She watches him look at the photograph for a long time and go very still, and she doesn't know why. She says so plainly. She has no theory and doesn't make one up. It is the first thing on the feed she can't read. (Drafter: she must not point toward the interlude or say that she found out later. The outline's "the reader will, after the interlude" is an instruction to the drafter, not something Iris says. The not-knowing is the Turn, and the Turn ends there. Do not describe what he does after "Cam?", and do not describe him pocketing the phone.)

- **Sensory lead:** Sound first: the room's noise through laptop speakers, flattened and slightly delayed, voices over an HVAC hum, the *tock* of a phone set down on the table. Then light: the blue of phones from below, then the lit room after the wall screen goes dark. Temperature: the laptop warm on her thighs and the rest of her cold. Texture: the trackpad under her thumb. Taste: tea gone cold beside her, which she does not drink.
- **Candidate jokes / dry observations:**
  - The staff photographing the screen "the way tourists photograph the Mona Lisa: not to see it, but to prove they were in the room with it."
  - "Counsel said 'nobody say anything' four times, which is three more times than counsel says anything without billing for it."
  - On the room's anatomy: "twelve people, and between them the anatomical knowledge of a butcher's calendar."
- **The body:** Cold, apart from where the laptop sits. Her hands on the trackpad, and her thumb stopping on it when she pauses the feed. The rhyme with Brandt's thumb stopped on his glass is for the reader after the interlude, and Iris never notices it. Unconsciously she goes still while she watches him go still. There is no eating, and the tea is untouched.
- **Motifs:** Hands (the thumb). The grade (the War Room graded as a body, Brandt checked against his file). Tape, in the photograph only: the black over Diane's eyes, which she knows is there from the faces, at two removes. Vinyl, as a near-rhyme: a man in charcoal standing as still as a record that has stopped.
