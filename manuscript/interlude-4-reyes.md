# Interlude — Reyes

It was Saturday, and Halloween, and anyone who could be somewhere else was. The floor had its weekend sound: the ventilation, and down the row a copier on standby that cycled every few minutes.

Reyes had the two pages side by side under the desk lamp. Photocopies; the originals were in evidence, and she had read the copies until the toner had worn smooth at the folds. *Confused process for protection. Mistook clarity for cruelty.* Above each line a job title was circled in red. The same pressure both times, each circle closed in a single stroke. Whoever drew them had not hesitated.

She read the two lines the way she would have read an evaluation of herself. They were fair and they were specific, and they had been written by someone who had once been graded and had kept the paper. She went no further than that.

The pattern took two sentences. Two bodies, a month apart, each with a page pinned to the chest, a circled role, one typed line of judgement and tape across the eyes. Diane Pell's mouth had been full of paper from the campaign's own agreements, and every scrap of footer carried the same matter number under a thin rule. Carmichael Brandt had been opened on the edit desk in his suite and closed again, roughly, by someone who had not needed to look anything up. In her copy of the Pell file one word was underlined twice. *Practised.*

The profile had three terms, and she knew where each had come from. Medically trained: the two autopsy files. A signatory: the agreements in the mouth. Female: counsel, whose brief against the subpoena had argued for the privacy of "former female staff and volunteers". He had protected them so carefully that he had described them for her. She had held all three terms for a week, and in her head the killer had been *she* for most of it. What she had not had was a name.

The order had come down on Friday. Counsel complied at twenty to six on Saturday evening, two days before Election Eve. In a law firm's mouth, *promptly* meant the last hour before it became contempt.

The courier envelope was sealed with red tamper tape. She slit it with the letter opener, and it gave with a small, dry tearing sound.

One page. Weeks of motions for one sheet of paper, on better stock than anything the Bureau bought. Matter LC-0311 in the header. Eleven lines: a surname, an initial, a date of signing, a control number, a last known contact. Every date fell inside the same eight months, three years ago. The page did not say why. Clipped behind it was the protective order. The signatories were to be approached in a fixed and non-prejudicial sequence, which counsel had helpfully specified as alphabetical. Any departure went back to the judge. She read that paragraph twice.

Her coffee had been stewing since three. She held it for the heat. The weekend heating was set low, dusk had come up against the window, and her hands were cold, the biro mark on the right middle finger dark against them.

She laid the lab's reconstructions against the header, wet footers pieced back together. Three of them carried LC-0311. Someone had put the right paper in the right mouth. In another job she might have called what she felt pleasure. In this one it was a match, and she wrote it down.

The list had two holes. One line gave only a forwarding address abroad. The eleventh gave a disconnected number and an address years out of date. She marked both for counsel, and put in for background checks on all eleven. The system said three to four days. It was election week, and every agent in the building was on a rope line.

She stood at the window while the copier cycled and ate a fun-size chocolate bar from the bowl at the lobby desk, and noticed only when she was folding the wrapper. The clocks went back tonight. The government was giving her an hour, and she knew already where it would go.

The tenth line read *VALE, I.*

She knew the name the way she knew a signature block: the reviewer's line at the foot of the Pell file, and the office's one-line refusal to comment. She wrote *reviewer* in the margin, and a question mark, and stopped her pen. A name that fit was not evidence. It was a temptation, and counsel's order existed to punish temptation. Nine names came first. The sequence was the sequence.

She opened the paper desk calendar. Sunday. Monday morning, before she reported to the hotel for the Election Eve detail, which was where protection put you when it wanted you seen. Tuesday, between the polls opening and the returns. She skipped the woman abroad, since no approach could be made, and logged it anyway, because the paper was what would be read later. She filled the squares in her own hand, the names in block capitals, eight of them, the ninth noted and passed over.

Vale was tenth. Reyes booked her for the day after the election, Wednesday at ten, and wrote the name in the square in block capitals, the same as all the others.
