# Interlude — Reyes

Reyes had the two pages side by side under the desk lamp. It was Saturday, and Halloween, and anyone who could be somewhere else was. The floor had its weekend sound: the ventilation, and down the row a copier on standby that cycled every few minutes.

Photocopies; the originals were in evidence, and she had read the copies until the toner had worn smooth at the folds. *Confused process for protection. Mistook clarity for cruelty.* Above each line a job title was circled in red marker, the same stroke both times, each circle closed in one movement. Whoever drew them had not hesitated.

She read the two lines once each, the way she would have read an evaluation of herself, and wrote nothing next to them.

The pattern took two sentences. Two bodies, a month apart, each with a page pinned to the chest, a circled role, one typed line of judgement and tape across the eyes. Diane Pell's mouth had been full of paper from the campaign's own agreements, and every footer the lab could piece together carried the same matter number under a thin rule. Carmichael Brandt had been opened on the edit desk in his suite and closed again, roughly, by someone who had not needed to look anything up. In her copy of the Pell file one word was underlined twice. *Practised.*

The profile had three terms, and she knew where each had come from. Medically trained: the two autopsy files. A signatory: the agreements in the mouth. Female: counsel, whose brief against the subpoena had argued for the privacy of "former female staff and volunteers". He had protected them so carefully that he had described them for her. She had held all three terms for a week, and in her head the killer had been *she* for most of it. What she had not had was a name.

The order had come down on Friday. Counsel complied at twenty to six on Saturday evening, two days before Election Eve. In a law firm's mouth, *promptly* meant the last hour before it became contempt.

The courier envelope was sealed with red tamper tape. She slit it with the letter opener, and the tape tore louder than anything had all afternoon.

One page. Weeks of motions for one sheet of paper, on better stock than anything the Bureau bought. Matter LC-0311 in the header. Eleven lines: a surname, an initial, a date of signing, a control number. Every date fell inside the same eight months, three years ago. The page did not say why. Clipped behind it was the protective order. The signatories were to be approached in a fixed and non-prejudicial sequence, which counsel had helpfully specified as alphabetical. Any departure went back to the judge. She read that paragraph twice.

The weekend heating was set low, dusk had come up against the window, and her hands were cold. She warmed them one at a time on the metal shade of the lamp, the biro mark on the right middle finger dark against the knuckle.

She laid the lab's reconstructions against the header, wet footers pieced back together. Three of them carried LC-0311. The number she had been carrying since September had eleven names under it now. In another job she might have called what she felt pleasure. In this one it was a match, and she wrote it down.

The list had two holes, and counsel's covering note named them in a paragraph that was careful to be helpful. One signatory had only a forwarding address abroad. For the eleventh the firm held a disconnected number and an address years out of date. She marked both lines for counsel, and put in for background checks on all eleven. The system said three to four days. It was election week, and every agent in the building had been pulled onto it.

She stood at the window while the copier cycled and ate a fun-size chocolate bar from the bowl at the lobby desk, and noticed only when she was folding the wrapper. The clocks went back tonight. The government was giving her an hour, and she knew already where it would go.

She opened the paper desk calendar. Sunday. Monday morning, before she reported to the hotel for the Election Eve detail. Tuesday, between the polls opening and the returns. She skipped the woman abroad, since no approach could be made, and logged it anyway, because the paper was what would be read later. Then she filled the squares in her own hand, block capitals all the same height, the biro scratching between the copier's cycles, eight squares across three days, a ninth name noted and passed over, until the sequence came to Wednesday.

Vale was tenth. Reyes booked her for the day after the election, Wednesday at ten, and wrote the name in the square in block capitals, the same as all the others.
