# Cast & World

## Iris Vale, MD — Narrator

Senior forensic pathologist at a major-city Medical Examiner’s office. Mid-thirties, unmarried, a minimalist apartment whose only excess is a writing desk with a locked drawer beneath it and a kitchen she uses more than she admits. Roughly 1,400 autopsies. Death is her first language. She is funny about it in the way surgeons are funny — reflexively, defensively, and because it is, in fact, funny.

Skills earned legitimately: anatomy, toxicology, evidence handling, chemical access, knowledge of what investigators look for and therefore what to remove. Not a trained fighter. Her violence is research, preparation and patience expressed as physical action — and, she discovers, as pleasure.

Her body: she eats seriously after every kill and tells the reader it is physiology. She sleeps badly and well in alternation. She has sex in the book exactly twice, both times with strangers, both times after a kill, neither time narrated at length — the point is what she does the morning after, which is go to work. The knife wound in Chapter 12 is a presence from then on; she touches it when she is thinking.

She signed the campaign’s NDA three years before Chapter 1. The syllabus has existed in the drawer for two years. The cuff in Chapter 1 does not create it; it gives her permission.

> *Editorial note:* Placeholder name. Iris is precise without being cold-coded; Vale gives her a faintly funereal surname without overplaying.

## Diane Pell — Lesson One

Campaign Chief of Staff and Compliance Director. 52. Single mother of Olivia, at Wellesley. Overcame a childhood stutter through brutal discipline; still pauses once in a while in a way staff mistake for thoughtfulness. Sunday 4 p.m. calls to her mother; a Sunday reservoir walk to be unreachable. Believes the Candidate is all that stands between the country and worse. Manages the abuse of women like Iris as risk, not wrong. Holt knows Olivia’s name.

## Carmichael ‘Cam’ Brandt — Lesson Two

Lead fixer, lead spin doctor. 48. Two years divorced. Mingus, Dolphy, Coltrane on vinyl; Tuesday piano at the Tabard Club. Twenty years a fixer and knows exactly what he has become; has stopped lying to himself, which is why he lies so well to others. Recognises the typeface on the shredded paper in the dead woman’s mouth. Runs off-book surveillance through a shell company whose officer of record was Diane Pell. The only person in the book who finds Iris as interesting as she finds herself — which is why the scene between them is the love scene.

## Senator Holt Marchetti — Lesson Three

The Candidate, 54. Magnetic; kinder in small rooms than any of his staff. Brother Mark, a Marine, died in 2003; the loss drives a sincere veterans’ policy. One minute alone before every speech with Mark’s photograph, detail outside by standing instruction. Memorises every staffer’s children’s names. Did to Iris what was done. Whether he has forgotten or has metabolised it so completely that forgetting and remembering are the same thing to him, the book never confirms. The reader is never inside his head.

## Dr. Mara Lin — The Unwitting Witness

Junior forensic pathologist, eight years younger than Iris, being mentored by her and a little in love with the way Iris works. Performs Diane Pell’s autopsy because the Chief wants ‘a face that has never been near politics’. First to notice the injuries show practised anatomical expertise. Does not yet know what she has noticed. In first person, Mara is the reader’s stand-in — the person who admires Iris and is slowly working out what she admires.

## Hannah Vale-Sorensen — The Sister

Younger sister. Married, two children, a life Iris will not return to in the same way. Calls on weekends. Does not know what happened. By the end she knows something has changed, not what.

## Special Agent Eleanor Reyes — The Investigator

FBI, mid-forties, violent-crimes background, recently moved to a campaign-protection detail she did not want. Builds her case on paper — control numbers, the autopsy finding, the subpoena for the list. Forty-eight hours behind Iris when the book ends, nine of eleven interviewed. The reason a sequel is possible.

## Tessa Quinn — The Collateral

The real woman Brandt’s fabricated whistleblower profile points at, chosen for a resemblance to Iris. Doxxed and harassed inside forty-eight hours. Leaves one voicemail at the ME’s office asking for ‘the pathologist in the news’. Iris deletes it. Chapter 18 finds it beside the other one.

## Sloane Marchetti — The Wife

Present on Election Eve. Knew enough not to let herself know more. In Chapter 24 she is the name Holt does not say; the name he says is Diane’s.

## The Interludes

Five short close-third sections, 600–900 words each, set between chapters and headed with the character’s name: Diane (after Chapter 3), Cam (after 8 and after 13 — the second absorbs v3’s Chapter 14 and 15 Cam beats), Reyes (after 20 and after 26). Mara has no interlude; she is seen only through Iris. They never appear inside Iris’s chapters and never share a scene break with her. Each carries one Turn of its own. Chapter 16’s Cam beats from v3 are absorbed into Iris’s first person — she is in the room, she can see him.

> *Editorial note:* Flagged: the interlude scheme is mine. The alternative is pure first person with the other POVs cut and their information delivered through what Iris finds — surveillance, Diane’s phone, Brandt’s files. That is purer femgore and leaner. The interludes keep the reader’s dramatic irony — knowing what Brandt knows before Iris does — which the Dent structure leans on. Either works; the breakdown below assumes interludes.
