# The Psychological Anchor — The Designed Failure, and the Appetite Under It

She did everything right. She reported, she kept records, she signed. She learned the system was not broken but functioning as designed — to absorb women like her and protect the man. Justice, she concluded, is a fiction maintained to keep victims docile; her kills are corrections.

That is what she tells the reader. What the reader watches, from Chapter 1, is that she loves this. The morgue, the planning, the red marker, the kills, the enormous meals after. v3 interrogated the philosophy. v4 interrogates the pleasure: the philosophy is the syllabus’s cover story for an appetite, and Chapter 18 is where she hears, from a dying man, that the appetite had a cost she never looked at. She chooses to continue anyway — not because the philosophy holds but because she wants to. That honesty is the femgore version of the All Is Lost.
