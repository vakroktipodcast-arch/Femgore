# Brandt’s Constraint — Why Act II Is a Private War

Brandt knows who Iris is by the end of Chapter 9. Three walls keep him from ending it there, dramatised in Chapter 9 as three things he reaches for and puts down:

- No police. Identifying her means explaining the off-book surveillance and the NDA signatory list. Both are crimes; both end the campaign ten weeks out.
- No Holt. Holt cuts away anything that smells, and Brandt has watched him do it for twenty years. The moment Holt knows about ‘the old files’, Brandt is the problem.
- No clean kill. He does not know what she has or who she has told. A dead pathologist with a dossier in a safe-deposit box is worse than a live one. The Chapter 12 contractors are there to take her, not kill her — which is why her killing one of them is the first thing in the book that is not on the syllabus.
