# Voice Register

Iris’s sentences are exact, anatomically literate, dry, and alive. The v3 note ‘not cold — exact’ stands, and gains a third term: hungry. She notices like a pathologist and wants like a person who has been told for three years that she is not allowed to.

- Rhythm: short declaratives for action; long, subordinate, almost greedy sentences for the body — hers and others’. The long sentences are where the pleasure lives.
- Vocabulary: Latinate for anatomy, always — she cannot help it and it is funny; Anglo-Saxon and frankly obscene for appetite, sex, food, and the dead. The collision of the two registers in one sentence is the voice.
- Humour is required, not permitted. Iris is funny about bodies the way surgeons are — reflexively, defensively, accurately. One joke per page is a floor in Acts I and II. The jokes thin in Act III and the reader should feel the loss before they can name it.
- The syllabus voice — formal, evaluative, second-person, grading — is hers. It leaks into narration whenever she is in control: she grades Diane, Brandt, the janitor, Mara, the reader. It disappears entirely in Chapters 22–25, when she is not.
- She is an unreliable narrator about one thing only: why she is doing this. She is scrupulously reliable about everything else, including what she ate.
